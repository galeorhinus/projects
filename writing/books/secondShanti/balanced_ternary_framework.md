## The Balanced Choice Ternary

#### Satya and Truth

"*Satyameva Jayate*," which roughly translates as "Truth Alone Triumphs," is an ancient Sanskrit mantra, from the *Mundaka Upanishad*.  It serves as the national motto of India, prominently inscribed below the national emblem. More than just a phrase, it embodies a profound philosophical principle deeply embedded in Indian culture and values. It signifies an unwavering belief in the ultimate victory of *Satya*, which is often translated as truth but also represents righteousness and integrity.

This concept of *Satya* is perhaps best elucidated in the epic Mahabharata, where an insightful philosophical discussion unfolds between Yudhishthira and Bhishma, delving into the very nature of *Satya*.  Bhishma, on his deathbed of arrows, imparts timeless wisdom to Yudhishthira, who is grappling with the moral complexities of war and kingship. 

When Yudhishthira asks Bhishma for the definition of Satya, Bhishma responds with a couplet:

*Satyasya vachanam shreyah, satya-gnyanam tu dushkaram*

सत्यस्य वचनं श्रेयः सत्यज्ञानं तु दुष्करम् ।

It is always advisable to speak the truth, but trying to exactly understand what truth is, is difficult.

This statement alone offers a glimpse into the nuanced view Indic philosophy holds regarding *Satya*.

When Yudhishtira probes further, Bhishma tries to explain what Satya is in the broadest sense, and provides a foundational understanding of not only Satya, but the deeply empathetic worldview embedded in Hindu thought.

*Yat-bhoota-hitam ati-antam tat-satyam-iti dhaarana*

यद्भूतहितमत्यन्तं तत्सत्यमिति धारणा |

यत् भूतहितं अति अन्तं तत् सत्यं इति धारणा ।

That which is beneficial to *all* beings in every respect, can be considered as *Satya*.

Their conversation emphasizes that Satya isn't merely factual accuracy but is deeply intertwined with the ultimate welfare of all beings, highlighting a compassionate and pragmatic view of dharma.

सत्यं वद । धर्मं चर।

#### The Truth Binary

Further building upon the understanding of what Satya is, let's explore a very powerful concept that is embedded in the Sanskrit term: सतसत्विवेकबुद्धि \- Sat-asat-vivek-buddhi.  This term is a combination of four words, which roughly translate to:

* **सत् \- Sat:** Truth as described earlier   
* **असत् \- Asat:** Untruth or deception,   
* **विवेक \- Viveka:** Discernment or discrimination  
* **बुद्धि \- Buddhi:** Intellect or understanding

Sat-asat-vivek-buddh is a binary term, which can be interpreted as **"the intellect that discriminates between truth and untruth."** For the purpose of this discussion let's describe this term as the Truth Binary.   This truth binary has significant applications in *vendantic* and other philosophies, but at the most basic level it can also provide us an insight into how the human intellect differs from other beings.

While animals have been known to display a high level of consciousness, awareness and intelligence, humans have the ability to use the Truth Binary, which gives us a unique capacity for *intentional* deception and truth-telling.

In mathematics, binary logic is often represented with the use of 0 and 1, in our discussion we will use **\-1** for **intentional deception** and **\+1** for **intentional truth-telling.**  This allows us to build a balanced Ternary with zero representing a neutral or natural state.

#### The Choice Ternary

Let's use the Truth binary and use it in the context of choices that humans make, but add a neutral value of zero to represent the choices that not only humans, but all beings make.  Thus zero represents a neutral or a "natural" choice.

Thus \-1 would be the choices or actions that humans make that are intentionally deceptive or negative.  Zero would represent choices or actions that all animals make in nature and \+1 would be a choice or actions that are positive or are based on intentional truth telling.   Thus we have a Choice Ternary with values \-1, 0 and \+1.

This can be used to classify the impact of how human actions and choices affect other beings .
