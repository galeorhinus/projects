### Decisions made

- **Wave 1 / Wave 2 framing keyed to Pāṇini.** Wave 1 = pre-Pāṇinian (transmits the *Vedas* + *Vedāṅga* apparatus + the *padapāṭha* tradition + named pre-Pāṇinian grammarians whom Pāṇini cites). Wave 2 = post-Pāṇinian (transmits the formalized *Aṣṭādhyāyī* + *Trimuni Vyākaraṇam* apparatus). Each wave has inner (within subcontinent) and outer (transcontinental) reach. Replaces an earlier inner/outer-only framing that was insufficient.
- **Wave 3 (contemporary).** Transmits the *recovery* of the engineered apparatus — the engineering thesis itself — into a global discourse that has lost access to it. The *Atomic Sanskrit* book is itself a Wave 3 instrument. Readers are positioned as Wave 3 *ṛṣis* in potentia.
- **Agastya as canonical Wave 1 inner figure.** Vedic ṛṣi (Saptaṛṣi on the Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa list, co-author of RV 1.165–1.191 with Lopāmudrā). Travels south, composes the *Agattiyam* (first Tamil grammar). His recalibrant transmission was Vedic + *Vedāṅga* apparatus, NOT the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* (which had not yet been formalized in the Indic frame). The structural move is the same as Wave 2 (single expert recalibrating a new linguistic area through pedagogical transmission); the apparatus carried differs by phase.
- **Other Saptaṛṣis as Wave 1 outer roster.** Kaśyapa to Kashmir / northwest, Bharadvāja as the explicitly-grammarian ṛṣi, Bhṛgu and Aṅgiras lineages with possible Iranian extensions, Pulastya through Lanka, Atri. **Mitanni evidence** (Hittite-Mitanni treaty deities, Kikkuli horse-training Sanskrit numerals, Indo-Aryan throne names, *marya* warriors) as the historical-empirical anchor for transcontinental Vedic-tradition transmission.
- **Triple convergence on "British were *mleccha*."** Sidebar 4. The British uniquely fit *mleccha* in three senses simultaneously: Sanskrit technical (could not produce the *mūrdhanya* sounds), 17th-century Marathi political (foreign occupiers in Samarth Ramdas's frame), 20th-century Marathi political (Savarkar's audience). The AIT framework that imported Max Müller to assign the racial *ārya* role to Europeans was constructed by people who, in the Indic tradition's own three-fold classification, were themselves the *mlecchas*.
- **Samarth Ramdas verse as textual-tradition source for Savarkar's deployment.** The verse "बहुत लोक मेळवावे | एक विचारे भरावे | कष्टे करोनी घसरावे | म्लेंच्छांवरी ॥१४॥" is from Samarth Ramdas's strategic-advice sequence to Sambhaji Maharaj. Savarkar's 20th-century *mleccha-kṣaya-kari* deployment continues a Maharashtrian textual tradition with documented 17th-century lineage.
- **The Rigvedic call is the full mantra, not just the half.** Sidebar 5. *kṛṇvanto viśvam āryam* + *apaghnanto arāvṇaḥ* together form a complete program — make the world *ārya* (positive: pedagogical-civilizational extension) AND drive away the *arāvan*-illiberals (negative: clearing the structural opposition). The two halves are inseparable.
- **Progressivism = contemporary *arāvan* = "illiberal" in etymological sense.** Latin *liber-* (free, generous, open-handed) parallels Sanskrit *rā-* (give); English "liberal" once meant "generous" before its political-party narrowing; "illiberal" preserves the negation that *arāvan* names directly. Those who today call themselves "progressives" or "liberals" are exactly the opposite — they are the *arāvan*-illiberals (Pyramid + Centralized voice + Linear progressivism + Shepherding all sit on the *arāvan* side of the *arāvan*/*ārya* axis). Surface-generous (caring, redistributing, expanding rights), structurally ungenerous (every move flows through controlling frameworks).
- **Two-stage book structure for the *arāvan*/illiberal apparatus.** Ch2 §2.4 introduces *illiberal* / *arāvan* without yet revealing the Rigvedic mantra. Epilogue lands the full mantra and ties Ch2's contemporary identification back to its primary-source ground. Designed so the reader carries the framework forward through middle chapters and the loop closes at the close.
- **Śākalya *padapāṭha* and the named pre-Pāṇinian grammarians as documentary evidence.** Sidebar 6. Pāṇini cites Śākalya by name in multiple *Aṣṭādhyāyī* rules (1.1.16, 6.1.127, 8.3.18, 8.4.51). Pāṇini cites at least nine other named grammarians (Āpiśali, Kāśyapa, Gārgya, Gālava, Cākravarmaṇa, Bhāradvāja, Saunaga, Senaka, Sphoṭāyana). Bhāradvāja appears on both Sidebar 3's Saptaṛṣi roster AND Sidebar 6's named pre-Pāṇinian grammarians — the connection between the two traditions is direct.
