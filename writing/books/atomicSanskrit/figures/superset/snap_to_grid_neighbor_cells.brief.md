# Figure 8.6 — The Gaps Are Neighbors

**Where it lives:** Chapter 8 §8.9 ("Snap to Grid"), the closing reveal
of the southern subcontinent coverage discussion. §§8.6–8.8 ran the
three coverage surveys (southern, forest-belt, Indo-Iranian / Central
Asian) against Sanskrit's 23-cell base. §8.9 lands the structural
point those surveys reveal: the *gaps* — the southern field's missing
ल, स, श coordinates — are not failures. They are the moments where
Sanskrit takes a continuous neighboring zone and *snaps* it to a
crisp coordinate. Field-vs-coordinate, made visible.

**The argument the figure must land:**
The southern subcontinent's coronal / sibilant-lateral region is a
**continuous neighboring sound-zone**, not a set of discrete points.
Sanskrit's ल · स · श are **engineered coordinate choices** taken from
within that zone. The "missing cell" in the southern survey is not
absence — it is a different choice about where in the zone the
coordinate sits. The field gives zones. Sanskrit chooses coordinates.

**Title:** *The Gaps Are Neighbors*
**Subtitle:** *The field gives zones; Sanskrit chooses coordinates.*

---

## Layout

Two side-by-side panels with a restrained "snap" arrow between them.
Same horizontal weight, sharing one row of visual real estate.

```
┌──────────────────────────┐         ┌──────────────────────────┐
│   Left panel             │ ─snap─▶ │   Right panel            │
│   The neighboring zone   │  to grid│   Sanskrit's coordinates │
│   (continuous band)      │         │   (crisp cells)          │
└──────────────────────────┘         └──────────────────────────┘
```

## Left panel — "The neighboring zone"

A **soft, continuous gray band** running roughly horizontally across
the panel — the coronal / sibilant-lateral sound-zone of the southern
field. The band should feel like a *region*, not a set of points:

- Diffuse gradient in a single muted gray tone (e.g. **#d8d4cc**
  fading to **#ecebe7** at the edges)
- No hard boundaries — the band has soft falloff top and bottom
- A few **faint dots** or **ghost labels** scattered inside the band
  showing where neighboring sound-realizations sit — e.g. small
  italic ghost-text like *l-ish*, *retroflex-ish l*, *alveolar l*,
  *dental sibilant*, *alveolar sibilant*, *palatal sibilant* — all
  in light gray (**#a8a39a**), no boxes, no tile-grid

The point: this is **field**, not **grid**. No tiles. No coordinates.
Just a continuous neighborhood of sound-possibilities.

**Panel caption beneath:** *Continuous neighboring sound-region · no
fixed coordinates.*

## Middle — the "snap to grid" arrow

A clean horizontal arrow pointing left-to-right in **#2b2b2d**, with
a two-line label above:

- **Line 1 (bold):** *snap*
- **Line 2 (italic, smaller):** *to grid*

Optional tiny CAD / Illustrator-style grid icon **in the corner of
the arrow column** (small, ~10 pt, **#9a9384**), the way design tools
mark snap-to-grid mode. **Keep small.** The figure is about sound,
not about design software. If in doubt, drop the icon — the *snap to
grid* label alone carries the engineering analogy.

## Right panel — "Sanskrit's coordinates"

The same three sound-positions, now as **crisp Sanskrit cells**.
Three rounded tiles in the book's standard cell idiom, arranged
horizontally with clear spacing:

| ल | स | श |
|---|---|---|

**Cell style:** Match the book's existing tile idiom — rounded
rectangle, fill **#e6e3db**, no outline, Devanāgarī letter centered
in **#2b2b2d**. Same proportions as the App 4 / Figure 8.1 cells.

Below each cell, a **short italic gloss** in **#2b2b2d** at ~7.4 pt:

- **ल** — *lateral, regularized to the dental / front-coronal point*
- **स** — *front-coronal fricative, regularized to the dental
  sibilant point*
- **श** — *palatal member of the three-sibilant regularization
  (with स and ष)*

These glosses are short — the figure should not turn into a paragraph.
If a gloss runs long, prefer a one-clause version that names the
**point** the sound is snapped to, not the full phonological
discussion.

**Panel caption beneath:** *Three engineered coordinates inside the
zone.*

## Visual conventions

- Title: Gentium Book Plus or Charter, bold, ~10.9 pt at 4.5″ wide
- Subtitle: italic, ~8 pt

## Things to avoid

1. **Do not make the missing cells look like failures.** The whole
   point of the figure is that what the orthodoxy reads as "Sanskrit
   missing southern sounds" is actually *Sanskrit choosing
   coordinates inside a zone the southern field gives as continuous*.
   The left panel must read as *abundance of neighboring sound*, not
   as *empty space*.
2. **Do not show too many languages or too many ghost labels.** This
   is a conceptual figure, not another coverage survey. Three to six
   ghost labels in the zone is plenty.
3. **Do not put the zone in a box.** Boxes turn the zone into a cell.
   The zone has soft falloff and no boundary. That's the point.
4. **Do not use color accents.** Grayscale only — same palette as
   every other figure in the book.
5. **Do not over-decorate the snap arrow.** A clean horizontal arrow
   with the *snap / to grid* label. The CAD-grid corner icon is
   optional and should stay tiny if used.
6. **Do not show the full *varṇamālā* or any other grid in this
   figure.** The figure is about the field-to-coordinate move at
   exactly three sites: ल, स, श. Showing the full grid steals the
   reveal Ch 9 carries.
