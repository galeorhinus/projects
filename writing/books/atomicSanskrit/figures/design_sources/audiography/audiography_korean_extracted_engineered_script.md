## Shape and marker contents

**Single shape: Square (matching the comparative figure's Korean marker).**

- Solid darker grey fill (#888888)
- Thin charcoal border (~1pt)
- Slightly rounded corners (2–3px radius)

Each square contains:
- **Top (larger):** Hangul jamo (e.g., ㄱ)
- **Bottom (smaller):** Romanization in italic serif (e.g., *g/k*)

Korean Romanization conventions vary; use Revised Romanization (RR, the standard modern system) with phonetic notation in slashes where two values are possible (e.g., *g/k* for ㄱ which is [g] intervocalically, [k] elsewhere). For tense and aspirated consonants, use the conventions above (e.g., ㅃ = *pp*, ㅍ = *p'*).

**Hangul positioning:** Hangul jamo are generally centered well by default. Standard optical centering applies.

**Romanization positioning:** beneath the Hangul, centered, in italic serif at ~60–70% of the jamo size.

**Square size:** uniform across all cells. Same size as the squares in the comparative figure.

## Visual highlight: the three-way contrast block

The plosive 3×3 block (rows 1–3 × velar/alveolar/bilabial columns) plus the affricate 3-row column at Post-alveolar form Korean's distinctive **3-way feature contrast** (plain / aspirated / tense — *jeoncheong* / *chacheong* / *jeontak*). This is the architectural signature of Hangul's design and Korean phonology's structure.

Apply visual emphasis to this region:
- **Option A:** A thin rounded border around the plosive 3×3 block (rows 1–3, velar/alveolar/bilabial columns); separate thin border around the affricate column (rows 4–6, Post-alveolar)
- **Option B:** Very faint warm background tint (~3–5% opacity) inside both regions
- **Option C:** A combined treatment showing the affricate column as the "addition" to the plosive 3×3 structure

The visual goal: the reader sees Korean's signature contrast — *3-way plain/aspirated/tense at four places, plus three-way affricate at one place* — as a contiguous architectural block in the upper part of the matrix.

Optional caption near the block: *"the three-way contrast — jeoncheong · chacheong · jeontak"* — emphasizes the Hangul tradition's own framing.

## Consistency notes

This is one of three "extracted" figures, all paired with the companion comparative figure:

1. **The Sonomer Garland** — Sanskrit's *varṇamāla* in bead form
2. **Sound, Script, Standard** — three-language comparison (comparative figure)
3. **Sanskrit Extracted: The Sonomer Grid** — Sanskrit isolation with Pāṇinian classification
4. **Korean Extracted: Engineered Script, Existing Sound** (this figure) — Korean isolation with Hunminjeongeum classification
5. **Arabic Extracted: Codified Sound Tradition** — Arabic isolation with Makhārij classification

All five figures share typography, color palette, and the engineering thesis. The three "Extracted" figures form a triptych: each shows its language's selection from the shared articulatory field in that tradition's own classification system.

**The structural argument the Korean Extracted figure makes:**

Korean's Hunminjeongeum is one of the world's most explicitly engineered scripts (1446 CE, Sejong). But the phonology it encodes is a natural Korean language that pre-existed the script by centuries. The figure visualizes this asymmetry: a relatively compact inventory (only 6 places used) with a distinctive 3-way phonation contrast (plain / aspirated / tense) that the Hangul script's geometric shape system encodes systematically. The "tall thin" matrix shape — fewer columns than Sanskrit, fewer rows than Arabic — shows what an engineered script does when it formalizes a naturally-evolved phonology: clean axes, deep at few places.

The reader sees: **engineered script encoding existing sound**.
