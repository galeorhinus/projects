# Design Brief — The Vaidika / Laukika Chart (Ch 0)

> **For: Claude Design.** Produce **3–4 distinct iterations** of a single figure for Chapter 0 of *Atomic Sanskrit* under seeking.html. The figure has **two stacked halves that share one horizontal time-axis**: the **eternal vaidika** domain on top, the **cyclical laukika** domain below. Deliver each iteration as a clean SVG (see *Style* and *Print* below).

---

## The idea in one line
Above: the Veda — **eternal, one, timeless** (stretching with no beginning and no end). Below: the worldly cycle — **laukika**, the sign wave of clarity and darkness. The single vertical line **kaliyuga** is the hinge that runs through both halves: above it, the Veda's four streams sharpen into focus.

---

## Composition: two halves, one shared time-axis

- **One horizontal axis = time**, shared by both halves and running the **full width**.
- **Left end = अनादि (*anādi*, "beginningless"); right end = अनन्त (*ananta*, "endless").** At **both** ends draw a **"continues" break mark** (a small zig-zag / torn-edge glyph) to show the axis runs on forever in both directions. Put the Devanāgarī **and** IAST label under each end (अनादि *anādi* left; अनन्त *ananta* right).
- **Two vertical lines cross the whole figure**, aligned across both halves, each labelled *below* the figure:
  - **kaliyuga** (कलियुग) — near the **left** (see position below).
  - **today** (near the **right**).
- The vertical dimension is **NOT time** — do not let it read as a timeline. Vertically the top half is *structure/text-order* and the bottom half is *the cyclical wave*. Time is only the horizontal.

## TOP HALF — the vaidika domain (build fresh)

- **Four horizontal layers** spanning the full width (anādi → ananta).
- **Before the kaliyuga line:** the demarcations *between* the four layers are **blurry / soft / feathered** — the four are present but not yet separated (the undivided Veda).
- **At / after the kaliyuga line:** the demarcations become **crisp and clear**, and the four layers are **labelled** — the **four Vedas**, each with its associated **Brāhmaṇas / Āraṇyakas / Upaniṣads**:
  - **ऋग्वेद (Ṛgveda)** — with its Brāhmaṇas · Āraṇyakas · Upaniṣads
  - **यजुर्वेद (Yajurveda)** — with its Brāhmaṇas · Āraṇyakas · Upaniṣads
  - **सामवेद (Sāmaveda)** — with its Brāhmaṇas · Āraṇyakas · Upaniṣads
  - **अथर्ववेद (Atharvaveda)** — with its Brāhmaṇas · Āraṇyakas · Upaniṣads
- The **kaliyuga line is the moment of clarification** — the blurry→sharp transition happens *at that x-position*, aligned exactly with the laukika kaliyuga line below.
- Each layer runs the **full width** (anādi → ananta) and fades into the "continues" zig-zags at both ends — the Veda has no beginning and no end; only its *inner demarcation* changes at kaliyuga.

**Claude Design decides:** the vertical spacing of the four layers; how wide the blurry→sharp transition zone is; whether each layer is additionally sub-striped into Saṃhitā / Brāhmaṇa / Āraṇyaka / Upaniṣad sub-bands, or whether the Brāhmaṇa/Āraṇyaka/Upaniṣad association is carried in the label only; label typography and density.

## BOTTOM HALF — the laukika domain (extract from the existing SVG)

**Source SVG:** [`../yuga/yuga_combined_wave.svg`](../yuga/yuga_combined_wave.svg) (generated by `../yuga/generate_yuga_wave.py`).

**Extract ONLY these three things:**
1. **The plotted curve** — the SVG element `<path id="combined-wave">` (the combined sine wave). This is the laukika cycle.
2. **The two vertical marker lines** — one at the SVG's **kaliyuga** marker (x = 14,850 in the SVG's coordinate space) and one at **today** (x = 19,900).
3. **The two labels below** them: "**kaliyuga**" and "**today**".

**Ignore everything else in that SVG:** the background rectangle; the title text ("Combined Sine Wave…"); **all** gridlines (the vertical major/minor gridlines and the dashed horizontal y-gridlines); **all** x-axis tick numbers; **all** y-axis value numbers; the "x − 17,900" axis label; and the "Local y-range … Formula …" line. None of the numbers or the formula appear in the final figure.

**X-range = the span the SVG shows:** the plotted domain is x = 14,100 → 20,100. Map that whole span to the figure's width. Consequently:
- **kaliyuga** sits ~**12.5 %** in from the left; **today** sits ~**97 %** across (near the right). **Preserve these relative positions** (kaliyuga near the left, today near the right). The eternal continuation beyond each edge is shown by the anādi/ananta zig-zags, not by the numbers.

## Alignment (critical)
The **kaliyuga vertical line is the same x in both halves** — the top-half blurry→sharp transition must line up exactly with the bottom-half kaliyuga marker. The **today** line likewise sits at one x across the figure. Draw both verticals as single continuous lines (or clearly aligned segments) through both halves; label them once, below.

## Style
Match the existing yuga SVG's warm, printed-book aesthetic (see `../_shared/` for the shared palette / design-system):
- **Background:** warm cream, `#f7f3ea`.
- **Ink / curve:** near-black, `#1f1710`; wave stroke ≈ 4.2.
- **Marker lines / secondary:** muted brown, `#7f7666`.
- **Text:** `#4c453b` (labels), `#21190f` (headings if any); **font `EB Garamond, Georgia, serif`.**
- Palette family = the book's warm "calibration" tones. Devanāgarī must render cleanly at small size; pair Devanāgarī + IAST for anādi / ananta (and Devanāgarī कलियुग for kaliyuga).
- Keep it **quiet and architectural** — the geometry carries the argument. No decorative flourish.

## Print
Design the SVG to sit at ~**4.5 in** intrinsic width in the book (tune font sizes / stroke weights for that size; markdown will render it at width=100%). Landscape aspect similar to the source SVG (≈ 1200 × 520, i.e. ~2.3:1) is a good starting ratio, but adjust for the two-half stack (it may want to be a bit taller).

## What to vary across the 3–4 iterations (so the choices are visible)
1. **The blurry→sharp treatment** in the vaidika half — e.g. (a) soft gradient blend between layers before kaliyuga; (b) dotted/feathered boundaries that become solid; (c) overlapping translucent bands that separate; (d) a single band that visibly *splits* into four at kaliyuga.
2. **Layer labelling** — Veda-name only vs. Veda-name + Brāhmaṇa/Āraṇyaka/Upaniṣad, and left-margin vs. inline placement.
3. **The vaidika/laukika divide** — a hairline rule, a hand-drawn seam, or a soft gradient hand-off between the halves.
4. **The zig-zag "continues" glyph** and how anādi/ananta are set.

---

## Conceptual guardrails (so the meaning stays correct)
- **Eternal above, cyclical below.** The straight, layered band (vaidika) vs. the sine wave (laukika) *is* the argument — timeless vs. turning.
- **Vertical ≠ time.** Time is horizontal only. The four layers are text-streams, not epochs.
- **kaliyuga = the clarification, not the origin.** The Veda doesn't *begin* at kaliyuga; its four streams merely *sharpen into focus* there (the four remain eternal — hence the anādi/ananta continuation).
- Keep numbers out of the laukika half entirely — the wave and the two labelled verticals are all that survive from the source SVG.

## Optional (one iteration may explore)
A faint **fifth strand descending from the vaidika band into the laukika wave at the kaliyuga line** — the Itihāsa-Purāṇa ("fifth Veda") crossing from the eternal into the cyclical domain. Optional; only if it can be done without clutter.
