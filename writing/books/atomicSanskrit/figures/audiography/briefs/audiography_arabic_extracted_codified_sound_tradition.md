## Shape and marker contents

**Single shape: Circle (matching the comparative figure's Arabic marker).**

- Solid medium grey fill (#A8A8A8)
- Thin charcoal border (~1pt)
- Pharyngealized phonemes (ط ض ص ظ) may use a slightly thicker border to mark their emphasis status visually (optional)

Each circle contains:
- **Top (larger):** Arabic letter (e.g., ت) in naskh with full diacritical dots
- **Bottom (smaller):** IAST transliteration in italic serif (e.g., *t*)

**CRITICAL — Arabic font requirement:**

Use a standard Arabic naskh font with full diacritical dots: **Amiri**, **Noto Naskh Arabic**, or **Scheherazade New**. Calligraphic / rasm / kufic styles that omit dots are NOT acceptable. The dots are what distinguish ت from ب from ث from ن, ج from ح from خ, ف from ق, etc. Without them, the Arabic characters become unreadable.

**Arabic letter positioning:** Arabic naskh letters are visually right-aligned by default but should be centered in the circle. Apply optical centering — the visual center of Arabic letters may differ from geometric center due to baseline/dots.

**IAST positioning:** beneath the Arabic letter, centered, in italic serif at ~60–70% of the Arabic letter size.

**Circle size:** uniform across all cells. Same size as the circles in the comparative figure.

Empty cells display nothing — just background. Use:
- Faint grid lines between cells (very light grey, ~10% opacity)
- Slightly tinted backgrounds for rows belonging to the same manner-class group (alternating bands, very subtle — financial-table style)

## Visual highlight: the pharyngealized "emphasis" axis

The pharyngealized series (ط ض ظ ص — Arabic's distinctive emphatic consonants) forms a vertical band across rows 2, 4, and 12. These are concentrated at Dental + Interdental + Alveolar columns. This is Arabic's **unique structural axis** — the tafkhīm contrast that distinguishes it from Sanskrit and Korean.

Apply visual emphasis to this region:
- **Option A:** A thin rounded border around the pharyngealized cells, connecting them as a "vertical band"
- **Option B:** Very faint warm background tint (~3–5% opacity) inside the pharyngealized rows (rows 2, 4, 12) at the Dental/Interdental/Alveolar columns
- **Option C:** Both, plus a labeled bracket at the side: *"tafkhīm — the emphatic axis"*

The visual goal: the reader sees Arabic's signature structural feature — the systematic pharyngealization of dental/alveolar consonants — as a coherent vertical block.

---

## Visual highlight: the throat depth gradient

A second optional emphasis: Arabic's three-layer throat (ḥalq) classification shows up as the leftmost three columns (Glottal / Pharyngeal / Uvular). All three are populated almost exclusively by Arabic fricatives (rows 10–11) plus ء (row 1). This is the second distinctive Arabic feature.

- **Option:** A subtle background banding along the leftmost 3 columns at rows 10–11 to show the *ḥalq* gradient (deepest → mid → shallowest throat).

This is optional; skip if the layout reads cleanly without it.

## Consistency notes

This is one of three "extracted" figures, all paired with the companion comparative figure:

1. **The Sonomer Garland** — Sanskrit's *varṇamāla* in bead form
2. **Sound, Script, Standard** — three-language comparison (comparative figure)
3. **Sanskrit Extracted: The Sonomer Grid** — Sanskrit isolation with Pāṇinian classification
4. **Korean Extracted: Engineered Script, Existing Sound** — Korean isolation with Hunminjeongeum classification
5. **Arabic Extracted: Codified Sound Tradition** (this figure) — Arabic isolation with Makhārij / Ṣifāt classification

All five figures share typography, color palette, and the engineering thesis. The three "Extracted" figures form a triptych: each shows its language's selection from the shared articulatory field in that tradition's own classification system.

**The structural argument the Arabic Extracted figure makes:**

Arabic phonology was not engineered — it developed naturally in pre-Islamic Arabia and stabilized through codified preservation: Sibawayh's grammar (8th century CE), the Tajwīd recitation discipline for Quranic transmission, and centuries of formalized classification. The figure visualizes this asymmetry: a wide place-of-articulation reach (11 of 12 places used, including pharyngeal and uvular depths that no other tradition reaches), a sparse-density matrix (28 of 132 cells filled), and a distinctive pharyngealized axis (the tafkhīm series — ط ض ص ظ) that is structurally unique among the three languages compared.

The reader sees: **inherited phonology stabilized by recitation, grammar, script, and authority.**

The Arabic figure visualizes a **codified tradition**: the *tajwīd* discipline documented and preserved what Arabic already had; it did not engineer the phonology itself. This is structurally different from Sanskrit (engineered phonology + engineered script) and Korean (existing phonology + engineered script).
