*A Sanskrit-only extraction from the comparative figure "Sound, Script, Standard". Same matrix grammar (rows × columns × hexagons) but isolated to Sanskrit phonemes only and rendered with bilingual headers.*

## Row specification (7 rows, bilingual labels)

Each row label is **two-line**:
- Top line: speech-science description (regular serif)
- Bottom line: Sanskrit features in IAST (italic) + Devanagari (smaller)

| # | Speech-science | Sanskrit IAST · Devanagari |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plosive — voiceless, unaspirated | *sparśa · aghoṣa · alpaprāṇa* (स्पर्श · अघोष · अल्पप्राण) |
| 2 | Plosive — voiceless, aspirated | *sparśa · aghoṣa · mahāprāṇa* (स्पर्श · अघोष · महाप्राण) |
| 3 | Plosive — voiced, unaspirated | *sparśa · ghoṣa · alpaprāṇa* (स्पर्श · घोष · अल्पप्राण) |
| 4 | Plosive — voiced, breathy | *sparśa · ghoṣa · mahāprāṇa* (स्पर्श · घोष · महाप्राण) |
| 5 | Nasal | *anunāsika* (अनुनासिक) |
| 6 | Semivowel / Liquid (palatal · retroflex · dental · labial) | *antaḥstha* (अन्तःस्थ) |
| 7 | Fricative — voiceless | *ūṣman* (ऊष्मन्) |

**Manner-class groupings and dividers:**
- **Plosives** (rows 1–4): four cells filled per row (the 5×4 stop matrix is the *varga* heart)
- **Nasal** (row 5): the 5th of each varga, full row filled
- **Antaḥstha** (row 6): all four semivowels (य र ल व) in one row — Sanskrit classifies these as a single class, one per non-velar place
- **Fricative** (row 7): the *ūṣman* series

Horizontal dividers between rows 4, 5, 6, and 7.

**Note on the single antaḥstha row:** Sanskrit's Pāṇinian classification treats य र ल व as a single class of sonorants at slight contact (one phoneme per place of articulation: palatal, retroflex, dental, labial). The figure honors this single-class structure rather than splitting into the speech-science "Liquid" and "Glide / Semivowel" distinction used in the comparative figure. The four phonemes form a clean 1-1-1-1 distribution across four of five columns (no antaḥstha at velar in Sanskrit; ह is *ūṣman*, not antaḥstha).

## Hexagon contents — Devanagari + IAST

Each Sanskrit hexagon contains:
- **Top (larger):** Devanagari character (e.g., क)
- **Bottom (smaller):** IAST transliteration in italic (e.g., *ka*)

Both elements MUST appear. The two-script display anchors the figure for readers at multiple levels of Sanskrit familiarity — a Devanagari-literate reader reads the top; an IAST-fluent reader reads the bottom; a non-Sanskrit reader sees both as a single phoneme cell.

Sizing the hexagon: large enough that both Devanagari and IAST are comfortably readable. The hexagon will be slightly larger than the comparative figure's hexagons (more space available per cell in the 5-column layout).

**Devanagari positioning:** shift the Devanagari character DOWN slightly within the upper portion of the hex by approximately 5–8% to compensate for the *shirorekhā* top-bar (Devanagari's mathematical center sits above its visual center).

**IAST positioning:** beneath the Devanagari, centered, in italic serif at ~60–70% of the Devanagari character size.

**Hexagon size:** uniform across all cells.

## Empty-cell treatment

Empty cells display nothing — just background. Use:
- Faint grid lines between cells (very light grey, ~10% opacity)
- Slightly tinted backgrounds for rows belonging to the same manner-class group (alternating bands, very subtle — financial-table style)

---

## Varga block highlight (INCLUDE)

The 5 × 5 stops + nasals block (rows 1–5, all 5 columns) forms Pāṇini's *sparśa-varga* — the engineered stop grid. Apply visual emphasis to this 5×5 block to make the architectural core distinct from the *antaḥstha* + *ūṣman* rows below.

**Recommended treatment** (pick whichever reads cleanest at print resolution):
- **Option A:** A thin rounded border (charcoal, ~0.5pt, ~3px corner radius) around the entire 5×5 block, drawn outside the cells
- **Option B:** A very faint warm background tint (~3–5% opacity) inside the 5×5 block, distinguishing it from the lower rows
- **Option C:** Both A and B together for stronger emphasis

The visual goal: the reader sees instantly that the *sparśa-varga* (25 stops + 5 nasals = 30 phonemes) forms a complete symmetric grid — the engineered architectural heart of the Sanskrit consonant inventory. Below the highlighted block sit the 8 phonemes (4 antaḥstha at row 6 + 4 ūṣman at row 7) that complete the inventory.

A small caption near the block could optionally name it: *"the sparśa-varga"* or *"5 × 5 = 25 stops + 5 nasals"*. Optional; skip if it crowds the layout.

**The antaḥstha row (row 6) shows the 1-1-1-1 distribution** of य र ल व across palatal · retroflex · dental · labial — Sanskrit's symmetric one-per-place semivowel architecture. The empty velar cell in this row is structurally meaningful: ह occupies the velar position in row 7 (*ūṣman*), not row 6.

## Consistency notes

This is one of three companion figures in the book:

1. **The Sonomer Garland** — Sanskrit's *varṇamāla* in bead form with Om as convergence
2. **Sound, Script, Standard** — three-language comparison with the 12-column matrix
3. **Sanskrit Extracted: The Sonomer Grid** — this figure — the Sanskrit selection isolated from the comparative matrix

All three share typography, color palette, and the engineering thesis. This figure is the *bridge* between the comparative matrix and the Sonomer Garland — it shows the Sanskrit-only matrix structure as the engineered selection from the broader articulatory possibility space.

The reader's journey across the three figures:
- Sonomer Garland → "this is the Sanskrit varṇamāla as a strung garland of beads, with Om at the convergence"
- Sound, Script, Standard → "here is the same Sanskrit content in matrix form, placed alongside Arabic and Korean for comparison"
- Sanskrit Extracted → "here is the Sanskrit content alone in matrix form, with both speech-science and Pāṇinian classification labels"

The three figures together teach the *varṇamāla* through three different visual rhetorics: organic (garland), comparative (matrix), and isolated-engineered (grid).
