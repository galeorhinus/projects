# Generativity Reporting Ladder

This document preserves the incremental path from Sanskrit's semantic atoms to its wider generated word-space. It is a reporting record for later use in the book. It does not replace the underlying ledgers or their stricter methodological reports.

The eventual body passage should reveal the architecture in stages. Each stage must tell the reader:

1. what Sanskrit starts with;
2. which operation is added;
3. how many word-meanings that operation contributes;
4. what the cumulative subtotal becomes;
5. one familiar example;
6. one less obvious but meaningful example; and
7. what has still not been counted.

## Reader-Facing Language

The research record must retain grammatical names such as **सनादि**, **उपसर्गः**, **कृदन्त**, **तद्धित**, **स्त्रीप्रत्ययः**, and **नामधातुः** because those names identify the operations actually counted. The body should not expect readers to know them. It should lead with the work performed by an operation and give its Sanskrit name only afterward, when the name helps the argument.

For example, the body should not begin with “three general **तद्धित** relations.” It should first explain that a noun can generate further words for a quality, a state, possession, descent, origin, use, measure, or custom. It may then identify this large family of noun-derived formations as **तद्धित (taddhita)**.

Every major stage should use an example pair:

- a familiar word whose construction the reader may never have noticed, such as **दर्शन (darśana)** from ⟪दृश्⟫ (*dṛś*, to see), or **आगमन (āgamana)** from आ (*ā*) + ⟪गम्⟫ (*gam*, to go);
- a less familiar but immediately understandable formation, such as **श्येनायते (śyenāyate)**, “acts like a falcon,” or **ज्ञानवत् (jñānavat)**, “possessing knowledge.”

The familiar example establishes trust. The unfamiliar example reveals generative capacity. Neither should be included merely because it is convenient; both must come from the ledger for the operation being reported.

Inflected grammatical cells must remain separate from word-meanings. A later report may say that the lexical engine generates a stated number of word-meanings and then show how conjugation and declension expand those words across tense, mood, person, number, gender, and case.

The figures above are exact research subtotals. After all intended layers are complete, ordinary body prose can describe the sequence without requiring the reader to know the grammatical categories:

> Sanskrit begins with roughly two thousand verbal atoms. Operations that express causation, desire, repetition, and intensity, together with a first layer of prefixes, expand them into more than three hundred thousand verbal meanings. Allowing two ordered prefixes carries the count beyond one million.

> The language can then turn those verbal meanings into actions, agents, instruments, qualities, obligations, and other noun-like or descriptive meanings. **दर्शन (darśana)**, “seeing,” and **कर्तव्य (kartavya)**, “something that ought to be done,” are familiar results of this operation. These layers carry the bounded count beyond two million before a finite verb has been conjugated, a noun declined, or a compound assembled.

> A generated noun can itself become the starting point for another operation. Sanskrit can form new meanings for quality, state, possession, descent, origin, use, measure, and custom. **कर्तृत्व (kartṛtva)** names the quality or state of being a doer; **कौन्तेय (Kaunteya)** identifies a descendant or son of Kuntī. This layer carries the bounded count beyond 4.7 million word-meanings.

> Sanskrit can also turn nouns back into verbs. **नमस्यति (namasyati)** means “offers honor or worship”; **श्येनायते (śyenāyate)** means “acts like a falcon.” The first bounded count of this operation carries the subtotal beyond 7.9 million. These are still generated meanings and verbal bases. Conjugation has not yet begun.

> The current noun and adjective inventory can also generate a negative counterpart for each meaning. **ज्ञान (jñāna)** becomes the familiar **अज्ञान (ajñāna)**, “absence of knowledge.” **कर्तृत्व (kartṛtva)**, “agency or the state of being a doer,” can become **अकर्तृत्व (akartṛtva)**, its absence or negation. Counting this operation once per eligible meaning raises the bounded subtotal beyond 11.1 million. The initial **अ-** or **अन्-** depends on the following sound; it does not create two meanings.

> A finished ending does not reveal which operation produced it. **अग्नीषोमीय (agnīṣomīya)** and **अग्नीषोम्य (agnīṣomya)** express the same kind of relation through two different suffixes. **तावत्क (tāvatka)** expresses purchase for a stated measure. Recovering twenty-four relations stored in source helpers raises the bounded subtotal to 11,116,430.

> The source also gives hundreds of ordinary examples under named rules. Returning each active example to its governing rule recovers its meaning before the engine verifies its form. **मूल्य (mūlya)** can mean something acquired for a stated price or something equal to that price. **कथम् (katham)** asks “how,” while **तत्र (tatra)** means “there.” After repeated examples and earlier overlaps are removed, this source-example pass adds 236 word-meanings and raises the bounded subtotal to 11,116,666.

> The comparative and superlative rules then allow those nominal meanings to become new starting points. **कर्तव्यतर (kartavyatara)** expresses a greater degree of what ought to be done, while **कर्तव्यतम (kartavyatama)** expresses the highest degree within the intended comparison. The general suffixes apply across the nominal inventory. Rule 5.3.58 limits two alternatives to the traditional गुणवचन class; they remain outside the count until that class is independently identified. After one earlier overlap is removed, this layer adds 1,588,155 word-meanings and raises the bounded subtotal to 12,704,821.

> Agent nouns can then express a female agent through a feminine suffix. **कर्तृ (kartṛ)** becomes **कर्त्री (kartrī)**, and **दातृ (dātṛ)** becomes **दात्री (dātrī)**. This pass reconstructs each source operation before generation rather than guessing from a finished spelling. After two earlier overlaps are removed, the layer adds 140,566 word-meanings and raises the bounded subtotal to 12,845,387. Feminine agreement in participles and adjectives remains for the separate grammatical-form count.

> Sanskrit can also join complete members under a stated semantic relation. **राजन् + पुरुष (rājan + puruṣa)** produces **राजपुरुष (rājapuruṣa)**, “the king's man,” while **ग्राम + गत (grāma + gata)** produces **ग्रामगत (grāmagata)**, “gone to the village.” The present pass counts 113 unique active relations directly demonstrated in the pinned source tests and raises the bounded subtotal to 12,845,500. It does not yet estimate Sanskrit's recursive compound capacity.

> A compound can itself become the input to another operation. **महापुरुष (mahāpuruṣa)**, “a great person,” produces **महापुरुषत्व (mahāpuruṣatva)**, “the state or quality of being a great person,” **महापुरुषवत् (mahāpuruṣavat)**, “possessing or characterized by a great person,” and **अमहापुरुष (amahāpuruṣa)**, “one who is not a great person.” Applying one further established operation to the 96 nominal compounds adds 958 meanings and raises the bounded subtotal to 12,846,458. The experiment stops after that second derivational step.

That paragraph is a reporting model, not final manuscript prose.

## Completed Grammatical Expansions

These capacities describe grammatical views of existing lexical meanings. They do not raise the 12,846,458 word-meaning subtotal.

|---|---:|---:|
| 4,540,848 लौकिक action meanings | 45,408,480 cells across ten time-and-mood settings in the familiar singular citation form | 408,676,320 cells across ten settings, three speaker roles, and three number values |
| 8,084,287 लौकिक name, object, quality, state, and relation meanings | 8,084,287 citation cells | 194,022,888 cells across eight relations and three number values |

The verbal matrix attaches परस्मैपदम् and आत्मनेपदम् outputs to one semantic coordinate rather than doubling it. The name-form matrix uses gender to generate the output but does not automatically triple the coordinate count. Complete unchanging words enter neither matrix.

The [integrated synthesis](generativity_six_pass_synthesis.md) now combines those two matrices with the **7,925** complete unchanging meanings. The resulting लौकिक capacity is **602,707,133 semantic grammatical cells**:

> 408,676,320 action-form cells + 194,022,888 name-form cells + 7,925 unchanging-word cells.

This is a replacement view of the **12,633,060 लौकिक lexical meanings**, not an addition to them. The **213,398 Vedic meanings** remain a separate lexical inventory. The [publication cascade](publication_capacity_cascade.md) carries the reader from the 2,634 starting atom-meanings through the bounded 12,846,458 lexical subtotal and then through the लौकिक grammatical expansion.

## Reporting Discipline

- Never replace an incremental explanation with a single large number.
- Lead with what an operation does; introduce its Sanskrit grammatical name afterward, if the name is needed.
- Distinguish an added layer from the cumulative subtotal after that layer.
- Call the unit a **word-meaning** until inflection begins.
- Report तिङन्त and सुबन्त cells as grammatical expansion, not new lexical meanings.
- Keep लौकिक and वैदिक subtotals visible before combining them.
- State the operation boundary beside every subtotal.
- Preserve explicit exclusions and unresolved operations; do not treat them as either generated or prohibited.
- Use examples that are present in the ledger being reported.
- Keep a familiar example and a surprising example for every major operation family.
