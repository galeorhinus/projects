## Canonical-source principle

**Chapter notes are canonical for argument structure.** The draft is canonical for prose-level voice and concrete examples, and supplies content that has not yet been written into the notes at prose density. Where the draft and the notes conflict, the notes win unless the draft contains a substantive argument the notes have lost track of.

## Tags used in this document

- **IN-VOICE** — Ready or near-ready prose. Pulls into the destination chapter with light edits.
- **LIFT** — Argument is sound; voice register needs adjustment (more polemic, hammer close, scene-pivot, etc.).
- **SURVEY** — Currently surveying or cataloging rather than arguing. Needs structural revision before insertion.
- **STRUCT** — Structural problem in the draft itself (numbering, duplicate heading, missing content).
- **EXTEND** — Argument is correct but needs extension (comparativism, additional civilizational anchors, hammer line).
- **CONFLICT** — Conflicts with a settled decision in the chapter notes; needs revision against current notes before insertion.
- **STUB** — Research or analytical work still required, not merely a prose pass.
- **RESOLVED** — Originally a CONFLICT or other open tag; resolution applied. The accompanying note describes how.

**Section VII disposition note (the heaviest revision job).** This section is currently structured as a six-paragraph survey ("For the learner / for the student of grammar / for computational linguistics / for comparative linguistics / for the larger thesis / for the civilizational vocabulary"). The voice calibration explicitly warns against this structure: "Don't write both-sides essays. Always offer the orthogonal third option." The fix is structural, not cosmetic — Section VII needs to be reshaped from a *catalog of benefits* into an *argument about what becomes possible* once the architectural framework is accepted. Recommended approach:

1. Open with VII-1 (the architecture-vs-vocabulary reframe).
2. Lead with VII-5 (the genealogical-vs-architectural reframe) as the *primary* benefit, since it is the orthogonal third frame the calibration calls for.
3. Compress VII-2, VII-3, VII-4 (learner / grammarian / computational linguistics) into a single dense paragraph that *commits* to specific consequences rather than enumerating audiences.
4. Cut VII-6 and VII-7 (redundant with Ch1 and Ch5 respectively).
5. Compress VII-8, VII-9 (redundant with Chs 12, 13).
6. Close with VII-10 → VII-11 hammer.

This produces an Epilogue benefits section of ~four to five paragraphs rather than eleven, and converts the survey into an argument.
