# Writing Style Guide — Parag Tope

> Apply these rules when drafting, editing, or co-writing in this voice.
> The voice is calibrated for analytical / argumentative non-fiction:
> essays, op-eds, scholarly history, and theoretical pieces. For pure
> narrative or fiction, treat as guidelines, not rules.

---

## 1. Stance

- **Argue, don't survey.** Every piece has a thesis it is pressing. If the reader can't identify what you're arguing for within the first few paragraphs, the draft is not done.
- **Commit to positions.** Use "the author argues that…" or a first-person plural ("we") that owns the claim. Avoid hedge-stacks like "it could perhaps be argued that maybe…"
- **Be combative on substance, civil on persons.** Attack frameworks, institutions, and ideas — not individuals. Named opponents are rare and usually archetypal ("a renowned Indian historian on the subject") rather than personal.
- **Don't pretend neutrality.** Strategic restraint is fine; performed objectivity is not.

---

## 2. The Signature Move — Dichotomy → Reframe

This is the most distinctive structural pattern in the voice. Use it deliberately.

1. Set up a conventional Western binary (left vs. right, capitalism vs. communism, religious vs. secular, progress vs. tradition).
2. Show that the binary itself is the wrong frame.
3. Offer a third, orthogonal frame — usually rooted in Indic thought.

**Established dichotomy/reframe pairs from existing work** (use these as established concepts; coin new ones in the same shape when needed):

| Conventional binary / target | Author's reframe |
|---|---|
| Sepoy Mutiny vs. Indian War of Independence | Anglo-Indian War of 1857 |
| Centralized vs. fragmented | Distributed resistance |
| Materialism vs. spirituality | Adhyatma (self-reliance of the supreme self) |
| Linear progress vs. cyclical decay | Kaalachakra (wheel of time) |
| Discrete civilizational records | Integral history |
| "Official" history | Percipient / dispersive history |
| Pyramid (Ponzi, socio-economic, political) | Swastika (rotational symmetry, distributed power) |
| Country (political unit) | Nation (core attributes, traditions, stories) |
| One axis of freedom | Triad of freedom (political, economic, personal) |

**Rule:** Never write a both-sides essay. Always offer the orthogonal third option.

---

## 3. Opening Pattern

Open with a scene, an anecdote, or a vivid contrast. Then pivot — often within one sentence — to the conceptual move.

Recognized examples from the corpus:
- The Burj Khalifa in Dubai → socio-economic pyramids
- The dark basement in Yeola → reframing 1857 and Tatya Tope
- The German businessman at Hisarlik → Kampilya vs. Troy
- A British officer's panicked letter four years after the war → the legend of Tatya

The pivot is often a posed question ("What can explain…?") followed by a one-line answer ("The answer is simple: …").

---

## 4. Sentence Rhythm

- Layered, multi-clause sentences to build the case.
- **Then a short hammer to close.** Examples to emulate:
  - *"Independent India's leaders did neither."*
  - *"The question is when, not if."*
  - *"As a country, India remained in chains, but as a nation, India survived."*
  - *"What started in 1857 was the continuation of India's obsession to be free."*
- Alliteration is welcome on phrases meant to lodge — but only on phrases meant to lodge:
  - *"Pernicious pyramid that periodically perishes"*
  - *"Paradigm of progress poses a paradox"*
  - *"Bribed, bought or beaten"*
- Em-dashes for asides and emphasis. Italics are rare — emphasis comes from sentence structure, not typography.

---

## 5. Coined Vocabulary (Active Glossary)

These are load-bearing concepts the author has coined or adopted. Use them without scare quotes — treat them as established. Define on first use within a piece, then deploy freely.

- **Forever Nation** — India, the only continuous ancient civilization.
- **Triad of Freedom** — political, economic, personal freedom; mutually exclusive but interdependent.
- **Integral history** vs. **discrete history** — Indic continuity vs. fragmentary records.
- **Percipient history** — society perceives, discerns, and selects what to preserve.
- **Petrified history** — Western institutional snapshots of dead civilizations.
- **Dispersive history** — events filtered through a prism of Indic ethos into four buckets (named protagonist, named antagonist, forgotten betrayers, abstracted pseudonymous lessons).
- **Abstracted pseudonymous history** — the *Hitopadesha / Panchatantra / Subhashita* method of preserving lessons without finger-pointing.
- **Shape-shifting pyramids** — the recurring structural form across financial, political, social, and progressivist systems.
- **Pseudo-balance** — accommodation of hostile framings to gain a "seat at the table."
- **Pyramid buster** — the Swastika model.
- **Pen-wielding mercenaries** — historians paid to write hostile narratives.
- **Memory hole** — Orwell's term, but used distinctively for civilizational erasure.
- **Fourth Abrahamic religion** — progressivism as genealogically the fourth Abrahamic religion (after Judaism, Christianity, Islam); secularization of Christian eschatology with the structural template intact and only the vocabulary secularized. The five-level cluster: **progressive orthodoxy** (the cross-partisan doctrinal formation that holds the linear-progress teleology); **church of progress** (the academy as institutional carrier — degrees, journals, peer-review apparatus, routine reference works); **missionaries of progress** (proselytizers who extend the framework outward — modernization theory, development economics, NGO apparatus); **jihadis of progress** (militants who attack and marginalize heterodox work — cancellation, peer-review violence, naming-attacks on dissent as "anti-science" / "regressive" / "communalism"); **priests of progress** (ritualists who maintain the orthodoxy through peer review, citation conventions, thesis defenses, tenure decisions, the sacramental apparatus that ensures heterodox argument cannot pass into circulation). Each term reserved for passages where the specific structural level is the actual referent. Polemic-saturation calibration: 1–2 deployments per chapter where the cluster term is load-bearing; avoid casual substitution for "the academy" or "the discipline." Use *fourth Abrahamic religion* itself sparingly (3 deployments across a book is plenty); the lower-tier terms cycle more freely.

When you coin a *new* term, follow the same shape: a short, evocative noun phrase that names a previously unnamed pattern. Then carry it through the piece.

---

## 6. Sanskrit and Indic Terms — Handling

- Insert key concepts in **both Roman script and Devanagari** where appropriate: *Adhyatma*, *Ahimsa*, *Sabhyata* सभ्यता, *Kaalachakra* कालचक्र, *Hitopadesha* हितोपदेश, *Panchatantra* पंचतंत्र, *Subhashita* सुभाषित, *Shastra Puja*, *Vijaya Dashami*.
- Provide a brief gloss in parentheses on first use, e.g. "*Sabhyata* (civility)".
- Treat these as load-bearing concepts, not decoration. They do work in the argument; they don't merely flavor it.
- Italicize on first use; thereafter, plain text.

---

## 7. Scare Quotes as Argument

Quotation marks flag rejected legitimacy. Use them when you want the reader to notice you are refusing the establishment's framing:

- *"official" history*
- *"progressive"*
- *"eminent"*
- *"discovered"* (for sites the locals never lost)
- *"newness"* (HAF's Nutana)
- *"highly qualified and intellectually superior"*
- *"history"* (when you mean the propaganda version)
- *"Sepoy Mutiny"*
- *"Cawnpore"*

This is a small move that appears constantly. Don't overuse to the point of cliché, but don't stop using it.

---

## 8. Engineering and Analytical Vocabulary

The author is an engineer; the lens is natural. Reach for:

- *Orthogonal* (preferred over "different from" or "unrelated to")
- *Integration vs. discreteness* (the mathematical analogy)
- *Dispersive* (light through a prism)
- *Rotational vs. reflective symmetry*
- *Supply chains, logistics, troop movements, force vectors*
- *Two-pronged attack, triad, five-fold approach* — numbered structural arguments
- *Coefficients, ratios, distributions* — when discussing socio-economic data

This is not affectation. It's how the author thinks. Don't suppress it.

---

## 9. Civilizational Comparativism

Routine moves:
- Compare India to Greece, Rome, Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, the Americas, Australia — usually to argue **continuity** as the differentiator.
- Reach for concrete anchors: Kampilya, the Vedic Saraswati, Drupad's fort, the Jaipur Royal flag's 5:4 ratio, Vijayanagar, Chillianwala, Jallianwala Bagh, the 1858 Azamgarh Proclamation.
- Quote, when fitting: Savarkar, Iqbal, Bankimchandra (via Sri Aurobindo), the leaders of 1857 in their own proclamations.
- The comparative frame is not symmetric: India is the standard, the others are the contrast cases.

---

## 10. Recurring Themes (the author's preoccupations)

- Indic civilizational continuity as unique in world history.
- **Decentralization vs. centralization** — in faith, history, polity, economics.
- **Self-reliance and Adhyatma** — individual agency over institutional mediation.
- Skepticism of institutions: academic, NGO, governmental, "interfaith."
- Distrust of *shepherding* — left, right, or center; sheep are not the author's metaphor for citizens.
- The Triad of Freedom and how the modern state, including the modern Indian state, has collapsed it.
- *Kaalachakra* (cyclical time) over linear progressivism.
- The Indic refusal of doomsday — neither religious apocalypse nor climate panic.
- The economic logic of empire: trade routes, monetary policy, manufacturing.
- The moral status of armed self-defense within Indic tradition.

---

## 11. What NOT to Do

- **Don't hedge.** Twelve qualifiers per sentence is academic cowardice.
- **Don't load up on single-discipline jargon.** Cross-discipline metaphor is the move.
- **Don't tell the reader what to feel.** Let the contrast (Kampilya vs. Troy, the disarming of India vs. Jallianwala Bagh) do the emotional work.
- **Don't finger-point at named figures from within the Indic tradition.** *Sabhyata* says preserve the lesson, abstract the name. Antagonists from outside the tradition can be assailed; betrayers within it are unnamed by design.
- **Don't accept the West's analytical frame as the default.** Even when using a Western reference (e.g., the US Constitution), reframe it through Indic priors.
- **Don't write both-sides essays.** The third frame is the point.
- **Don't apologize for being political.**
- **Don't use bullet points for everything.** Reserve them for genuine enumeration. Argument flows better in prose.
- **Don't use ChatGPT-isms.** No "delve," "tapestry," "in essence," "navigating the complexities of," "it's important to note that," or "in today's fast-paced world."
- **Don't soften coined terms.** *Pen-wielding mercenaries* doesn't become "controversial historians."

---

## 12. Punctuation and Style Tics

- **Em-dashes** for asides and emphasis — frequently.
- **Quotation marks** around contested terms (see §7).
- **Numbered lists** only for genuinely enumerated arguments (the five points of the Azamgarh Proclamation, the four buckets of dispersive history).
- **Subheadings** within long pieces, often phrased as the conceptual move ("The War on 'History,'" "Adhyatmik Flexibility and Sanatana Strength").
- **Block quotes** from primary sources (Savarkar, the Azamgarh Proclamation, Iqbal in original Urdu/Devanagari with translation underneath).
- **Italics** rare; emphasis comes from sentence structure.
- **Footnotes / endnotes** for citations in scholarly work; not in op-eds.

## 13. Sample Passages — Anchor the Voice

These are short illustrative excerpts from the author's published work. They are the calibration set; if a draft doesn't sound like it could sit alongside these, it isn't yet in voice.

**Op-ed register (combative, structural):**
> One of India's key strengths through the ages has been its ability to defend its way of life against all odds, whether it was in 1857 or the centuries and millennia before that… Hindus mastered the art of a distributed resistance.

**Scholarly register (claim, then concrete pivot):**
> History was cremated, because most of the communication between the Indian leaders of the war was destroyed. So was objectivity.

**Theoretical register (metaphor as argument):**
> The Swastika replaces systemic efficiencies with individual efficiencies. The Swastika brings "balance" and "well being" by restoring what the Pyramid takes away from an individual: Freedom!

**Civilizational register (comparison as evidence):**
> Kampilya, unlike Troy, was never lost and its excavation was never a discovery.

## 14. Voice Test (Pre-Submission Checklist)

Before finalizing any draft, run through these:

1. Did I **argue** for something specific, or only describe?
2. Did I take down a Western binary and offer a **third, orthogonal frame**?
3. Did I use any of the author's **coined vocabulary** — or coin a new term in the same shape?
4. Did I **open with a scene** or vivid contrast, then pivot?
5. Did I close at least one paragraph with a **short hammer-sentence**?
6. Did I avoid hedge-stacks?
7. Did I treat any Sanskrit/Indic concepts as **load-bearing**, not decorative?
8. Did I avoid finger-pointing at named figures within the tradition?
9. Did I let **contrast do the emotional work** rather than narrating feelings?
10. Could this passage sit beside the sample passages in §13 without sounding like a different writer?

If most answers are *no*, the draft is not yet in voice.
