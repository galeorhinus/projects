# Part VI — Dispelling Rāhu

*Not descended, not sibling.*

---

With the architecture in the light, the eclipse-device itself comes into view: Rāhu — PIE, the imaginary ancestor placed over the Sun. Part VI removes the blocks that subjugate Sanskrit to it.

The writing-label and early-literature reductions have now fallen with the earlier distortions. The remaining core obstructions are the descent-story and the sibling-language story, the two that subordinate Sanskrit to an invented ancestor.

Chapter 17 locates Sanskrit's mouth, grammatical postures, and architecture of order in the Indian subcontinent. Chapter 18 then asks what any account of Sanskrit's origin must explain. Genealogical models can compare linguistic forms, and genetic studies can compare samples, but neither can assign authorship of a linguistic architecture. The chapter places the pyramid's reconstruction beside an honest speculation that admits what remains unknown and examines what the preserved design requires.

Chapter 19 turns directly to PIE. It follows the imaginary ancestor from Schleicher's fable into dictionaries and classrooms, then tests it against Sanskrit's atoms and grammar. The Radiance Thesis reverses the direction: Sanskritic words and methods travel outward through people, while receiving languages reshape what reaches them into reflections of the calibrant.

By treating descent as the only possible explanation, PIE prevents the reader from investigating who engineered the recorded architecture. Once the reader recognizes Sanskrit as calibrated fractal architecture, PIE functions as enclosure rather than explanation: a head with no body.
