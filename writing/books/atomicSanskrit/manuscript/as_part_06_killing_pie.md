# Part VI — Dispelling Rāhu

*Not descended, not sibling.*

---

By the beginning of Part VI, five core blocks have fallen. Two remain. The block marked ***Descended*** represents the claim that Sanskrit inherited its architecture from PIE. The block marked ***Sibling Language*** places Sanskrit beside Greek, Latin, and other languages as equal descendants of that imaginary parent. Together, the two claims place PIE above Sanskrit and direct every similarity backward to the imaginary parent instead of asking whether Sanskrit's recorded architecture traveled outward.

PIE functions as Rāhu in the eclipse sequence: an imaginary head placed between Sanskrit and the observer. Part VI examines the two claims that keep it there.

The three chapters approach the origin claim from different directions.

Chapter 17 locates Sanskrit's speech anatomy, characteristic grammatical choices, and architecture of order in the Indian subcontinent. Chapter 18 then asks what any account of Sanskrit's origin must explain. Comparisons among words can identify relationships, and genetic studies can compare samples, but neither can identify who engineered a language. The chapter places the pyramid's reconstruction beside an honest speculation that states what remains unknown and asks what Sanskrit's preserved design requires.

Chapter 19 turns directly to PIE. It follows the imaginary ancestor from Schleicher's fable into dictionaries and classrooms, then tests the construction against Sanskrit's atoms and grammar. The Radiance Thesis offers the opposite direction. Sanskritic words and analytical methods travel outward through people, while other languages reshape what reaches them and preserve partial reflections of the calibrant.

Part VI tests whether descent from PIE is necessary to explain the similarities among Sanskrit and Eurasian languages. If outward contact can explain those similarities, the account no longer needs an imaginary ancestor. Rāhu loses his place over the Sun: the head has no body, and the two blocks that held it there can fall.
