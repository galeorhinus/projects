# Part V — The Sun Does Not Decay

*Calibration from within.*

---

The fractal architecture has now been built. Whether it survives is the next question.

The reader can now examine how Sanskrit builds from sound to sentence. Part V asks how that architecture endured. It targets two reductions: the claim that Indic writing must be filed under an external typological label, and the claim that the *Vedas* are mainly early literature, old texts useful for dating "Vedic Sanskrit."

![Figure E.9 — The Sun Does Not Decay. The blocks marked Botanical, Codified, and Alphabetic have fallen. Part V targets the blocks marked Abugida and Early Literature.](figures/eclipse_spine/eclipse_50_part05_no_decay_removed_2_3_4_targets_5_7.svg){#fig:eclipse-part05-no-decay width=100%}

The Vedas stand at the center of this movement as Sanskrit's primary calibrant. Their exact preservation keeps a large body of Sanskrit audible and available for comparison across generations.

Chapter 13 treats preservation as an engineering problem. It explains why writing alone was insufficient, distinguishes aural engineering from generic oral tradition, and exposes the external *abugida* label.

Chapter 14 develops the calibration matrix and shows how correction can remain distributed without giving an apex custody of the standard. Chapter 15 follows that matrix into trained hearing and the eleven पाठाः (*pāṭhāḥ*), where the architecture remains audible in living practice.

Chapter 16 brings the system together. The वैदिक (*vaidika*) domain preserves the received corpus exactly, while the लौकिक (*laukika*) domain keeps the same language available for new expression. Those complementary responsibilities defend Sanskrit against the two enemies introduced in Chapter 6: gradual entropy and deliberate asuric attack.

Anti-entropy becomes practice across the fractal. The Sun does not decay.
