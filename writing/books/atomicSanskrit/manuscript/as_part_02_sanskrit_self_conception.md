# Part II — The Sun's Own Account

*Created, anti-entropic, calibrated.*

---

Part I cracked the first three blocks by exposing the claims they represent. Part II now removes two of them. The block marked ***Botanical*** treats Sanskrit as a naturally changing organism. The block marked ***Codified*** claims that Pāṇini later stopped that change by imposing order. Part II tests both claims against the categories and procedures preserved within Sanskrit. As Chapters 5 and 6 establish what Sanskrit is and how it resists change, the two blocks fall. The block marked ***Descended*** remains cracked; Part VI will examine the ancestry claim directly.

Part II sets the pyramid's classifications aside and examines Sanskrit through the categories and procedures preserved within the language itself. Sanskrit belongs in the **Generative Architectures** quadrant because it is engineered and highly generative. The pyramid's account places the वैदिक (*vaidika*) domain among **Natural Languages** and the लौकिक (*laukika*) domain among **Petrified Languages**, then presents the two domains as languages from different periods. Part II shows that they belong to one wholly created, anti-entropic, and calibrated architecture.

The two chapters examine Sanskrit's established bonds and its protections against change.

Chapter 5 begins with सिद्ध (*siddha*): the bond between word and meaning is established rather than produced by later convention. Grammar regulates how speakers use that inherited architecture. It does not create the language or the bond between its words and meanings.

Chapter 6 asks what such an architecture must survive. अपभ्रंशः (*Apabhraṃśa*) identifies a departure from the established form. Entropy allows small departures to accumulate through ordinary use, while asuric attack tries to damage or capture the architecture deliberately. The chapter distinguishes deviation within Sanskrit, drift within a natural language, and divergence away from a calibrant. It then shows why Sanskrit needs an invariant Vedic calibrant distributed across society alongside a domain open to new expression.

Together, the two chapters establish three qualities that the Botanical and Codified blocks concealed. Sanskrit is wholly created, engineered against entropy, and able to identify and correct departures from its established forms. It does not begin from drifting words. It begins from established bonds and preserves the forms against which speakers can recognize a departure. Part III next examines the sounds Sanskrit selected and organized within the speaking body.
