# Part II — The Sun's Own Account

*Created, anti-entropic, calibrated.*

---

Now the Sun speaks for itself. The question is no longer what the pyramid said about Sanskrit, but what Sanskrit's own grammar says about the order it preserves.

The earlier chapters exposed the pyramid's game. Sanskrit belongs in the **Generative Architectures** quadrant because it is engineered and highly generative. The pyramid removes Sanskrit from that category, places the वैदिक (*vaidika*) domain among **Natural Languages**, and places the लौकिक (*laukika*) domain among **Petrified Languages**. It then presents two domains of one architecture as two languages from two periods. This is category theft.

Part II turns to Sanskrit's own account: wholly created, anti-entropic, and calibrated.

![Figure E.6 — Category Theft Exposed. The first three blocks are cracked as Sanskrit's own account comes into view.](figures/eclipse_spine/eclipse_20_part02_sun_account_cracked_1_2_3.svg){#fig:eclipse-part02-sun-account width=100%}

Chapter 5 begins with सिद्ध (*siddha*): the bond between word and meaning is established, not produced by later convention. Grammar acts upon that inherited architecture. It regulates use without creating either the language or the bond.

Chapter 6 asks what such an architecture must survive. अपभ्रंशः (*Apabhraṃśa*) identifies a departure from the established form. Entropy allows small departures to accumulate through ordinary use, while asuric attack tries to damage or capture the architecture deliberately. The chapter distinguishes deviation within Sanskrit, drift within a natural language, and divergence away from a calibrant. It then points toward the required response: an invariant Vedic calibrant held within a distributed architecture that remains available for new expression.

Together the two chapters restore three of the Sun's own qualities: wholly created, anti-entropic, and self-correcting. Sanskrit does not begin from drifting words. It begins from established bonds and preserves the ability to identify a falling-away. The next part descends into the speaking body and examines the sounds Sanskrit selected and organized.
