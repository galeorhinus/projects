# Part IV — The Sun's Atoms

*Particle, atom, molecule, assembly.*

---

By the beginning of Part IV, the blocks marked ***Botanical, Codified,*** and ***Alphabetic*** have fallen. Part IV does not target another block. It has a different purpose: to demonstrate the architecture that those three claims concealed. The chapters follow Sanskrit's construction from organized sound to semantic atoms, completed words, and sentences.

![Figure E.8 — The Sun's Atoms. Three blocks have fallen. Part IV examines how Sanskrit builds semantic atoms from sound-particles.](figures/eclipse_spine/eclipse_40_part04_sun_atoms_removed_2_3_4.svg){#fig:eclipse-part04-sun-atoms width=100%}

The phrase ***the Sun's atoms*** refers to the धातवः (*dhātavaḥ*): stable semantic units from which Sanskrit generates larger forms.

Chapter 10 shows how sonomers enter timed scaffolds and become धातुः (*dhātuḥ*) atoms. It tests those atoms against the six characteristics of a सूत्र (*sūtra*) and shows that the same design discipline recurs at several scales.

Chapter 11 follows the atom as it becomes a completed verb. Chapter 12 extends the construction into other words and complete sentences. Vedic examples demonstrate the same grammatical operations at every level. The Vedas preserve those examples as Sanskrit's calibrant, while the लौकिक (*laukika*) domain uses the same operations to create new expression. Pāṇini made the recurring procedures explicit; he did not create them.

The sequence is particle, atom, molecule, and assembly. Sanskrit builds each larger form from smaller constituents that remain recoverable within it. The same relationship recurs from sonomer to धातुः (*dhātuḥ*), from धातुः to word, and from word to sentence. That recurrence is the linguistic fractal demonstrated in Part IV.
