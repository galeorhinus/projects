# Part I — How the Shadow Is Cast

*The āsurī māyā.*

---

::: epigraph

> सत्येन देवान् असृजत् अनृतेन असुरान् ।\
> ते देवाः सत्यम् अभवन् अनृतम् असुराः ॥
>
> *satyena devān asṛjat anṛtena asurān |*\
> *te devāḥ satyam abhavan anṛtam asurāḥ ||*
>
> By truth were created the radiant ones; by untruth, their opposites — the asuras. The radiant ones became truth; the asuras, untruth.
>
> `\hfill`{=latex}*— Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā 1.9.3*[NOTE: maitrayani-samhita-1-9-3-satya-asura]

:::

\bigskip

Part I begins with the pyramid still whole. It targets three blocks. The block marked ***Descended*** represents the claim that Sanskrit descended from PIE. It places an imaginary ancestor above Sanskrit and turns the recorded language into inherited cargo. The block marked ***Botanical*** represents the claim that Sanskrit grew through natural change. It describes the language through plant-parts — roots, stems, branches, and daughter languages. The block marked ***Codified*** represents the claim that Pāṇini stopped this natural change by imposing rules on a drifting language.

Together, these three blocks cast the first shadow. They replace Sanskrit's own category with a story of natural drift followed by authoritative repair. Sanskrit before Pāṇini becomes **प्रकृति (*prakṛti*)**: a changing organism with roots, stems, branches, and daughters. Pāṇini then becomes the apex who supposedly imposes order and arrests that change. The story hides what Sanskrit's own name declares: **संस्कृति (*saṃskṛti*)**, a wholly created and calibrated architecture.

The Vedic account calls the darkener Svarbhānu, whose name contains the very radiance he covers.[NOTE: svarbhanu-svar-etymology] The pyramid repeats his action by placing false categories between Sanskrit and the observer. The story makes an apex necessary: if Sanskrit drifted naturally, someone had to impose order and stop the drift. A distributed calibrant needs no such apex.

The chapters in Part I test these claims directly. **सत्-असत्-विवेक (*sat-asat-viveka*)** provides the standard: not the pyramid's approval, but **यत् भूतहितम् अत्यन्तं तत् सत्यम् (*yat bhūta-hitam atyantaṃ tat satyam*)**.[NOTE: satyam-bhutahitam-mahabharata] The reader can compare each inherited category with the language that category claims to explain.

Chapter 2 tests the codification story against Arabic and Tamil. Formal Arabic remained stable under institutional authority, while Tamil continued to change despite the extensive grammar preserved in the *Tolkāppiyam*. Sanskrit remained invariant without the authority that held Formal Arabic in place, and it did not continue changing as Tamil did. Chapter 2 identifies the Vedas as the missing calibrant and examines concealment and projection as the two movements of आसुरी माया (*āsurī māyā*).

Chapter 3 asks why the pyramid needs these false categories. Its racial, theological, and progress claims all serve an architecture of containment. The chapter examines why the pyramid obsesses over labels such as ***asura*** and **आर्य (*ārya*)**. That obsession replaces an inquiry into conduct with a conflict between factions. The Vedas instead require the listener to exercise **विवेक (*viveka*)** and examine the actor's purpose and actions.

Chapter 4 examines the institutions that preserve and enforce the false categories. It identifies their carrier as the church of progress. By the end of Part I, the reader will know how the first shadow was cast and whose authority depends on preserving those categories.
