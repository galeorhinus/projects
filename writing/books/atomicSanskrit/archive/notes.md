Within the atomic corollary, each वर्णः (varṇaḥ) may be assigned a phonetic weight. Among स्वराः (svarāḥ), the lightest are the mobile vowels इ (i), ऋ (ṛ), ऌ (ḷ), and उ (u), because they generate the semivowels य (ya), र (ra), ल (la), and व (va). Long vowels carry greater weight through duration, and diphthongs carry still greater weight through compound vocalic movement. Among व्यञ्जनानि (vyañjanāni), the lightest class may be treated as the अनुनासिकाः (anunāsikāḥ), the nasals, followed by अघोष-अल्पप्राण (aghoṣa-alpaprāṇa), घोष-अल्पप्राण (ghoṣa-alpaprāṇa), अघोष-महाप्राण (aghoṣa-mahāprāṇa), and finally घोष-महाप्राण (ghoṣa-mahāprāṇa). Thus phonetic weight is not arbitrary: it arises from duration, resonance, voicing, aspiration, obstruction, and articulatory placement.

In a small exploratory sample of fifty familiar धातवः (dhātavaḥ), only ten contained the heavier vowels आ (ā), ई (ī), ऊ (ū), ए (e), ऐ (ai), ओ (o), or औ (au), while forty were built without them. This preliminary result supports the hypothesis that Sanskrit’s verbal atoms tend toward phonetic economy: the most common root-forms often remain short, light, and structurally compact, while heavier vowels appear selectively in high-density semantic atoms such as भू (bhū), दा (dā), धा (dhā), नी (nī), स्था (sthā), and ज्ञा (jñā).
