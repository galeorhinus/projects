# The Encyclopaedic Confirmation

In 1948, S.M. Katre at Deccan College, Pune, initiated a project that has continued ever since: the *Encyclopaedic Dictionary of Sanskrit on Historical Principles*. Volume 1 appeared in 1976. Thirty-plus volumes have been published as of recent years. Completion is projected to take another half-century. The project's methodological frame is in its title.

*Historical principles* is the methodology of the *Oxford English Dictionary*, conceived in the 1850s and stabilized by James Murray in the 1880s: track each word's attested usages chronologically through the textual corpus; treat the earliest attestation as the etymological origin and subsequent attestations as the trajectory of development. The methodology was built for natural-historical languages — languages whose history is exactly what the methodology describes, a chronology of attested variation through which the modern form gradually emerges from the earlier one. Applied to Sanskrit, the methodology imports its conclusion. *On historical principles* presupposes that Sanskrit's history is the chronological-evolutionary kind English's history is — that the difference between Vedic Sanskrit and Pāṇinian-codified Sanskrit is the kind of difference *hlāfweard* and *Lord* exhibit, only longer. The project's title alone is the indictment. It assumes the framework the engineering thesis is built to dismantle, and proceeds inside that framework with admirable thoroughness.

The data the project assembles is empirical, exhaustive, and welcome. Across thousands of years of attested Sanskrit usage, the lexicographers compile variant phonetic forms, semantic extensions, regional spellings, scribal corrections, manuscript divergences, technical-vocabulary coinages from successive intellectual traditions — Buddhist philosophical literature, **नव्य-न्याय (*Navya-Nyāya*)** logical apparatus, **ज्योतिष (*Jyotiṣa*)** mathematical astronomy, **रसशास्त्र (*Rasaśāstra*)** alchemical taxonomy, mediaeval commentarial diction, regional grammatical traditions. The corpus is vast. What the methodology supplies on top of the data is the interpretation: each variant is a *stage*; each shift is *evolution*; the cumulative picture is *Sanskrit changes over time, like any natural language*.

The interpretation is exactly the picture the engineering thesis was built to absorb. The variants the project documents fall into three distinct categories. The methodology, operating in the family-tree frame, conflates them as one phenomenon called *linguistic change*. The engineering thesis distinguishes them.

The first category is *apabhraṃśa*. Every variant phonetic form the project documents — regional spellings, manuscript divergences, scribal substitutions, attested mispronunciations — is a documented instance of the phenomenon Patañjali named, quantified, and exemplified in the **पस्पशाह्निक (*Paspaśāhnika*)** — the introductory section of his *Mahābhāṣya*:

> **भूयांसोऽपभ्रंशाः, अल्पीयांसः शब्दाः ।**
> *bhūyāṃso 'pabhraṃśāḥ, alpīyāṃsaḥ śabdāḥ.*
> *Many are the corruptions; few are the words.*

The canonical **गौः (*gauḥ*)** example supplied four variants of one engineered form — *gāvī*, *goṇī*, *gotā*, *gopotalikā*. The Deccan College dictionary, after decades of work, will document many attested variant forms for every engineered word in the *Dhātupāṭha* and across the lexicon Pāṇini's apparatus generates. That is direct empirical confirmation of Patañjali's quantitative observation. The project documents the slip. The engineering thesis predicted the slip and named the apparatus that holds against it.

The second category is *generative output*. Every new technical vocabulary the project documents — Buddhist **क्षण (*kṣaṇa*, the moment as time-quantum)**, *Navya-Nyāya*'s **अवच्छेदक (*avacchedaka*, delimiter)**, *Jyotiṣa*'s **ज्या (*jyā*, chord)** and **त्रिज्या (*trijyā*, sine)**, *Rasaśāstra*'s **भस्म (*bhasma*, calcined oxide)**, **वेदान्त (*Vedānta*)**'s **उपाधि (*upādhi*, conditioning attribute)** — is the engineered system being used to generate molecules its civilization needed for new intellectual territory. The grammatical engine did not change. Pāṇini's rules for compound formation (*samāsa*), derivation by **कृत् (*kṛt*)** and **तद्धित (*taddhita*)** affixation, and the productive *dhātu*-*gaṇa* apparatus did not change. The civilization spent thousands of years executing the same engineered specification to generate new molecules for new realities. A computer does not decay because new software runs on it. The project documents the molecules. The grammar documents the engine. They are not in conflict; they document different objects.

The third category is *semantic extension*. **यन्त्र (*yantra*)** meant restraining apparatus in early texts, geometric diagram in mediaeval *tāntric* literature, and machine in modern usage — across thousands of years of attestation, with the phonetic form *yantra* unchanged across every attested century. **धर्म (*dharma*)** meant maintained order in Vedic usage, moral category in **स्मृति (*smṛti*)** literature, technical metaphysical primitive in **मीमांसा (*Mīmāṃsā*)** and *Vedānta*, and civilizational orientation in modern usage — phonetic form unchanged. **ब्रह्म (*brahman*)** moved from prayer-formula in Rigvedic usage to ultimate reality in Upaniṣadic usage to systematic technical term in *Vedānta* — phonetic form unchanged. The phonetic structure stays intact; the meaning extends to cover new conceptual territory. This is what an engineered linguistic system does when the civilization that uses it encounters new conceptual ground: it assigns new semantic payloads to existing molecules, without breaking the molecules.

Three layers of attested variation, three distinct phenomena. Phonetic *apabhraṃśa* is user-side noise; new compound coinage is engine-side generation; semantic extension is payload-extension within a fixed structure. The Deccan College methodology, operating in the family-tree frame, treats all three as "linguistic change." The engineering thesis names them separately, locates them in distinct architectural layers, and identifies them as exactly what the engineered system was designed to permit, generate, and resist.

What the project would have to document to falsify the engineering thesis — but has not and cannot — is variation in the specification itself. The *Aṣṭādhyāyī*'s rules across thousands of years of continuous transmission in the Pāṇinian tradition: unchanged. The *varṇamālā* organization by **स्थान (*sthāna*)** and **करण (*karaṇa*)**: stable across all systematic descriptions in the tradition. The *Dhātupāṭha* enumeration and the ten *gaṇāḥ*: stable in the Pāṇinian core. The phoneme inventory of the engineered system: stable, with the **भाषायाम् (*bhāṣāyām*)** / **छन्दसि (*chandasi*)** difference — including the retention of ळ in the Vedic mode — accounted for by the synchronic mode distinction Pāṇini himself names. The engineered specification is invariant across the entire period the project documents.

The architectural fact the project cannot reach is here. Its corpus operates entirely at the attested-usage layer — where speakers and authors and scribes work, where slip and extension and generation happen, exactly as the architects of Sanskrit described. The specification layer — where Pāṇini's rules, the *varṇamālā*, and the *Dhātupāṭha* operate — is not in the project's scope. The project cannot document specification change because there is none to document.

The polemic verdict lands cleanly. After decades of exhaustive labor in the colonial-philological frame, the *Encyclopaedic Dictionary of Sanskrit on Historical Principles* has produced — without recognizing it — the most thorough empirical confirmation of Patañjali's quantitative observation ever assembled. The project's data is welcome. The project's framing is the indictment. What the orthodoxy assembled as evidence of Sanskrit's evolution is, read through Patañjalian specification, evidence of the engineered system functioning exactly as Patañjali described: speakers slipping, authors extending, the engine generating, the specification holding. Many corruptions per correct form. The work has always been to keep the small set visible against the large.

The project measures the large set. The grammar specifies the small set. The framework's failure to recognize what it has documented is itself the indictment.
