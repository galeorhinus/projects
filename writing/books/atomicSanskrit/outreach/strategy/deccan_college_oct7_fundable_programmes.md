# Deccan College: Fundable Programmes for the October 7 Meeting

Prepared: 3 October 2026.

Purpose: Identify what Deccan College's published rules permit and turn those powers into specific proposals for Sanskrit research, teaching, and patronage. This is an internal discussion brief, not an approved programme or an institutional commitment.

Companion document: [Earlier research and funding proposals](deccan_college_research_proposals.md).

## Executive Recommendation

Propose a Programme in Sanskrit Architecture and Knowledge Transmission within existing academic structures. Begin with a twelve-month project, two named fellowships, practitioner participation, and one short course. Develop a chair only with an agreed research remit, team, appointment route, and operating budget.

The College has more options than scholarships and chairs. Its published rules expressly cover certificate courses, visiting scholars, Professors of Practice, consultants, fellowships, prizes, and donor-funded expenditure. Existing donor-supported activity shows that teaching, research, internships, and publication can be combined under a named programme.

For prospective patrons from former Maratha ruling families, the proposition is to renew a documented tradition of supporting Sanskrit through people and results. Family naming and recognition must be agreed with the institution; no general naming-rights policy has been verified.

### The Important Limits

1. **New employee posts require prior state approval.** Rules 11(3)(vi) and 22(I)(a) do not provide an explicit donor-funded exemption. Obtain a written route for temporary project engagements; do not assume that calling a post a fellowship or a residency removes this requirement.
2. **Existing staff cannot simply receive donor-funded salary supplements.** Rule 22(I)(b)-(c) restricts changes to remuneration and extra payments without state approval. Have the Registrar distinguish approved visitor or practitioner payments from additional payments to employees.
3. **The VC is not automatically the sole approving authority.** Executive Council can delegate powers under Rule 14. The relevant delegation has not been obtained.
4. **A grant for research does not automatically confer data-reuse rights.** Dictionary records, recordings, manuscripts, software and publications need clear access and licensing terms.
5. **A new centre is not needed to begin.** A programme hosted by existing departments is the practical starting recommendation, not a claim that its approvals are automatic.

## 3. The CSU Boundary

CSU Ordinance 48 makes the IKS-EDS dictionary centre at Deccan an off-campus centre of CSU. Its Governing Committee, chaired by CSU's VC, approves programmes and budgets. Deccan's VC is a member. Other funding sources need CSU Executive Council approval under section 6(3). [S4](#s4-csu-ordinance-48)

Consequently, proposals involving the dictionary centre's staff, records or institutional publications should be discussed jointly. A separate College research project can investigate an independently assembled corpus, but that does not confer authority to change the dictionary or reuse its database.

CSU also publishes an endowment framework covering research, fellowships, scholarships, training, libraries and visiting chairs. This is an additional possible funding route, not permission to route any Deccan grant through CSU without agreement. [S5](#s5-csu-endowment-framework)

## 4. Specific Proposals

The scopes and durations below are recommendations for costing, not existing programmes, guarantees of completion or quotations from the universities. Academic findings should remain open to correction and disagreement.

### A. Fellowship in Sanskrit Word Formation and Meaning

**Question:** How do Sanskrit's word-forming rules produce new expressions, and how reliably can their meanings be explained?

- Format: twelve-month named research fellowship, with a grammar supervisor and a technical collaborator.
- Scope: about 100 root families drawn from the existing generativity analysis. Review common and unfamiliar formations, alternative derivation paths and unresolved cases.
- Outputs: source-linked derivations, a correction log, a reviewed sample dataset, and a small public demonstration showing how words are formed.
- Distinguish grammatical generation, attestation and inferred meaning. Absence from a corpus is not automatically a grammatical rejection; a generated form is not automatically a verified distinct meaning.
- Proposed host: Sanskrit and Lexicography, with computational/linguistic collaboration; coordinate with IKS-EDS if its resources are used.
- Route: fellowship scheme and approved project budget; technical engagement and software/data rights agreed separately.
- Donor proposition: support a researcher whose work creates something people can examine and use.

Starting materials: [Generativity research bundle](../../analysis/generativity/README.md) and [Word Engine implementation plan](../../working/10_active/as_word_engine_preliminary_implementation_plan_codex.md).

### B. Fellowship in Vedic Transmission and Error Correction

**Question:** Which features of recitation make particular errors detectable and correctable?

- Format: twelve-to-eighteen-month fellowship with paid traditional-practitioner participation.
- Scope: approximately 30 passages from two or three participating teaching traditions. Document metre, accent, sound specification, recitation patterns and correction practices.
- Outputs: consented recordings, an annotated account of the checks on each passage, teaching demonstrations, and a research paper.
- Keep permitted differences among traditions distinct from mistakes. Present-day demonstrations test mechanisms; they do not by themselves establish an unchanged history over millennia.
- Proposed host: Linguistics and Sanskrit jointly; practitioners are research partners, not merely recorded subjects.
- Route: research/fellowship approval plus consent, access, recording and payment arrangements.
- Donor proposition: support living expertise and the study of how it is passed on.

### C. Dictionary Research Pilot: Variation, Meaning and Rule

**Question:** When a dictionary records different forms, what kind of difference is it recording?

- Format: six-to-twelve-month joint project with one fellow and specialist reviewers.
- Scope: approximately 300 entries, categorized as permitted grammatical alternatives, spelling or manuscript differences, newly formed expressions, meaning extensions, departures from a defined standard, or unresolved cases.
- Outputs: an annotation guide, source-linked sample, independent review of a subset, and an account of disagreements. Retain the original entries alongside new analysis.
- Proposed host: IKS-EDS with the College's relevant faculty.
- Route: CSU centre programme/budget approval and data permission; confirm the grant recipient before donor discussions.
- Donor proposition: make a major Sanskrit research resource more informative and usable through a specific, inspectable project.

This is the strongest project to discuss with both VCs together. It is not a request for an immediate dictionary-wide rewrite.

### D. Certificate Course: Sanskrit as a Language System

**Purpose:** Help Sanskrit students, teachers, engineers and interested readers understand the construction and transmission of the language through worked examples.

- Format: proposed 40-60 teaching hours, delivered over eight to twelve weeks; cohort size and any credit value to be approved.
- Modules: the sound grid; formation of words; sentence construction; Vedic and worldly usage; metre and accent; recitation and correction; a small analysis or demonstration project.
- Teaching: Sanskrit examples with accessible Marathi/Hindi/English explanation as staffing allows. A general introductory module can be open to non-Sanskrit readers; advanced grammatical exercises need an appropriate entry requirement or bridge module.
- Outputs: approved syllabus, source reader, exercises and assessed learner projects; recordings only where rights permit.
- Proposed host: Sanskrit and Lexicography with Linguistics and invited practitioners.
- Route: Board of Studies syllabus recommendation, Academic Council approval, and approved finances. A preliminary workshop with a participation certificate should not be presented as an approved academic qualification.
- Donor proposition: fund course development, practitioner teaching and student bursaries. Teaching the book's questions is appropriate; requiring agreement with its conclusions is not assessment.

The existing Jain course establishes that a donor-supported certificate programme is workable at this institution. It does not settle the credit calculation for this proposed course.

### E. Traditional Sanskrit Scholar Residency

**Purpose:** Bring expertise maintained outside regular university appointments into sustained contact with students and researchers.

- Format: four-to-eight-week residence, or a series of visits, for a recitation teacher, grammarian or other specialist.
- Outputs: supervised source-reading sessions, a documented demonstration, mentoring and agreed contributions to Projects A, B or D.
- Route: visiting-scholar or other approved arrangement under Rule 11(3)(iii). Do not promise a professorial title, degree-equivalence decision or PhD supervision without checking eligibility.
- Donor proposition: support a living teacher and the next generation of learners; include travel, accommodation and preparation time, not just lecture fees.

### F. Maratha Sanskrit Patronage and Living Learning Project

**Question:** What did particular patrons fund, how did teaching and recitation institutions function, and what can present-day patrons learn from those arrangements?

- Format: twelve-month archival and field project, potentially supported by a named fellowship or a small group of student grants.
- Scope: one consenting family/trust archive and two or three connected institutions. Examine grant records, correspondence, teaching arrangements, manuscript colophons and other relevant evidence. Include Modi, Marathi and Sanskrit expertise where required.
- Outputs: a source catalogue, selected transcriptions and translations, a documented account of funding arrangements, and recommendations for a contemporary patronage programme.
- Proposed host: a cross-department team involving history/archaeology and Sanskrit, subject to supervisor availability and archive access.
- Route: research and budget approval, with owners' permission for access, reproduction and publication.
- Donor proposition: reconnect documented family patronage with present-day Sanskrit learning. Support a real researcher or teacher alongside the historical study so it is not merely a commemorative publication.

No specific family's archive, patronage history or willingness to participate has been verified for this project.

### G. Sanskrit and the Subcontinent: A Comparative Sound Atlas

**Question:** How are Sanskrit's sound distinctions distributed across selected Indian languages, and how do specified external comparison languages differ?

- Format: a twelve-to-eighteen-month pilot requiring phonetics supervision and recording capacity.
- Scope: a manageable selection across northern, southern, eastern and western India, with explicitly chosen external comparisons. Use comparable recording tasks and document dialect and speaker variation.
- Outputs: recordings, an annotated sound inventory, articulatory comparisons and teaching diagrams.
- Proposed host: Linguistics with Sanskrit expertise and local language partners.
- Route: research budget, participant consent, recording permissions and data-release terms.
- Donor proposition: investigate the subcontinent's linguistic connections using a dataset others can inspect.

Contemporary sound distribution alone cannot establish when or in which direction a language spread. This project can test the book's comparisons without presupposing its historical conclusion.

### H. Longer-Term Chair in Sanskrit Architecture and Knowledge Transmission

**Purpose:** Sustain the research, course, fellowships and practitioner partnerships after a successful initial programme.

- Proposed responsibilities: lead research, mentor fellows, develop teaching, coordinate external partners and publish annual results.
- Necessary resources: the chair holder's approved remuneration, research assistance, operating expenses and teaching support. A title without these is not the proposed programme.
- Route: selection and Executive Council approval; obtain the applicable state sanction for new posts and the donor agreement before promising an appointment.
- Funding choice: a renewable fixed-term grant or a corpus-based endowment. Ask the College for the cost of each; no minimum corpus, approved spending rate or naming policy has been verified.
- Donor proposition: a lasting institution of Sanskrit patronage, grounded in a functioning programme rather than an inaugural ceremony alone.

## 5. What to Start First

| Priority | Proposal | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| First joint institutional discussion | C: Dictionary pilot | Both VCs can address its academic scope and the CSU governance boundary. |
| Strongest ready research starting point | A: Word-formation fellowship | Existing computation and documentation provide starting material; scholarly review remains substantive work. |
| Closest to the book's systems thesis | B: Transmission fellowship | Investigates the mechanisms of preservation through practitioners and examples. |
| Visible teaching outcome | D plus E: Course and residency | Builds a bridge between existing expertise, students and technical readers. |
| Strongest tailored donor narrative | F: Patronage and living learning | Connects a documented family legacy to present-day activity. |
| Follow-on comparative research | G: Sound atlas | Needs more field coordination and specialist capacity. |
| Multi-year consolidation | H: Chair | Requires sustained funding and an approved appointment structure. |

Do not launch all eight at once. A sensible first-year package is two fellowships (choose A/B or A/C), a practitioner residency, and a course built from the research. If staffing cannot support that scope, start with one fellowship and a workshop.

2. Nominate an academic lead and an administrative contact from each institution as needed.
3. Confirm the approved scheme for donor-funded fellows, visitors and technical collaborators, including whether state approval is required for each engagement.

6. Agree donor recognition, restrictions on use of funds, reporting and publication expectations; confirm the legal recipient and any applicable donation permissions separately.
7. Request a target date, for example within four weeks, for the written scope and budget. This is a proposed timetable, not a forecast of approvals.

Suggested opening:

Could Deccan College and CSU identify one or two projects, nominate the academic leads, and prepare the scope, approval route and budget?
