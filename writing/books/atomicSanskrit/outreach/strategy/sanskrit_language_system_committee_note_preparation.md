## Editorial Choices

- Project 4 now explicitly uses a listening-first approach for people interested in Gītā chanting. Recognition and error identification precede any requirement to perform; chanting remains optional. Exercises progress from reference comparisons to independent listening and include correct examples and permitted variations. Gītā material is not presented as Vedic स्वर instruction.
- Project 3 is now a Sanskrit-to-Sanskrit dictionary builder, beginning with approximately one hundred word families. The Bṛhaspati-Indra account introduces formation through rules in simple language. The pilot distinguishes valid formation, recorded use, and possible meaning, and proposes transparent collaboration with the existing dictionary rather than an undisclosed takeover. The source and locator were checked against the manuscript's paspashahnika-generativity record.
- Retain the simple public title “Sanskrit as a Language System.” Introduce SAILS (“Sanskrit as an Invariant Language System”) only as a proposed programme name in the first October 7 decision request, after the substance has been explained. The title and opening remain acronym-free.
- “श्रुति Needs श्रोता” is now standalone Project 4: a listening curriculum linked to Project 1, a first-year community pilot, support for local reuse, and before/after listening exercises. Its distinct deliverables and funding cover curriculum development, practitioner and speech-science educator participation, audio preparation, coordination, and community sessions. The former teaching subsection was moved and expanded, not duplicated. The first-year package now explicitly includes Project 3's software developer and Project 4's teaching support.
- Familiar Sanskrit terminology now accompanies the English explanations: सन्धि, व्याकरण, छन्दस्, शिक्षा, स्वर, पाठ, स्थान, प्रयत्न, उपसर्ग, प्रत्यय, समास, and वर्णमाला. The worked examples also identify तृतीया बहुवचनम्, अक्षराणि, पाद, त्रिष्टुभ्, दन्त्य, and मूर्धन्य. Terms are explained on first use; this does not introduce the book's coined vocabulary.
- The purpose now includes practical support for Sanskrit's continuation, not study alone. Societal guardianship and the universities' supporting role are explicit. Projects include practitioner review, return of agreed materials, independent checking, and teaching outside campuses; first-year evaluation asks whether these capacities grow across society.
- Explain “language system” before naming the proposed projects. Explain calibrant through checking and correction; distinguish entropy from the source uses of अपभ्रंश.
- State the complementary-domain and distributed-preservation propositions explicitly. Describe resilience against central capture or destruction without promising immunity.
- Provide three research projects, but recommend launching preservation and word formation first. The comparative sound project begins with a method review and recording plan to avoid implying that all three can be completed by two fellows.
- Keep the research independence and institutional approval details in a small number of clear sentences. No unsupported fellowship rates, donor amounts, staffing commitments, or chair naming rights.
- Include three worked examples in an explanatory attachment rather than shrink existing book figures into the note. The domain table gives the main visual comparison. A technical illustration can be added at layout stage if needed, but the current draft is self-contained without one.
- Use original-script examples and familiar explanations; omit the presentation's sonomer, audiograph, and fractal vocabulary here.
