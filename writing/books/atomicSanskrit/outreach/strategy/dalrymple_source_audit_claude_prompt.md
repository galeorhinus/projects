## Claims to Test

1. *The Golden Road* begins its principal celebration after Buddha and leaves earlier Vedic achievements unexplained.
2. Dalrymple protects Greek priority or treats comparable Indian achievements as later or derivative.
3. He rejects Out of India, including whether he addresses evidence or dismisses positions through political associations.
4. He endorses an external origin for Indo-Aryan languages and describes Vedic culture or the Vedas as a Steppe-Harappan synthesis.
5. He confuses evidence of genetic ancestry with direct evidence of language or Sanskrit's origins. Find his specific statement and compare it with the genetic paper he invokes. DNA can inform population history; it does not directly identify ancient speech. Treat the book's Indian sound-architecture argument separately, using Chapters 8 and 18 and their underlying sources.

The proposed article opens with Max Muller, then a specific scholar who praises Panini while crediting codification with Sanskrit's stability, then Dalrymple. It ends by examining whether these constitute what Parag calls heroic erasure: celebrating an achievement while obscuring the system that made it possible. Test the parallel; do not assume the conclusion.

## Evidence Requirements

- Find Dalrymple's own words, not reviewers' descriptions or AI summaries.
- For each finding record a stable URL, title, publication date, edition and printed page or audio timestamp, a brief exact quotation, surrounding context, and what it establishes.
- Separate quotations from paraphrases and the proposed essay's interpretation. Include evidence that contradicts the proposed critique.
- Distinguish population ancestry, language migration, cultural transmission, biological race, and racial hierarchy. Do not silently treat them as synonyms.
- Distinguish the international spread of an achievement from its origin. A chronological starting point alone does not prove a claim about origins.
- Distinguish a statement about Vedic imagery from a statement about the composition of the Vedas.
- Keep excerpts short. Do not reproduce long copyrighted passages or reconstruct an unavailable book from search snippets.
- A timestamp generated by a third-party transcript is a lead until checked against the audio. Identify the actual speaker, especially where a guest is answering.
- Mark inaccessible sources, missing locators, and unresolved attribution explicitly. Never invent quotations, episode titles, page numbers, or motives.
