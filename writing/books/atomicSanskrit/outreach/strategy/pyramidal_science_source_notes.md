## C1. NASA's Explanation for Children

- **Source:** NASA Space Place, *What Is the Big Bang?*
- **URL:** https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/big-bang/en/
- **Locator:** Opening, "The Short Answer"; compare "A Super Long Time."
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-26.
- **Finding:** The opening presents the Big Bang as an explanation of how the universe began and describes an initial single point. The article includes observations of expansion and presents an age of 13.8 billion years.
- **Use:** A concrete public-education formulation to examine beside C2. The skeleton quotes only the first sentence.
- **Limit:** It is a simplified educational page, not a technical demonstration that all cosmologists equate the observable universe with all existence or claim perfect knowledge. Its initial use of "explain" and "idea" must not be concealed when quoting it.

## C2. NASA's Fuller Cosmic History

- **Source:** NASA Science, *Overview*, under Explore the Universe / Cosmic History.
- **URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/
- **Locators:** "The Universe's History," "Cosmic Inflation," and "Recombination."
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-26.
- **Finding:** The overview introduces the account as current cosmological theories and explicitly states uncertainty about what preceded inflation and what powered it. It identifies the cosmic microwave background as observable radiation from the early universe.
- **Use:** Establish the distinction between measurements, reconstruction, and unresolved origins. Read beside C1 rather than treating either as the whole institutional position.
- **Limit:** This is evidence that an institution also communicates uncertainty. It cannot honestly be cited as a statement that cosmologists possess or claim complete knowledge. The critical reading of the contrast with C1 belongs to the author.

Short excerpts used in the proposed opening, with accent marks omitted for the article's reading format:

> को अद्धा वेद क इह प्र वोचत्  
> *ko addhā veda ka iha pra vocat*

Working rendering: Who knows for certain? Who here can declare it?

> सो अङ्ग वेद यदि वा न वेद  
> *so aṅga veda yadi vā na veda*

Working rendering: He indeed knows, or perhaps he does not know.

The preceding pāda identifies an overseer in the highest heaven. The closing uncertainty is the basis for the article's contrast with a claim to final authority. Preserve the surrounding context when checking the final translation. The new English renderings are working translations, not quotations attributed to a published translator.

### Interpretive Boundaries

- अनादि (*anādi*) and अनन्त (*ananta*) are the book's broader philosophical frame, not words being quoted from these two mantras.
- The hymn raises origins as a subject of inquiry; do not say it simply denies any beginning or proves physical infinity.
- Use its closing uncertainty as the basis of the author's interpretation. Do not claim that this is the only traditional reading or that it predicts/refutes a modern cosmological theory.
- Do not translate devas as gods or asuras as demons in new article prose. The selected excerpts do not require either substitution.
- The UT page is a metrically restored research edition. Check publication excerpts against an appropriate saṃhitā edition as well; do not reproduce the full modern edited edition or an entire modern translation.

## B1. Full-Book Argument

- **Source:** [Chapter 1, One, the Apex, and the Finite](../../manuscript/as_1_01_one_oppressors_finite.md), §§1.1-1.2.
- **Role:** Governs the author's philosophical argument and meaning of structural finitism; it is not independent empirical evidence for cosmological claims.
- **Important distinction:** The chapter's "effectively zero" means finite observation considered beside the infinite, not no observations. Treat it as a philosophical comparison, not literal arithmetic with infinity or proof of the universe's total extent.
- **Article adaptation:** At the author's request, the proposed opening imagines observations extending across 93 billion, 93 trillion, or 93 quadrillion light-years. These are illustrative magnitudes, not three measured diameters. The book remains unchanged. Adding zeroes changes the finite magnitude, not the limiting comparison: hold any one finite numerator fixed while the denominator grows without bound, and the ratio tends to zero. This supplies the meaning of "effectively zero" without treating infinity as an ordinary divisor.
