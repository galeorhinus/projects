# Deccan College: Research and Funding Proposals

Prepared: 19 September 2026.

Status: Internal discussion brief following Parag Tope's meeting with the Vice-Chancellor. These are proposed projects, not commitments by the College or donors. No manuscript changes have been made.

## Recommendation

Propose a **Programme in Sanskrit Architecture and Knowledge Transmission**. Begin with a twelve-month pilot, supported by donors and led by College faculty. Use its results to decide whether to establish a longer programme and an endowed chair.

The proposed change in direction is substantive: study how Sanskrit generates expressions, how the Vedic disciplines maintain shared standards, and how knowledge continues across generations. These subjects connect the language research to the broader inquiry into civilizational order in *Atomic Sanskrit* and *Second Shanti*.

The College need not endorse the book before investigating these questions. A fundable proposal should identify the evidence, method, researchers, outputs, and conditions under which an explanation would need revision. The donor funds the inquiry and its public results, not a required conclusion.

### The Dictionary Has a Separate Approval Route

CSU Ordinance 48 establishes the dictionary's IKS-EDS Centre as a Central Sanskrit University off-campus centre under CSU supervision. Its governing committee approves programmes and budgets; Deccan's VC is a member, not its chair. Dictionary-wide changes therefore require coordination with CSU. Data licensing remains to be checked in the underlying MoUs. [Source 3](#source-3-csu-dictionary-centre-ordinance)

This affects implementation of the dictionary proposals in Appendix Part 2. It does not require abandoning them. A separately approved research pilot can examine selected evidence before proposing changes to the dictionary itself.

## Concrete Project Menu

The quantities below are proposed pilot scopes, to be settled with the faculty. They are not existing College commitments.

### 1. Read the Dictionary Through Sanskrit's Architecture

**Research task:** Select approximately 300 entries across several subject areas and source types. Distinguish grammatical alternatives, manuscript or spelling differences, departures from a specified standard, newly formed expressions, and extensions of meaning. Investigate cases that do not fit rather than forcing them into a category.

**Why this matters:** This turns the methodological argument in Appendix Part 2 into a research exercise that others can inspect. Does a recorded difference indicate changed grammar, a different permitted operation, a manuscript practice, or a new use of an existing word?

**Deliverables:** An annotated sample with exact source references; a classification manual; independent review of a subset; a report explaining agreements, disagreements, and unresolved cases. Preserve the original dictionary record alongside the added analysis.

**Funding package:** A named research fellowship, review honoraria where permitted, and technical assistance. Twelve months.

**Starting material:** [Appendix Part 2, especially sections 2.5-2.8](../../manuscript/as_3_02_encyclopaedic.md).

**Dependency:** Agree access, reproduction, and publication rights with the relevant institution. Do not treat an online dictionary as permission to redistribute its data. If broader access takes time, begin with an independently assembled sample from appropriately licensed sources.

### 2. Vedic Transmission and Distributed Error Correction

**Research task:** Study how selected passages are maintained through sound specification, meter, accent, recitation patterns, and teacher-student correction. Work with traditional practitioners as research partners. Compare approximately 30-50 passages, documenting the particular lineage and permitted variations in each case.

**Why this matters:** This is the closest empirical project to the book's central systems-engineering thesis: how can a shared standard remain available without one institution controlling every copy or performance?

**Deliverables:** A consented, annotated recording collection; maps of the checks available for each passage; a study of how errors are recognized and corrected; teaching demonstrations agreed with the practitioners. Any controlled error-detection experiment should use separately identified experimental copies and practitioner-approved procedures.

**Funding package:** A fellowship, practitioner compensation, field visits, recording equipment, and linguistic analysis. Twelve to eighteen months.

**Boundary:** A present-day experiment can investigate mechanisms of preservation. Historical continuity needs its own documentary evidence. Keep those two findings separate.

### 3. Scholarly Validation of the Word Engine

**Research task:** Select approximately 100 families built from Sanskrit atoms. Examine the operations, resulting forms, meanings, and source references in the completed generativity analysis. Include common words, less familiar formations, and unresolved cases.

**Why this matters:** Much of the computational groundwork already exists. The College could contribute specialist grammatical judgment, improve explanations of meaning, and train researchers through a concrete dataset.

**Deliverables:** A reviewed sample of derivation paths, a correction ledger, reusable tests, and a small public demonstrator. Distinguish meanings stated by sources from meanings estimated through composition. Do not present a sample review as validation of every generated record.

**Funding package:** A Sanskrit research fellowship plus a defined technical contract or collaboration, subject to the approved engagement route. Six to twelve months.

**Existing handoff:** [Atomic Sanskrit Word Engine plan](../../working/10_active/as_word_engine_app_plan_codex.md), [generativity reproducibility bundle](../../analysis/generativity/README.md), and [preliminary implementation plan](../../working/10_active/as_word_engine_preliminary_implementation_plan_codex.md).

**Ownership:** Agree the relationship between the existing author's research, College contributions, software licences, attribution, and public releases before work begins.

### 4. A Dissertation and Small-Project Fund

Create a fund for approximately six selected student projects. MA awards can support a bounded dissertation; doctoral awards should support defined research costs or a properly funded fellowship period. A one-year grant must not be described as funding an entire PhD.

Possible topics:

1. How रुद्रैः (*rudraiḥ*) and रुद्रेभिः (*rudrebhiḥ*) serve grammatical and metrical requirements in a selected corpus.
2. What written copies retain or omit when compared with specified recitation practices.
3. How a selected Sanskrit technical vocabulary expands through documented word-forming operations.
4. How students and teachers recognize and correct particular types of recitation error.
5. How dictionaries distinguish a new word, a new meaning, a spelling difference, and a grammatical alternative.
6. How one traditional learning community maintains standards, distributes responsibility, and addresses disagreement.

**Deliverables:** A dissertation or research paper, source records, and a public presentation. Release data where permissions allow. Selection, supervision, admission, and examination remain academic responsibilities.

**Donor appeal:** Each project has an intelligible subject, a named investigator, a timetable, and something concrete to read at the end.

### 5. A Course on Sanskrit Architecture and Transmission

Propose an initial short course, followed by an elective if the faculty finds it useful. Students would examine the sound grid, word formation, Vedic and worldly usage, transmission practices, and competing explanations of stability and change.

Use Sanskrit's analytical categories and worked examples before introducing abstract comparisons. The course should teach students how to examine the claims, including those in *Atomic Sanskrit*.

**Deliverables:** A syllabus, source reader, recorded demonstrations where permitted, and exercises that other institutions can reuse.

**Funding package:** Course development, visiting teachers, practitioner participation, and student assistance. Have the College distinguish a non-credit workshop from an approved credit-bearing course.

### 6. Follow-on Research: Sound, Comparison, and Civilizational Order

Keep three larger subjects in the programme's longer-term agenda rather than attempting them all in the first year:

**Sanskrit's subcontinental sound field.** Collect comparable recordings from a defined selection of Indian and external languages. Document articulation, sound inventories, and contrasts through a consistent method. Sound resemblance alone does not determine the direction or date of a historical movement.

**Comparative word families.** Develop a source-by-source comparison of selected Sanskrit and other language families, including alternative accounts of contact and ancestry. The [Radiance Mapping proposal](../../working/10_active/as_sanskrit_radiance_mapping_project_proposal.md) provides a starting record structure. Researchers should test historical direction rather than build it into the admission rules.

**Calibrant order and civilizational memory.** Examine how specific learning and recitation communities sustain a standard, correct error, distribute responsibility, and interact with patrons or governments. This preserves the connection to *Second Shanti*: Sanskrit is the first case, while the wider research concerns how societies maintain knowledge and order. Identify the extent and limits of the comparison rather than assuming that a linguistic mechanism proves a political system.

## What to Launch First

Offer a **twelve-month pilot with two research fellowships and a small student-project fund**. Ask the faculty to choose two of Projects 1-3 after checking supervision and permissions. My preferred intellectual core is the dictionary analysis and Vedic transmission; the Word Engine is a useful alternative or parallel project because its starting materials already exist.

Proposed sequence:

1. **First month:** The VC nominates a faculty coordinator. A small meeting defines two projects, the appropriate institutional partners, and the questions each will investigate.
2. **Months 2-3:** Faculty prepare scopes and budgets; the Registrar confirms approvals and donor terms; the College selects researchers through its procedures.
3. **Months 4-9:** Researchers produce samples and interim reports. External reviewers and traditional practitioners assess the relevant outputs.
4. **Months 10-12:** Publish completed pilot results and a next-stage proposal, including unresolved findings.

Treat these as target intervals, not promises about approval time.

## Chair or Centre?

A possible later title is **Chair in Sanskrit Architecture and Knowledge Transmission**. Its remit should include research supervision, primary-source study, reproducible data, teaching, and practitioner partnerships.

Ask for the approved terms and establishment route of the Jain Archaeology chair as a practical precedent. Agree measurable responsibilities and an appointment process before discussing a name or corpus. A chair with no researchers, operating budget, or research plan will accomplish little.

A centre is a later possibility if a team and sustained programme justify it. Naming a centre should follow an institutional proposal, not substitute for one.

The Chancellor's published message already discusses Vision 2047, a Bharat Vidya programme, Sanskrit/data-science collaboration, and the launched Digital KoshaSHRI portal. Present this proposal as a contribution to that direction. Do not offer simply to digitize a dictionary that already has a digital portal. [Source 4](#source-4-published-institutional-direction)

## Funding and Documents to Request

Before approaching donors for a specific sum, ask the College for a costed version of the pilot. Separate fellowship support, fieldwork, practitioner payments, equipment, technical services, review, overheads, and publication. For an endowment, request its corpus, investment, spending, and renewal policies; no required chair corpus has been verified in this research.

Request these documents or written confirmations:

1. Operative Memorandum and Rules, amendments, and relevant delegated financial powers.
2. The approved Jain Archaeology chair agreement and appointment/establishment route.
3. A model donor-funded fellowship or project agreement, eligible costs, overhead rate, and reporting format.
4. The 3 January 2024 and 17 February 2025 CSU-Deccan MoUs, or the relevant provisions on dictionary governance, access, publication, and intellectual property.
5. Existing staff capacity, potential supervisors, and the engagement route for additional researchers.
6. The legal recipient of donations and applicable receipts or registrations. If donors are overseas or seek CSR treatment, have the College confirm the appropriate foreign-contribution or CSR route before accepting funds. Those permissions were not verified here.

A restricted grant can specify its research purpose and outputs while leaving findings and academic assessment independent. Agree milestone payments, publication rights, attribution, and the treatment of unfinished work at the outset.

## Suggested Opening to the Vice-Chancellor

> Alongside your reading of *Atomic Sanskrit*, I would like to explore whether Deccan College could initiate a focused programme on Sanskrit's architecture and the systems through which its knowledge has been transmitted. I may be able to bring donors to support specific fellowships, student projects, and research outputs. Could we begin by identifying two projects with members of your faculty, then have the College prepare the academic scope, approval route, and budget? A successful pilot could become the basis for a longer programme or endowed chair.
