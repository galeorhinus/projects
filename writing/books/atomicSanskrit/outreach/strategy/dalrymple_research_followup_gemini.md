# Research Verdict
The proposed "heroic erasure" comparison requires careful handling. The evidence confirms that Dalrymple praises Indian civilization and endorses external Indo-Aryan origins, forming the desired structural parallel with Müller and Macdonell. However, attributing deliberate concealment, a racial hierarchy motive, or a belief that genetics directly identifies language to Dalrymple is not supported by the inspected evidence. Such attributions are actively contradicted by his stated anti-colonial positions and his explicit rejection of a biological "Aryan race."

# Summary Lists

### (a) Claims Ready to Use
- Müller and Macdonell's specific quotations pairing high praise of Indian thought with external-origin and post-Pāṇini codification frameworks.
- Dalrymple's explicit endorsement of Indo-Aryan migration (NYRB 2005) and public recommendation of Joseph/Reich (2022).
- The historical use of anthropometry for racial classification (Risley/Galton) and the specific premise of modern genetic research providing a "plausible explanation" for linguistic similarities (Narasimhan).

### (b) Claims Requiring Author Revision
- **Deliberate Concealment:** The assertion that Dalrymple's 250 BCE scope is designed to deliberately conceal or protect the "Greek Miracle" is undermined by his explicit complaints in interviews (e.g., *The Week*, 2024) that colonial education prioritized Pythagoras over Indian mathematicians.
- **Racial Arya Thesis Endorsement:** Dalrymple explicitly rejects the concept of a biological "Aryan race" in his 2005 NYRB article, emphasizing ethnically diverse speakers. Equating his endorsement of genetic/linguistic migration with Risley's racial hierarchies is factually unsupported by his own statements.

### (c) Unresolved Research Gaps
- **Syncretic Vedic Composition:** No direct quotation from Dalrymple has been found where he claims DNA proves the Vedas were a Steppe-Harappan synthesis.
- **Full Book Verification:** *The Golden Road* analysis remains limited to the authorized preview. The full scope of his arguments regarding the pre-500 BCE period and early Greek contact cannot be conclusively assessed without the complete text and its endnotes.
