# Shelley Walia — *Atomic Sanskrit* Outreach

## Why Reach Out

Shelley Walia reviewed *Tatya Tope's Operation Red Lotus* for *The Hindu* in 2010 under the title ["Revisionism at its best"](https://www.thehindu.com/books/Revisionism-at-its-best/article15902895.ece). He therefore provides a natural point of continuity between the two books. He has already engaged with an earlier argument that challenged an inherited Western framework and reconstructed the evidence from within the Indian continuum.

The immediate purpose of the first email is to renew that connection and discover whether *Atomic Sanskrit* interests him. Do not combine that approach with requests for a review, an article, and an editorial introduction.

The strongest possible outcome is that he becomes interested enough to write independently about the book. Such an article would carry more weight than an essay written by the author. If he responds positively but does not propose writing, a later message can ask whether one of the standalone essays might suit *The Hindu* and whether he would introduce Parag to the appropriate editor.

## Recommended Subject

**Following Up on Your 2010 Review of *Operation Red Lotus***

Warmer alternative:

**Sixteen Years After *Operation Red Lotus*: A New Book**

## Recommended First Email

Dear Professor Walia,

You reviewed my first book, *Tatya Tope's Operation Red Lotus*, for *The Hindu* in 2010 under the title "Revisionism at its best." I have remembered that review with gratitude because you recognized what the book was attempting to do: question the inherited framework through which the evidence of 1857 had been presented.

I am now completing a new book, *Atomic Sanskrit*, the first volume of the *Second Shanti* series. The book examines Sanskrit as an engineered language and uses that architecture to compare two architectures of order: one creates order through a distributed calibrant, while the other imposes order from an apex. Like *Operation Red Lotus*, it challenges an established Western account, but this time at the linguistic and civilizational scale.

Given your engagement with my first book, I wondered whether you might be interested in seeing a short overview or one of the standalone essays developed from the new book. If the argument interests you, I would then be glad to send the manuscript.

Warm regards,

Parag Tope\
WhatsApp: +1 [number]

## Follow-Up Sequence

1. Send only the first email. Do not attach the full manuscript unless he has requested it.
2. If he expresses interest, send the short overview or the strongest standalone essay and ask for his thoughts.
3. If he wants to write about the book, send whatever material he needs and do not simultaneously ask him to place Parag's essay.
4. If he responds positively but does not propose writing, wait until he has engaged with the material before asking about *The Hindu*.

## Later Editorial Request

Use this only after he has responded positively to the material:

> I have also developed this argument as a standalone essay of about 1,000 words. Do you think it might be suitable for *The Hindu*? If so, would you be comfortable connecting me with the appropriate editor?

The introduction is the second possibility. His independent interest in writing about the book would be the stronger outcome.
