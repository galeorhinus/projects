# What Do Max Müller, Arthur Macdonell, and William Dalrymple Have in Common?

*Parag Tope*

They belong to a long tradition of European writers who selectively praise India while hiding something far more important. I call this pattern **heroic erasure**: celebrating the achievements of a person or a civilization while obscuring the architecture that made them possible. Even their praise ties India’s greatness to an imaginary “Aryan race” that all these writers are obsessed with. I call this obsession **RAT - the Racial Arya Thesis.**

Müller’s praise was lavish. Readers who knew Plato and Kant, he argued, would find Indian philosophy worthy of their attention. Indian literature could even correct the limitations of a European education built on Greek, Roman, and Jewish thought. Yet Müller championed RAT by placing Hindus and Europeans within an “Aryan race” embracing “all the greatest nations of the world.” His praise made India’s achievements the inheritance of that imaginary race.[^muller]

Arthur Macdonell praised Indian grammatical analysis as surpassing that of every other nation. He described Sanskrit literature as dominated by the “linguistic standard stereotyped by” पाणिनि (*Pāṇini*). Yet Macdonell gave RAT the language of conquest. He called the “Aryan” warriors “the first Western conquerors of Hindustan” and assigned the earliest Vedic poetry to “this invading race.” His praise celebrated Indian grammar while his history credited "invaders" with bringing the language and culture.[^macdonell]

Similarly, William Dalrymple's *The Golden Road* celebrates India's influence across Asia and beyond. He writes that “Indian learning, Indian religious insights and Indian ideas are among the crucial foundations of our world.”[^golden-road]

Yet Dalrymple also promoted RAT, celebrating a racial invasion account as a victory for science. In June 2017, he shared an article with the words: “Has the Aryan Invasion Theory been Finally Proven Right? Science - 1, Hindu Right-Wing - 0”. Its author, Ganesh Prasad, described “two distinct racial groups” and argued that an invading army destroyed the Indus civilization. Prasad himself acknowledged that the researchers described migration rather than invasion; Dalrymple nevertheless promoted his invasion argument as a victory for science. His praise of India's achievements sits alongside his promotion of a racial account of its origins.[^post][^raiot]

In 2022, Dalrymple recommended Tony Joseph's *Early Indians* and David Reich's *Who We Are and How We Got Here*. His recommendations again direct readers toward incoming ancestry to explain Sanskrit’s origins. The language of explanation has changed; the external origin remains.[^dalrymple-origins]

Dalrymple stretches the praise while conveniently narrowing the meaning of *ancient*. His title promises ancient India; his main story begins in 250 BCE, after Buddha and after the period celebrated as the “Greek miracle.” For Hindus whose civilizational memory reaches back thousands of years earlier, that is a late starting point. Within that frame, India transforms the world only *after* Greece has secured its place at the beginning of Europe’s intellectual history.[^golden-road]

His Vedic-to-Buddhist narrative is equally repulsive and dangerous to the Indic seeker culture. He miscasts Vedic *sacrifice* as the “traditional” old order and presents Buddhism as progress.[^golden-road] I recognize the same story in progressivism, which I describe in *Atomic Sanskrit* as the fourth Abrahamic religion. Hindu inheritance becomes the obstacle, and advancement begins by breaking with it.

In *Atomic Sanskrit*, I use **asuric pyramid** for an order in which authorized interpreters and certified intellectuals claim the right to define historical narratives, while “common” people are expected to accept their verdict.

The new vocabulary and Dalrymple’s praise make RAT easier for some Indians to accept. The latest version of the asuric pyramid’s thesis can be summarized as follows:

> India may be brilliant, generous, and influential, as long as Sanskrit’s beginnings can still be traced to incoming “Aryans.”

The compliment celebrates what India gave the world while the origin story keeps directing the credit back to “foreign DNA.”

The asuric pyramid has not given up its obsession with race “science” and has evolved from anthropometry to genetic ancestry. At one time, the pyramid measured skulls and bones and made claims about intelligence. Today, it compares DNA across populations and makes claims about language.[^bodies]

> **Just as bones don’t measure intelligence, DNA does not speak.**

Why do these writers keep returning to the ancestry of Sanskrit's speakers, rather than explaining how the language works and what has kept it unchanged for thousands of years? Ancestry can help trace population movement. It cannot establish what newcomers learned, which institutions they encountered, or whether they learned the language in India rather than brought it with them.

*Atomic Sanskrit* examines Sanskrit's organized sounds, its rules for creating words, and the Vedic system that keeps them calibrated. A calibrant is a shared standard people can consult to detect errors and correct them. My argument is that the Vedas demonstrate how such a standard can endure across generations without belonging to a single commanding institution.[^atomic]

*Atomic Sanskrit* argues that Sanskrit and India shared their radiance with the world for thousands of years before the period Dalrymple covers, providing the foundation for Greek, Latin, and other languages.[^radiance]

From Drāviḍa through the Vindhyas to the Himalayas, India’s distinctive inheritance is its seeker-based, calibrant culture. People from elsewhere may have entered that civilization and contributed to it, just as Indians may have travelled abroad and carried its ideas outward. The arrival of newcomers does not establish that they brought its foundations with them.[^migration]

> DNA does not speak. 
>
> DNA does not reveal a seeker culture. 
> 
> DNA does not distinguish सत् (*sat*) from असत् (*asat*).

India’s distinctiveness lies in its intellectual inheritance: the practice of seeking, the exercise of विवेक (*viveka*), and the responsibility to judge conduct rather than ancestry. This was, is, and always will be the true meaning of आर्यत्व (*āryatva*). **No agent of the asuric pyramid can hijack that definition.**

Indians who still hunger for white approval need to ask what that hunger makes them willing to swallow. Does selectively praising India give Dalrymple permission to promote RAT without challenge? A semi-flattering book does not erase the asuric pyramid’s obsession with race “science.” When admiration silences scrutiny, heroic erasure succeeds with the reader’s cooperation.

What has changed since the days of Müller and Macdonell?

*India has.*

Assertive Hindus are challenging the racial histories they were taught to accept. The asuric pyramid hates that defiance. I view Dalrymple’s “Science - 1, Hindu Right-Wing - 0” as a political response to Hindu assertiveness. Hindus were challenging RAT, and he presented its supposed *vindication* as their defeat. The scoreline makes the hostility unmistakable.[^post]

Today, Hindus are examining the pyramid’s claims, exposing its contradictions, and refusing to let white approval decide what they may say about their own civilization.
