## Core thesis

The similarity across the languages the academy files as *"Indo-European"* and *"Indo-Iranian"* is real. The explanation is inverted. They do not descend from a lost common parent (PIE). They are **calibrands** — languages calibrated by a single engineered **calibrant**, Sanskrit — and what each one carries is a **Pratibimba**, a reflection of the calibrant, produced by **vivimorphosis**. "PIE" is the *average of the reflections*, mistaken for their source.

> Philology took the reflections, averaged them, and called the average an ancestor. The original was never lost. It is still recited.

## A. Newspaper article skeleton (~1,200–1,500 words; Swarajya / Firstpost / Organiser)

**Hook.** Every educated reader has met the family tree: Sanskrit, Greek, Latin, Persian, Lithuanian — all "daughters" of a vanished mother tongue called Proto-Indo-European, reconstructed with asterisks because no one ever spoke it or wrote it down. The tree is taught as settled fact. It rests on a choice no one announces.

**Beat 1 — The real observation, kept.** The correspondences are not in dispute. *mātṛ / māter / mētēr / mother*; *pitṛ / pater / father*; *devaḥ / deus*. Something connects them. The question is *what*.

**Beat 2 — Two explanations for the same data.** Common descent from PIE is one. Common *contact* with one stable, prestigious, engineered language is the other. Both explain the similarity. Only one of the two languages actually exists: Sanskrit is preserved, recited, grammatically mapped, and still alive. PIE is assembled by procedure. To rank the reconstructed ancestor above the preserved calibrant is not science by default — it is a category choice. *(Reuse the calibrant-thesis section from the "movement is not authorship" essay.)*

**Beat 3 — The mechanism the academy lacks a word for.** Contact linguistics already knows that grammar and sound move between languages (Thomason–Kaufman). Its closest model is Malcolm Ross's *metatypy* — one language (the model) restructures another (the replica) while the replica keeps its own vocabulary. But every such model assumes contact between *ordinary* languages. None has a slot for a *deliberately engineered, anti-entropic* language in the model role — because only one civilization ever built one. The book names the gap: **calibrant contact**. *Public name, with a wink:* **calibrotypy** — metatypy's missing sibling (see *Public device*). They minted *metatypy* for ordinary languages; they never needed a word for an engineered model, because they never admitted one. We mint it for them.

**Beat 4 — How a word travels: vivimorphosis (the engine, in plain English).** A Sanskrit word is a built molecule (*śabda*). It enters a non-Sanskrit listener's mind as a seed (*bīja*). When that seed is finally spoken in the receiving language, it sprouts as a living, drifting form (*apaśabda*). *mā* (to measure) → *mātṛ* (the measurer, mother) → seed → *māter / mōdor / mother*. The Sanskrit chain is *visible* — atom, molecule, derivation. The European form is the sprout. **What philology calls a "root," the book shows is a sprouted seed.** Name it: **vivimorphosis** — the engineered form coming alive in another mouth.

**Beat 5 — The ṛṣis carried it: Waves 1 and 2.** It did not need a migrating race. It needed experts. **Wave 1** — pre-Pāṇinian: Sanskrit-bearing specialists (the Saptaṛṣi lineage) in multi-generational contact with the languages of Central and West Asia; the Mitanni treaty-names are direct documentation. **Wave 2** — after Pāṇini: the *method* of formal grammar itself spreads (Greek, Latin, Tibetan, Arabic, Hebrew all build grammars downstream of the Pāṇinian posture). The transmission unit is the trained expert, not the population.

**Beat 6 — Indo-Iranian, on the nose.** *asura* → *ahura*. The Avestan religious vocabulary carries a Sanskrit reflection for the whole span of its tradition. The "Indo-Iranian similarity" is the calibrant's footprint, not shared parentage.

**Beat 7 — The inversion stated.** "PIE" is the *average of the Pratibimbas* — a summary statistic of the reflections, printed as if it were the thing reflected. Remove the imaginary ancestor and the data still has a source: the one language that was never lost.

**Close.** The West reconstructed, with asterisks, a language it could not find. India kept reciting the one it never lost. The reflections were always reflections of something real.

## B. Long-form essay skeleton (longform; Swarajya longform / Pragyata / Indic platforms)

The longform earns the vocabulary the newspaper version compresses, and adds the naming argument.

1. **The asterisk.** Open on what a reconstructed form *is* — a starred non-form, by the discipline's own notation. A non-existence cannot be a source. Set the stakes: the entire family tree hangs on treating an average as an ancestor.
2. **What is not in dispute.** Lay out the correspondences honestly (*mātṛ*, *pitṛ*, *devaḥ*, *asura*…). Concede everything the comparison gets right. The fight is over interpretation, not data.
3. **The two readings of one dataset.** Descent vs. contact. The asymmetry that decides it: one candidate is *preserved* (recited, mapped, calibrated, alive), the other *reconstructed*. Why the preserved system was demoted beneath the invented one — and whose interests that served (link the *asuric pyramid* / foundational-dogma frame lightly, for the Indic audience).
4. **Sanskrit as calibrant.** The third category — not a natural language, not a codified one, but *saṃskṛti*, engineered and anti-entropic. A calibrant is a standard that is not itself moved by what it measures. (Pull from Ch 5 §5.6 / Ch 6.)
5. **The gap in contact linguistics.** Thomason–Kaufman; Ross's *metatypy* and the Takia/Waskia case; substrate / superstrate / adstrate. Show methodically that Sanskrit fits none of the slots, because all of them assume natural-to-natural contact. Name the missing category: **calibrant contact** — the engineered-model case the discipline never had to build a word for. Then, for the public essay, give it the discipline's own suffix and needle them with it: **calibrotypy**. *Metatypy* assumes the model is just another language; *calibrotypy* names the case where the model was engineered to be the measure — same morphology, the slot they left empty. (Body keeps *calibrant contact*; the wink belongs to the essay.)
6. **Vivimorphosis — the three-stage engine.** *śabda* (built molecule) → *bīja* (seed in the receiving mind) → *apaśabda* (the living sprout). Walk *mātṛ → mother* and *devaḥ → deus* as full chains; contrast with the philological "root," which is the sprout mistaken for the seed. This is the section that *only this piece* delivers publicly.
7. **The carriers: the ṛṣis, not a race.** Expert-to-expert transmission. **Wave 1** (Saptaṛṣi roster; Mitanni anchor; the structural features into Central/West Asian languages). **Wave 2** (Pāṇinian *methodological metatypy*: Greek/Latin/Tibetan/Arabic/Hebrew grammars downstream of the Aṣṭādhyāyī posture; Chinese as the contrast case that disciplines the claim). The transmission unit is the trained specialist; demography is not required.
8. **Indo-Iranian and Indo-European as calibrand sets.** Reframe the two "families." They are not branches of a tree; they are **calibrand languages** — the set calibrated by Sanskrit, each carrying its own *Pratibimba*. The *asura → ahura* case for Indo-Iranian; the *mātṛ / devaḥ* cases for Indo-European.
9. **The naming move (the essay's payload).** *Indo-European* is a geography plus a fake genealogy. Offer the replacements the book actually uses and coins: **Pratibimba languages** (evocative — reflections of the calibrant; ties to the Sūrya / eclipse frame: the touched languages reflect the Sun's light) and **calibrand** (precise — *that which is calibrated*; sibling of *calibrant*, formed like *operand* / *analysand*). A term that encodes the relation — *downstream of a standard, not descended from a parent* — instead of a map and a guess.
10. **The inversion, fully argued.** PIE is the *average of the Pratibimbas* — a summary statistic misrepresented as a source. The correspondences haven't changed; the explanation has. The Vedic order is the right one: *formed Speech first, reflections afterward.*
11. **Close — what was never lost.** The reconstructed ancestor was an attempt to find a source for reflections whose source was always recited and never gone. Re-pointing the comparative project at *Pratibimba*-mapping (Sanskrit → calibrand) is not surrender; it is the corrected research program.
