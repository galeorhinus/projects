## Principal Argument

The essay begins with a sound that readers already know. When the traditional
**अ-उ-म् (*a-u-m*)** expansion is pronounced on one breath, the body moves from
open voiced resonance through oral shaping into lip closure and nasal
resonance. Oṃ therefore gives the reader a compact physical experience of the
major systems that produce speech.

The **वर्णमाला (*varṇamālā*)** performs a different task. It divides the
speaking instrument into measured sound-particles that Sanskrit can teach,
repeat, combine, and preserve. The essay calls each independently reusable
sound-particle a **sonomer**.

The essay then introduces the book's fractal thesis in plain English: Oṃ,
sonomers, *dhātavaḥ*, words, and sentences display related engineering at
different scales.

## Editorial Decisions

1. **Oṃ is an *akṣara*, not a sonomer.** The essay must not describe Oṃ as one
   bead within the *varṇamālā*.
2. **The meru-bead analogy is retired.** It blurs the scale of the *akṣara* with
   the scale of the sonomer inventory.
3. **"Whole instrument" requires an explicit boundary.** Oṃ engages the major
   systems that turn breath into speech; it does not touch every anatomical
   position used by the *varṇamālā*.
4. **Do not equate every *varṇa* directly with a sonomer.** The book uses
   *sonomer* for a measured sound-particle available for independent reuse. On
   the consonant plane, a selected consonantal sound is a sonance, and it
   becomes a sonomer when Sanskrit assigns it an independent grid address. The
   essay does not need the additional term *sonance*.
5. **Use one coined term.** The newspaper essay introduces *sonomer* but omits
   *audiograph*, PASS, sonance, and off-grid sonance.
6. **Keep the anatomy concrete.** The lungs maintain pressure, the vocal cords
   vibrate, the mouth reshapes resonance, the lips close, and the nasal passage
   opens.
7. **Keep the Upaniṣadic claim separate from the anatomical inference.** The
   Upaniṣads do not function as phonetics manuals. Their treatment of Oṃ across
   time gives the anatomical compression a larger Sanskrit frame.
8. **Retain the closing beat only after the explanation is complete:**
   "One breath. One *akṣara*. The major systems of speech. The whole span of
   time. Then the garland opens."

## Primary Texts

- *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 1.1.1–3: Oṃ as the *udgītha* and the essence of
  essences.
- *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* 1 and 8–12: Oṃ as “all this,” its extension across the
  three times, and its unfolding as **अ-उ-म्** and the fourth.
- *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* 1.8.1: Oṃ is Brahman; Oṃ is all this.
- *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* 1.2.15–17: the goal declared by the Vedas condensed into Oṃ.
- *Yoga Sūtra* 1.27: **तस्य वाचकः प्रणवः (*tasya vācakaḥ praṇavaḥ*)**.
