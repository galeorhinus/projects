INTRODUCTION 

- Kerala’s contribution to Sanskrit grammatical’ studies is signi- 
ficant. The history of this branch starts from about 9th century A.D 
Of the eighteen Sabhamathas of Kerala which were established to 
promote studies in the different Sastras, the Covvanntr Sabhamatha 
specialised in grammatical studies. From an inscription found at the 
Parthivasekharapuram temple in Kanyakumari District we know that 
king Karunantatakkan has endowed a chair for. Vyakarana in th 
academy attached to it- Kerala kings generally encouraged such studies 
by giving awards like Bhattasthánam (Pattattanam) to the proficient 
scholars in the respective Sastras. The family of Küdallür Nampátiris 
won recognition in this field to such an extent that even outsiders eame 
to Kerala to study under their guidance. 

Among the different systems of grammar Panini’s Astadhyayi 
was the most popular in Kerala. Several recasts of Panini‘s work 
appeared as a result. Grammatical 90215 too were written here. But 
the Kerala scholars specialised ia other systems also. Both Sanskrit 
and Prakrt grammars Were composed by Keralites. The Trivandrum 
Manuscripts Library has done significant work in bringing out 
many of these publications. The first book in the famous Trivandrum 
Sanskrit Series Was significantly, a work on grammar, viz; Daivam by 
Deva with the commentary styled Purusakara composed by Krsnalis 


la$uka. Vararucasangraha which is spoken of as the work of a Keraliya 
Vararuci. is the earliest grammatical work in metrical form produced 


in Kerala, and was published in the seríes. It is again in this series 


that the Prakriyásarvasva of Melputtur Nàrayanabbatta appeared for 
the first-time-. The present publication Dháturatnávali is yet anothr 
work on grammar now issued in the series

Dhaturatnavali 

There are several works in Sanskrit explaining the different verbal 
forms. The Paryayapadavali of Vasudeva is one such work aiving 
synonymns of verbal forms having the same sense. Dhaturatnivil 
is the work of an young scholar who derived benefit Mrom the tamou- 
Matrdatta, the father of Melputtür Nàràyapabhatta. As supe: 
by the name itself it isa collection of homonymous verbal forms but 
having a difference in their meanings. The nature of the work is ex-

Nature of the work 


The present work purports to enumerate the root forms having 
an identity in their outward appearance and having a difference in 
meaning. The study of such forms is apparently useful to poets. Daiva 
of Deva also is composed in a similar manner. The author of Dhitu 
ratnavalí has admittedly followed the footsteps of his predecessor 
Deva to whom he is indebted in a large measure. The author has. 
acknowledged his indebtedness in the following stanza:

of 64] roots alone are dealt with Dhaturatnavali enumerates 1038 
roots with similar forms and different meanings. Hence we 


may say that our author improved much on his earlier writers. 


Similarity in roots 
ee 


It is said that the work enumerates similar forms with dissimilar 
sense. What exactly is the similarity implied here?, one may ask. A 
close study of the work enables one to find out certain norms for the 


similarity intended here. Thus three different norms can be applied 


inthis connection. 


(1) Total similarity in the outward form with a difference in 


meaning. For instance there are two different verbs having the form Bhi. 


——— 


The first is Bhá having the meaning of ‘existence’ and belonging to 


Parasmaipada (भूसत्तायाम्‌ TRAIT). The second form is Bhi

different. 


(2) Though there is a slight diflerence due 10 indicatory letters 
there may be similarity in roots. For instance, there are two roots as 
‘Agi and ‘Agi’. [t may be noted that the similarity consists only in 
the root proper, viz, “Ag? to which the indicatory “a” and “i” are 
added in the root form. Naturally the inflected forms differ as “Agati” 
and “Aiigati™ in Lat 3rd person singular. Still our author considers 


these verbs as having some similarity. 


(3) Thethird form of similarity is based on the resultant forms 
only. Here the root forms do suggest this possibility. For instance 
there are two roots as **Nedr" and “धाता. But the resultant inflected 


form is the same for both namely **Nedati"".
