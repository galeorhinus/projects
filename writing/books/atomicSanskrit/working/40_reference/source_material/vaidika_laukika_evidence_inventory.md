# Vaidika-Laukika Evidence Inventory

## Purpose

This inventory separates primary evidence, reliable working examples,
interpretive proposals, and open research. The appendix can argue from verified
examples while placing unresolved tests in notes instead of turning them into
body claims.

Status terms:

- **Verified**: the cited primary text or rule has been checked directly.
- **Strong**: the form and operation are secure; exact source presentation still
  needs final editorial checking.
- **Partial**: part of the claim is secure, while another part needs
  verification or narrower wording.
- **Open**: do not deploy as fact.

## Primary Texts Checked

### Ṛgveda 1.16.1

The Śākala text and *padapāṭha* preserve:

> आ त्वा॑ वहन्तु॒ हर॑यो॒ वृष॑णं॒ सोम॑पीतये ।
> इन्द्र॑ त्वा॒ सूर॑चक्षसः ॥

The *padapāṭha* separates **आ । त्वा । वहन्तु**, and the verse is identified as
Gāyatrī.

Source: [Ṛgveda 1.16.1, Saṃhitā and Padapāṭha](https://sri-aurobindo.co.in/workings/matherials/rigveda/01/01-016.htm)

## Interpretive Claims That Must Remain Tests

### Productive leṭ

The formal overlap between first-person *leṭ* and *loṭ* is real. The stronger
claim remains open: Sanskrit's engineers may have kept *leṭ* from unrestricted
laukika generation because its added semantic benefit did not justify the
larger ambiguity surface. Before this enters body prose as a conclusion, the
research must compare:

1. complete *leṭ* and *loṭ* paradigms;
2. Vedic accents;
3. actual Vedic passages;
4. laukika means of expressing the same semantic range;
5. forms whose analyses remain distinct despite surface similarity.

### Social division of responsibility

The architectural model does not require separate populations, and the same
person can plainly study both domains. The proposed household and lineage matrix
needs historical examples before the appendix describes a specific social
pattern across India.

### Every visible difference has one functional cause

The fixed-corpus / open-generation distinction explains several important
differences. It should not become a universal explanation imposed before the
forms are examined. Each example must establish its local operation.
