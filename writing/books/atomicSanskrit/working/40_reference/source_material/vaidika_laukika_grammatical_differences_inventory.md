# Vaidika-Laukika Grammatical Differences Inventory

## Purpose

This inventory supports the proposed composite table in Appendix Part 8. It
collects the categories in which vaidika and laukika Sanskrit deploy the shared
language architecture differently. The table in the appendix will summarize
categories; the sections beneath it will explain representative forms and
passages.

The inventory does not turn difference into chronology. A form preserved in a
Vedic passage and not ordinarily generated in laukika composition establishes
different deployment. Any claim that one form changed into another requires
independent evidence of sequence and replacement.

## Source Method

Pāṇini, the Prātiśākhyas, the Vedic passages, and the recitation disciplines
supply the evidence used in the book's own explanation. Arthur Macdonell's
Appendix III, "Chief Peculiarities of Vedic Grammar," supplies a useful external
catalogue of the differences that Western teaching commonly places on a
timeline. Its catalogue is useful; its chronological framing is not adopted.

## Status Terms

- **Ready**: a verified or strong example already supports appendix prose.
- **Partial**: the category is secure, but its representative passage or local
  function needs further checking.
- **Research**: retain in the map, but do not offer a causal explanation until
  the Sanskrit evidence has been assembled.

## Composite Category Map

| Category | Vaidika deployment to examine | Laukika deployment to examine | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitch accent** | *udātta, anudātta,* and *svarita* remain part of the transmitted expression | ordinary composition does not require the same three-way marking | Ready |
| **Duration** | *pluta* and other governed durations remain available where the expression requires them | ordinary generation principally uses the reusable short-long vowel relations | Partial |
| **Lineage-preserved realizations** | contextual **ळ / ळ्ह** and other Veda-specific realizations remain exact in their stated environments | the ordinary sonomer grid does not give every realization an independent coordinate | Ready |
| **Contextual articulations** | *upadhmānīya, jihvāmūlīya,* and related boundary sounds are preserved under their conditions | the productive grid treats them as governed junction-results rather than independent sonomers | Ready for *upadhmānīya*; remaining examples need checking |
| **Sandhi and hiatus** | received passages can preserve hiatus, non-elision, and domain-scoped junction behavior | productive sandhi operates across newly generated expressions | Ready for *pragṛhya* and three preserved-hiatus passages |
| **Nominal endings** | alternate instrumental, locative, vocative, dual, and plural formations remain available where passages use them | open composition relies on consistently reusable declensional paradigms | Ready for **-ebhiḥ / -aiḥ**; remaining endings need checking |
| **Pronouns** | additional case distinctions and alternate forms occur in the Vedic corpus | ordinary productive use employs the laukika pronoun paradigms | Ready for the five-way personal-pronoun dual, compact **एनोः**, and *pragṛhya* e-forms |
| **Augment and injunctive** | augmented and unaugmented finite forms remain preserved; unaugmented forms can perform the operation commonly called injunctive | ordinary productive paradigms do not deploy the injunctive as a complete independent resource | Ready for **प्र वोचम्** at RV 1.32.1 |
| **Verbal modes** | *leṭ* and a wider distribution of mode-forms remain available under Vedic scope | laukika composition uses the productive *lakāra* system assigned to its scope | Ready for *leṭ* distribution; its architectural cause remains open |
| **Personal endings** | endings such as **-masi, -thana, -tana,** and imperative **-tāt** extend the available verbal shapes | ordinary productive paradigms principally use **-mas, -tha, -ta,** and their laukika counterparts | Ready for **इमसि / इमः** at RV 1.1.7 |
| **Participles** | the corpus preserves additional participial formations, including aorist participles | laukika generation uses its productive participial system | Partial: **चिकित्वः / चिकित्वन्** passage verified; local contribution open |
| **Gerunds** | forms in **-tvī** and rare **-tvāya** occur beside **-tvā**, with additional prefixed forms | laukika generation principally uses **-tvā** and **-ya** under their productive conditions | Partial: **पीत्वी / पीत्वा** verified; local contribution open |
| **Infinitives** | several case-shaped infinitive formations remain available, including **-tum, -tave, -tavai, -dhyai, -tos,** and others | laukika generation principally uses the productive **-tum** formation | Ready for **अन्वेतवै** and **प्रतिधातवे** at RV 1.24.8 |
| **Upasarga placement** | an operator may precede, follow, or stand apart from its atom in a fixed expression | ordinary generation binds the operator locally before its atom | Ready |
| **Particles and case-governing forms** | particles, enclitics, and forms functioning like prepositions interact with Vedic accent and case relations | laukika use preserves the relevant indeclinables and case architecture under its productive syntax | Research |
| **Sentence accent** | vocatives, finite verbs, subordinate clauses, particles, and separated operators participate in sentence-level accent | ordinary laukika expression does not preserve the same accentual specification | Ready for the sentence-initial and non-initial **अग्ने** comparison |
| **Compounds and derivation** | the corpus preserves the compounds and domain-scoped affixes selected for each expression | laukika Sanskrit recursively generates compounds and derivatives for new use | Partial |
| **Composition and style** | the Vedas, Brāhmaṇas, Āraṇyakas, and Upaniṣads preserve several distinct styles inside the vaidika domain | laukika authors generate prose, poetry, analysis, drama, story, and individual styles | Ready as a domain distinction; worked pairs still needed |

## Detailed Research Groups

### 1. Sound, Accent, and Junctions

Ready examples:

- Ṛgveda 1.1.1 **ईळे (*īḷe*)** and the Ṛgveda-Prātiśākhya
  **ड → ळ / ढ → ळ्ह** operation;
- Ṛgveda 1.1.2 **अग्निः पूर्वेभिर् / अग्निᳶ पूर्वेभिर्** and the
  governed *upadhmānīya*;
- *udātta, anudātta,* and *svarita* in Vedic recitation;
- sentence-initial **अग्ने॑** at Ṛgveda 3.25.1 beside non-initial,
  unaccented **अग्ने** at Ṛgveda 1.1.7.

Evidence still needed:

1. one secure *pluta* passage with its local function;
2. integration of the verified *pragṛhya* and preserved-hiatus examples into
   the appendix prose;
3. one example each for *jihvāmūlīya* and any other contextual articulation
   retained in the final table;
4. an explanation of which junction differences belong to Vedic scope and
   which belong to a particular śākhā.

### 2. Nouns and Pronouns

The complete category-level catalogue now lives in
[`vaidika_laukika_declensional_variations_complete.md`](vaidika_laukika_declensional_variations_complete.md).
It separates system-wide alternatives, ending-class differences, pronoun
distinctions, numeral behavior, and rare lexical forms before selecting the
smaller set of examples for Appendix Part 8.

Ready example:

- Ṛgveda 10.125.1 places **रुद्रेभिः / वसुभिः** beside **आदित्यैः /
  विश्वदेवैः**, showing the longer **-ebhiḥ** and shorter **-aiḥ**
  *tṛtīyā bahuvacanam* forms within one mantra.

Categories requiring representative passages:

- singular instrumental alternatives;
- locative alternatives;
- vocative alternatives;
- dual endings and case distinctions;
- plural **-āsas / -ās** and other plural alternatives;
- Vedic pronoun forms and their case distinctions.

The appendix should not claim that meter explains every alternative. Each
passage must show what meter, accent, emphasis, syntax, or another condition
contributes.

### 3. Verbs

Ready or partially ready:

- Pāṇini 3.4.6-8 and 3.4.94-98 document *leṭ* under Vedic scope;
- the visible **भवानि, भवाव, भवाम** overlap with laukika *loṭ* supports
  a benefit-to-confusion test without yet proving the reason for the
  distribution;
- Ṛgveda 1.32.1 **प्र वोचम्** supplies an exact unaugmented form whose
  performative function comes from the passage;
- Ṛgveda 1.1.7 **इमसि** supplies a personal ending whose additional syllable
  completes a Gāyatrī *pāda*;
- Ṛgveda 3.40.7 **पीत्वी** supplies a Vedic gerund beside laukika **पीत्वा**,
  although the local reason for the alternate ending remains open;
- Ṛgveda 1.24.8 supplies **अन्वेतवै** and **प्रतिधातवे** beside laukika
  **अन्वेतुम्** and **प्रतिधातुम्**;
- Ṛgveda 3.25.1 supplies participial vocative **चिकित्वः** beside laukika
  **चिकित्वन्**, while the local contribution remains open.

Further categories:

- additional augment behavior beyond the worked **वोचम्** example;
- personal endings beyond the worked **-masi** example;
- verbal formations in the perfect and aorist systems;
- participles beyond **चिकित्वः**;
- gerunds in **-tvāya** and further **-tvī** passages;
- infinitive formations beyond the worked **-tavai / -tave** examples;
- domain-scoped affixes and endings.

### 4. Operators, Particles, and Sentence Relations

Ready example:

- Aitareya-Brāhmaṇa prose **आ ... दत्ते** and **निर् ... नुदते**, read with
  Pāṇini 1.4.80-82, demonstrates separated operators outside metrical verse;
- Ṛgveda 3.25.1 and 1.1.7 show how sentence position changes the accent of
  vocative **अग्ने**.

Further categories:

- sentence-level accent on finite verbs;
- accent and placement of operators in principal and subordinate clauses;
- enclitic particles and pronouns;
- case-governing indeclinables;
- the relation between fixed sequence in the Veda and local binding in newly
  generated laukika prose.

### 5. Compounds, Derivation, and Style

The shared compound and derivational architecture is secure. The appendix still
needs paired examples before it assigns a specific function to differences in
compound frequency, shape, or style. Style must remain separate from grammar:
the four Vedas and their associated prose can sound different while using one
architecture, just as laukika authors can develop distinct voices without
creating new languages.

## Proposed Appendix Deployment

The technical portion of Appendix Part 8 should follow the inventory:

1. **The Grammatical Differences at a Glance** — the composite table;
2. **Sound, Accent, and Junctions** — pitch, duration, contextual sounds,
   lineage realizations, sandhi, and hiatus;
3. **Operators, Particles, and Sentence Relations** — *upasargas*, particles,
   case government, binding, and sentence accent;
4. **Nouns and Pronouns** — case endings, word classes, and pronoun forms;
5. **Verbs and Their Forms** — augment, injunctive, *leṭ*, endings,
   participles, gerunds, and infinitives;
6. **Composition and Style** — compounds, derivation, Vedic styles, and open
   laukika composition.

The appendix may state the existence and distribution of every secure category.
It should explain architectural purpose only where the evidence shows that
purpose. Open categories remain in the Source and Reference Companion until a
passage-level analysis is ready.
