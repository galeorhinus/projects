## 1. Purpose

The main body should not become a catalogue of opponents. The main body prosecutes the architecture: Sanskrit as *saṃskṛti*, Pāṇini as decoder, PIE as manufactured ancestor, and the *asuric pyramid* as the institutional formation that required the theft.

The appendix does a different job. It gives serious readers, reviewers, and allies a clean map of the battle positions. It explains where the named figures belong without letting them occupy the central stage.

Recommended title:

> **Appendix Part X — The Battle Positions**

Alternative titles:

- **The Three Positions on Sanskrit Engineering**
- **Heroic Erasure and Cultural Construction**
- **The Containment Positions**
- **Three Ways to Misread Sanskrit**

Recommendation: **The Battle Positions**. It is shorter, clearer, and matches the book's prosecutorial frame.

---

## 2. Core Claim

The appendix should state the taxonomy plainly:

> The battle is not between "Sanskrit was engineered" and "Sanskrit was not engineered." The contemporary apparatus has learned to concede limited sophistication while denying the architecture that matters. Its two containment positions are heroic erasure and cultural-political construction.

The main body can keep the argument clean. The appendix can name the positions.

## 3. Proposed Structure

### X.1 Why This Appendix Exists

Open with the body-vs-appendix discipline:

> The main chapters do not give these positions more space than they deserve. The architecture is the evidence. This appendix names the battle positions so that the reader can recognize the moves when they appear in academic books, journalism, popular histories, and institutional speech.

Purpose:

- Keep named opponents out of the Preface body.
- Avoid turning the book into a literature review.
- Give allies a compact companion.
- Prevent reviewers from pretending the book failed to know the existing positions.

### X.2 Position 1 — Pre-Pāṇinian Engineering

This is the book's position.

Core statement:

> Sanskrit's architecture was operating before Pāṇini. The *varṇamālā*, the Vedic corpus, the *Prātiśākhya* disciplines, the *Nirukta*, the *Dhātupāṭha*, the calibration matrix, and the *pāṭha* systems all point to an already operating architecture. Pāṇini decoded and compressed the system. He did not manufacture the language.

Named lineage belongs here:

- The *paramparā* itself.
- Pāṇini, Patañjali, Bhartṛhari, Yāska, Sāyaṇa as internal witnesses.
- Dayānanda, Aurobindo, Kapali Sastry, Sampadananda Mishra, Ojha, Kak, Kapoor, and Malhotra as modern or recent Position-1 / adjacent advocates.

Use the appendix to clarify that this lineage is not a modern invention.

### X.3 Position 2 — Heroic Erasure

Definition:

> Position 2 praises Pāṇini as formal genius, codifier, or proto-computational grammarian while denying that the architecture he documented existed before him.

Key move:

> Praise the hero. Hide the architecture.

Named figures go here, mostly in endnote form:

- Frits Staal
- Paul Kiparsky
- George Cardona
- Rick Briggs
- Others only if needed for a later source apparatus

Treatment:

- Do not over-litigate them.
- Give one structural paragraph.
- Send individual source details to endnotes.
- Use them as evidence of the position, not as central antagonists.

Draft paragraph:

> Heroic erasure is effective because it looks like praise. Pāṇini is celebrated as formal genius, codifier, or precursor to modern computation. The praise is real enough to disarm suspicion, but its function is containment. It places the engineering downstream of a named individual and removes the pre-Pāṇinian architecture from consideration.

### X.4 Position 3 — Cultural-Political Construction

Definition:

> Position 3 treats Sanskrit as elite cultural-political construction: a language of power, court, caste, cosmopolis, and social domination.

Named figure:

- Sheldon Pollock is the central contemporary exemplar.

Structural placement:

> Pollock is not the old racial science. He is its postwar academic reincarnation: the same category-theft, cleaned of biological vocabulary and recoded as culture, power, and elite production.

Important distinction:

- Do not call this "plainly 21st-century racial science" in the body.
- Say: *post-racial successor-form of the racial Arya machinery*.
- The vocabulary changes: race becomes culture; skulls become power; biological hierarchy becomes social construction; Sanskrit's architecture becomes elite production.

Draft paragraph:

> Position 3 is what the racial Arya machinery becomes after race-language is no longer usable. The skull-measuring apparatus changes vocabulary. Biology becomes culture. Race becomes power. Civilizational architecture becomes elite construction. The old denial survives in a cleaner academic dress.

### X.5 What Positions 2 and 3 Share

This is the appendix's central comparison.

Position 2 and Position 3 disagree about:

- whether the key figure is Pāṇini or later elite producers,
- whether the construction is formal-linguistic or cultural-political,
- whether the key language is grammar or power.

They agree on the move that matters:

> Pre-Pāṇinian engineering must not be on the table.

Draft hammer:

> Position 2 says: Pāṇini did it. Position 3 says: elites did it. The book says: the architecture was already there.

### X.6 Genealogy — From Racial Science to Culture-Power Vocabulary

Purpose:

- Explain how old racial science mutates after WWII.
- Connect this appendix to Ch 2, Ch 3, Ch 17, Ch 18, Appendix Part 1, and Appendix Part 2.

Core sequence:

1. Nineteenth-century racial Arya thesis: language, race, skull, nose, bloodline.
2. Post-WWII embarrassment: explicit race-science vocabulary becomes difficult to use.
3. Successor vocabulary: culture, power, elite construction, social dominance, cosmopolis.
4. Contemporary public layer: popular books and journalism translate the doctrine outward.

Draft paragraph:

> The postwar academy did not abandon the machinery. It changed its dress. The biological vocabulary became unusable, so the apparatus learned new speech: culture, power, elite production, social dominance, cosmopolis, migration, genetics, and public history. The target remained Sanskrit's category. The method remained containment.

### X.7 The Missionary Layer

This section handles journalists, popular historians, prestige-press writers, and public intellectuals who carry the doctrine outward.

Use **lower-tier missionaries of progress** or **popularizing missionaries of progress**, not *low-level*. The claim should be structural, not personal. Avoid asserting private motive.

Core framing:

> Below the priestly layer sits the missionary layer: journalists, popular historians, public intellectuals, and prestige-press translators who carry the doctrine outward. Their role is not to decide the doctrine. Their role is to make the doctrine portable, respectable, and emotionally usable for the public.

Applied to Sanskrit:

> In the Sanskrit case, they translate contested reconstructions into common sense: PIE becomes "the ancestor," the steppe becomes "the homeland," Sanskrit becomes "one branch," and the civilizational memory of Bhārata becomes myth.

Pyramidal incentive:

> The incentive is pyramidal: carry the doctrine outward, attack the civilizational architecture that threatens the pyramid, and receive platforms, reviews, prestige, access, and ascent.

Laura Spinney placement:

> A recent public-facing example is Laura Spinney's ***Proto: How One Ancient Language Went Global*** (2025), which presents PIE for a general readership through the now-standard synthesis of linguistic reconstruction, archaeology, ancient DNA, migration, and the steppe / Yamnaya story. In this book's taxonomy, that is missionary work for the progressive dogma: contested reconstruction is turned into public narrative.

Endnote stub:

`popularizing-missionaries-of-progress-pie`

Endnote coverage:

- Laura Spinney, *Proto: How One Ancient Language Went Global* (William Collins, 2025).
- Tony Joseph, *Early Indians* (2018), if used.
- David Reich, *Who We Are and How We Got Here* (2018), if used.
- David W. Anthony, *The Horse, the Wheel, and Language* (2007), if used.
- Any additional public-facing or semi-public-facing PIE / migration / ancient-DNA works the book chooses to cite.

Endnote discipline:

- Keep the body focused on the role.
- Put names, titles, dates, and source-specific details in the note.
- Avoid assigning private motive. Describe function and incentive.

### X.8 Verdict

Close the appendix with containment.

Draft close:

> These are not neutral alternatives. They are containment positions. Heroic erasure praises the documenter and hides the architecture. Cultural-political construction praises sophistication and relocates it into power. The missionary layer then carries the doctrine outward as common sense. The positions differ in vocabulary. They serve the same pyramid.

Final hammer:

> The book does not ask which containment position is more elegant. It rejects the containment.
