# Vaidika–Laukika Declensional Variations: Complete Category Inventory

**Status:** research inventory  
**Scope:** nouns, adjectives, pronouns, and numerals  
**Purpose:** retain every category of declensional variation before deciding what belongs in Appendix Part 8 and what belongs in the Source and Reference Companion

This inventory is complete at the **category level**. It records every systematic, class-level, and exceptional category identified in the declension chapters of the reference grammars listed below. It is not yet a concordance of every occurrence in the Vedic corpus. Each form selected for body prose still requires a verified passage, *padaccheda*, translation, and local explanation.

The categories are divided into three levels:

1. **System-wide or productive Vedic alternatives** — the clearest appendix candidates.
2. **Ending-class or word-class differences** — useful for the fuller table and endnotes.
3. **Rare, lexical, or isolated forms** — retained for completeness but unsuitable for the main explanation unless a passage gives them special value.

The word **ending-class** is used instead of the botanical term *stem*. Thus, *akārānta* means a word whose base form ends in **अ (*a*)**.

## 6. Categories That Affect Declension Without Changing the Visible Ending

These belong in the total inventory but should not be mixed into the table of alternate written forms.

| ID | Category | Vaidika difference | Why it remains separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC-01 | Accent of the vocative | The first syllable of a Vedic vocative expression receives the accent, while later words in the same vocative expression lose theirs | Recitation and sentence accent |
| AC-02 | Accent movement within declension | Several ending classes move the pitch to the ending in cases where laukika writing does not preserve the contrast | Audible rather than segmental difference |
| AC-03 | Resolved endings | Forms such as **-enā, -ebhiaḥ, -ānām** can occupy more syllables in Vedic recitation than their written form suggests | Meter and recitation rather than a separate *vibhakti* |
| AC-04 | *Pragṛhya* case forms | Forms such as **-ī** or **-e** can resist the expected sound combination at a following vowel | Junction behavior attached to a declined form |

## 7. Appendix Candidates

The appendix can explain the architecture without presenting dozens of paradigms. The strongest representative set is:

1. ***तृतीया एकवचनम्, अकारान्त*** — ***यज्ञेन / यज्ञा (*yajñena / yajñā*)***.
2. ***तृतीया एकवचनम्, आकारान्त*** — ***मनीषया / मनीषा (*manīṣayā / manīṣā*)***.
3. ***सप्तमी एकवचनम्, इकारान्त*** — ***अग्नौ / अग्ना (*agnau / agnā*)***.
4. ***प्रथमा–द्वितीया–संबोधन द्विवचनम्, अकारान्त*** — ***देवौ / देवा (*devau / devā*)***.
5. ***प्रथमा बहुवचनम्, अकारान्त पुल्लिङ्ग*** — ***देवाः / देवासः (*devāḥ / devāsaḥ*)***.
6. ***प्रथमा–द्वितीया बहुवचनम्, अकारान्त नपुंसकलिङ्ग*** — ***विश्वानि / विश्वा (*viśvāni / viśvā*)***.
7. ***तृतीया बहुवचनम्, अकारान्त*** — ***रुद्रैः / रुद्रेभिः (*rudraiḥ / rudrebhiḥ*)***.
8. ***ईकारान्त स्त्रीलिङ्ग द्विवचनम्*** — ***देव्यौ / देवी (*devyau / devī*)***.
9. ***ईकारान्त स्त्रीलिङ्ग प्रथमा बहुवचनम्*** — ***देव्यः / देवीः (*devyaḥ / devīḥ*)***.
10. Personal-pronoun dual — the Vedic five-way distinction against the three laukika combined forms.

These ten examples cover:

- singular, dual, and plural;
- masculine, feminine, and neuter;
- nominal and pronominal forms;
- shorter and longer alternatives;
- added syllables, removed syllables, and preserved case distinctions;
- Vedic flexibility that the productive laukika domain does not need.

### Working conclusion

The declensional difference is much larger than the single **-ebhiḥ / -aiḥ** contrast. It includes:

- alternative singular instrumental and locative forms;
- a widespread Vedic preference for **-ā** in the masculine dual;
- shorter neuter plurals;
- expanded masculine plurals;
- separate dual cases in the personal pronouns;
- distinctive vocatives;
- additional feminine **ī** forms;
- ending-class redistribution;
- numeral forms and case-agreement patterns;
- accent and syllabic realizations attached to declined words.

The appendix should demonstrate the categories through a small number of memorable passages. The Source and Reference Companion can preserve this complete map and the remaining examples.
