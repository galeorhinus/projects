# Vaidika-Laukika Terminology Decisions

## Purpose

This file settles the vocabulary for the proposed appendix **One Architecture,
Two Domains**. It prevents a useful computer-science comparison from implying
that Sanskrit contains two separate engines, that the Veda is inert, or that
visible differences establish chronology.

These decisions govern the working draft. They do not alter the manuscript
until the appendix and its body deployment have been reviewed.

## Core Formulation

Sanskrit uses one language architecture under two different permissions:

- the **vaidika domain** preserves a read-only reference corpus whose transmitted
  content cannot be revised;
- the **laukika domain** provides a write-enabled generative space in which
  speakers can create new expression through the same Sanskrit architecture.

The permission applies to **content**, not to the language architecture. The
shared architecture remains invariant across both domains.

## The Computer-Science Comparison

Use this developed explanation on first deployment:

> Computer systems distinguish between information that may be used and
> information that may be changed. A read-only file can be opened, consulted,
> and run, but its contents cannot be overwritten. A write-enabled space allows
> users to create and revise new material. Sanskrit makes a comparable
> distinction between its two domains. The vaidika corpus remains read-only: it
> is recited, studied, analyzed, and used as a calibrant, while its transmitted
> content stays unchanged. The laukika domain is write-enabled: speakers use the
> same Sanskrit architecture to create new words, poems, arguments, sciences,
> and stories.

After that explanation, use:

- **read-only reference corpus**
- **write-enabled generative domain**
- **one language engine**
- **shared architecture**
- **permission boundary**

Avoid:

- **ROM language / RAM language**
- **Vedic ROM**
- **two engines**
- **static language / dynamic language**
- **closed language / open language**

RAM implies volatility, while the laukika domain remains calibrated Sanskrit.
The word *static* implies inactivity, although the Veda continues to sound,
teach, calibrate, and support analysis.

## Domain, Mode, Scope, and Style

### Domain

***Vaidika* (वैदिक)** and ***laukika* (लौकिक)** describe the two broad
civilizational domains in which Sanskrit operates.

### Operational scope

***Chandasi* (छन्दसि)** and ***bhāṣāyām* (भाषायाम्)** are scope markers used
inside Pāṇini's documentation. They tell the reader where a stated operation
applies.

The two pairs are related, but they are not automatic synonyms:

- *vaidika* is broader than a single Pāṇinian rule scope;
- *chandasi* cannot be reduced to metrical verse because rules marked
  *chandasi* also govern Vedic prose;
- *laukika* denotes a civilizational domain, while *bhāṣāyām* marks the scope of
  an operation in Pāṇini's analysis.

The two scopes also support different acts of learning. Pāṇini's *chandasi*
documentation helps students understand the Sanskrit preserved in the Vedas.
His *bhāṣāyām* documentation helps them understand laukika compositions and
create new ones. The Vedas did not become rigid because Pāṇini documented their
language; exact transmission already protected their received form.

### Mode

Use **mode** when the sentence concerns a way Sanskrit operates, especially when
explaining Pāṇini's *chandasi* and *bhāṣāyām* distinctions. Do not use *mode* as
a hidden chronological stage.

### Style

Use **style** for differences among the Vedas, Brāhmaṇas, Āraṇyakas,
Upaniṣads, laukika authors, kāvya, śāstra, drama, and prose. Style can vary
inside one invariant language.

### Banned substitute

Do not use **register** for any of these distinctions. Choose *domain, mode,
scope, style, form,* or the specific corpus.

## Corpus, Passage, Expression, and Recitation

- **corpus**: a body of preserved compositions;
- **passage**: a specific stretch of a preserved composition;
- **expression**: a form or sequence when neither *sentence* nor *mantra* is
  exact enough;
- **sentence**: ordinary prose with a recoverable grammatical boundary;
- **mantra**: a mantra, never a generic synonym for sentence;
- **recitation**: the voiced transmission of a preserved passage.

Avoid **utterance** when *expression, sentence, mantra, passage,* or
*recitation* can state the fact more plainly.

## Bounded and Unbounded

**Bounded** refers only to the fixed set of expressions after they enter exact
Vedic transmission. It does not establish when a mantra was seen, when it
entered its Veda, or whether mantras preserved in one Veda were seen before
those preserved in another.

**Unbounded** refers to the open number of new expressions that laukika Sanskrit
can generate for changing circumstances.

Preferred formulation:

> The mantras were seen by mantra-seers, but we do not know when each mantra was
> seen. The read-only comparison begins with the preservation duty: once
> received into exact transmission, a mantra may be recited, studied, and
> decoded, but not rewritten. The laukika domain can generate expressions that
> have never appeared before.

## Invariance

State the two forms of invariance separately:

- **Vaidika:** language invariant; transmitted content invariant.
- **Laukika:** language architecture invariant; usage and composition remain
  open; the surviving corpus is selected, accretive, and lossy.

Do not say that laukika Sanskrit changes when the intended meaning is that
speakers apply its generative architecture to a changing world.

## Generativity

Use **generative architecture**, **productive kernel**, or **generative
capacity** for the operations that produce new expressions from a stable system
of sonomers, atoms, affixes, case relations, compounds, and sentence patterns.

The phrase **productive kernel** requires an initial explanation:

> A reusable set of operations can create words and sentences that no preserved
> text has used before. That reusable center is the productive kernel.

Do not use **kernel** alone before the explanation.

## Calibration

The Veda is the **primary reference corpus** and the **calibrant**. The
analytical disciplines decode, explain, and apply the measure preserved there.
The relationship has a direction:

1. the Veda preserves the architecture in operation;
2. śikṣā, Prātiśākhya, nirukta, vyākaraṇam, chandas, teaching, and commentary
   make its operations explicit;
3. laukika speakers apply the same architecture to new expression;
4. new laukika composition does not alter the Veda.

Avoid language that turns the analytical disciplines into an authorized office.
They form a distributed explanatory and corrective apparatus.

## People and Responsibilities

The permission boundary separates responsibilities, not populations. The same
person, household, or lineage can:

- preserve a Vedic śākhā;
- study and explain the Veda;
- teach laukika Sanskrit;
- remember itihāsa-purāṇa and local narratives;
- compose new laukika material.

Their new composition cannot become a new Vedic mantra. Use **controlled
permeability** only after explaining this concrete social arrangement.

Avoid **recension**. Use:

- **śākhā**
- **received form**
- **lineage-preserved form**
- **recitational form**
- **textual witness**, when a manuscript is specifically meant

## Operators

Use **operator** for an *upasarga* when the sentence explains how it changes the
action expressed by an atom. Use **particle** only when the grammatical item is
functioning independently. Avoid translating every *upasarga* as a preposition.

The operator comparison should say:

- a fixed Vedic passage can preserve a separated operator because its complete
  sequence and inherited explanation travel together;
- open-ended laukika composition benefits from local attachment because a new
  sentence may contain several actions;
- this is a functional difference, not a claim that unattached particles
  evolved into attached prefixes.

## Sound

Distinguish:

- **independent sonomer**: a reusable coordinate expected to combine throughout
  the generative grid;
- **governed contextual articulation**: a sound produced under a stated
  condition, such as *upadhmānīya*;
- **lineage-preserved realization**: a sound preserved in a particular Vedic
  environment, such as Ṛgvedic **ळ**.

The vaidika domain can preserve every realization required by a fixed passage
without promoting each realization into the productive sonomer grid.

## Pāṇini

Pāṇini **documented, decoded, analyzed, distinguished, specified,** and
**explained**. He did not codify Sanskrit and did not create the features he
documented.

Preferred refrain:

> Sanskrit was engineered. Encoded in the Vedas. Decoded by many. Pāṇini's
> decoding is the finest.

Historical actions by Pāṇini take the past tense.

## Chronology

Difference establishes distribution before it establishes sequence. A
chronological account requires separate evidence that one form preceded and
changed into another.

Preferred formulation:

> Pāṇini marks where an operation applies. He does not turn the scope marker
> into a date.

Do not use:

- earlier layer / later layer
- archaic survival
- Vedic stage / Classical stage
- evolved into
- disappeared

unless the sentence is reporting and criticizing the pyramid's account.

## Evidence Language

Do not use **attested, unattested, canonical, formalized,** or **codified** in the
book's own account.

State the concrete fact:

- preserved in recitation;
- found in a Vedic passage;
- documented in a manuscript;
- listed in the Dhātupāṭha;
- described by a Prātiśākhya;
- absent from the surviving corpus;
- reconstructed by comparative philologists.
