# Retired Endnote: Sanskrit's 5.2-Billion Reach

**Retired:** 2026-08-22
**Former ID:** `sanskrit-field-52b-reach`
**Former deployment:** Chapter 0 §0.3

## Reason for Retirement

Chapter 0 §0.3 now establishes Sanskrit's reach through familiar words and continuing Indian use rather than through an aggregate population estimate. The 5.2-billion figure depended on the Radiance Thesis for its largest component before the book had established that thesis. It also combined overlapping populations and treated residence within a transmission zone as evidence of Sanskrit's reach. Any future numerical claim should count a clearly defined category, state its exceptions, and prevent overlap.

The subcontinental population estimate and the three transmission zones remain available below as source material. The aggregate 5.2-billion claim should not be restored without a new counting method.

## Retired Endnote

**Short:** Chapter 0's "more than 5.2 billion" estimate is an order-of-magnitude estimate of civilizational reach, not a census category: roughly two billion people in the Indian subcontinent plus more than three billion more in the Indo-European, Indo-Iranian, and Buddhist-transmission populations.

The estimate counted language communities and transmission zones rather than first-language speakers of Sanskrit or people consciously aware of Sanskrit's operation. It combined three proposed zones: the Indian subcontinent; the Indo-Iranian and wider Indo-European language sphere, which the book attributes to Sanskrit's outward radiance; and the Buddhist-transmission zone across Asia, where Sanskritic vocabulary, categories, scripts, phonetic habits, and conceptual architecture traveled through Buddhist scholastic and ritual transmission.

The "roughly two billion" subcontinental figure came from population estimates for India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, Bhutan, and Maldives. The United Nations *World Population Prospects 2024* country table gives India at about 1.44 billion, Pakistan about 249 million, Bangladesh about 172 million, Afghanistan about 42 million, Nepal about 30 million, Sri Lanka about 22 million, with Bhutan and Maldives under one million each. Taken together, the subcontinental population is just under two billion by the UN 2024 table and just over two billion by rolling estimates available when the note was written.

The "more than three billion more" component began with Ethnologue's count of more than 3.3 billion Indo-European-language speakers, then attempted to exclude the subcontinental overlap and add the Buddhist Asian transmission zone: China and the wider Sinitic sphere, Tibet, Korea, Japan, Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, Mongolia, and Southeast Asia. This procedure did not establish a non-overlapping population whose members could all be counted as reached by Sanskrit. That is the principal numerical defect for which the aggregate was retired.
