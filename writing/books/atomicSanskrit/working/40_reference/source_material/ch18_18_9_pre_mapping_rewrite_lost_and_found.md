## Former Title and Opening

> ## 18.9 Put the Star Beside the Atom
>
> We can now turn the case against PIE into a repeatable Sanskritic research method. The pyramid places each starred reconstruction above Sanskrit and treats the star as the ancestor. Reverse that procedure: place the star beside the recorded Sanskrit atom, trace every sound and semantic movement, and compare which account explains the whole word-family. The three examples that follow establish a method that Indian universities can apply across the reconstructed PIE inventory.
>
> Our approach brings four bodies of evidence together. It begins with Vedic forms and the listed Sanskrit atom that generates their family. Sanskrit's articulated grid and fivefold *vyutpatti* then show the physical and semantic movements, while Pāṇini receives credit for documenting operations already visible in the Veda. Where it is relevant to a family, Hemacandra's precise documentation of Prakrit and Apabhraṃśa shows how Sanskrit sounds and words change when they enter naturally flowing languages. The recorded Greek, Latin, Persian, Germanic, and other receiving-language forms can then be placed beside the Sanskrit family to test whether the same transformations recur across many atoms.

## Former Full Body Analysis of the Fivefold Formula

The verse and translation remain in the chapter. The full *padaccheda* and analysis remain in endnote `vyutpatti-fivefold-method`.

> ***Padaccheda:*** वर्ण-आगमः । वर्ण-विपर्ययः । च । द्वौ । च । अपरौ । वर्ण-विकार-नाशौ । धातोः । तत्-अर्थ-अतिशयेन । योगः । तत् । उच्यते । पञ्च-विधम् । निरुक्तम् ॥
>
> ***Word by word:*** **वर्ण-आगमः (*varṇa-āgamaḥ*)** — addition of a sound; **वर्ण-विपर्ययः (*varṇa-viparyayaḥ*)** — transposition of a sound; **च (*ca*, twice)** — and; **द्वौ (*dvau*)** — two; **अपरौ (*aparau*)** — two others; **वर्ण-विकार-नाशौ (*varṇa-vikāra-nāśau*)** — sound-modification and sound-loss; **धातोः (*dhātoḥ*)** — of the *dhātu*; **तत्-अर्थ-अतिशयेन (*tat-artha-atiśayena*)** — through an extension of its meaning; **योगः (*yogaḥ*)** — connection; **तत् उच्यते (*tat ucyate*)** — that is called; **पञ्च-विधम् निरुक्तम् (*pañca-vidham niruktam*)** — fivefold *nirukta*.

## Former Yoke-Family Layout

> | Sanskrit | Greek | Latin | Gothic / English |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **युञ्जन्ति (*yuñjanti*)**, **युगा (*yugā*)**, **युक्त (*yukta*)** | **ζυγόν (*zygon*)** | ***iugum*** | ***juk*** / ***yoke*** |
>
> Every column preserves the joining or yoking field. Greek renders the opening differently but keeps the velar **g**. Latin writes the initial glide with **i** and also keeps **g**. Gothic and English preserve the initial **y** sound and use **k**. Comparative philology reconstructs **\*yeug- / \*yug-** and installs that starred composite above the recorded family. Sanskrit supplies something the reconstruction cannot: a recorded atom, generated words, three Vedic sound-forms, and the documented coordinate relations among them.

The revised table rotates the languages into rows, begins with the PIE image, adds approximate Devanagari sound-renderings, and separates opening consonants, vowels, and closing consonants.

## Former Bhṛ-Family Layout

> | Sanskrit | Greek | Latin | Old English / English |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **भृ (*bhṛ*)**, **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)** | ***pherein*** | ***ferre*** | ***beran*** / ***bear*** |
> | voiced aspirated **bh**, with unaspirated **b** in the Vedic reduplicant | voiceless aspirated **ph** | voiceless labial fricative **f** | voiced unaspirated **b** |
>
> The labial place remains stable while voicing, aspiration, and closure change. Hemacandra later records similar instability of aspirated stops when Sanskrit words move through Prakrit, including changes such as *mukha → muha*, *megha → meha*, and *svabhāva → sahāva*. His rule does not derive Greek or Latin. It demonstrates how an Indic analyst records a natural transformation: identify the sound, state its environment, and show the resulting form.

The revised case preserves this distinction and explicitly marks the vowel mapping as unresolved.
