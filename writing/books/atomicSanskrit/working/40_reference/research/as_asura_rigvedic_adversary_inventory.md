# Can any Rigvedic *asura* be argued as *a-sura*? — A Focused Inventory

> **Superseded as a verdict 2026-09-02; retained as an evidence inventory.** Chapter 3 §3.6 now states that the surrounding Rigvedic words do not let us assign every occurrence to ***asu-ra*** or ***a-sura***. The inventory below remains useful because it records how the passages mark conduct and opposition. Its categorical opening verdict is research history and must not be restored to the manuscript.

**Question asked (2026-08-29):** across all Rigvedic occurrences of *asura*, is there any
that can be argued to be ***a-sura*** (the privative, the un-shining antagonist) rather than
***asu-ra*** (the breath-bearer)?

**Verdict: no — and the reason is more useful to the book than a bare negative would be.**
When the Ṛgveda wants the adversary sense, it marks it with an added privative on ***deva***,
not on ***sura***. See §3.

Companion to `as_asura_sura_asurya_vedic_survey.md`, which this closes one open item in
(its §2.1 marks the adversarial-sense locator list ❓ UNVERIFIED).

**Status legend** (same as the survey): ✅ verified against a fetched source · ⚠️ verified but
with a caveat · ❓ unverified.

## 1. Three structural obstacles to an RV-internal *a-sura*

### 1.1 *sura* does not occur in the Ṛgveda ✅

The survey's count table records **0** occurrences of standalone *sura-* against **88** of
*asura-*. A privative *a* + *sura* cannot be formed inside a corpus in which *sura* is not a
word.

**Corroborated externally:** Taraporewala, whom Hale follows, holds that *sura-* first occurs
in the **Maitrāyaṇī Upaniṣad**, and is "a late formation made after the initial *a* of
*asura-* was reinterpreted as the privative *a-*." Macdonell reaches the same conclusion by
calling *sura* an invention of folk etymology. ✅
Source: W.E. Hale, *Ásura- in Early Vedic Religion*, ch. I (previous theories)
[[archive.org](https://archive.org/details/MXPE_asura-in-early-vedic-religion-by-wash-edward-hale-motilal-banarsidass-delhi)].

**What this does and does not kill.** It kills the *noun*-based privative — *asura* as
"not-a-*sura*", i.e. not-a-deva. It does **not** touch the *root*-based reading the book
actually uses: *a-* + ⟪सुर्⟫ in the field ऐश्वर्यदीप्त्योः (*aiśvarya-dīptyoḥ*), "un-shining".
That reading survives, but it is an analysis the disciplines supply — it is not demonstrable
from Rigvedic usage, and this document found nothing that would make it so.

### 1.2 The named antagonists are *āsura*, not *asura* ✅

Ṛgveda 5.40.5, the eclipse-spine anchor, reads:

> यत्त्वा सूर्य स्वर्भानुस्तमसाविध्यद्**आसुरः** ।
> *yat tvā sūrya svarbhānus tamasāvidhyad **āsuraḥ*** |

**आसुर (*āsura*)** is the *vṛddhi* adjective built **from** *asura* (asura → āsura). It
presupposes the base noun, so it cannot be evidence for splitting that noun. The same holds
for Namuci (*āsura* at VS 19.34) and for the individuals called *āsura* in the Taittirīya
Saṃhitā (2.1.2.2, 2.5.11.1, 2.6.9.4–5), both recorded in the survey §2.3.

`as_1_03_strategic.md` already distinguishes असुर and आसुर correctly; the manuscript is not
exposed here. But Svarbhānu is not a privative witness, and should not be cited as one.

### 1.3 Even the designated adversary hymn keeps the sovereign function ✅

At **RV 10.124.3** — the survey's "pivot to the adversary function", *pitre asurāya*, "to the
father-asura" — the asura is still **वि मिमे ऋतस्य धाम**, *vi mime ṛtasya dhāma*, measuring out
the abodes of Order. The measurer survives into the hymn that turns against him.

## 2. Where the adversary sense actually sits

Per Hale's own division, the Ṛgvedic material splits into **singular *asura-*** as an epithet of
devas, and **plural *asurāḥ*** as hostile beings; von Bradke had already observed that the
divine uses are mostly singular and the pejorative ones usually plural. ✅ (Hale, ch. I)

The survey's counts fit that shape: of **88** occurrences, **71 singular · 4 dual · 10 plural ·
3 in compound**. So the adversarial candidates are concentrated in the **10 plural** occurrences
plus the lineage verses.

| locator | form | sense | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RV 8.96.9** | *asurā adevāḥ*, nom. pl. | **adversary** — the clearest case in the corpus | ✅ verbatim, §3 |
| RV 10.124.3 | *asurāya*, dat. sg. | adversary by context; still the measurer (§1.3) | ✅ survey §3.7 |
| RV 3.53.7 · 3.56.8 · 10.10.2 · 10.67.2 | "asura's sons" / lineage | adversary by context | ❓ not re-verified here |
| the remaining plural occurrences | — | — | ❓ **the open gap; see §5** |

## 3. The decisive pair — and why it answers the question

The Ṛgveda uses the *same* stem for both poles, and disambiguates by an **added qualifier**:

**Sovereign pole — RV 8.25.4** ✅
> महान्ता मित्रावरुणा सम्राजा **देवाव् असुरा** ।
> *mahāntā mitrāvaruṇā samrājā **devāv asurā*** |
> "Great Mitra and Varuṇa, the two sovereigns, **devas who are asuras**…"
[[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc836100.html)]

**Adversary pole — RV 8.96.9** ✅
> अनायुधासो **असुरा अदेवा**श्चक्रेण ताँ अप वप ऋजीषिन् ॥
> *anāyudhāso **asurā adevāś** cakreṇa tām̐ apa vapa ṛjīṣin* ||
> "The **asuras, non-devas**, weaponless — sweep them away with your wheel, O Ṛjīṣin."
[[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc837166.html)]

**The finding.** To say "the hostile ones", the Ṛgveda does not rely on *asura* alone. It adds
**अदेव (*adeva*)** — a privative **on *deva***. Had *asura* itself carried a privative, *adevāḥ*
would be redundant, and 8.25.4's *devāv asurā* ("devas who are asuras") would be a
contradiction rather than a formula.

So the corpus does exactly what the book already argues: **one sound-form, two words, and the
action described in the mantra identifies which one is meant.** What it does *not* do is
perform the split *a* + *sura*. The negation the Ṛgveda reaches for is on *deva*.

---

## 4. Consequence for the argument

The *a-sura* split is not weakened by this — it is relocated, and its witness is stronger than
a contested Rigvedic reading would have been:

- **The morphological split is the Kauthuma Sāmaveda Padapāṭha's**, which segments *asurasya*
  as *a + surasya* — a recitation discipline actually dividing the word (survey §5.5).
- **The shining field is the Dhātupāṭha's** ⟪सुर्⟫, ऐश्वर्यदीप्त्योः.
- **The Ṛgveda supplies the two functions**, not the segmentation.

This also matches the survey's own §7.2 recommendation, reached independently: reframe
*asuratva* from "not-light" to **measuring sovereignty turned to withholding** — one power in
two orientations. RV 8.96.9 and 8.25.4 are that recommendation's cleanest textual pair.

**Practical guidance:** do not argue an RV-internal *a-sura*. Argue the two functions from the
RV, and the segmentation from the Padapāṭha and the Dhātupāṭha.

## 5. What remains open ❓

- **The full plural inventory.** 10 plural occurrences are counted; **1** (8.96.9) is pinned
  here with verbatim text. The other nine are unlocated in this pass.
- **Why they are unlocated:** Hale catalogs every occurrence by deity and tabulates the
  adversarial ones, but archive.org's OCR view exposes only a window of the scan — three
  separate fetches returned Chapter I (previous theories) and never reached the analysis
  chapters II–IX. The book is the right instrument; automated retrieval is not.
- **Two instruments that would close it:** Hale's chapters II–IX by eye, or **Grassmann's
  *Wörterbuch* s.v. *ásura***, which lists every occurrence by case
  [[Cologne](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/GRAScan/2020/web/index.php)] — the
  case listing is what isolates the plurals directly.
- **The lineage verses** (3.53.7, 3.56.8, 10.10.2, 10.67.2) are carried from the survey and were
  not re-verified in this pass.

**Would finding the other nine change the verdict?** Only if one of them segmented the word or
opposed *asura* to *sura* rather than to *deva*. Given that *sura* does not occur in the corpus
at all (§1.1), that is close to excluded on structural grounds — so the expected result of
closing this gap is more instances of the 8.96.9 pattern, not a counter-example.
