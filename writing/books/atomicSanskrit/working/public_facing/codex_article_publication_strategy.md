# Codex Notes — When to Publish the Public Articles

## Recommendation

Publish some articles before the book. Do not publish all of them before the book.

The *Tatya Tope* example is the model. A good article before submission can create the public fact that makes a publisher pay attention. It gives the manuscript social proof before the editor reads page one.

The standard publicity rhythm is also pre-publication: build awareness before launch, then keep it alive after publication. The working rule for *Atomic Sanskrit* should be:

> one strong article before publisher submission; a few more during the pre-launch window; the rest after publication.

## Before Publisher Submission

Publish **one strong article** in a serious Indian outlet.

The goal is not to release the whole argument. The goal is to create a clip that says: this book has a public argument.

Best candidate:

> **Pāṇini Did Not Codify Sanskrit. He Decoded It.**

Why this one works:

- It is sharp.
- It is intelligible.
- It is not niche.
- It goes directly to the book's central correction.
- It signals that the book is not just Sanskrit appreciation, not just devotional writing, and not just anti-PIE polemic.
- It establishes the book as a category argument: codification vs decoding, authority vs architecture.

## Outlet Targeting

Target outlets in tiers, not one at a time.

### Best First Target

Try **Swarajya** first for the Pāṇini article.

Why:

- It is serious enough to matter.
- It is culturally aligned enough to understand the argument.
- It has a clear submission path.
- It can carry a civilizational argument without requiring the article to be stripped of its force.

Recommended first piece:

> **Pāṇini Did Not Codify Sanskrit. He Decoded It.**

This article is civilizational, but not too explosive. It can pass as intellectual correction, not only polemic.

Swarajya's own submission page accepts commentary, longform, and book-related pieces; commentary is typically 750-2,000 words. Submission email: `editor@swarajyamag.com`.

Source: <https://swarajyamag.com/write-for-us>

### Tier 1 — Publisher-Attention Outlets

These matter because a publisher will notice the clip.

- **Swarajya** — best fit for Pāṇini, sonomer, dhātu, and ārya.
- **Firstpost** — good for punchier public arguments; has a visible Opinion section. Source: <https://www.firstpost.com/opinion/>
- **Hindustan Times Opinion** — broader mainstream credibility; harder to place, but useful if the polemic is softened. HT lists editorial contact at `editorial@hindustantimes.com`. Source: <https://www.hindustantimes.com/contact-us>
- **Indian Express Opinion** — high prestige, but likely only for a more neutral version: "Pāṇini as decoder, not codifier" or "Why Indian scripts are more than phonetic." Source: <https://indianexpress.com/article/opinion/>

### Tier 2 — Civilizational / Indic Audience

These are useful for building the reader base.

- **Organiser** — good for the retroflex, ārya, Sanskrit continuity, and civilizational pieces. Source: <https://organiser.org/submit-your-article/>
- **Standpoint Bharat** — good for thoughtful 1,000-1,500 word culture / politics pieces. Source: <https://www.standpointbharat.com/submission-guidelines>
- **Pragyata / Indic Today / IndiaFacts-style platforms** — useful for deeper civilizational essays, though less publisher-prestige than mainstream outlets.

### Tier 3 — Policy / Intellectual Class

Use these only for specific angles.

- **Bharat Shakti** — not for Sanskrit itself, but useful if the article connects language, civilizational security, education policy, or knowledge sovereignty. Their guideline allows op-eds up to 1,000 words and longer pieces up to 1,500 words; exclusivity matters. Source: <https://bharatshakti.in/op-ed-contributor-guidelines/>
- **Ideas for India** — more evidence-policy oriented, less polemical. Use only for a tamer education / language-policy version. Source: <https://www.ideasforindia.in/publication-policy>

### Recommended Outlet Sequence

1. **Swarajya:** "Pāṇini Did Not Codify Sanskrit. He Decoded It."
2. **Firstpost:** "The Indian Accent That Breaks the Aryan Migration Story."
3. **Hindustan Times or Indian Express:** "Before the Letter: India's Discovery of the Sonomer."
4. **Organiser:** "What the World Got Wrong About Ārya."
5. **Swarajya / longform:** "Don't Call Them Roots: Why Dhātus Are Atoms."

### Submission Discipline

Do not send the same article to multiple places at once if the outlet asks for exclusivity.

Pitch one article to one outlet. Wait a reasonable window. Then move on or rewrite the article for another outlet.

For publisher attention, the strongest first clip is not the most aggressive piece. It is the most credible surprising piece. That is the Pāṇini article.

## While Pitching Publishers

Send the manuscript with:

- the article link
- a one-page book summary
- a short public-argument summary
- the article skeleton list as evidence that the book can generate media

This repeats the successful pattern from the earlier book, but deliberately. Instead of waiting for a journalist to stumble into the story, create the public entry point first.

## After Publisher Interest / Contract

Publish two or three more pieces in the pre-launch window:

1. **Before the Letter: India's Discovery of the Sonomer**
2. **The Indian Accent That Breaks the Aryan Migration Story**
3. **Don't Call Them Roots: Why Dhātus Are Atoms**

These give different entry points into the book:

- script
- speech
- grammar

Together they show that the book's argument is not a single claim repeated in different language. It has multiple public doors.

## Launch Window

In the final weeks before release, publish the broadest essay.

Best candidates:

- **The Seven Claims of Atomic Sanskrit**
- **Sanskrit Was Engineered, Not Codified**

This piece should name the book directly and make readers want the full argument.

## After Publication

Use the remaining articles as sustaining fire:

- **PIE Is Not Evidence**
- **The Vedas Are Not Merely Scripture**
- **Vedic and Classical Sanskrit Are Not Two Languages**
- **What the World Got Wrong About Ārya**
- **Sanskrit Has Not Stopped Working**

These can keep the book alive in public conversation after launch.

## Caution

Do not publish too much of the book's actual prose before a publisher sees it.

Publish arguments, not chapters.

Standalone op-eds are useful. Long excerpts should wait until a publisher is involved.

## Practical Sequence

1. Draft the Pāṇini article first.
2. Place it in a serious Indian outlet.
3. Use the clip in the publisher submission package.
4. Hold the sonomer, retroflex, and dhātu essays for the pre-launch window.
5. Save the PIE, Vedas, Vedic/Classical, and ārya essays for launch and post-launch momentum.

## Bottom Line

The first public article should function as the publisher-attention piece.

The best first piece is:

> **Pāṇini Did Not Codify Sanskrit. He Decoded It.**

It is the cleanest public doorway into *Atomic Sanskrit*.
