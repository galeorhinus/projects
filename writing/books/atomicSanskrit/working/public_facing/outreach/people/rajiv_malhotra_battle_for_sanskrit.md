## What the Book Does

The book is a sustained prosecution of the **Pollockian school** of Sanskrit studies — the academic apparatus organized around **Sheldon Pollock** (Columbia) and his ***The Language of the Gods in the World of Men*** (2006) — and a call for an alternative Indian-led scholarly position Malhotra calls **swadeshi indology**.

The immediate trigger for the book was the **Murty Classical Library of India** funding controversy: a $5.2 million endowment from Rohan Murty (Infosys co-founder N. R. Narayana Murthy's son) to Harvard, with Pollock as general editor. Malhotra's prosecution: Indian wealth was funding an institutional position that operates on a hostile frame toward the *paramparā* the donors emerge from. The book makes that case across its full arc.

## The Battle Frame

The book organizes around an ***insider*** vs. ***outsider*** structural division — who has standing to study, define, and represent Sanskrit and Sanskriti.

- **Insiders:** Indian scholars rooted in the *paramparā*; dharmic civilizational scholars; ***swadeshi indology*** as the alternative institutional position.
- **Outsiders:** the ***American Orientalists*** / ***Pollockian school*** — Western academic apparatus reading Sanskrit through a power / oppression / political-construction lens.

The "battle" is not only intellectual. Malhotra treats it as a multi-level engagement (see *Whether He Includes the Ecosystem* below).

## Method — *Pūrvapakṣa* / *Pratipakṣa*

The book's methodological commitment is the classical Hindu debate method:

- ***पूर्वपक्ष (pūrvapakṣa)*** — first, master and steelman the opponent's position. Do not strawman.
- ***प्रतिपक्ष (pratipakṣa)*** — then articulate your own counter-position, having earned the right to it by genuine engagement.

Malhotra commits substantial space to ***pūrvapakṣa*** on Pollock: restating Pollock's positions seriously, in his own vocabulary, before mounting the prosecution. The method is explicit in the book and is one of its most distinctive features.

## The Three Spine Questions

The subtitle — ***Is Sanskrit Political or Sacred? Oppressive or Liberating? Dead or Alive?*** — gives the three structuring oppositions through which the prosecution runs.

| Pollock's answer (as Malhotra reads it) | Malhotra's answer |
|---|---|
| Political | Sacred |
| Oppressive | Liberating |
| Dead | Alive |

The book threads these three contrasts through every prosecutorial chapter.

## Whether He Includes the Bigger Ecosystem

**Yes, extensively.** This is one of the book's strongest features. Malhotra distinguishes three battles operating at different institutional levels:

| Level | What's at stake | Specific touchpoints I'm confident he develops |
|---|---|---|
| **Intellectual** | The frame through which Sanskrit is read | Pollock's argument that Sanskrit was a *varṇa*-system power-instrument vs. the *paramparā*'s self-understanding; the *liberation-theology reading* charge. |
| **Institutional** | Funding, credentialing, journals, faculty hiring | The Murty Library controversy as the immediate case; the broader pattern of Indian wealth flowing to Western Sanskrit programs that operate on the Pollock frame; the doctoral pipeline that makes Pollock-school PhDs the default expertise the academy recognizes. |
| **Public** | How the educated public understands Sanskrit | Media coverage, popular books, prestige-press treatment of Indic topics. |

He distinguishes the three levels deliberately and develops each. The institutional analysis is not adjacent to the intellectual prosecution — it is treated as part of the same engagement.

## Key Terms Deployed

| Term | Function in the book |
|---|---|
| ***Pūrvapakṣa*** / ***pratipakṣa*** | Methodological commitment (see *Method* above). |
| ***Swadeshi indology*** | The alternative institutional position the book proposes. |
| ***American Orientalism*** / ***Pollockian school*** | The dominant frame being prosecuted. |
| ***Liberation-theology reading*** | What Malhotra accuses Pollock of: treating Sanskrit as something that needs to be liberated from its oppressive past, modeled on Latin American liberation theology applied to Hindu texts. |
| ***Insider / outsider*** | The structural division between *paramparā*-internal standing and Western-academic external standing. |
| ***Digestion*** | Pattern by which Western academia absorbs Indic concepts on alien terms. Developed more fully in *Being Different*; referenced here. |

## How the Book Relates to *Atomic Sanskrit*'s *Battle Positions* Appendix

Malhotra's prosecution and *Atomic Sanskrit*'s run on complementary axes:

- **Malhotra:** *authority and representation*. Who has standing. Whose frame is legitimate. Who controls the institutional credentialing system.
- ***Atomic Sanskrit*:** *architecture and engineering*. What Sanskrit *is*. What Pāṇini *did*. What the *paramparā* preserves.

In the appendix's three-position taxonomy, *The Battle for Sanskrit* is the **standing Indic-side prosecution of Position 3** (Pollock / *Sanskrit cosmopolis* / sociopolitical construction). The appendix should:

- Credit Malhotra explicitly as the figure who has done the institutional-axis prosecution; *Atomic Sanskrit* does not duplicate it.
- Position *Atomic Sanskrit* as the architectural-axis prosecution — covering what Malhotra's prosecution did not need to cover (the engineering thesis at the *varṇa* / *dhātu* / *kriyā* / calibration-matrix level).
- Cross-reference at the appendix's Position 3 entry: *"For the institutional prosecution of Pollock's frame, see Malhotra, ***The Battle for Sanskrit*** (2016)."*
