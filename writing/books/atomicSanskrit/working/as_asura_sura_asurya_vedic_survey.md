# *Asura* / *Sura* / *Asurya* in the Vedas — A Reference Survey

> **Working research document.** Compiles the Vedic attestations of the *asura*-word-family, their padaccheda + translation, and the treatment of the word in the analytical disciplines (Nirukta, Brāhmaṇa etymologies, Uṇādi/Pāṇini, Prātiśākhyas). Feeds Ch 3 §3.6 (the *svar / suraḥ / asuraḥ* morphology; the asuric operating-mode diagnosis) and Ch 18 §18.7 (the *asura* / PIE case). Endnotes: `asura-standard-etymology-contested`, `deva-pie-etymology`.

## 0. Method, sources, and status legend

**What this compiles.** (1) Every locatable Vedic occurrence of *asura-*, *asurya-/asuryam*, and *sura-*; (2) padaccheda (pada-pāṭha) + translation for the key verses; (3) whether Yāska (*Nirukta*), the Brāhmaṇas, the Uṇādi-sūtras / Pāṇini, and the Prātiśākhyas analyze the word.

**The book's frame — read before using this document.** The Veda has **no chronological strata**. The maṇḍalas are differentiated by *function*, not by date. Where *asura* varies in sense across the corpus, that is a *functional* variation (sovereign-epithet function vs. adversarial function), **never** an "early → late" evolution. Any "early-Rigvedic / post-Rigvedic" language in a cited secondary source is the source's framing, reported as such and not adopted.

## 1. The word-family

- **असु (*asu*)** — life-breath, the vital force; the animating principle. (The base most traditional derivations attach *asura* to: *asu* + *-ra*.)
- **असुर (*asura*)** — the word under study. Functional value established in §6.
- **असुर्य / असुर्यम् (*asurya / asuryam*)** — the abstract: "lordship, sovereign might, the quality/power of an *asura*."
- **सुर (*sura*)** — "deva/light"; **the standalone form does not occur in the Ṛgveda** (see §2.5). Its emergence is treated as the artifact of parsing *asura* as *a-sura*.

**Two morphological accounts (kept distinct):**
1. **The book's architecture (Ch 3 §3.6):** स्वर् *svar* (self-luminous heaven / "to shine") → सुरः *suraḥ* (engineered *śabda* "light") → असुरः *asuraḥ* (privative *a-* + *suraḥ* = "not-light"). Structural, not attestation-ordered.
2. **The traditional/lexical derivation:** *asu* ("breath") + suffix *-ra* → *asura* = "one possessing/animated by *asu*, the life-lord." (Uṇādi / Nirukta lineage — see §4.)

These are not rivals to be adjudicated by date; §6 shows how both land on the same functional value.

**The adversarial function.** *Asura* also carries the sense of the *opponents* of the *devāḥ*. This function is concentrated in a small set of hymns — above all **RV 10.124**, the "departing *asura*" hymn (Agni / Soma / Varuṇa crossing over from the *asura*'s side to Indra), plus the lineage verses above — while the family-book hymns deploy *asura* almost entirely in the sovereign function. The secondary sources describe this distribution *chronologically* ("later strata," "Maṇḍala 10"); **this document reads it functionally** — different hymns doing different work — per the frame in §0. ❓ A full list of adversarial-sense locators is UNVERIFIED here; Hale tabulates them.

### 2.2 Atharvaveda (Śaunaka = AV; Paippalāda = AVP)

**The functional split is grammatical number, not date.** In the AV the **singular** *asura* keeps the **sovereign function**; the **plural** carries the **adversary function**. This is a number-keyed functional axis — precisely the reading in §0/§6, visible on the page rather than inferred from a timeline.

**Singular — sovereign function (8 verses, mostly positive):** ✅
- AV 5.27.1 — **Agni** as *asura*
- AV 1.10.1 — **Varuṇa**, an *asura* who "rules the devas"
- AV 4.15.11–12 — **Prajāpati** as *asura*, "our father" (first Prajāpati use)
- AV 5.11.1 — **Varuṇa**, "the great *asura*" (Varuṇa–Atharvan dialogue)
- AV 19.56.1 — the "place of the *asura*" (likely Yama)
- AV 6.72.1 — an *asura*'s *māyā* (virility charm)
- *Adversarial-sense singular* (the minority): AV 8.6.5 ("the *asura* who is black, hairy, tufted…" — a hostile being); AV 13.7.13–14.

**Plural — adversary function (dominant):** AV 2.27.3–4, 6.7.2–3, 6.65.3, 6.86.3, 6.100.3, 8.5.3, 8.9.24, 8.13.1–4, 9.2.17–18, 10.3.2, 10.3.11, 10.6.10, 10.10.26, 11.7.7, 11.12.15, 12.1.5, 19.13.1, 19.56.3, 19.66.1, 4.19.4, 4.23.5, 2.3.3, 6.108.3, 6.109.3 — the *asuras* as enemies opposed by Indra or the *devāḥ*, frequently paralleling the *dāsa/dasyu* enemy-classes. ✅ (Hale + Bloomfield)

**Derivatives / compounds:** *āsurá-* 1× (AV 3.22.4, "asuric elephant," positive); *āsurī́-* fem. 4× (AV 1.24.1–2, 7.39.2, 8.5.9); *asuramáya-* 1× (AV 3.9.4); *asurakṣíti-* "asura-destroying" (AV 10.6.22ff); *asurakṣáyaṇa-* "asura-destroying weapon" 3× (AV 11.12.10/12/13). *asurya-* in the AV appears mainly in the RV-borrowed Book 20.

**Paippalāda (AVP):** partial (corrupt MSS). Of ~9 readable, only AVP 10.3.6 is positive ("*asura* of Indras"); the rest are enemy-sense (AVP 19.31.8, 9.25.14, 20.20.3, 17.28.3). ⚠️

### 2.3 Yajurveda (Kṛṣṇa: TS, MS, KS · Śukla: VS/VSK)

**Mantra portions — sovereign-function singular:** ✅
- **VS 27.12** (= TS 4.1.8.1 = MS 2.12.6 = KS 18.17; cf. AV 5.27.1) — Tanūnapāt/**Agni**, *asuro viśvavedāḥ*. **Note:** this is the *same sovereign-function formula* as RV 8.42.1's Varuṇa (*asuro viśvavedāḥ*) — the ordering-sovereign epithet is stable across the corpus, re-anchored on Agni.
- **MS 4.14.14** — Āditya, "*asura* of the gods"
- **VS 13.44** (= TS 4.2.10.3 = MS 2.7.17 = KS 16.17) — "the *māyā* of the *asura*" (Agni hymn)
- **VS 8.55** — "the *asura* when bargained for" (Soma ritual)

**Mantra portions — adversary-function plural:** MS 2.9.1 ("first-born of gods, seers, and *asuras*"); MS 4.14.11 ("on whom the gods overcame the *asuras*"); **TS 4.3.11.3** (Indra "slayer of *asuras*" — *notable:* the AV variant AV 3.10.12 reads *dasyu-* where TS reads *asura-*, showing the two opponent-words were near-synonyms); VS 2.30; **VS 2.29** — the famous ritual formula *ápahatā ásurā rakṣáṃsi vediṣádaḥ* ("the asuras are slain, the rakṣases who sit by the altar" — here *asura* ≈ *rakṣas*). ✅

**Prose portions (Black YV brāhmaṇa layer):** the gods-vs-*asuras* battle frame dominates; *asura* does not occur in the singular there; an individual is called an *āsura* (TS 2.1.2.2, 2.5.11.1, 2.6.9.4–5); Prajāpati creates gods and *asuras* (TS 3.3.7.1). This is the *narrative* deployment of the adversary function.

**Abstract / derivative:** *asuryàm* VS 8.24 (= TS 1.4.45.1 = MS 1.3.39 = KS 4.13) "protecting *asura*-ness" (positive); *asuryà-* adj. VS 40.3 (= ŚB 14.7.2.14 = BṛhU 4.4.14, the Īśā-Up. section) "worlds called *asuric*, wrapped in blind darkness"; *āsurá-* of **Namuci** 3× (VS 19.34, 20.67, 20.68); *āsurī́-* of the earth's *māyā* (VS 11.69, positive). ✅

### 2.4 Sāmaveda

Essentially **no independent *asura* material** — the SV's *asura* verses are Ṛgvedic, redeployed for chant. Hale: only **one** SV-specific verse (SV 2.1219 = AV 19.13.1, *asuras* as conquered enemies of Indra); all others are RV-borrowings (e.g., SV 2.804 = RV 10.170.2 *asurahā*; SV 2.762 = RV 8.90.6; SV 1.78 = RV 7.6.1). That the chant-Veda contributes nothing new is itself the datum. ✅

### 2.5 Standalone *sura-* (सुर, "deva/god") — attestation

**Absent from all four Saṃhitās (RV, AV, YV, SV).** The word *sura* meaning "god" does not occur anywhere in the Saṃhitā layer. ✅
- **Monier-Williams** cites *sura* सुर "god" only from the **Maitrī / Maitrāyaṇīya Upaniṣad** and the **Mahābhārata** — no Vedic-Saṃhitā citation — and derives it as a **back-formation**: "*probably from asura as if from a-sura, and as sita from a-sita*." *Sura* is the fictitious positive retro-created by re-analyzing *a-sura* as "not-*sura*." [[MW s.v. sura](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/sura)]
- **Disambiguation (critical):** the RV/AV "sura" hits attach to *different words* — **सूर *sūra*** (long ū, "sun/shining one," RV+AV), **शूर *śūra*** ("hero," RV), **सुरा *surā*** ("liquor," RV; MW: from √su "distil," **not** connected to *sura* "god"). The *surā*/*sura* ("gods drank *surā*") link is later folk etymology (Rāmāyaṇa: *surāpratigrahād devāḥ surā ity abhiviśrutāḥ*).
- **Earliest secure *sura* "god":** Upaniṣadic (Maitrī) + epic. ✅

## 3. Padaccheda + translation — key verses

*(Curated set spanning the functional range. Full padapāṭha for every occurrence is available per-verse on VedaWeb; this section works the load-bearing verses in full.)*

### 3.1 RV 8.42.1 — Varuṇa (the sovereign / ordering function) ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> अस्तभ्नाद् द्यामसुरो विश्ववेदा अमिमीत वरिमाणं पृथिव्याः ।
> आसीदद्विश्वा भुवनानि सम्राड् विश्वेत्तानि वरुणस्य व्रतानि ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> अस्तभ्नात् । द्याम् । असुरः । विश्वऽवेदाः । अमिमीत । वरिमाणम् । पृथिव्याः ।
> आ । असीदत् । विश्वा । भुवनानि । सम्राट् । विश्वा । इत् । तानि । वरुणस्य । व्रतानि ॥

**Translation:** "The *asura*, all-knowing, propped up the heaven; he measured out the breadth of the earth. As *samrāṭ* (universal sovereign) he took his seat over all beings. All these are the *vratāni* (ordinances) of Varuṇa."

**Word gloss:** अस्तभ्नात् *astabhnāt* propped up, pillared apart (√stambh, impf.) · द्याम् *dyām* heaven (acc.) · असुरः *asuraḥ* the asura · विश्वऽवेदाः *viśva-vedāḥ* all-knowing (compound) · अमिमीत *amimīta* measured out (√mā, impf. mid.) · वरिमाणम् *varimāṇam* breadth (acc.) · पृथिव्याः *pṛthivyāḥ* of the earth (gen.) · आ *ā* preverb (with *asīdat*) · असीदत् *asīdat* took his seat (√sad, impf.) · विश्वा *viśvā* all (n. pl.) · भुवनानि *bhuvanāni* beings/worlds · सम्राट् *samrāṭ* universal sovereign · इत् *it* indeed (emph.) · तानि *tāni* those · वरुणस्य *varuṇasya* of Varuṇa · व्रतानि *vratāni* ordinances.

**Function/role of *asura* here:** the one who *props* (astabhnāt), *measures* (amimīta), and *legislates* (vratāni) — sovereignty exercised as cosmic maintenance. See §6.

### 3.2 RV 2.1.6 — Agni as Rudra (the commanding-might function) ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> त्वमग्ने रुद्रो असुरो महो दिवस्त्वं शर्धो मारुतं पृक्ष ईशिषे ।
> त्वं वातैररुणैर्यासि शंगयस्त्वं पूषा विधतः पासि नु त्मना ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> त्वम् । अग्ने । रुद्रः । असुरः । महः । दिवः । त्वम् । शर्धः । मारुतम् । पृक्षः । ईशिषे ।
> त्वम् । वातैः । अरुणैः । यासि । शम्ऽगयः । त्वम् । पूषा । विधतः । पासि । नु । त्मना ॥

**Translation:** "You, Agni, are Rudra, the *asura* of the great heaven; you rule (*īśiṣe*) the Marut host and the nourishing power. You travel with the ruddy winds, blessing the household; as Pūṣan you protect the worshipper by your own self (*tmánā*)."

**Word gloss:** त्वम् *tvam* you · अग्ने *agne* O Agni (voc.) · रुद्रः *rudraḥ* Rudra · असुरः *asuraḥ* the asura · महः दिवः *mahaḥ divaḥ* of the great heaven (gen.+gen.) · शर्धः मारुतम् *śardhaḥ mārutam* the Marut host · पृक्षः *pṛkṣaḥ* nourishment/vigor · ईशिषे *īśiṣe* you rule over (√īś, 2sg mid.) · वातैः अरुणैः *vātaiḥ aruṇaiḥ* with the ruddy winds (instr.) · यासि *yāsi* you travel (√yā) · शम्ऽगयः *śaṃ-gayaḥ* blessing the household (*śam* weal + *gaya* home) · पूषा *pūṣā* Pūṣan · विधतः *vidhataḥ* the worshipper(s) · पासि *pāsi* you protect (√pā) · नु *nu* now/indeed · त्मना *tmanā* by your own self (instr.).

**Note:** the wisdomlib gloss on this verse reads *asura* here in "the older sense — 'Sire'/'Powerful' — not 'A'Sura', the 'Anti-Shining' Demon," articulating the *suraḥ / a-suraḥ* split from within the reception tradition.

### 3.3 RV 1.24.14 — Varuṇa (vocative; the sovereign as sin-releaser) ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> अव ते हेळो वरुण नमोभिरव यज्ञेभिरीमहे हविर्भिः ।
> क्षयन्नस्मभ्यमसुर प्रचेता राजन्नेनांसि शिश्रथः कृतानि ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> अव । ते । हेळः । वरुण । नमोभिः । अव । यज्ञेभिः । ईमहे । हविर्भिः ।
> क्षयन् । अस्मभ्यम् । असुर । प्रऽचेतः । राजन् । एनांसि । शिश्रथः । कृतानि ॥

**Translation (Wilson, rendering Sāyaṇa):** "O Varuṇa, we soften your wrath with obeisances (*namobhiḥ*), with sacrifices (*yajñebhiḥ*), with oblations (*havirbhiḥ*). Ruling over us, O wise *asura* (*asura pracetaḥ*), O king (*rājan*), loosen from us the sins we have committed."

**Form / role:** *asura* = vocative sg., epithet of **Varuṇa** — the sovereign addressed as the one with authority to *release* (*śiśrathaḥ*) the bonds of sin: sovereignty as the power to bind and to loose. [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc829155.html)]

### 3.4 RV 1.174.1 — Indra (vocative; "the gods are subject to you") ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> त्वं राजेन्द्र ये च देवा रक्षा नॄन्पाह्यसुर त्वमस्मान् ।
> त्वं सत्पतिर्मघवा नस्तरुत्रस्त्वं सत्यो वसवानः सहोदाः ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> त्वम् । राजा । इन्द्र । ये । च । देवाः । रक्ष । नॄन् । पाहि । असुर । त्वम् । अस्मान् ।
> त्वम् । सत्ऽपतिः । मघऽवा । नः । तरुत्रः । त्वम् । सत्यः । वसवानः । सहःऽदाः ॥

**Translation (Wilson):** "You, Indra, are king; those who are gods are (subject) to you; O *asura*, protect and cherish us men; you are the lord of the good (*sat-patiḥ*), the bountiful (*maghavā*), our deliverer (*tarutraḥ*); you are true (*satyaḥ*), the giver of strength (*sahaḥ-dāḥ*)."

**Form / role:** *asura* = vocative sg., of **Indra** — the sovereign to whom "the gods themselves are subject." (Padapāṭha confirmed verbatim against wisdomlib and, for the first line, the Ṛgveda-Prātiśākhya example at §5.1.) [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc830892.html)]

### 3.5 RV 7.65.2 — Mitra-Varuṇa (nominative **dual**; the asuras "among the gods") ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> ता हि देवानामसुरा तावर्या ता नः क्षितीः करतमूर्जयन्तीः ।
> अश्याम मित्रावरुणा वयं वां द्यावा च यत्र पीपयन्नहा च ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> ता । हि । देवानाम् । असुरा । तौ । अर्या । ता । नः । क्षितीः । करतम् । ऊर्जयन्तीः ।
> अश्याम । मित्रावरुणा । वयम् । वाम् । द्यावा । च । यत्र । पीपयन् । अहा । च ॥

**Translation (Wilson):** "They two indeed are the *asuras* among the gods (*devānām asurā*); they two are the noble rulers (*aryā*); may they make our dwellings (*kṣitīḥ*) full of vigour (*ūrjayantīḥ*). Mitra and Varuṇa, may we win you, wherever heaven (*dyāvā*) and the days (*ahā*) nourish us."

**Form / role:** *asurā* = nominative **dual**, of **Mitra-Varuṇa** — *devānām asurā*, "the *asuras* among the gods": the sovereign function ranking them at the head of the *devāḥ*, in the dual. [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc835124.html)]

### 3.6 RV 4.42.2 — Varuṇa's self-declaration (*asuryā́ṇi*; the abstract) ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> अहं राजा वरुणो मह्यं तान्यसुर्याणि प्रथमा धारयन्त ।
> क्रतुं सचन्ते वरुणस्य देवा राजामि कृष्टेरुपमस्य वव्रेः ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> अहम् । राजा । वरुणः । मह्यम् । तानि । असुर्याणि । प्रथमा । धारयन्त ।
> क्रतुम् । सचन्ते । वरुणस्य । देवाः । राजामि । कृष्टेः । उपमस्य । वव्रेः ॥

**Translation:** "I am king Varuṇa; to me [the gods] first assigned (*dhārayanta*) those *asuryā́ṇi* (lordly / asura-powers); the gods attend the will (*kratum*) of Varuṇa; I rule (*rājāmi*) over the highest realm of the peoples (*kṛṣṭeḥ upamasya vavreḥ*)." *(Wilson/Sāyaṇa color *asuryā́ṇi* as "powers destructive of the asuras" — a Sāyaṇa gloss; the plain sense is the lordly powers Varuṇa holds.)*

**Form / role:** *asuryā́ṇi* = nom.-acc. pl. neuter of *asurya-*, the **abstract "lordships"**, in Varuṇa's first-person sovereignty declaration — the canonical *asurya* verse. [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc832717.html)]

### 3.7 RV 10.124.3 — the "departing *asura*" (dat. sg.; the pivot to the adversary function) ✅

**Saṃhitā-pāṭha:**
> पश्यन्नन्यस्या अतिथिं वयाया ऋतस्य धाम वि मिमे पुरूणि ।
> शंसामि पित्रे असुराय शेवमयज्ञियाद्यज्ञियं भागमेमि ॥

**Padaccheda (pada-pāṭha):**
> पश्यन् । अन्यस्याः । अतिथिम् । वयायाः । ऋतस्य । धाम । वि । मिमे । पुरूणि ।
> शंसामि । पित्रे । असुराय । शेवम् । अयज्ञियात् । यज्ञियम् । भागम् । एमि ॥

**Translation (Wilson):** "Beholding the guest of another lineage (*anyasyāḥ vayāyāḥ atithim*), I have measured out (*vi mime*) the manifold abodes (*dhāma purūṇi*) of Order (*ṛtasya*); I speak (*śaṃsāmi*) good fortune (*śevam*) to the father-*asura* (*pitre asurāya*); I pass (*emi*) from the non-sacrificial (*ayajñiyāt*) to the sacrificial (*yajñiyam*) portion."

**Form / role:** *asurā́ya* = dative sg. (*pitre asurāya*, "to the father *asura*") — the Agni/Soma hymn where the speaker crosses from the *asura*'s side to the *yajña*; the pivot where the adversary function crystallizes. **Note the persistence of the measuring motif:** even here the speaker *vi mime* — "measures out" — *ṛtasya dhāma*, the abodes of Order. The asura-as-measurer (see §6) survives into the very hymn that turns against him. [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc840034.html)]

### 3.8 Cross-corpus and frame-relevant lines (noted)
- **VS 27.12** = TS 4.1.8.1 = MS 2.12.6 = KS 18.17 — *asuro viśvavedāḥ* re-anchored on **Agni/Tanūnapāt**: the *same sovereign-epithet formula* as RV 8.42.1 (Varuṇa), stable across the corpus (§2.3).
- **RV 5.63.3** — *asurasya māyáyā*, "by the *māyā* (measuring/forming power) of the *asura*": the asura's signature power named directly (§6).
- **RV 3.55** (all 22 vv.) — refrain *mahád devā́nām asuratvám ékam*, "Great and single is the *asura*-hood (*asuratva*) of the gods": the collective abstract.

## 4. The analytical disciplines — Nirukta, Brāhmaṇa etymologies, Uṇādi / Pāṇini, Sāyaṇa

**Two indigenous morphological parses of *asura* stand side by side** — and both are frame-relevant:
- **Etymologists' parse: *asu* + *-ra*** ("possessing *asu*, life-breath / life-force" → the *life-lord*) — Yāska, the Uṇādi-sūtras, the Brāhmaṇa *asu*-cosmogonies, Sāyaṇa, Monier-Williams.
- **Recitation-discipline parse: *a-* + *sura*** (the privative — "not-*sura*") — the Sāmaveda-Padapāṭha (§5.5), and the parse the book's own *svar → suraḥ → asuraḥ* architecture uses (Ch 3 §3.6; §1.2 above).

They are not adjudicated by date here; §6 shows the sovereign function is what the word *does* under either parse. What follows catalogs the sources for each, with attestation status.

> ⚠️ **For verbatim quotation:** several Nirukta/Brāhmaṇa passages below are locator-solid but their exact Sanskrit/English wording rests on web transcriptions with OCR noise. Before any verbatim quote reaches the book, confirm against a print edition (flagged per item).

### 4.1 Yāska, *Nirukta* / *Nighaṇṭu*
- **Nighaṇṭu 1.10** — *asura* is listed as a synonym for **megha (cloud)**, item #29 of 30 in the Naighaṇṭuka cloud-word list. ✅ [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/nighantu-critical-study/d/doc1502436.html)] (This is the word-list Yāska comments on, not his derivation; *asura*-as-cloud is itself rare in the RV.)
- **Nirukta 3.8** (Naighaṇṭuka-kāṇḍa) — Yāska gives a **menu of derivations** for *asura* (the Nirukta's characteristic *iti vā … iti vā* style). Full passage — *verbatim per Sarup's edition (via external review, 2026-07-02); the core fragment is independently corroborated by Apte/wisdomlib; ⚠️ confirm the full wording (and the sandhi at* asur iti *) against print Sarup:*
  > असुर इति प्राणनाम । अस्तः शरीरे भवति । तेन तद्वन्तः ॥ अपि वासुरिति प्राणनाम । अस्यत्यनर्थानिति वा । असुराः सुरविरोधिनः । असुरताः स्थानेष्विति वा । असुरताः स्वेष्विति वा ॥
  >
  > *asur iti prāṇanāma | astaḥ śarīre bhavati | tena tadvantaḥ || api vāsur iti prāṇanāma | asyaty anarthān iti vā | asurāḥ suravirodhinaḥ | asuratāḥ sthāneṣv iti vā | asuratāḥ sveṣv iti vā ||*

  **The derivations (working translation):** (1) ***asu* = breath** (*prāṇa*), "set in the body," so *asura* = **one who possesses *asu*** (the life-force reading); (2) from **√as "to cast/throw"** — *asyaty anarthān*, "**casts away evils/misfortunes**" (an action-derivation); (3) ***asurāḥ suravirodhinaḥ*** — "**the *asuras* are opponents of the *suras***" (the sura-opposition reading); (4) *asuratāḥ sthāneṣu / sveṣu* — "**cast down from their stations / among their own**" (the fallen reading, again √as). **Two payoffs:** (a) Yāska *himself* holds **several parses at once** — the classical proof that the continuum "rightly debated" the word; (b) derivations (2) and (4) read *asura* **from the verb √as** — *नामान्याख्यातजानि* applied to *asura* itself. **Note the sura-opposition reading (3) presupposes *sura* as a word — a *laukika* development;** the *Nirukta* is itself laukika *śāstra*, so this is consistent with "the light/dark *sura/asura* binary is laukika." **Numbering:** cite **Nirukta 3.8** (the Nirukta is numbered *adhyāya.khaṇḍa*, two-part); the floating "**3.8**" is a ghost-citation, most likely a conflation with **Vākyapadīya 3.8** (Bhartṛhari on action/existence, cross-cited with Nirukta commentaries) — external review, 2026-07-02.
- **Nirukta 10.34** — reported to derive *asuratva* from *asu* (← √as). Locator plausible; verbatim ❓ UNVERIFIED.
- **Nighaṇṭu 3.9** — a claim that *asu* is listed among the "wisdom-words" (*prajñānāmāni*) is ❓ CONFLICTING (transcriptions disagree on whether *asuḥ* is in the list). Do not assert without a print Nighaṇṭu.

### 4.2 Brāhmaṇa etymologies (the *asu*-cosmogonies)
The Brāhmaṇas ground *asura* in *asu* (breath) through creation word-play (*nirvacana*), not formal derivation:
- **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 11.1.6.7–8** — Prajāpati creates the devas by the breath of the mouth (entering the sky) and the **asuras by the downward breath** (entering this earth); "then there was, as it were, darkness." ✅ verbatim (Eggeling, SBE 44). [[sacred-texts](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/sbr/sbe44/sbe44008.htm)] The canonical "asura from *asu*/breath" source.
- **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 2.2.9.5–8** — *sa jaghanād asurān asṛjata, sa mukhād devān asṛjata* ("from the loins he created the asuras, from the mouth the devas"); **2.3.8.2,4** — *sor devān asṛjata tat surāṇāṃ suratvam; asor asurān asṛjata tad asurāṇām asuratvam* (the *su-/asu-* parallel). Locators ✅; Sanskrit wording ⚠️ (verify against print TB). [[bvparishat](https://groups.google.com/g/bvparishat/c/1upJUorV_j8)]
- **Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa 3.35.3** — *asura* from *asu* + √**ram** ("delighting in the vital airs," the *asuṣu ramante* gloss commonly quoted without a locator). Locator attested; verbatim ❓. [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/asura)]
- **Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā 4.2.1** — an in-corpus gloss: Prajāpati created the *asuras* from his *asu* (via Hale, §2.3). ⚠️
- **The *surā* (liquor) etymology is Purāṇic, NOT Vedic** — *a-sura* = "those without *surā* (wine)" appears in the **Viṣṇu / Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa** churning narrative; flag as later folk etymology. (The Śatapatha *does* narrate *surā*-liquor + the asura Namuci in the Sautrāmaṇī rite, SB 12.7.1–3 — but that is ritual narrative, not an etymology of *asura*; do not conflate.) [[Wikipedia *Asura*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asura)]

### 4.3 Uṇādi-sūtras & Pāṇinian grammar
- **Uṇādi-sūtra 1.42** (per Apte) — *asura* = **asu + -ra**. ✅ (Apte) ⚠️ recension numbering (Pañcapādī vs. Daśapādī / Ujjvaladatta) varies — verify "1.42". [[Apte via wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/asura)]
- **Alternative: √as + *uran*** → *asura* (Monier-Williams). ✅ Two grammatical analyses circulate: *asu-ra* (Apte) and *as+uran* (MW).
- **Uṇādi-sūtra 1.1** (per Apte) — *asu* = **as + un** (*asyate* "is cast/impelled"; root *as* "to throw"). ✅ (Apte). [[wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/asu)]
- **Pāṇini, Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.4.123** — **asurasya svam** — the *yat*-formation for *asurya / āsurya* ("belonging to / property of an *asura*"). ✅ verbatim sūtra. **Pāṇini does not derive *asura* itself** (that is the Uṇādi domain); the Aṣṭādhyāyī bears on the derivative *asurya*. [[GRETIL Pāṇini](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/6_sastra/1_gram/paniniiu.htm); [wisdomlib *asurya*](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/asurya)]
- **Dhātupāṭha** — no specific "-ura/-ra → asura" entry located; relevant roots are *as* "to be" (adādi) and *as* "to throw" (Uṇādi context). ❓ UNVERIFIED.

### 4.4 Sāyaṇa & the lexical / comparative tradition
- **Sāyaṇa** (general pattern): glosses *asura* as *prāṇavantam / balavantam* ("possessing life-breath / mighty," via *asu-ra*) in the sovereign contexts, and via √as ("to hurl") in some Marut/warrior contexts. ⚠️ His **verbatim glosses on RV 8.42.1 and RV 2.1.6 are ❓ UNVERIFIED** — check the print Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya ([RV w/ Sāyaṇa Part 3](https://archive.org/details/RgVedaWithSayanasCommentaryPart3), Maṇḍala 8; [Part 2](https://archive.org/details/RgVedaWithSayanasCommentaryPart2), Maṇḍala 2).
- **Monier-Williams** (1899): √as ("to be") → *asu* ("breath") → *asura* ("spirit / life-force"), with the later re-analysis as *a-* (privative) + *sura* ("not-a-god → demon"); also gives *as + uran*. ✅
- **Comparative (Mayrhofer & standard)** — Proto-Indo-Iranian *\*Hásuras* "lord," cognate with **Avestan *ahura*** and *ahu-* "life." ✅ This is external comparative reconstruction, distinct from the indigenous *asu*+*ra* / √as analyses (and it is the account the book contests in Ch 18 §18.7).

## 5. Prātiśākhya findings

**Framing (important).** The Prātiśākhyas operate on **phonetic classes and junctions** — sandhi, accent (svara), retroflexion (*ṇatva*), the saṃhitā↔pada conversion, *abhinidhāna*, *avagraha*, pause forms — **not on individual lexemes as topics**. There is **no Prātiśākhya rule whose subject is the word *asura***. Where *asura* appears, it is an *incidental example-word* sitting inside a quoted Vedic *pratīka* used to illustrate a class-level rule, or a worked *padapāṭha* separation. Honest framing for the book: *asura* is *attested inside Prātiśākhya example-material*, never singled out for its own treatment. Result: **YES in 3 of 5 traditions** (RV, Taittirīya, Sāmaveda-editorial), **no occurrence** in the Vājasaneyi, **inconclusive** for the Atharva.

## 6. Synthesis — what *asura* does (function and role)

Read on the mantras' own merit, *asura* names **the wielder of formative-ordering power** — divine sovereignty exercised as the structuring and sustaining of the cosmos. Three strands of the word's semantics converge:

1. **असु *asu*** (life-breath) → animated with the *power to enact*.
2. **माया *māyā*** (in the Ṛgveda: the power to *measure / form / fashion*; Varuṇa is *māyin*) → RV 8.42.1 literally has the asura *measuring* (amimīta) the earth. The asura's signature act is measuring-and-forming.
3. **स्वर् / सुर** (the luminous heaven) → the asura is "of the great heaven" (*maho divaḥ*), bound to light and height.

**One power, two orientations (no timeline needed):**
- Formative power exercised *for* the order (ṛta) → the *asura* as **sovereign** — props, measures, legislates (Varuṇa; Agni-Rudra).
- The same formative / shape-shifting power exercised *against* the order → the *asura* as **adversary** — as *māyā* slides from "measuring power" to "deceptive conjuring," the *asura* slides with it.

The word does not change meaning; the *function it serves* changes — sovereign where it upholds the measure, adversary where it counterfeits it. This is internal to the mantras (the propping, the measuring, the *vratāni*, the *māyā*) and grounds the book's *svar → suraḥ → asuraḥ* architecture without adjudicating any etymological dispute: one can *point at Varuṇa measuring the earth* as the asura-function in action.

**What the full-corpus survey adds (all sourced above):**

1. **The functional axis is on the page, not inferred.** In the Atharvaveda the split is keyed to **grammatical number** — singular *asura* keeps the sovereign function (Agni, Varuṇa, Prajāpati), plural *asuras* carries the adversary function (§2.2). Number, not date, sorts the two roles.

2. **The sovereign formula is stable across the corpus.** *asuro viśvavedāḥ* names the ordering-sovereign at RV 8.42.1 (Varuṇa) and again at VS 27.12 (Agni/Tanūnapāt) — the same epithet-formula re-anchored on a different deity (§3.8). A stable formula is engineering, not drift.

3. **The measuring motif is the through-line.** *māyā* = the power to measure/form (RV 5.63.3 *asurasya māyáyā*); Varuṇa *measures* the earth (RV 8.42.1 *amimīta*); and even the hymn that turns against the *asura* still has the speaker *measure out* the abodes of Order (RV 10.124.3 *vi mime … ṛtasya dhāma*). The asura-as-measurer survives the polarity flip.

4. **Both indigenous parses land on sovereignty.** The etymologists' *asu + ra* ("possessing life-force → life-lord": Yāska *Nirukta* 3.8; Uṇādi 1.42; ŚB 11.1.6.8; JB 3.35.3) and the recitation-discipline's *a- + sura* (the privative: Sāmaveda-Padapāṭha, §5.5; the book's Ch 3 §3.6 architecture) reach the same functional value from different morphology. The book uses the privative parse; neither parse needs the other to be wrong.

5. **The privative is primary; *sura* is the artifact.** Standalone *sura* "god" is absent from **all four Saṃhitās** (§2.5); it is the back-formation from re-analyzing *a-sura*. The recitation tradition's own word-separation (*a + surasya*) shows the privative segmentation was operative from within the discipline, not imposed from outside.

## 7. Narrative implications — what this does for the book *(analyst's commentary, 2026-07-01)*

This section is strategic, not evidentiary: it reads §§2–6 for what they do to the argument. Recommendations are flagged as such.

### 7.1 Established narratives this challenges

**(a) The "asura degraded from god to demon" evolution.** The standard account has *asura* start positive (Varuṇa, Indra) and *become* pejorative across the Rigveda. That "became" is not in the text — it is an inference requiring an internal chronology of the maṇḍalas. §2.2 supplies a synchronic alternative that fits at least as well: in the Atharvaveda the sovereign/adversary split tracks **grammatical number** (singular = lord; plural = the opposing class), not date. Remove the timeline and there is no drift to narrate — one word, two concurrent functions. This is category-before-calendar with a concrete lexical case behind it.

**(b) The Indo-Iranian *asura/ahura ↔ deva/daeva* "reversal" and the "great schism."** The migration-era religious history leans on a mirror-image: *asura* good→bad in India, *ahura* supreme in Iran; *deva* good in India, *daeva* demonic in Iran — read as the fossil of a common Indo-Iranian religion that split (Zarathustra's reform). That structure needs the Indian side to have flipped *over time*. Two findings remove the mechanism: the flip is the chronological artifact of (a); and the deva/asura polarity is generated *inside* the system as a structural-cosmological duality — the Brāhmaṇa cosmogonies derive devas from the up/mouth-breath and asuras from the down-breath (ŚB 11.1.6.8; TB 2.2.9 mouth vs. loins; the *su-/asu-* pair at TB 2.3.8) — i.e. order vs. counter-order manufactured as a pair, not remembered from a neighbor-tribe schism. Feeds the RAT/PIE dismantling (Ch 18) directly.

**(c) The PIE reconstruction of *asura* is superfluous.** The indigenous disciplines carry a complete internal etymology (Yāska *Nirukta* 3.8; Uṇādi 1.42; the Brāhmaṇa *asu*-cosmogonies; Pāṇini 4.4.123 for *asurya*). This reinforces Ch 18 §18.7's "*contested* is the confession": Sanskrit explains the word from within; *\*h₂nsu-* adds only a hedge. And *sura*'s absence from all four Saṃhitās (§2.5) supports Sanskrit-as-source / Avestan *ahura*-as-receiver (*vivimorphosis / Pratibimba*) over co-descent — it removes the *need* for a shared ancestor. **Honest limit:** it does not by itself prove borrowing over cognate; it shifts the burden.

### 7.2 The upgrade for Ch 3 §3.6 *(recommendation)*

Reframe *asuratva* from "not-light" to **the measuring sovereignty turned to withholding.** In the Veda the *asura* is the sovereign *measurer*: Varuṇa *amimīta* — "measured out the earth" (8.42.1) — by *māyā*, which in the Veda is the power to *measure and form* (5.63.3, *asurasya māyáyā*). That is the book's engineering faculty in Vedic dress. So *asuratva* is one power in two orientations: it upholds the measure (suric; *lokakṣema*) or it hoards and darkens the light (asuric; the pyramid). This maps onto the church of progress better than flat "darkness" — the pyramid is *real sovereign power that withholds*, not weakness — and it feeds the eclipse spine (Svarbhānu = the measurer-turned-withholder; light darkened, not destroyed).

### 7.3 The vulnerability to close *(flag)*

The dominant *indigenous* etymology is ***asu* + *-ra*** = "possessing life-force" — **positive** — and the Vedic *asura* is overwhelmingly the **sovereign lord**. The book's *svar → suraḥ → asuraḥ* ("not-light") is the *privative* parse: real, and native to the recitation discipline (the Sāmaveda-Padapāṭha splits *a + surasya*, §5.5), but a **minority parse**, not the mainstream one. A reviewer who knows the Nirukta/Uṇādi tradition will object that the book built its central antagonist-word on a term the Vedas apply to the highest gods. Three defenses: (i) the antagonist-*asura* is the **Itihāsa/Purāṇic** figure the reader already knows (Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa) — uncontested; (ii) "not-light" is the book's **structural/diagnostic** segmentation, explicitly distinct from the word's Vedic *use*; (iii) **decisive — the thesis is etymology-independent:** under *either* parse (*asu + ra* "life-lord" or *a + sura* "not-light") the shift from the sovereign who *measures* (Varuṇa) to the one who *walls up the flow* (Vṛtra) is identical, so containment carries the charge whichever derivation wins *(external review, 2026-07-01)*. Strongest constructive framing: fold in the "one power, two orientations" frame (§7.2) so the positive Vedic asura becomes *part of* the argument rather than a fact against it. Ch 3 §3.6 should not imply the privative "not-light" is the word's *original/primary* Vedic meaning.

### 7.4 Net
- **Ch 18 §18.7** — reinforced: add "the reversal/schism is a chronology artifact" and "deva/asura is a structural duality, not a schism-memory"; the native etymology strengthens the existing PIE-superfluity move.
- **Ch 3 §3.6** — upgrade to "the measurer / one power, two orientations"; this simultaneously closes §7.3's vulnerability and strengthens the pyramid diagnosis.
- **Eclipse spine** — the asuric mode as light-*withholder* (not light-absence) resonates with Svarbhānu / Rāhu.

### 7.5 Not power but withholding — the containment-operation in the vaidika domain *(vaidika-only; laukika material deliberately excluded)*

**Claim.** In the vaidika domain, power and singular sovereignty are never condemned; what is condemned is the *withholding / enclosing* of what should flow. That withholding-enclosure is the Vedic form of the pyramid — the *architecture of containment* (Ch 3 §3.5).

**The sovereign side (praised).** The all-powerful singular *asura* is the highest praise: Varuṇa *samrāṭ*, all-knowing, props the sky and *measures* the earth (RV 8.42.1); the sovereign who *releases* — *śiśrathaḥ*, "loosen the sins we have committed" (RV 1.24.14). Sovereignty exercised as ordering-and-release.

**The problem-side (the withholding-operation).** The vaidika adversary is defined by *enclosure / hoarding*, and the heroic act is *release*:
- **Vṛtra** (√*vṛ*, "to cover, obstruct") **dams the waters**; Indra shatters the enclosure and frees them (the Vṛtra-slaying hymn, RV 1.32). ✅
- **The Paṇis hoard the cows** (the dawn-herd / light / wealth) walled in the **Vala** cave; the act is to break it open and release them (the Sarama–Paṇi dialogue, RV 10.108; the Vala myth). ✅
- **Namuci** — named *āsura* in the Yajurveda (VS 19.34; 20.67–68, §2.3) — the withholder who carried off Indra's strength (ŚB 12.7.1–3). ✅

So the vaidika moral axis is **release vs. withholding** = *swastika vs. pyramid*: power that lets things flow and shine, versus power that walls them up and hoards.

**Two precisions that keep the claim exactly vaidika-true:**
1. **The vaidika domain sorts the two orientations across *different agents*** — the releasing sovereign (Varuṇa, Indra) vs. the enclosing withholder (Vṛtra, the Paṇis, Namuci). It does *not* depict one sovereign *asura* who *flips* into a pyramid-builder; that single-figure reversal is a structural (and laukika) reading, held out of this note.
2. **Building is not the problem; enclosure is.** Varuṇa *builds* — props the sky, measures the earth. What is condemned is not raising a structure but raising one that *withholds the flow* (Vṛtra's dam) rather than *holding the frame open* (Varuṇa's propped sky, under which the waters and light move).

**Distributed, not apex-monopolized.** The sovereign power is stated as shared: RV 3.55, *mahád **devā́nām** asuratvám ékam* — the one sovereign power is *of the gods*, held across the many, not seized at a single peak.

**Feeds:** Ch 3 §3.5 (*architecture of containment* — Vṛtra's dam and the Paṇis' cave as the Vedic archetypes of enclosure / blockade / quarantine / concealment); the *swastika vs. pyramid* reframe; the eclipse spine (withholding = darkening, not destroying, the light). ⚠️ **Not yet in the manuscript — awaiting review.**
