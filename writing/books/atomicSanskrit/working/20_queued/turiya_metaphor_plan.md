# Turīya — Metaphor Leverage Plan (options)

*Working doc. How **तुरीय (*turīya*)** — "the fourth" — can be leveraged in *Atomic Sanskrit*'s metaphor system. Proposal stage. Nothing is deployed in the book yet except one author seed at Ch 20 §20.4 (~line 119): "Atri finds the hidden Sun through **turīya brahman**, the fourth formulation of disciplined speech." Drafted 2026-06-15.*

---

## The core insight

Turīya's force is that it is **the fourth that is not a fourth item** — not a state beside the other three, but the witness / ground that holds them. That maps onto two distinct load-bearing structures in the book, which must be kept apart:

- **Turīya-as-witness** (Māṇḍūkya: A‑U‑M + the silent fourth) → the **recognition / Wave 3** axis.
- **Turīya-as-source-of-speech** (the fourth / transcendent *formulation* of Vāk) → the **PIE-replacement / descent** axis.

## The grounding that makes it work: *brahman = formulation*

"Turīya brahman" reads cleanly as **"the fourth formulation"** when *brahman* is taken in its **archaic Vedic sense** — *bráhman* (neuter), from √*bṛh* "to swell / strengthen," = the **formulated sacred utterance** ("*Formulierung*," Paul Thieme, "Brahman," *ZDMG* 102, 1952). This is the older meaning (the formulated power of sacred speech), distinct from the later Vedāntic *brahman* = the Absolute.

- **Why it's ideal here:** it ties turīya to **speech / formulation** — the book's subject — rather than to abstract metaphysics. "The fourth formulation" = the transcendent fourth utterance from which the engineered architecture descends.
- **Mark the choice:** the book uses the archaic *brahman = formulation* sense (Thieme), explicitly distinguished from the Vedāntic Absolute. Both readings exist; the book picks the speech-grounded one. This grounding lets us **drop the RV 1.164.45 angle entirely** (see precision note).

## The textual keystone — RV 5.40.6 (sourcing RESOLVED ✓)

Turīya is **not** an external Vedāntic graft. It sits *inside the book's own eclipse hymn.* **RV 5.40.6** — the Svarbhānu verse the whole eclipse frame already rests on — reads (Padapāṭha): *gūḷhaṃ sūryaṃ tamasā apavratena **turīyeṇa brahmaṇā** avindat atriḥ* — "Atri found the Sun, hidden by hostile darkness, **by the fourth formulation** (*turīyeṇa brahmaṇā*, instrumental — the tool he used)."

- **Sourcing flag closed:** *turīyeṇa brahmaṇā* is verbatim in RV 5.40.6. The line-119 endnote (`rigveda-5-40-atri-clearing`) cites **5.40.6** for the fourth-formulation claim; **5.40.9** stays the "none but the Atris could find it" capstone. (Confirm the printed edition — Geldner / Jamison–Brereton — for the citation.)
- ***brahman = formulation* confirmed on the surface,** not just via Thieme: standard translations render *brahmaṇā* here as "prayer / sacred formulation." "Fourth formulation" is the *received* reading of this verse, so it needs no defense; Thieme is the deep backing, not the load-bearer.
- **The verse maps the whole frame:** *svarbhānoḥ māyāḥ* = the pyramid's **asurī māyā** (Ch 2 title); *gūḷhaṃ sūryaṃ* = Sanskrit's hidden category; *tamasā apavratena* = the asuric darkness ("by the lawless / hostile"); *turīyeṇa brahmaṇā* = the recovery-means = the engineered architecture this book restates.

## ⚠️ Precision — two "fourths" point opposite ways

- **Māṇḍūkya** turīya = the **transcendent** fourth (the witness). ← **use this**
- **RV 1.164.45** (*catvāri vāk… turīyaṃ vāco manuṣyā vadanti*): turīya = the **manifest / spoken** quarter, three hidden. ← **opposite; do not cite for the transcendent reading.**
- **Resolution:** turīya in the Māṇḍūkya / transcendent sense + *brahman = formulation* (Thieme) + *parā vāc* (the supreme level in the Bhartṛhari four-Vāk scheme). Avoid leaning on RV 1.164.45's literal "spoken fourth."

## The options (menu — pick and combine)

### Option A — Wave 3 **is** Turīya (the recognition) — *PRIMARY*

§20.4 already frames Wave 3 as *relearning* and closes on "Recognition can now become invitation." Turīya names exactly that: the first three movements act; the fourth *sees*.

**Proposed prose (early in §20.4, after "three deployments"):**
> The first three moved outward — corpus, method, restatement carried the architecture into other mouths and other ages. The fourth moves the other way. It is not a fourth deployment; it is the **तुरीय (*turīya*)** of the other three — not a state beside waking, dream, and sleep, but the awareness that holds all three. Wave 3 is turīya: not a new thing Sanskrit *does*, but Sanskrit's bearers waking to what Sanskrit *is*. The first three waves act. The fourth sees. The restatement is only its instrument; the recognition is the work.

- *Light variant:* one sentence naming Wave 3 as turīya at the §20.4 open + the existing line-119 seed.
- *Heavy variant:* the full passage above + an Epilogue echo (the Atris find the Sun = recognition, not rediscovery; the reader-as-Wave-3-ṛṣi = the witness waking).

### Option B — the four movements as **Oṃ unfolding** (A‑U‑M‑Turīya) — *LIGHT, once only*

Tie the waves to the Oṃ the book already venerates (Ch 7):

| Movement | Māṇḍūkya | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 — pre-Pāṇinian, outward | **A / Jāgrat** (waking, outward) | exact |
| Wave 2 — method / grammar | **U / Svapna** (dream / subtle) | **loosest joint** |
| Diasporic Wave — substrate carried unawares | **M / Suṣupti** (deep sleep, dormant) | exact |
| Wave 3 — recognition | **Turīya** (the witness / silence after A‑U‑M) | exact |

- **Caveat:** deploy **once, lightly** (a single reveal in §20.4, or a figure caption / sidebar). Three joints are exact; Wave 2 ↔ dream is a stretch. Used once = elegant; repeated = gimmick.

### Option C — Turīya as the **source**: the fourth formulation (descent vs. PIE) — *PRIMARY*

The most on-thesis lever, because the book is about Sanskrit's **origin**. PIE asks for a *horizontal* ancestor on a timeline. The four-Vāk / fourth-formulation frame answers **vertically**: the architecture descends from the fourth / transcendent formulation of speech, three quarters hidden, one spoken.

**Proposed prose (Ch 19 close or §20.4):**
> PIE looked sideways for a parent. The Vedic account looks *down* a different axis: speech in four quarters, three hidden, the engineered architecture descending from the fourth. The ancestor philology could not find was never beside Sanskrit on a line. It was above it — in the formulation men do not ordinarily speak.

- Reframes "where Sanskrit comes from" from **genealogy** to **descent from the fourth**.
- Dovetails with the eclipse: the Sun was never *beside* us to be lost; it was *above*, only obscured.

### Option D — early seed (Ch 0 / Ch 1) + eclipse tie — *LIGHT*

So the Wave-3 payoff is not cold:
- **Ch 0** (Seekers / Infinite): turīya = the experiential **पूर्णम् (*pūrṇam*)** the seeker's *jijñāsā* approaches — the fourth, the whole.
- **Ch 1** (the apex): *"The apex freezes a state and calls it the whole. Turīya is the witness behind every state — the one thing the apex can never own."* = "the boundless defeats his ownership" given its Vedic name.
- **Eclipse tie:** **Svarbhānu darkens the states; the witness was never eclipsed.** Reinforces the optimism (the Sun was only obscured).

### Option E — Indra's *force* vs. Atri's *formulation* (the method) — *STRONG, fully grounded in RV 5.40.6*

The keystone verse stages **two** responses to Svarbhānu's eclipse-māyā: **Indra strikes** it down by force (*ava ahan*, "struck down"); **Atri finds** the hidden Sun *turīyeṇa brahmaṇā* — by the fourth precise formulation. That is the book's method in a single verse: the eclipsed category is not restored by force or counter-polemic but by **precise architectural formulation** — *vyākaraṇam*, the calibrant, the engineered grid. Atri = the caretaker-lineage (the Atris of the eclipse frame); the fourth formulation = the architecture this book restates.

**Proposed prose (Epilogue, or §20.4, or Ch 2's asurī-māyā close):**
> Indra struck the illusion; Atri found the Sun. Two ways to meet an eclipse — force, and formulation. The Sun behind the asurī māyā is not punched back into the sky. It is found, by the fourth formulation — the precise, disciplined speech the darkness could hide but never unmake.

- Ties the book's **method** (recovery by formulation, not force) to its keystone verse — and to the courtroom frame (fight on the pyramid's ground with exact formulation, not violence).
- Pairs with Option C: the fourth formulation is both the **source** of the architecture and the **means** of its recovery.

## Worked example — the A + C + E thread as continuous narrative

To see that A / C / E are **one arc, not three inserts** — a single motif (*the fourth formulation*) doing three jobs — here is the thread written as continuous prose (a demonstration; in the book these would seed and pay off across the spine, not sit as one block):

> *[E — method]* The oldest account of the eclipse already carries its remedy. In Ṛgveda 5.40, Svarbhānu pierces Sūrya with darkness and the worlds go blind; the asura's *māyā* spreads down from the sky. Two answers follow. Indra strikes the illusion by force. Atri does something else — he finds the hidden Sun **turīyeṇa brahmaṇā**, *by the fourth formulation*. The Sun is not punched back into the sky; it is found, by a precise and disciplined utterance the darkness could cover but never unmake. That is this book's method in a single verse. The pyramid's *asurī māyā* hides Sanskrit's category the way Svarbhānu hides the Sun, and the category is recovered not by force or counter-polemic but by formulation — by *vyākaraṇam*, by the calibrant, by the engineered grid restated with enough precision that the darkness has nothing left to hold.
>
> *[C — origin]* That fourth formulation also answers the question philology could not. The comparative method looked sideways, along a timeline, for a parent standing beside Sanskrit — and reconstructed PIE when it could not find one. But the architecture has no horizontal parent. Its source is not on the line at all. Remove the imaginary ancestor and what remains is not an orphan waiting to be adopted; it is a descent — from the fourth, the formulation *above* the line rather than *behind* it. PIE was a search in the wrong direction.
>
> *[A — recognition]* And the finding was never a discovery. **तुरीय (*turīya*)** is the fourth that is not a fourth thing — not a state beside waking, dream, and deep sleep, but the witness that holds all three. The first three waves carried the architecture outward: corpus, method, restatement moved it into other mouths and later ages. The fourth wave moves the other way. It carries nothing anywhere; it turns to *see*. Wave 3 is turīya — not a new thing Sanskrit does, but its bearers waking to what Sanskrit *is*. The reader who recognizes the grid is Atri at the end of the eclipse, finding by the fourth formulation a Sun that was never gone. The first three waves act. The fourth sees. Recognition is the recovery.

**The combination in one line:** one motif (*the fourth formulation*), three jobs — *how* the recovery works (method), *where* the architecture comes from (origin), and *what* the recovery actually is (recognition). Drop any one and the arc loses a beat.

**Placement when deployed (not as a block):** **E** → Ch 2 *asurī-māyā* close or the Epilogue; **C** → Ch 19 (PIE removal); **A** → §20.4 / the Epilogue handoff.
