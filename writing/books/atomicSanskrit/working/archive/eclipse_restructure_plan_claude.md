*Drafted 2026-06-14. Status: PLAN — pre-execution. The canonical source of truth for the pyramid-led / Eclipse-frame reorganization. Supersedes scattered decisions in chat; reconcile `as_epigraph_plan.md` and `chapter_sequence_flow_audit.md` to this doc before executing.*

## 1. The revised premise (pyramid-led)

> Sanskrit is engineered order — created, not grown — and India has been its caretaker. The asuric pyramid, which can neither build such an order nor abide one above its own, makes war on both the language and its defenders. Its subtlest weapon is *asuri māyā*: PIE, an invented ancestor that makes the Calibrant look botanical.

The earlier framing led with the *linguistic* claim (engineered-not-grown; *dhātuḥ* ≠ root). The revision makes the **civilizational conflict** the frame and demotes the engineering proof to the *stakes and the evidence*. The old opening battle (*dhātuḥ* miscategorization) becomes one exhibit inside the larger war.

## 2. The Eclipse frame

The book's organizing figure, drawn from **Ṛgveda 5.40** (Svarbhānu eclipses Sūrya; the Atris alone recover him):

| Figure | Maps to |
|---|---|
| **Sūrya** | Sanskrit — engineered, radiant, generative light |
| **Svarbhānu** | the asuric pyramid — the masculine apex-form and its actor-network |
| **Rāhu** | PIE — the eclipse-device / false head placed over Sanskrit |
| **Atri / the Atris** | Hindus / *paramparā* — the caretakers who can find the Sun again |

| ***kṛṇvanto viśvam āryam*** | the final rallying cry, after recovery |

**Naming rule — call this "the Eclipse frame," never "the mythic frame."** See §10 (never "myth" for the Itihāsa).

**Svarbhānu / Rāhu — own the interpretive layering.** Canonically Rāhu *is* Svarbhānu's severed head (beheaded at the churning; head = Rāhu, tail = Ketu). The Svarbhānu(apex-network) / Rāhu(eclipse-device) split is therefore an *interpretive* move, not a textual given — state it deliberately. The payoff: **Rāhu is a head with no body — and PIE is exactly that, an ancestor with no speakers, immortal in the academy because it was never alive to be killed.** Resonance: Rāhu is a *chāyā-graha* (shadow-planet) ↔ **"PIE in the Sky"** (Ch 18). Ketu (the tail/body) is available if ever needed for the comparative *method* as trailing remnant.

**Synergy with the masculine-apex thread (already executed):** Svarbhānu is the *named* face the thread lacked — the male eclipse-asura. The AP0 keystone ("every apex is a he") should seat **Svarbhānu** at its head. **Atri is a *male* restorer**, which balances the feminine Devī Māhātmya restorers (Durgā/Kālī) and holds converse-#2 exact: *the apex is invariably male; the restorer varies.*

## 3. Three frames, three layers (so they don't compete)

- **Eclipse** (Sūrya / Svarbhānu / Rāhu / Atri) = the **diagnosis** — *what happened*. Lives in the epigraphs, the bookends, and the closing restoration.
- **Courtroom** (charge → evidence → verdict) = the **method** — *how the book proves it*. This is the **Part spine** (Parts I–VI).
- **"Sanskrit vs the Pyramid" / the war** = the **stakes/register** — *why it matters*. Lives in the prose voice, not in any structural label.

Consequence: **Part labels stay in the courtroom register; the bookends carry the eclipse; the war lives in the prose.** This is why the opening bookend is **Śaṅkha** (not "The War" — that would put a martial label next to "The Prosecution" and read as a metaphor-switch).

**War vs battle.** The **war** is older and larger than this book — the asuric pyramid's ongoing assault on Sanskrit and its caretakers. The **Śaṅkha** *reminds* the reader the war is already underway and that caretaking now requires action; it does not start the war. The **courtroom** is narrower — *this volume's battle within that war*, fought deliberately on the pyramid's own ground, because the pyramid understands the courtroom (its judicial imagination is Abrahamic) and its crimes can be prosecuted inside its own machinery of accusation, evidence, and verdict. That is the **courtroom-rationale passage** (kept; §9 save #3). *Dropped: "matching footnotes with footnotes" — the rationale passage already carries it.*
