# BORI Renaming Candidate

**Created:** 2026-09-05
**Status:** Initial research note for a possible future campaign

## Recommended Individual

### Śrīpād Dāmodar Sātavaḷekar (1867–1968)

Sātavaḷekar offers the clearest contrast with R. G. Bhāṇḍārkar. Both were Maharashtrian scholars with substantial work in Sanskrit and Vedic studies. The colonial state rewarded Bhāṇḍārkar with council appointments and two grades of the Order of the Indian Empire. It confiscated and burned Sātavaḷekar's work, jailed him, watched his movements, and repeatedly disrupted his life.

The contrast is supported by a substantial scholarly record:

1. Sātavaḷekar studied and taught the Vedas, translated Vedic works, and wrote approximately four hundred books in Marathi, Hindi, and English.
2. He founded the Swādhyāya Maṇḍala in 1918 for Vedic study, research, translation, and publication.
3. His *Vaidika Rāṣṭragīta* and essay on the brilliance of Vedic prayers angered the British government. He was imprisoned, and copies of the Hindi *Vaidika Rāṣṭragīta* were confiscated and burned.
4. In 1942, he printed material against British rule in support of the Quit India movement.
5. His Sanskrit self-teaching series and low-cost Vedic publications were designed to place knowledge directly in the hands of readers.

This gives a renaming campaign a clean proposition: replace the name of a Sanskrit scholar honored by the empire with that of a Vedic scholar punished by it.

## Possible Name

**Śrīpād Dāmodar Sātavaḷekar Institute for Vedic and Sanskrit Research**

The full name is long. **Sātavaḷekar Institute for Vedic and Sanskrit Research** is easier to use publicly while retaining the contrast.

## Architecturally Strong Alternative

An institutional name may fit the calibrant argument better than another personal name:

**Pune Institute for Vedic and Sanskrit Research**

This avoids replacing one elevated individual with another. It places the work, place, and purpose above a single scholar. A campaign could still ask the institute to establish a named Sātavaḷekar program or chair for Vedic research and public access.

## Before Any Public Campaign

The next research pass should examine Sātavaḷekar's own writings on Sanskrit history, the Vedas, and the Aryan framework. Opposition to British rule does not by itself establish agreement with *Atomic Sanskrit*. The renaming argument will be strongest if his scholarship as well as his public conduct supports the contrast.
