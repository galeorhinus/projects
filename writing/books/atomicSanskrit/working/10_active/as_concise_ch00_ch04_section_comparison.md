# Chapters 0–4: What the Concise Edition Keeps and Loses

**Date:** 28 September 2026; restoration update 29 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Compare each full-book section with the concise draft, including its sharpness, personal voice, and explanation. The approved restoration pass is now reflected in the statuses below. See the [restoration report](as_concise_ch00_ch04_restoration_review.md) for changes and counts.

The sentences below represent each section of the full book, in its original order. They include accusations, stories, personal connections, examples, and emphatic lines, not just thesis statements.

- **✓ Included:** the concise prose retains the substance of this sentence. Wording may differ unless marked as an exact line.
- **△ Partly included / changed:** the broad point remains, but an example, explanation, emphasis, or personal element is missing. The difference is stated.
- **✗ Excluded:** this material is absent from the concise prose. A further-reading pointer is not counted as inclusion. If the material appears elsewhere in the current concise drafts, that location is identified.

Quotation marks identify wording from the full book. Other sentences are representative paraphrases, not proposed replacement prose. Status describes what is there now, not whether the omission was a good decision.

**The two examples that prompted this review:** burning competing Quranic copies remains **✓ included** in concise §2.1. “I am one of them” is now **✓ restored** at the end of concise §2.3.

### 0.2 A Culture of Seekers

### 0.3 Sanskrit in Plain Sight

[Full §0.3](../../manuscript/as_1_00_seekers.md:61) · [Concise §0.2](../../concise/manuscript/as_1_00_seekers.md:31)

- ✗ The chapter promises to show Sanskrit reflected in familiar English words such as king, station, genesis, native, constant, and state. That invitation to recognize the reader's own vocabulary is absent here.

**Attribute, Not Label**

- ✓ कौन्तेय (*Kaunteya*) identifies Arjuna through his relationship to Kuntī; a Sanskrit name can tell us something about its bearer.
- ✗ Savyasācin and Dhanañjaya describe other attributes of the same person. The concise edition keeps only the mother–son relationship.
- ✗ Six words for water describe its changing states, movement, reach, nourishment, and other attributes. None of those demonstrations remains locally; a pointer sends the reader to the full section.
- ✓ Candrayāna shows that the same word-building system can name a modern spacecraft. This example remains, with its construction explained.

### 0.4 संस्कृतम् (*Saṃskṛtam*) and प्राकृतानि (*Prākṛtāni*)

[Full §0.4](../../manuscript/as_1_00_seekers.md:81) · [Concise §§0.3–0.4](../../concise/manuscript/as_1_00_seekers.md:39)

- ✓ Sanskrit is wholly formed, while natural speech changes through use; both can serve a changing society.
- ✓ The Vedic domain keeps language and content exact; the laukika domain permits new compositions through the same language. Figure 0.1 retains all three streams, including changing natural speech.
- △ The full section gives natural speech its own dignity through songs, idiom, sayings, and everyday use. The concise explanation retains its function but loses much of that warmth and explicit respect.
- ✓ Invariance allows later students to understand old compositions without handing their meaning to an exclusive interpreter; its purpose is to keep civilizational memory understandable.
- ✓ Writing cannot be trusted with that responsibility alone: copies decay, burn, change, or fall under an authority's control, and scripts change with technology. This remains in concise §0.4.
- △ The fact that the book can examine these ideas in their original Sanskrit is itself evidence of success. The concise prose retains a student's access to old compositions but removes the author's direct observation about this book.

- △ Indra uses strength to release the waters, and actions must serve living beings. The concrete example remains, but the Mahābhārata's welfare criterion and quoted wording are absent.

## What the Original Review Revealed

Before restoration, the concise drafts retained much of the argument and some important sharp material: burning Quranic copies, punishment, Nehru's ridicule, the categorical refusal to recognize Sanskrit's engineering, RAT, the fourth Abrahamic religion, named Indian institutions, and Bandin's gate.

But retaining the argument did not always retain its character. The reductions often removed:

1. **The person speaking:** “I am one of them,” belonging, and invitations to share the caretaking responsibility.
2. **People and stories readers recognize:** the disguise narratives, Raktabīja, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāma/Rāvaṇa, Durgā/Mahiṣāsura, and people finding refuge in India.
3. **The concrete basis of the accusation:** some named institutions remain, but the Boden Chair, Müller, translation contrasts, recruitment through honors, money, and extended curriculum examples often disappear.
4. **The memorable comparison:** bread-guardian/Lord, zero/counting, Jñāneśvarī, and a story's ending versus its date of composition.
5. **The closing force:** several conclusions remain in substance while their established emphatic lines disappear.

The author selected a restoration batch from this inventory. Those changes are now implemented and marked above; the remaining ✗ entries are not an instruction to restore everything.
