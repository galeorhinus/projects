# Sanskrit Radiance: Dhātu vs PIE Candidates

## Research Objective
This document evaluates candidate word families to test whether reconstructed Proto-Indo-European (PIE) forms or recorded Sanskrit *dhātavaḥ* offer a stronger foundational explanation for linguistic similarities across the Indo-European family. The methodology uses Indic methods of sound analysis (*varṇamālā* mechanics) and semantic derivation (*vyutpatti*) to explain sound correspondences as engineered or natural transformations of Sanskrit atoms, comparing this "Sanskrit-Radiance" model against conventional PIE reconstructions.

## Finalist 1: जन् (jan) → kin / genus (Introductory Example)

**1. Sanskrit dhātu and traditional meaning:**
**जन् (jan)**. Dhātupāṭha definition: *janī prādurbhāve* (to be born, to come into existence, to produce).

**2. Sanskrit derivational chain:**
*jan* + *ti* → *jāti* (birth, kind). *jan* + *as* → *janas* (race, kind). 

**3. Conventional PIE chain:**
PIE `*ǵenh₁-` (to produce, beget) → Proto-Germanic `*kunją` → English *kin*. PIE `*ǵenh₁-es-` → Latin *genus*, Greek *genos*. 
*Explanation:* The PIE palatovelar `*ǵ` shifts to velar `g` in centum languages (Latin/Greek) and devoices to `k` in Germanic (Grimm's Law).

**4. Proposed Sanskrit-to-receiving-language chain:**
The Sanskrit palatal atom **j** acts as the baseline. It shifts backward in the vocal tract to the corresponding velar **g** (Latin/Greek), and subsequently devoices to **k** (Germanic).

**5. Sound changes:**
- Change A: `j` (palatal) → `g` (velar).
- Change B: `g` (voiced) → `k` (voiceless).

**6. Varṇamālā coordinates:**
- `j`: Row 2 (Palatal), Column 3 (Voiced Unaspirated).
- `g`: Row 1 (Velar), Column 3 (Voiced Unaspirated). *(Shift of row, preservation of column).*
- `k`: Row 1 (Velar), Column 1 (Voiceless Unaspirated). *(Shift of column, preservation of row).*

**7. Engineered inside Sanskrit:**
- **Pāṇini 8.2.30 (*coḥ kuḥ*)**: "A palatal (cu) is replaced by a velar (ku) when occurring at the end of a pada or before a jhal affix." 
  - *Example:* *vāc* → *vāk* (voice). 
  - *Source:* [Ashtadhyayi.com 8.2.30](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/8/2/30)
- **Pāṇini 8.4.55 (*khari ca*)**: "A jhal letter is replaced by a car (voiceless unaspirated) letter when followed by a khar letter."
  - *Example:* *dig* + *pāla* → *dikpāla* (guardian of direction).
  - *Source:* [Ashtadhyayi.com 8.4.55](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/8/4/55)

**8. Natural occurrence in Prakrit/Apabhraṃśa:**
- NOT FOUND directly for `j` → `g`. Prakrit tends to elide these consonants rather than harden them.

**9. Hypothetical step:**
Applying the internal Sanskrit sandhi rule (*coḥ kuḥ*) across language borders to explain the Latin/Greek velar forms.

**10. General Audience Suitability:**
**Perfect.** It clearly demonstrates how the "mysterious" shifts in Grimm's Law can be described using established rules already engineered into the Sanskrit phonetic grid.

## Finalist 2: सद् (sad) → sit / sedere (Sound Change Example)

**1. Sanskrit dhātu and traditional meaning:**
**सद् (sad)**. Dhātupāṭha definition: *ṣaḍlṛ viśaraṇagatyavasādaneṣu* (to sit, to sink, to be dejected).

**2. Sanskrit derivational chain:**
*sad* + *ana* → *sadana* (seat). *sīdati* (he sits).

**3. Conventional PIE chain:**
PIE `*sed-` (to sit) → Proto-Germanic `*sitjaną` → English *sit*. PIE `*sed-` → Greek *hedzomai*.
*Explanation:* PIE initial `*s-` regularly becomes aspiration `h-` in Greek. PIE voiced `*d` devoices to `*t` in Germanic.

**4. Proposed Sanskrit-to-receiving-language chain:**
The Sanskrit sibilant **s** degrades to breath **h** in Greek. The Sanskrit dental **d** devoices to **t** in Germanic.

**5. Sound changes:**
- Change A: `s` (dental sibilant) → `h` (glottal fricative/aspiration).
- Change B: `d` (dental voiced) → `t` (dental voiceless).

**6. Varṇamālā coordinates:**
- `s`: Sibilant (ūṣman), Dental.
- `h`: Sibilant (ūṣman), Glottal. *(Erosion of oral contact).*
- `d`: Row 4 (Dental), Column 3 (Voiced Unaspirated).
- `t`: Row 4 (Dental), Column 1 (Voiceless Unaspirated). *(Shift of column, preservation of row).*

**7. Engineered inside Sanskrit:**
- **Pāṇini 8.3.15 (*kharavasānayor visarjanīyaḥ*)**: "A r (ru) is replaced by a visarga (ḥ) when followed by a khar letter or a pause." This engineers the degradation of a sibilant into pure breath.
  - *Example:* *rāmas* → *rāmaḥ*.
  - *Source:* [Ashtadhyayi.com 8.3.15](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/8/3/15)
- **Pāṇini 8.4.55 (*khari ca*)**: Devoicing (as seen in Finalist 1).
  - *Example:* *tad* + *kāla* → *tatkāla*.

**8. Natural occurrence in Prakrit/Apabhraṃśa:**
- **Hemacandra 8.1.260 (*śasāṃ haḥ*)**: (ANALOGY) Hemacandra documents the natural decay of sibilants `ś` and `s` into `h` in various specific vocabulary words in Prakrit.
  - *Example:* *daśa* → *daha* (ten). 
  - *Source:* Siddha-Hema-Śabdānuśāsana Book 8.

**9. Hypothetical step:**
Mapping the Greek initial `h` directly to the Sanskrit `visarga` / decay mechanism.

**10. General Audience Suitability:**
**Excellent.** It demonstrates two different types of change: the physical erosion of a consonant into breath (s → h) and the mechanical devoicing of a stop (d → t), both mapped cleanly on the *varṇamālā*.

## Finalist 3: भृ (bhṛ) → bear / ferre (Appendix Example)

**1. Sanskrit dhātu and traditional meaning:**
**भृ (bhṛ)**. Dhātupāṭha definition: *ḍubhṛñ dhāraṇapoṣaṇayoḥ* (to hold, bear, maintain, nourish).

**2. Sanskrit derivational chain:**
*bhṛ* + *ti* → *bhṛti* (maintenance). *bhāra* (burden). *bibharti* (he bears).

**3. Conventional PIE chain:**
PIE `*bher-` (to carry, bear) → Proto-Germanic `*beraną` → English *bear*. PIE `*bher-` → Latin *ferre*, Greek *pherein*.
*Explanation:* PIE voiced aspirate `*bh` loses aspiration to become `b` in Germanic, and devoices to `ph` / `f` in Greek and Latin.

**4. Proposed Sanskrit-to-receiving-language chain:**
The Sanskrit voiced aspirate **bh** undergoes two distinct modifications in receiving languages: complete deaspiration to **b** (Germanic), and devoicing to **ph** (Greek) which frictionizes to **f** (Latin).

**5. Sound changes:**
- Change A: `bh` → `b` (loss of aspiration).
- Change B: `bh` → `ph` → `f` (loss of voicing, followed by fricatization).

**6. Varṇamālā coordinates:**
- `bh`: Row 5 (Labial), Column 4 (Voiced Aspirate).
- `b`: Row 5 (Labial), Column 3 (Voiced Unaspirated). *(Shift of column 4 to 3).*
- `ph`: Row 5 (Labial), Column 2 (Voiceless Aspirate). *(Shift of column 4 to 2).*

**7. Engineered inside Sanskrit:**
- **Pāṇini 8.4.53 (*jhalāṃ jaś jhaśi*)**: "A jhal letter is replaced by a jaś letter (voiced unaspirated) when followed by a jhaś letter." This engineers internal deaspiration (Grassmann's Law equivalent).
  - *Example:* *labh* + *dha* → *labdha*.
  - *Source:* [Ashtadhyayi.com 8.4.53](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/8/4/53)

**8. Natural occurrence in Prakrit/Apabhraṃśa:**
- **Hemacandra 8.1.187 (*kha-gha-tha-dha-bhām haḥ*)**: (ANALOGY) Documents the instability of aspirates, noting that the aspirate component often detaches and decays entirely into `ha`. 
  - *Example:* *sabhāva* → *sahāva*.
  - *Source:* Siddha-Hema-Śabdānuśāsana Book 8.

**9. Hypothetical step:**
Attributing the Latin `f` to a natural fricatization of the devoiced Sanskrit `ph`. 

**10. General Audience Suitability:**
**Good for Appendix.** The split trajectory (`bh` to `b` vs `bh` to `f`) makes it slightly more complex to visualize on a single axis than Finalist 1, but it perfectly illustrates the instability of Column 4 (Voiced Aspirate) phonemes.

## Final Recommendation
- **Best Introductory Example:** **जन् (jan)**. The shift from palatal to velar (`j` → `g` → `k`) maps perfectly to the *varṇamālā* columns and is explicitly engineered by Pāṇini (`coḥ kuḥ`), requiring zero theoretical leaps.
- **Best Second Example:** **सद् (sad)**. It introduces a different mechanism (degradation of a sibilant to breath) which is universally relatable and easily demonstrated by Hemacandra's Prakrit rules.
- **Appendix Examples:** **भृ (bhṛ)** and **पद् (pad)**. Both require slightly more technical explanation regarding fricatization (Grimm's Law) but remain exceptionally strong semantic and structural matches.
- **Rejection:** **गम् (gam) → come**. Do not use this. The PIE explanation relies on a highly speculative labiovelar (`*gʷem-`) which complicates the visual comparison and distracts from the clean elegance of the *varṇamālā* matrix.
