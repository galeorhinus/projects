# Manuscript Language Audit — 2026-09-04

## Purpose

This audit looks for prose that is grammatically correct but sounds unnatural, compressed, abstract, repetitive, or unnecessarily academic. It follows the standard established by the September 2026 Epilogue revision: name the concrete subject, explain the action, and make each causal step available to an intelligent reader who has no specialist training.

This is a language audit, not an argument rewrite. It does not reopen the book's claims, evidence, structure, or polemical position.

## Rules

- Preserve the author's argument, rhythm, deliberate repetition, and force.
- Record the current wording before proposing a replacement.
- Explain why the current language interrupts comprehension.
- Do not alter manuscript prose during the audit.
- Do not turn confident body prose into legal or academic qualification.
- Do not use a banned word merely to replace another awkward phrase.
- Prefer complete explanations over compressed slogans.
- Leave technically precise prose alone when simplification would remove meaning.

## Finding Format

Each finding records:

- **Location:** clickable manuscript reference.
- **Action:** `REPHRASE`, `EXPLAIN`, `JOIN`, `SPLIT`, or `REMOVE`.
- **Current:** exact saved wording.
- **Proposed:** replacement wording.
- **Reason:** the specific language problem.

No proposal is applied until the author approves it.
