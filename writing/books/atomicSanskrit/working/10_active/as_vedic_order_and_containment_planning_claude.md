## 0. Why both moves, and why now

The author's own diagnosis, verbatim from the working conversation:

> "the vedas are the highest eternal layer and have an abstracted idea of sat / we then say that in every variant time frame - that the continuum delves deeper and in mahabharata sat is described as [...] so we use that in ch0 itself and part intro refers back to it / we use sat and the different rc, rg, rt etc and discuss vedic order / ch1 - then has a parallel section where we introduce the asuric tendencies - and introduce containment thoroughly."

Two consequences follow directly:

1. **सत् is currently asserted, not earned.** The Part I opener (`as_part_01_wrong_metaphor.md:31`) hands the reader सत्-असत्-विवेक cold, backed only by the Mahābhārata's *bhūtahitam* line. The reader has not yet been shown *why* सत् means what it means. Chapter 0 is the right place to earn it, because Chapter 0's job is exactly this: establish vocabulary and character before the prosecutorial turn begins.
2. **"Architecture of containment" is currently asserted at Chapter 3, not earned.** Chapter 1 already runs an entire chapter on asuric tendencies (§1.2 *vikṛti*, §1.4 the ten Purāṇic operations) but never names containment as such — the word appears only as scattered ordinary English (*enclosure*, *concealment*) inside the apex-pyramid description. The formal four-sense apparatus (*concealment / blockade / quarantine / enclosure* under the *architecture of containment* umbrella, per CLAUDE.md) is not named until Ch3 §3.5. The same earn-early-deploy-late shape that fixes सत् should fix containment.

**Correction logged 2026-08-04, after the author flagged the first draft of this section.** The first draft of this doc framed the Veda as holding a "pre-ethical, structural" सत् that the Mahābhārata later "operationalized" into an ethical test. The author corrected this directly: **सत् was and always will be an ethical test.** Human consciousness itself runs on सत्-असत् discernment — it is not a physics-like structural fact that acquires ethical content only at a later or more worldly stage. The correct frame is not depth-vs-surface or structural-vs-ethical. It is the book's own existing two-domain vocabulary: **वैदिक सत्** and **लौकिक सत्** — the same ethical सत्, active in both domains, exactly as the book already treats *saṃskṛta* itself (Ch0 §0.4: the *vaidika* domain keeps language and content invariant; the *laukika* domain applies the same invariant language to a changing world). The Mahābhārata's *bhūtahitam* line is not a deeper specification of an eternal abstraction — it is **लौकिक सत्**: the same सत् doing its ordinary ethical work inside the changing, worldly domain. Chapter 0 should show **वैदिक सत्** through examples drawn from the Veda itself, and Chapter 1 should show **वैदिक असत्** through the same kind of Vedic examples — the two chapters become a matched pair rather than a "structure then ethics" progression. **Draft language must never suggest सत् becomes ethical only in the Mahābhārata, or that the Vedic सत् is somehow pre-moral. Both domains carry the full ethical weight; they differ in where they operate, not in what they are.**

**Second correction logged 2026-08-04 (same day, later pass).** The author flagged three more things directly: (1) "cosmic order" is banned — it names nothing and must not appear in the manuscript; the correct term is **सनातनी सात्त्विक order** (*sanātanī sāttvika* — eternal because सनातन, of सत्'s own nature because सात्त्विक); (2) Ch0 introduces the Veda as calibrant in **two** registers, not one — calibrant for language (already established) and calibrant for **architecture and order** (new, carried by ऋत); (3) asuric battles are always about power and powerful beings, while सानातन battles — including the Vedic ones — are about सत्-असत्, never about power as such. All three are folded into A.1 and A.3 below, and (3) is also pulled forward into Part B as the new general principle Ch1 introduces and Ch3 §3.6 proves.

## PART A — Chapter 0: सत्, ऋत, and the Vedic Order

### A.1 वैदिक सत् and लौकिक सत् — one ethical fact, two domains

- **वैदिक सत् (Veda, invariant domain).** सत् is the ethical fact that a conscious being can align with ऋत — the track, the **सनातनी सात्त्विक order** ("cosmic order" is banned; it names nothing) — or produce friction against it (असत्). This is not a structural fact awaiting ethical content; it is already the discernment human consciousness runs on, shown at its most invariant register. The ऋत-family (ऋत, ऋजु, ऋच्/ऋग्, ऋषि) makes the mechanism visible: alignment, straightness, the verse that carries alignment, the seer who perceives it. Ch0 should ground this with **examples of सत् actually at work in the Veda itself** — see A.3B below — not only the etymology.
- **लौकिक सत् (Mahābhārata, changing-world domain).** The same सत्, the same ethical fact, doing its ordinary work inside **लौकिक**, the domain where content and application change even though the underlying invariant does not — exactly as Ch0 §0.4 already treats *saṃskṛta* itself. **यद् भूतहितम् अत्यन्तं तत् सत्यम्** — "that which fully and ultimately serves the welfare of living beings is held to be truth" (Vana Parva; endnote `satyam-bhutahitam-mahabharata` already in the book) is सत् applied as a test a person can run on a specific, worldly, situational claim.
- **The calibrant, in two registers.** Ch0 §0.5 ("Are the Vedas Archaic?") already establishes the Veda as calibrant for **language** — `as_1_00_seekers.md:133` ("the Vedas are the calibrant. Every recitation is measured against the received form"), `:169` ("the invariant calibrant against which the turning world is measured"). The claim is fully argued later, at **Ch18 §18.5** (`as_1_18_wrong_question.md:204, 229` — the Vedas as primary calibrant, the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* as Pāṇini's compressed working calibrant for *laukika* Sanskrit) and anchored precisely in `as_endnotes.md:5271` and `reference/as_calibration_hierarchy.md`. §0.8 already implies a second register without stating it ("Sanskrit operates as a calibrant through a fractal architecture, in which the same organizing principle recurs across scale"). §0.6B should make that second register explicit: the Veda is *also* calibrant for **architecture and order** — not sound and word measured against drift, but action measured against friction. ऋत is what that second calibration measures. This is a formalization of something the chapter already implies, not a new claim grafted on — and it should sit *after* §0.5's language-calibrant claim, echoing its exact phrasing ("X is the calibrant... measured against Y") so the parallel is visible on the page.
- **सात्त्विक order — the name, and why coining it is on-thesis (author decision, 2026-08-04).** The Vedic order is named the **सात्त्विक order** ("cosmic order" banned). The book is transparent that the Vedas *do not use the word सात्त्विक* — and that transparency is the point. The term is derived by **Sanskrit's own generativity**: सत् → सत्त्व (*sattva*, "sat-ness," via the *-tva* abstract suffix) → सात्त्विक (*sāttvika*, "pertaining to sat-ness," via *vṛddhi* + *-ika*). Naming the Vedic order सात्त्विक is therefore not reading a word into the Veda; it is *using the language's generative capacity to name what the Veda demonstrably enacts* — a live micro-demonstration of the book's central generativity thesis, right in Ch0. It also connects to vocabulary the book already runs: Ch3 §3.1 (`as_1_03_strategic.md:81`) uses सत्त्व/तमस् as the *kālacakra*'s own pair, and CLAUDE.md anchors *tamas* as *asuratva*'s substrate — so सात्त्विक order (सत्-side) vs. तमस्/asuric (असत्-side) is the missing other half of an opposition the book already half-states.
  - **The sharp risk to build the framing around:** installing a word "behind" a text is *exactly* the pyramid's move the book prosecutes (e.g., planting reconstructed *h₂ḿ̥suros* behind *asura*, Ch3 §3.6). The book must make the distinction airtight, or a hostile reader will call it the same crime: the pyramid installs an **unattested reconstruction** and claims it is the Veda's **source**; the book coins a **transparent generative derivative** and claims it openly as **the book's own naming**, not the Veda's, licensed by the very generativity the book documents. Per the coinage doctrine (CLAUDE.md, updated earlier this session), the book *owns its original coinages out loud* — so this is stated confidently, not hedged, but the "the Vedas don't use it; the book derives it" transparency is non-negotiable.
  - **Deployment (author's steer):** a *slight mention in prose* (one clause flagging सात्त्विक as the book's generatively-derived name) + the **full defense in an endnote** — the etymology, the Vedas-don't-use-it acknowledgment, and the distinction-from-the-pyramid's-reconstruction. New endnote needed; proposed stub `sattvika-order-generative-coinage`.
  - **Still open:** whether सात्त्विक stands alone or keeps the fuller **सनातनी सात्त्विक** (सनातनी = eternal); and the exact grammatical/gender form (treated for now as an English-adjective loan, "the sāttvika order," on the *asuric* pattern).
- **Attributive naming — the order has many names because Sanskrit names by attribute, not by label (author principle, 2026-08-04).** Sanskrit is *attributive rather than nominative*: an object is defined by its attributes, not fixed to a single name. The classic illustration is water — its "many names" (*āpaḥ, jala, vāri, salila, udaka, toya*…) are not competing labels but attributes, each disclosing water by a different quality. **This principle already has a home in the manuscript, three sections upstream of §0.6B:** Ch0 §0.3 (`as_1_00_seekers.md:85`) already states it — *"Sanskrit trains the ear to hear a proper name as an attribute... Sanskrit labels the world and discloses its structure in the same stroke"* — with endnote `sanskrit-names-as-attributes` and the देव example (*deva* from *dā*/*dīp*/*dyut*, Nirukta 7.15). **Consequence for §0.6B:** the order needs no single name. ऋत (the track it runs on), सात्त्विक (its rooting in सत्), सनातनी (its permanence), *Vedic order* — each names the same order by a different true attribute. This turns the "three names crowding" concern into a *payoff of §0.3*: the reader was taught attributive naming three sections earlier and now watches it do real work. The देव example lives at **Ch0 §0.3 (`as_1_00_seekers.md:85-87`)** — it *is* the illustration where §0.3 establishes the attributive principle (a second, unrelated देव derivation, the *div→deva* gaṇa table, sits at App Part 7 `as_3_07_vedic_carrier.md:96`). **The water example is NOT redundant with §0.3's देव — they demonstrate different structures:** §0.3's देव = *one word → many verbal derivations* (dā/dīp/dyut), the नामान्याख्यातजानि / derivation direction; the water example = *one object → many attribute-synonyms* (āpaḥ, jala, vāri, salila, udaka…), the naming direction. **The "order has many names" point needs the second structure**, which देव structurally cannot supply.

**Placement decision (author, 2026-08-04):** the attributive-vs-nominative framing + the water example go into **§0.3 (the earlier section)**, strengthening the principle already there; **§0.6B then *directly deploys* सात्त्विक order** as a fait accompli, leaning on §0.3 rather than re-arguing. This is a new manuscript edit to §0.3 — logged in A.4 and the impact map.

**0.7 Sat, Ṛta, and the Vedic Order**

The Vedas act as a calibrant for both Sanskrit and *saṃskṛti*. On the linguistic level, this means what §0.5 already demonstrated: an unbroken system where every spoken syllable can be measured against the invariant vedic form. The rest of this book demonstrates how the atomic, generative machinery of Sanskrit is perfectly preserved within that Vedic standard.

On the civilizational level, the Vedas serve as the calibrant for order itself, allowing action in the changing world in the *laukika* domain against the invariant trajectory of ऋत (*ṛta*).

Most English translations render ऋत (*ṛta*) as “cosmic order.” The phrase makes ऋत (*ṛta*) sound like the default order that already exists throughout the universe. But how can harmony be the default when असत् (*asat*) and the असुराः (*asurāḥ*) also exist and act upon the world? The Vedas say something far more consequential: ऋत (*ṛta*) had to be created, upheld, and defended.

> **देवानां पूर्व्ये युगेऽसतः सदजायत**
> *devānāṃ pūrvye yuge 'sataḥ sad ajāyata*
> “In the earlier age of the *devāḥ*, *sat* was born from **असत् (*asat*)**.” (RV 10.72.2)[NOTE: rv-10-72-2-sat-born-from-asat]

Like Sanskrit, **सत् (*sat*)** and the **ऋत (*ṛta*)** built upon it did not appear automatically. They were deliberately brought into being, and had to be actively protected against the rival order the asuric hand builds upon **असत् (*asat*)**: an architecture of containment, possession, and imposed power. The Vedic order is therefore neither inevitable nor self-sustaining. Each generation must choose it, build through it, and defend it.

A second hymn pairs ऋत (*ṛta*) with **सत्यम् (*satyam*)** — सत् (*sat*) expressed as truth (सत् + य) — at the same origin: **ऋतं च सत्यं चाभीद्धात्तपसोऽध्यजायत** (*ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhy ajāyata*) — "from *tapas* kindled to its height, *ṛta* and *satya* were born." (RV 10.190.1)[NOTE: rv-10-190-1-rta-satya-cocreated] The two arise together, of one heat, in one act; ऋत (*ṛta*) is no synonym for सत्यम् (*satyam*) but its companion at the source. Two verses later, in the same short hymn, the sequence completes: **सूर्याचन्द्रमसौ धाता यथापूर्वमकल्पयत्** (*sūryācandramasau dhātā yathāpūrvam akalpayat*) — "Dhātar formed Sun and Moon in due order." (RV 10.190.3) ऋत (*ṛta*) and सत्यम् (*satyam*) are born first; the Sun and Moon follow, in the same act of ordering.

ऋत (*ṛta*) comes from the atom ⟪ऋ⟫ (*ṛ*), to go, closed by the past participle **क्त (*kta*)**, which surfaces as -त: that which has moved, the course laid down. The universe the Veda describes is in motion, and ऋत (*ṛta*) is the path it can move along — a trajectory when followed brings harmony.


Three related words describe how **ऋत (*ṛta*)** becomes movement, speech, and perception. **ऋजु (*ṛju*)** is straight movement along the track; its Vedic opposite is **वृजिन (*vṛjina*)**, the crooked (RV 7.104.13).[NOTE: rv-7-104-12-13-sat-asat-vrjina-soma] The **ऋच् (*ṛc*)** — voiced as **ऋग् (*ṛg*)** before a voiced sound — is the verse that renders that order as measured speech. The **ऋषि (*ṛṣi*)** sees **ऋत (*ṛta*)** and sets it into verse; Yāska explains the word through seeing: *ṛṣir darśanāt*.

The Veda performs the discernment between सत् (*sat*) and असत् (*asat*) within the mantra itself: **सच्चासच्च वचसी पस्पृधाते । तयोर्यत्सत्यं यतरदृजीयः । तदित्सोमोऽवति हन्त्यासत्** (*sac cāsac ca vacasī paspṛdhāte, tayor yat satyaṃ yatarad ṛjīyaḥ, tad it somo 'vati hanty āsat*) — "*sat* and *asat* contend, word against word; of the two, whichever is true and straight, *ṛjīyaḥ*, Soma protects, and the *asat* he destroys." (RV 7.104.12)[NOTE: rv-7-104-12-13-sat-asat-vrjina-soma] Soma protects the straight and destroys the crooked, within the verse itself.

The protagonists bear सत् (*sat*) in their titles. Indra's is **सत्पति (*satpati*)**, the lord of *sat*.[NOTE: protagonist-sat-epithets]

सत् (*sat*) is flow: circulation kept open, release along that path — the path of **स्वस्ति (*svasti*)**, well-being, followed as the Sun and Moon follow their orbits: **स्वस्ति पन्थामनु चरेम सूर्याचन्द्रमसाविव** (*svasti panthām anu carema sūryācandramasāv iva*) — "may we follow the path of *svasti*, as the Sun and Moon do." (RV 5.51.15)[NOTE: rv-5-51-15-svasti-panthanam] This is the swastika. असत् (*asat*) is what came first: the prior condition of non-flow, out of which सत् (*sat*) was created (RV 10.72.2).

**ऋत (*ṛta*)** is the Veda's own word for this order of flow. The book adds two descriptors, each earned by what the order is: because it is eternal, the **सनातन (*sanātana*) order**; because it embodies सत् (*sat*), the **सात्त्विक (*sāttvika*) order**, the order of *sat*-ness.[NOTE: sattvika-order-generative-coinage] One order under several true names — Sanskrit working by attribute, not by fixed label, as §0.3 showed with water.

There's a fundamental asymmetry in the two orders. असत् (*asat*) aims for possession of power — who commands, who controls access, who monopolizes sovereignty. सत् (*sat*), on the other hand, seeks distributed order — circulation that keeps the gates of containment open. Sanātan does not hold a negative view of power; Indra wields the vajra. It judges power by what the action serves — Indra releases what Vṛtra withholds, and it is the action of releasing, not the strength, that differentiates सत् (*sat*) from असत् (*asat*).

This differentiation is **सत्-असत्-विवेक (*sat-asat-viveka*)**, and the Veda performs it verse by verse. It runs in the *laukika* domain as fully as in the *vaidika*, where circumstance changes and the discipline does not. The Mahābhārata states its worldly test: **यद्भूतहितमत्यन्तं तत्सत्यम्** (*yad bhūta-hitam atyantaṃ tat satyam*) — "that which fully and finally serves the welfare of living beings is सत् (*sat*)."[NOTE: satyam-bhutahitam-mahabharata]

The Veda preserves the invariant distinction; the *laukika* domain applies it to a changing world.

Chapter 1 follows असत् (*asat*) from cosmic condition into specific action: concealment, blockade, quarantine, enclosure — the architecture of containment, the pyramid.

## Calibrant / Calibrand framework (BOOK-WIDE; settled with Codex 2026-08-04)

*Emerged from the §0.6B calibrant discussion (A.1) but is book-wide — deployment spans Ch6, Ch16, Ch19, the glossary, and the PIE article, not Ch0/Ch1. Captured here so it isn't lost; likely wants its own deployment doc (offered). §0.6B itself is unaffected: it keeps "The Vedas are a calibrant for both Sanskrit and* saṃskṛti*"; the calibrand machinery lands downstream.*

**Three independent axes** (replaces the earlier designed-vs-drifting binary, which wrongly conflated architecture with behavior):
1. **Relational role:** calibrant / calibrand — *and this is orthogonal to prakṛti/saṃskṛti/vikṛti.* A calibrand can be saṃskṛta or prākṛta.
2. **Mode of calibration:** designed / contact.
3. **Behavior after contact:** anchored-orbital / drifting.

**Resulting terms:**
- ***Laukika* Sanskrit** — **designed calibrand** (saṃskṛta; calibrates against the Vedas by architecture; stays invariant).
- **Marathi, Hindi** — **contact calibrands that remain orbital** (prākṛta; Sanskritic gravity holds them).
- **Greek, Latin, Persian** — **contact calibrands whose reflections drifted** (prākṛta; far cognates). In the PIE argument use **contact calibrand** / **external calibrand**, never bare *calibrand*, to keep the demolition set unmistakable.

**The vṛjina/vikṛti correction (Codex, protecting Ch1 §1.2 doctrine):** natural non-calibration is NOT crooked. A calibrand can stay aligned, orbit, or drift after contact — all harmless. **Drift becomes वृजिन (*vṛjina*) only when an actor bends a form away from balance, toward *vikṛti*.** Deformation needs a deformer. The earlier thesis draft wrongly equated "refuses calibration" with "crooked"; that contradicted the book's *nastika*/*prākṛtika*-can-be-harmonious distinction.

**Precisification (Claude, on top of Codex — proposed):** *vikṛti is not a calibrand at all.* It does not align to the true calibrant; it **erects a false one** (the apex; PIE) and aligns to that. So the pyramid can never be mis-called a calibrand, and the framework ties to the PIE argument at the root: **PIE is a counterfeit calibrant; "reconstruction" is calibration against a fake standard.**

**PIE is *āsurī māyā* (author, 2026-08-04).** The counterfeit calibrant is not neutral illusion — it is ***āsurī māyā***, the asuric misuse of the measuring-and-forming power. The precision matters and stays consistent with the book's dual-*māyā* doctrine: *māyā* itself is the legitimate power to measure and form — Varuṇa wields it for *lokakṣema* (RV 8.42.1, already in Ch3 §3.7), and a calibrant is exactly a measure. PIE mimics that measuring function but as its asuric counterfeit — a false standard that conceals the true one. This is the same *āsurī māyā* theft Ch2 §2.1 develops and the Part I subtitle already carries, now given its sharpest single instance. It binds the framework to the eclipse: PIE is the *māyā* laid over the Sūrya (Sanskrit / the calibrant); clearing the *māyā* — as Atri clears Svarbhānu's in RV 5.40.6 — reveals the calibrant that was never gone. It also connects to the A.3E constellation: the antagonists are *māyin* (Vṛtra *māyāvinam*, Namuci *māyinam*, Svarbhānu's *māyā*), and PIE is the *māyin* move at the scale of a whole discipline. **Say *āsurī māyā*, not bare *māyā*** — bare *māyā* would wrongly dignify PIE with the legitimate measuring-power.
