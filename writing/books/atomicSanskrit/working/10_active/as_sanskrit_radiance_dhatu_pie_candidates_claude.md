## Two load-bearing caveats (read first)

**1. The Hemacandra / Prakrit leg is NOT FOUND for the operations this method needs — and worse, the documented Prakrit direction is the *opposite*.** No exact Siddha-Hema sūtra teaches j→g/k (fortition) or bh→b (de-aspiration to a plain stop). Confirmed 3-0. The changes Hemacandra *does* document run the other way: intervocalic **lenition/voicing** (voiceless stops soften, e.g. k→g, t→d, p→v — and vowels/glides elide) and aspirate **debuccalization** (bh→h). A claim that Book 8 documents systematic voicing usable here was refuted 0-3. **Consequence:** for jan and bhṛ the Hemacandra column is **NOT FOUND**; at most it is **ANALOGY** for the bare fact that Prakrit alters consonants. The natural-change leg should probably be *dropped* for fortition/devoicing cases; the method stands on the engineered Pāṇinian leg alone there.

**2. The nirvacana / vyutpatti method is validated as *semantic* derivation, not historical reconstruction.** Yāska's *nāmāny ākhyātajāni* (nouns arise from verbal roots) and the fivefold method are a disciplined *vyākaraṇa* tradition (confirmed; Kahrs 2-1) — **but Kahrs explicitly distinguishes nirvacana from "a primitive forerunner of historical linguistics."** The equation of nirvacana's *ādeśa/sthānin* substitution with the task's sound-operations was refuted 0-3. **Consequence:** the method licenses deriving Sanskrit words from *dhātavaḥ* semantically *within Sanskrit*. It does **not**, by itself, license a genetic Sanskrit→PIE claim. The **D layer below is a proposal, never a conclusion.**

## Finalists — detailed analysis

### Finalist 1 — भृ (bhṛ) · the best introductory case

1. **Dhātu & traditional meaning.** भृ (bhṛ), Dhātupāṭha class 1 (भरति *bharati*) and class 3 (बिभर्ति *bibharti*). Monier-Williams artha: *to bear, carry, convey, hold; support, maintain, cherish, foster, nourish.* ✅ (wisdomlib/MW).
2. **Sanskrit derivational chain.** भृ → भरति (bears) · भार (bhāra, load) · भर्तृ (bhartṛ, bearer→husband) · भरण (bharaṇa, maintenance) · संभार, भरत. Standard *kṛt*/*taddhita* derivation.
3. **Conventional PIE chain (C).** *bʰer- "to bear, carry"* (Wiktionary reconstruction ✅) → Skt bhṛ, Gk φέρω, Lat ferō, Goth baíran/Eng bear, OCS brati, Av bar-. Sanskrit bh- is treated as the **reflex** of PIE *bʰ, not the source.
4. **Proposed Sanskrit→receiving chain (D — hypothesis).** Sanskrit भृ as the engineered atom; the receiving languages carry a drifted labial: Gk ph-, Lat f-, Gmc b-. *Direction not established by any evidence here.*
5. **Every sound change, separately.**
   - **bh → ph** (Greek φ): voiced aspirate → voiceless aspirate (loses voice, keeps aspiration).
   - **ph → f** (Latin): aspirated stop → fricative (spirantization).
   - **bh → b** (Germanic *bear*): loses aspiration (Grimm's Law path).
6. **Varṇamālā coordinates.** भ (bh) = **oṣṭhya (labial), voiced mahāprāṇa** → फ (ph) = **oṣṭhya, voiceless mahāprāṇa** (row-shift only) → **f** = **labiodental fricative, outside the varṇamālā** (the open *oṣṭhya-ūṣman* coordinate — see Ch9's two open coordinates). All movement stays in the **labial column**.
7. **Engineered inside Sanskrit (A).** **Pāṇini 8.4.55 खरि च** (*khari ca*) ✅ — before a voiceless (*khar*) sound a consonant takes its voiceless (*car*) counterpart. Worked example **लभ् → लप्स्यति** (*labh → lapsyati*, bh→p). *(Cite the worked example, not the abstract "devoicing-and-deaspiration" gloss, which verified 1-2.)* The labial-field bh↔p machinery is thus documented *inside* Sanskrit.
8. **Natural in Prakrit/Apabhraṃśa (B).** **NOT FOUND / opposite.** Prakrit debuccalizes aspirates: **bh → h** (then often elided). This does **not** support bh→b/ph. Do not present a Hemacandra parallel here.
9. **Hypothetical step.** The *direction* (Sanskrit as source vs. shared descent) — undecided. The bh→ph→f *relationship* is documented (A + Grimm); the *priority* is the D-claim, unproven.
10. **Works in a general-audience book?** **Yes — the lead case.** One column (labial), one intuitive move (voice/aspiration/friction), a household English word (*bear*), and a real in-Sanskrit rule. Present A + C honestly; frame D as the open question.

### Finalist 2 — जन् (jan) · best second case (a different kind of change)

1. **Dhātu & meaning.** जन् (jan), *to be born, come into being, beget, arise* ✅ (Wiktionary lists जन्/जनति/जन्तु under the root).
2. **Sanskrit chain.** जन् → जनति/जायते · जन्तु (creature) · जन (people) · जननी (mother) · जाति (birth, kind) · जन्मन् (birth) · प्रजा (progeny).
3. **Conventional PIE chain (C).** *ǵenh₁- "beget, give birth"* (gloss ⚠ unverified-infra) → Gk génos/gígnomai, Lat genus/gignō/nātus, Eng kin/kind/king. **Sanskrit j- is the satem reflex of palatal *ǵ; Greek/Latin g- is the centum reflex** — i.e. the conventional account explains Skt *j* vs Gk/Lat *g* by the **centum-satem split of *ǵ**, not by a Sanskrit-internal j→g.
4. **Proposed chain (D — hypothesis).** Sanskrit जन् (with palatal j) as the engineered atom; the velar g of Greek/Latin as a drift of the palatal. *The centum-satem framing is the conventional rival; do not assert priority.*
5. **Every sound change, separately.**
   - **j → g**: palatal voiced → velar voiced (column shift, voice preserved).
   - **g → k**: velar voiced → velar voiceless (devoicing) — the Germanic *kin/kind* path (Grimm).
6. **Varṇamālā coordinates.** ज (j) = **tālavya (palatal), voiced** → ग (g) = **kaṇṭhya (velar), voiced** (column shift, same manner/voice) → क (k) = **kaṇṭhya (velar), voiceless** (row shift within velar). Two clean, adjacent moves.
7. **Engineered inside Sanskrit (A).** **8.2.30 चोः कुः** (*coḥ kuḥ*) ✅ — a palatal (*cu*: c/ch/j/jh) is replaced by the corresponding velar (*ku*: k/kh/g/gh) before a *jhal* or word-finally; then **8.4.55 खरि च** devoices. Worked example **भज् → भक्त** (*bhaj → bhakta*: j → g → k), filed under the velar row. **Sanskrit's own grammar contains the j↔g↔k machinery.** This is the strongest A in the set.
8. **Natural in Prakrit/Apabhraṃśa (B).** **NOT FOUND.** No j→g/k rule in Hemacandra; Prakrit lenites/elides intervocalic j. Opposite direction.
9. **Hypothetical step.** The Germanic *kin ← *kunją* ← *ǵ* devoicing step is ⚠ unverified (infra error); and the *direction* (Sanskrit j as source vs. satem reflex) is the unproven D-claim.
10. **Works in the body?** **Yes, as the second example** — it shows a *different* change type (palatal→velar + devoicing) than bhṛ's labial move, and भज्→भक्त is a real, showable Sanskrit derivation. **But** you must engage the centum-satem account honestly, or a linguist dismisses it in one line.

### Finalist 3 — पद् (pad) · appendix showcase (a third change type)

1. **Dhātu & meaning.** पद् (pad), *go, move; foot* (Dhātupāṭha ⚠ verify entry).
2. **Sanskrit chain.** पद् → पद (foot, step) · पाद (foot) · पद्धति (path) · पदाति (foot-soldier).
3. **Conventional PIE chain (C).** *pod-/*ped- "foot"* ⚠(textbook-secure, not fetched) → Gk poús/podós, Lat pēs/pedis, Goth fōtus/Eng foot. **The single most-cited IE cognate set.**
4. **Proposed chain (D — hypothesis).** Sanskrit pad/pāda as the atom; the receiving languages preserve p- (Gk/Lat) or drift p→f (Germanic).
5. **Every sound change, separately.** **p → f** (Germanic only): voiceless stop → voiceless fricative.
6. **Varṇamālā coordinates.** प (p) = **oṣṭhya, voiceless alpaprāṇa** → **f** = **labiodental fricative, outside the varṇamālā** (again the open *oṣṭhya-ūṣman* coordinate). Labial column throughout.
7. **Engineered inside Sanskrit (A).** **None.** Sanskrit has no p→f operation — f is not in the varṇamālā. *This is honest and important:* the change is **not** a Sanskrit operation.
8. **Natural change (B).** The p→f is **Grimm's Law in Germanic** — a *receiving-language* drift, exactly the "vivimorphosis" the book already describes (an engineered form re-organicizing in the host). Not a Prakrit change; **NF** for Hemacandra.
9. **Hypothetical step.** None on the sound side (Grimm is textbook); the *direction/priority* is the D-claim.
10. **Works in the body?** **Appendix, not body.** It's the cleanest Grimm example (foot/pēs/pous), but the sound change lives entirely in the *receiving* language (Germanic), so it illustrates vivimorphosis rather than a Sanskrit operation — better placed where Grimm/contact-drift is discussed.

## Final recommendation

- **Best introductory example → भृ (bhṛ).** One labial column, an intuitive voice/aspiration/friction move, a household word (*bear*), and a real in-Sanskrit rule (8.4.55, लभ्→लप्स्यति). Lowest cognitive load, highest verification.
- **Best second example (different change type) → जन् (jan).** Palatal→velar + devoicing, with भज्→भक्त showing the machinery *inside* Sanskrit (8.2.30 + 8.4.55). Must be paired with an honest engagement of the centum-satem account.
- **Two appendix-only → पद् (pad)** (Grimm p→f — the change is in the receiving language, so it demonstrates vivimorphosis, not a Sanskrit op) **and स्था (sthā)** (impeccable PIE side *steh₂-*, and the laryngeal does **not** disqualify it — that objection was refuted 1-2 — but s-mobile/cluster issues and no clean Sanskrit-internal op keep it out of the body).
- **Reject despite superficial similarity → गम् (gam)** — the labiovelar *gʷ scatters into b/v/k across branches (several disputed intermediate forms), violating the "no complicated intermediates" rule. **Also flag कृ (kṛ)** for rejection despite its centrality to the book: its non-Indic cognate reach appears thin (mostly Indo-Iranian + Balto-Slavic), likely failing "≥2 useful non-Indic branches" — ⚠ verify before relying on it.

**The honest through-line for the book.** What this evidence *does* establish is not Sanskrit priority but a **reframe**: the sound relationships the comparative method attributes to PIE divergence (j~g, bh~p) are **also documented as operations inside Sanskrit's own engineered grammar** (भज्→भक्त, लभ्→लप्स्यति). So the cross-language correspondences do not *require* an external ancestor to have the shapes they do — Sanskrit's documented operations plus ordinary drift in the receiving languages are a live alternative account. That is the **calibration-vs-reconstruction** contrast, applied to sound. It is a competing *explanation of the same data*, presented as such (D = proposal), **not** a proven descent claim — and the nirvacana method, being semantic, cannot be stretched to prove the genetic direction.
