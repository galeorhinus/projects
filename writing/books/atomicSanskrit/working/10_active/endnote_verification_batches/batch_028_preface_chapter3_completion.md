## Approved Manuscript Corrections

### 1. Chapter 0 — udaka

**Before:** “उदक (*udaka*) comes from उद् (*ud*) and ⟪अञ्च्⟫ (*añc*), to go forth: water as what springs and flows.”

**After:** “उदक (*udaka*) comes from ⟪उन्द्⟫ (*und*), to wet or moisten: water as that which wets.”

**Reason:** The current derivation is unsupported. Traditional lexical material gives **उन्दो क्लेदने** and derives *udaka* from the wetting or moistening root.

### 2. Chapter 1 — Boden Chair

**Before:** “The Boden Chair of Sanskrit at Oxford, endowed in 1832, made the pattern literal: Joseph Boden's will chartered the chair explicitly to convert Indians to Christianity through the agency of the Sanskrit-literate. Horace Hayman Wilson held the chair from 1832 to 1860; Monier Monier-Williams won the following election in 1860 against Max Müller himself, the evangelical charter deciding the contest in Williams's favor. The chair's first two holders produced Sanskrit-English dictionaries that remain widely used references.”

**After:** “The Boden Chair of Sanskrit at Oxford, established in 1832, made the pattern literal: Joseph Boden endowed the chair for the explicit purpose of converting Indians to Christianity through Sanskrit. Horace Hayman Wilson held the chair from 1832 to 1860. Monier Monier-Williams emphasized that purpose during the next election and defeated Max Müller in 1860. Both men produced Sanskrit-English dictionaries; the expanded Monier-Williams dictionary remains widely used.”

**Reason:** Boden's wish was expressed in his bequest, not enacted as a continuing university charter. The source also does not support treating both dictionaries as widely used today.

### 3. Chapter 2 — lord

**Before:** “Old English **hlāfweard**, the ‘bread-guardian,’ became **laverd**, then **lorde**, then modern **Lord**.”

**After:** “Old English **hlāfweard**, the ‘bread-guardian,’ contracted to **hlāford**. Middle English then recorded changing forms such as **laverd**, **loverd**, and **lorde**, before modern **Lord** became standard.”

**Reason:** The current sentence presents overlapping Middle English variants as a strict chronology.

### 4. Chapter 2 — Schleicher

**Before:** “In the 1860s, August Schleicher turned this process into the family tree of language.”

**After:** “In 1853, August Schleicher drew this process as a family tree. He developed the model through the following decade.”

**Reason:** Schleicher's first printed language-family tree appeared in 1853.
