# Endnote Verification Batch 043 — When Script Changes the Language

**Audit date:** 2026-09-11
**Scope:** The two new Chapter 13 §13.3 endnotes supporting English spelling pronunciation and the Indic treatment of recitation from writing.

## Results

| Endnote | Risk | Result | Finding and action |
|---|:---:|---|---|
| `writing-changes-english-pronunciation` | P0 | Verified | Deterding and Mohamad document all three English examples used in the body. *Forehead* and *often* recovered sounds under the influence of their spelling; *fault* acquired a pronounced **l** after the letter entered its spelling through Latin association. The body distinguishes these histories while using them to establish the same script-to-speech direction of change. |
| `paniniya-shiksha-likhitapathaka` | P0 | Verified | The Sanskrit Documents edition confirms **लिखितपाठकः (*likhitapāṭhakaḥ*)** in *Pāṇinīya Śikṣā* 32, PDF p. 4. The Wilbour Hall electronic *Mahābhāṣya* confirms Patañjali's protection statement at `(Pas_3)`, corresponding to Kielhorn-Abhyankar vol. I, pp. 1.14–2.2. The body does not claim that either work states a general theory of writing-induced language change. |

## Digital Evidence Records

The three sources are registered as `deterding-mohamad-spelling-pronunciation-2017`, `paniniya-shiksha-32-likhitapathaka`, and `patanjali-mahabhashya-raksha-paspasha` in `working/40_reference/sources/as_source_registry.md`. All three consulted PDFs are archived locally with SHA-256 checksums.

## Completion Tests

- The body, Short forms, full notes, exact locators, and digital source records were checked together.
- The English source establishes spelling pronunciation as language change flowing from script into speech.
- The Indic sources establish recitation from writing as a fault and active knowledge of sound operations as necessary for Vedic protection.
- Chapter 13 identifies the modern engineering relationship; it does not place that modern category into the ancient sources' mouths.
