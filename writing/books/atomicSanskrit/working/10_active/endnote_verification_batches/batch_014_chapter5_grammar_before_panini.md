## Proposed Manuscript Corrections

The source corrections above have been applied to the endnotes. Proposals 1, 2, and 4 were withdrawn because they would have weakened conclusions established by the book's cumulative argument. The narrow factual corrections in proposals 3 and 5-8 were approved and applied.

### 1. Chapter 5 §5.1 — retain the Sanskrit construction

**Decision:** Withdrawn. Retain the current body.

**Current:**

> The analytical discipline is **व्याकरणम् (*vyākaraṇam*)**: ***वि (vi-,*** apart) + ***आ (ā-,*** fully) + ***कृ (kṛ,*** to do, to make) — taking apart, unfolding, analyzing an already present system. The activity the word denotes is de-composition, not composition. The **वैयाकरणः (*vaiyākaraṇaḥ*)** is the one who performs that unfolding. The role-title designates a decoder, not a codifier.

**Reason:** Sanskrit does not require a dictionary to authorize its own construction. The chapter's explanation begins with **vi + ā + kṛ** and calls the operation *decoding* because the Vedas are the invariant calibrant and the architecture predates Pāṇini. The endnote now follows that order; lexicons serve only as an external cross-check.

### 2. Chapter 5 §5.1 — retain the role-title conclusion

**Decision:** Withdrawn. Retain the current body.

**Current:**

> Pāṇini's role-title provides the evidence: the Hindu continuum does not call him स्थपति (*sthapati*), architect; निर्माता (*nirmātā*), constructor; or anything cognate with *engineer* or *codifier*. It calls him **वैयाकरणः (*vaiyākaraṇaḥ*)**: an analyst, decoder, and documenter.

**Reason:** The paragraph follows evidence already presented in the section. It does not ask the role-title to carry the conclusion alone. The Vedic calibrant and pre-Pāṇinian analysis establish the architecture that Pāṇini decoded.

### 3. Chapter 5 §5.2 — restore Patañjali's actual question

**Decision:** Approved and applied.

**Current:**

> Patañjali does not leave the purpose of व्याकरणम् (*vyākaraṇam*) to inference. He asks **किं प्रयोजनं व्याकरणस्य (*kim prayojanam vyākaraṇasya*)** — what purpose does grammar serve? His answer gives five received purposes, the **पञ्च प्रयोजनानि (*pañca prayojanāni*)**:

**Proposed:**

> Patañjali states the purpose of grammatical instruction directly. He asks **कानि पुनः शब्दानुशासनस्य प्रयोजनानि (*kāni punaḥ śabdānuśāsanasya prayojanāni*)** — what, then, are the purposes of instruction in words? His compact answer names five, the **पञ्च प्रयोजनानि (*pañca prayojanāni*)**:[NOTE: prayojanani-paspashahnika]

**Reason:** This is the wording in the *Paspaśāhnika*. The current Sanskrit question is a paraphrase presented as a quotation.

### 4. Chapter 5 §5.2 — retain the preface sentence

**Decision:** Withdrawn. Retain the current body.

**Current:**

> **Pāṇini himself wrote no preface to the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*.**

**Reason:** The statement describes the work as transmitted and is not carrying the decoder thesis by itself. Replacing it with *the received text* would add distance without improving the reader's understanding.

### 5. Chapter 5 §5.2 — identify Kātyāyana's *vārttikam*

**Decision:** Approved and applied.

**Current:**

> Then Patañjali places the decisive वार्त्तिकम् (*vārttikam*) at the opening:

**Proposed:**

> Patañjali then takes up Kātyāyana's first **वार्त्तिकम् (*vārttikam*)**:

**Reason:** The source explicitly identifies the passage as the first *vārttikam*. Patañjali explains it; he is not its author.

### 6. Chapter 5 §5.2 — correct the translation

**Decision:** Approved and applied.

**Current:**

> *Given that the bond between word and meaning is established, and given that worldly word-usage is prompted by meaning, śāstra regulates correct usage.*

**Proposed:**

> *Given that word, meaning, and their relation are established, as ordinary usage shows, and that meaning prompts the use of a word, śāstra regulates correct usage.*

**Reason:** Patañjali parses the compound as established word, meaning, and relation. **Lokataḥ** answers how that established condition is known. It does not modify *word-usage* as the current translation suggests.

### 7. Chapter 5 §5.2 — correct the syllable count

**Decision:** Approved and applied.

**Current:**

> The epigraph uses the opening clause: **सिद्धे शब्दार्थसम्बन्धे (*siddhe śabdārthasambandhe*)**. Six syllables that contain the weight of the discipline.

**Proposed:**

> The epigraph uses the opening clause: **सिद्धे शब्दार्थसम्बन्धे (*siddhe śabdārthasambandhe*)**. Its eight syllables establish the premise for the discussion that follows.

**Reason:** The clause has eight syllables: two in **siddhe** and six in **śabdārthasambandhe**.

### 8. Chapter 5 §5.2 — explain the sequence accurately

**Decision:** Approved and applied.

**Current:**

> The full sentence then moves in exact order. First comes the established bond. Then comes **लोकतोऽर्थप्रयुक्ते शब्दप्रयोगे (*lokato 'rthaprayukte śabdaprayoge*)** — word-usage in the world, prompted by meaning. Only after those two premises comes **शास्त्रेण धर्मनियमः (*śāstreṇa dharmaniyamaḥ*)** — regulation by शास्त्र (*śāstra*) of correct usage. Patañjali gives the order: bond first, usage second, शास्त्र (*śāstra*) third. शास्त्र (*śāstra*) regulates usage. It does not manufacture the bond.

**Proposed:**

> The passage moves in a clear order. First come word, meaning, and their relation, which ordinary usage shows to be already established. Then meaning prompts the use of a word. Only after those premises comes **शास्त्रेण धर्मनियमः (*śāstreṇa dharmaniyamaḥ*)**: शास्त्र (*śāstra*) regulates correct usage. It does not manufacture the words or their relation to meaning.

**Reason:** This follows Patañjali's explanation of the compound and **lokataḥ** while preserving the chapter's central conclusion.
