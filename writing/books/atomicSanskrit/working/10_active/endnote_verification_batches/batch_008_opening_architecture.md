### 1. Chapter 1 §1.5 — distinguish stratagem from concealment

**Current:**

> **Concealment.** Vṛtra, Namuci, and Svarbhānu employ माया (*māyā*) to prevent others from seeing what remains present. Vṛtra is **मायाविनम् वृत्रम् (*māyāvinam vṛtram*)**, Vṛtra the deceiver (RV 2.11). Namuci is **नमुचिं नाम मायिनम् (*namuciṃ nāma māyinam*)**, Namuci the मायिन् (*māyin*) (RV 1.53.7). Svarbhānu works through **स्वर्भानोः मायाः (*svarbhānoḥ māyāḥ*)**, Svarbhānu's deceptions (RV 5.40).[NOTE: mayin-concealment-cluster] His माया (*māyā*) conceals Sūrya and places darkness before the worlds.

**Applied:**

> **Concealment and projection.** आसुरी माया (*āsurī māyā*) works through two linked actions. **आवरण (*āvaraṇa*)** conceals what remains present. **विक्षेप (*vikṣepa*)** places a false appearance before the observer. The Ṛgveda calls Vṛtra **मायाविनम् वृत्रम् (*māyāvinam vṛtram*)** and Namuci **नमुचिं नाम मायिनम् (*namuciṃ nāma māyinam*)**. Vṛtra hides among the waters and blocks them (RV 2.11). The Namuci verse identifies him as *māyin* without describing the stratagem (RV 1.53.7). Svarbhānu supplies the clearest act of **आवरण (*āvaraṇa*)**: his **मायाः (*māyāḥ*)** place darkness before Sūrya and prevent the worlds from seeing what remains present (RV 5.40).[NOTE: mayin-concealment-cluster] Pūtanā appearing as a nurse, Mārīca appearing as a deer, and Kālanemi appearing as an ascetic demonstrate **विक्षेप (*vikṣepa*)**. The projected appearance conceals the actor and his purpose.[NOTE: maya-concealment-projection]

**Reason:** The current opening assigns the same concealment action to all three figures. The verses support that action for Svarbhanu and, through the surrounding description, Vritra. RV 1.53.7 calls Namuci *mayin* but does not say what his stratagem does.

### 2. Chapter 18 §18.4 — preserve the same distinction

**Current:**

> The Ṛgveda describes the wider tactic through hostile **माया (*māyā*)**. Vṛtra and Namuci are **मायिन् (*māyin*)**, figures who use concealment and deception, while Svarbhānu's माया (*māyā*) hides the Sun. These ***a-suras*** conceal what remains present and demand that others accept the concealment as reality.[NOTE: mayin-concealment-cluster]

**Applied:**

> The Ṛgveda describes the wider tactic through hostile **माया (*māyā*)**. Vṛtra and Namuci are **मायिन् (*māyin*)**, figures who act through stratagem. Svarbhānu's माया (*māyā*) performs **आवरण (*āvaraṇa*)** directly: it places darkness before the Sun and prevents the worlds from seeing what remains present.[NOTE: mayin-concealment-cluster]
>
> Gaslighting weaponizes both actions of आसुरी माया (*āsurī māyā*). **आवरण (*āvaraṇa*)** conceals what happened. **विक्षेप (*vikṣepa*)** projects a replacement account that the victim is expected to accept. The false accuser in the chapter's epigraph projects his own identity onto another person and then declares himself pure.[NOTE: maya-concealment-projection]

**Reason:** The present final sentence attributes Svarbhanu's precise action to every named figure. The replacement keeps the chapter's argument and states only what the cited verses establish.

### 3. Chapter 1 §1.5 — translate the *avrata* cluster directly

**Current:**

> Indra **शासद् अव्रतान् (*śāsad avratān*)**, chastises the vow-less (RV 1.51.8); the दस्यु (*dasyu*) is **अकर्मा... अमन्तुः अन्यव्रतः (*akarmā... amantuḥ anyavrataḥ*)**, without constructive action, without structured thought, following an alien law (RV 10.22.8).[NOTE: avrata-vow-less-blockade]

**Applied:**

> Indra **शासद् अव्रतान् (*śāsad avratān*)**, chastises those who follow no vow or commandment (RV 1.51.8). Another mantra describes the दस्यु (*dasyu*) as **अकर्मा... अमन्तुः अन्यव्रतः अमानुषः (*akarmā... amantuḥ anyavrataḥ amānuṣaḥ*)**: marked by non-deeds, non-thought, other commandments, and inhuman conduct (RV 10.22.8).[NOTE: avrata-vow-less-blockade]

**Reason:** “Constructive,” “structured,” and “alien law” are interpretations. They are not translations of the three Sanskrit terms. The proposed sentence gives all four descriptions and leaves the blockade inference in the surrounding prose.

### 4. Chapter 1 §1.5 — identify isolation as the book's synthesis

**Current:**

> **Isolation.** Ṛgveda 4.5.5 describes people who are **अनृत (*anṛta*)**, opposed to ऋत (*ṛta*), and **असत्य (*asatya*)**, untrue in speech:
>
> **पापासः सन्तो अनृता असत्या इदं पदमजनता गभीरम्**  
> *pāpāsaḥ santo anṛtā asatyā idaṃ padam ajanata gabhīram*
>
> Their actions create “this deep station” for themselves. Their departure from truth and ऋत (*ṛta*) produces the isolation in which they remain.[NOTE: rv-4-5-5-gabhiram-padam-isolation]

**Applied:**

> Their conduct generates **गभीरम् पदम् (*gabhīram padam*)**, a deep place or state. The phrase can also mean a deep track or profound word in the hymn's speech context. This book calls the resulting separation *isolation*: departure from truth and ऋत (*ṛta*) separates its makers from the shared order.[NOTE: rv-4-5-5-gabhiram-padam-isolation]

**Reason:** The Sanskrit permits the “deep place/state” reading needed by the paragraph, while another major translation reads “deep track/profound word.” The revised prose retains isolation and states clearly where the verse ends and the book's architectural classification begins.
