## Manuscript Decisions

### Chapter 4 §4.1 — distinguish the bounded covenant from later expansionary forms

**Current:**

> Judaism built the foundation. The pyramid adapted that original dogma and forced it upon the world through Christianity and Islam. Each iteration preserved the structural template it inherited: a chosen community, an authorized doctrine, a rigid boundary between insider and outsider, an obligation to expand, and a history moving toward a promised end. The doctrines changed. The pyramid restructured.

**Revised:**

> Judaism built the foundation. Christianity and Islam took related structures outward through missionary and political expansion. Across those transformations, the pyramid retained a chosen community, an authorized doctrine, a rigid boundary between insider and outsider, and a history moving toward a promised end. The doctrines changed. The pyramid restructured.

**Reason:** Judaism has a chosen community and a covenantal boundary, but no universal command to convert humanity. The current sentence assigns the later expansionary form to every iteration.

### Chapter 4 §4.1 — state Hawking's use of “mind of God”

**Current:**

> The substitutions followed an exact pattern. Genesis became the Big Bang. God did not disappear from the vocabulary; Stephen Hawking invoked him repeatedly in *A Brief History of Time*. But God ceased to act as creator and became a metaphor for physical law. The fourth religion converted divine creation into the emergence of time and space, then presented the substitution as *"Science."*

**Revised:**

> The substitutions followed an exact pattern. Genesis became the Big Bang. Stephen Hawking still invoked God repeatedly in *A Brief History of Time*, and his “mind of God” meant complete knowledge of physical **law**. The fourth religion converted divine creation into the emergence of time and space, then presented the substitution as *"Science."*

**Reason:** The revision states Hawking's meaning directly. The endnote records Lemaître's insistence that cosmology and theological creation answer different questions.

### Chapter 4 §4.1 — retain the climate paragraph

The author reviewed the proposed qualification and retained the existing paragraph. The strengthened endnote now documents the apocalyptic, no-choice, and political-will narrative with an independent source.

### Chapter 4 §4.4 — use Rostow for the sequence he actually proposed

**Current:**

> The lineage runs back to Rostow's stages-of-growth model and continues through development economics, World Bank conditionalities, and the Sustainable Development Goals.

**Revised:**

> Rostow's stages-of-growth model placed every society on the same five-step ladder, from “traditional society” to “high mass consumption.”

**Reason:** Rostow directly supplies the five-stage sequence. His own qualification and the limits of the claimed institutional lineage remain in the endnote.
