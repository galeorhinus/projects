# Language Categories and Processes — Two-Figure Plan

*Status: active design and writing plan. No figure or manuscript changes have yet been made from this plan.*

## 1. Purpose

The present Chapter 2 matrix asks one figure to perform two different jobs. Its four quadrants classify languages, while the arrows introduce processes by which languages or linguistic material move between those categories. Separating those jobs will let the reader understand the categories before following movement through time.

The revised sequence uses two related figures:

1. **Figure 2.1 classifies four language categories.**
2. **Figure 2.2 shows movement between those categories.**

Both figures use the same axes, quadrant positions, colors, icons, and category labels. The first acts as the stable map; the second places processes over that map.

## 2. Figure 2.1 — Categories Only

### Working title

**Four Language Categories**

### Working subtitle

**Origin and generativity**

### Structure

Retain the current axes:

- horizontal: **Origin — Engineered / Organic**
- vertical: **Generativity — High / Low**

Retain the four categories:

| Position | Category | Core description |
|---|---|---|
| Engineered + high generativity | **Sanskrit** | engineered; generative by derivation; adapts in usage while calibration keeps the language invariant |
| Organic + high generativity | **Natural Languages** | generative through changing usage, contact, inheritance, and drift |
| Engineered + lower adaptive generativity | **Conlangs / Constructed Projects** | deliberately designed projects whose initial architecture and inventory may require later addition or community change |
| Organic + low generativity | **Petrified Languages** | a selected organic form held relatively fixed by authority while ordinary speech changes around it |

### Remove from Figure 2.1

- the **Vivimorphosis** arrow and label
- the **Revivification** arrow and label
- any process explanation that asks the reader to follow movement between cells

The category figure should remain intelligible without requiring the caption to explain arrows.

### Examples inside the conlang category

Use a compact exemplar line rather than additional bullets:

> Klingon · Dothraki · Quenya · Lojban

These examples identify deliberately constructed languages; the line does not claim that every one has identical morphology, productivity, community use, or subsequent history.

### Esperanto inside the upper-left quadrant

Place Esperanto beside Sanskrit as an initial-state example:

> **Esperanto began here. Figure 2.2 follows its movement.**

This placement is deliberate. Esperanto began as an engineered and substantially generative language, with a designed derivational system and a foundational lexical inventory. The first figure records that starting condition. It does not claim that Esperanto possessed Sanskrit's civilizational purpose, dual-domain structure, or calibration architecture.

The placement also changes how the upper-left cell must be explained. Sanskrit is not distinguished merely by having been engineered or by possessing generative machinery. Esperanto approximated both conditions at creation. Sanskrit's unique achievement is that it remains in the quadrant because civilizational motivation and calibrated execution sustain each other.

### Caption requirement

The caption should tell the reader that the matrix records a language's condition at a given point:

> **Figure 2.1 — Four language categories.** Origin and generativity place languages within four categories. Esperanto began as an engineered and highly generative project; Figure 2.2 follows what happened when community use began changing the language.

Revise the final caption after the finished figure makes clear how much explanation remains necessary.

## 3. Figure 2.2 — Processes Only

### Working title

**How Languages Move Between Categories**

### Working subtitle

**Petrification, revivification, and vivimorphosis**

### Structure

Reuse the same four-quadrant map, but reduce each category to:

- category name
- category icon
- one short identifying phrase, if needed

The arrows, traveling objects, and example callouts should carry the figure. Do not repeat the four bullet lists from Figure 2.1.

### Process A — Petrification

**Direction:** Natural Languages → Petrified Languages

Arabic requires a branching path. The figure must not imply that all Arabic became petrified:

- one branch represents spoken Arabic languages continuing to change botanically;
- another branch represents Quranic/Classical Arabic being held relatively fixed through text, education, office, and religious authority.

Possible callout:

> **Arabic:** spoken forms continue changing; Quranic/Classical Arabic is held fixed.

Use a solid stone-grey arrow for the petrifying branch. The botanical branch should continue into the Natural Languages cell.

### Process B — Revivification

**Direction:** Natural Languages → Petrified Languages → Natural Languages

Use Hebrew as the primary sequence:

> Ancient Hebrew → preserved textual and liturgical Hebrew → Modern Hebrew

The return to ordinary speech also returns the language to botanical change. Revivification does not transform Hebrew into an engineered calibrant.

Use a dashed green return arrow from the Petrified Languages cell to the Natural Languages cell.

### Process C — Vivimorphosis

**Direction:** Sanskrit → Natural Languages

Only a Sanskrit molecule crosses the boundary. Sanskrit itself remains in its quadrant.

The traveling object should begin as a gold hexagonal molecule and become a botanical form after entering the natural-language cell. This visually establishes the defining operation:

> An engineered Sanskrit molecule enters a natural language and begins changing organically within it.

The receiving language gains Sanskrit's radiance while remaining a natural language. Sanskrit does not become botanical.

Use a gold hexagonal flow or gold-to-green transformation arrow.

### Process D — Esperanto becomes botanical

**Direction:** Engineered + high generativity → Natural Languages

Esperanto begins beside Sanskrit, but the entire Esperanto object crosses into the Natural Languages cell once community usage begins enlarging, selecting, and changing the language.

The visual contrast with vivimorphosis is essential:

- **Vivimorphosis:** a molecule crosses; Sanskrit remains invariant.
- **Esperanto:** the language crosses; community usage changes it.

Possible arrow label:

> **Productive at creation; botanical in community use**

Use a broad outlined arrow that carries the complete Esperanto tile. Do not coin another process term unless the prose later demonstrates that one is necessary.

## 4. Why Sanskrit Remains

The Esperanto comparison exposes a condition that the two visible axes cannot fully represent. Engineering and generativity describe linguistic capability. Sanskrit also joins a civilizational motivation to calibrated execution.

### Motivation

Sanskrit preserves humanity's accumulated understanding of **sat** and **asat**:

- how consciousness recognizes and chooses *sat*;
- how balance is preserved or restored;
- how *asat* operates through containment;
- how human beings resist and defeat *asat* when it attempts to control the field.

The Vedas and the laukika stories preserve this purpose in complementary ways. The Vedas preserve the measure. The laukika corpus carries the human stories of balance, failure, recognition, struggle, and return. This purpose inspired millions of caretakers across millennia to preserve the architecture.

### Execution

Calibration converts that motivation into a durable distributed system:

- the vaidika domain preserves the invariant measure;
- the laukika domain applies the same invariant language to changing circumstances;
- recitation, meter, grammar, correction, lineage, family, and community distribute responsibility for transmission;
- no single apex owns the measure.

### Working body prose

> Sanskrit is more than a language designed with unusual precision. It preserves a civilizational purpose and supplies the method required to sustain that purpose. The Vedas preserve the measure of *sat* and the knowledge required to recognize *asat*, especially when *asat* operates through containment. The laukika stories carry that struggle into human lives, choices, failures, and recoveries. Millions across millennia therefore had a reason to protect the language, while calibration gave them the means to do so.

### Candidate refrain

> **The Vedas preserve the measure. The laukika stories preserve the struggle to live by it. Calibration keeps both available.**

The explanatory prose must come first. Judge the refrain later in the dedicated hammer pass.

## 5. Chapter 2 Sequence

The intended reading sequence is:

1. explain the two axes in ordinary prose;
2. introduce Figure 2.1 as the four-category map;
3. state why Esperanto begins beside Sanskrit;
4. explain that a category records a language's condition rather than an unchangeable essence;
5. introduce Figure 2.2 as the same map placed in motion;
6. explain each process in the order petrification, revivification, vivimorphosis, and Esperanto's botanical movement;
7. give only the compact motivation/execution distinction needed to explain why Sanskrit remains;
8. point forward to the chapters that develop each process and Sanskrit's calibration architecture.

Chapter 2 should not carry the entire civilizational argument. It should make the distinction clear enough that the reader understands why linguistic rules alone do not explain Sanskrit's endurance.

## 6. Cross-Chapter Deployment

- **Chapter 0:** establish Sanskrit's civilizational purpose and the complementary work of the vaidika and laukika domains.
- **Chapter 2:** classify the categories, introduce both figures, and distinguish Esperanto's movement from Sanskrit's endurance.
- **Chapter 6:** return to Esperanto when explaining why *apabhraṃśa* becomes an architectural concern only where an invariant measure exists.
- **Chapter 12:** develop vivimorphosis at the molecule boundary.
- **Chapter 13:** develop Hebrew revivification and explain its return to botanical change.
- **Chapter 14:** develop calibration as the execution architecture and petrification as authority holding a selected form fixed.
- **Chapter 18:** use vivimorphosis to reverse the PIE tree.
- **Epilogue:** return to the purpose of preserving the measure and the responsibility of present caretakers.
