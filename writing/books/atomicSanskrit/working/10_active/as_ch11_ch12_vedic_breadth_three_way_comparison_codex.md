## 1. Result

All three reports support the central claim: the Rigveda preserves enough verbal, nominal, and sentence breadth to demonstrate Sanskrit's grammatical architecture directly.

The reports also agree on the strongest additions:

- Chapter 11 should demonstrate more than five third-person singular forms ending in **-ति**.
- The body should use a few fully explained examples and summarize the wider range compactly.
- Chapter 12 should begin its ⟪कृ⟫ family with Vedic forms, including **संस्कृतम् (*saṃskṛtam*)**, before extending the same architecture into *laukika* formations.
- RV 1.1.1 and RV 1.164.39 are the strongest sentence examples for the body.
- RV 10.129.1 and RV 3.62.10 add valuable grammatical breadth.
- Pāṇini should enter after the reader has seen the grammar in the Vedic forms.
- The complete form inventory, source locations, and disputed analyses belong in Appendix Part 7.

The differences among the reports concern accuracy and body size, not the main conclusion.

## 2. How the Reports Were Weighted

### Codex

The Codex report rebuilt the search index from the local Rigvedic corpus, checked the high-value forms against exact pāda records, and rechecked several transmitted forms against independent editions. Its main contribution is the corrected evidence set and a body plan organized by visible formation procedure.

### Claude

Claude performed the most extensive corpus-wide audit. Its person-number counts, mood and tense counts, *vibhakti* counts, ⟪कृ⟫ family, RV 1.164.39 analysis, and correction of many entries in `working/10_active.md` are strong.

Two Claude findings require correction:

1. **तन्वे (*tanve*)** appears in the search results, but those occurrences are nominal forms of **तनू (*tanu*)**, not completed verbs from ⟪तन्⟫.
2. The automatic parse of the RV 8.77.11 form as feminine dual **संस्कृते** conflicts with the transmitted **सुसंस्कृत / सुसंस्कृता** agreeing with **उभा ... बाहू**, the two arms. This form cannot carry a gender-and-ending claim until it receives a separate grammatical check.

Claude recommends retaining the current five **-ति** examples unchanged and adding the breadth afterward. That would preserve a controlled comparison, but it would also make Chapter 11 longer and repeat several similar explanations. The author-approved plan calls for replacing some examples with forms that demonstrate different Vedic procedures. The reconciled plan therefore keeps the controlled comparison as a compact summary while changing the five detailed examples.

### Gemini

Gemini identified several useful categories and candidate passages, especially RV 10.90.1 as a possible sentence without an expressed finite verb. Its report cannot serve as the verification layer because it presents several generated or disputed analyses as settled facts.

The following Gemini claims must not enter the manuscript without correction:

- its person-number table prints **भवामि (*bhavami*)** and **भवाव (*bhavava*)** as though they occur in the Rigveda; they were not found in this corpus;
- it treats **धीमहि (*dhimahi*)** as securely derived from ⟪धी / ध्यै⟫, although the atom and mood have competing analyses;
- it calls **प्रचोदयात् (*pracodayat*)** both optative and *leṭ*, which combines two different grammatical labels;
- it attributes **ददर्श (*dadarśa*)** to RV 1.164.39, where that form does not occur;
- it gives RV 10.34.3c for **रुणद्धि**, while the exact pāda is RV 10.34.3a;
- it describes a full "chronological axis," which imports the chronology that this rewrite is intended to remove;
- it marks items as verified through GRETIL or BORI without providing a reproducible form-by-form audit.

Gemini's report is therefore useful for candidate discovery and not for final citation.

## 4. Approved Evidence Standard for the Rewrite

Every body example must meet four requirements:

1. the exact form occurs in the cited passage;
2. the displayed grammatical feature is secure;
3. the example contributes something not already demonstrated more clearly nearby;
4. a disputed analysis is either removed from the main proof or described plainly as disputed.

Corpus counts will establish breadth. Exact forms will teach the architecture. The body will not print generated paradigms as Vedic attestations.

## 5. Chapter 11 Preview

### 11.1 The chapter's task

The opening will preserve the current teaching sequence: a *dhatuḥ* is not yet a completed verb. Sanskrit prepares the atom, adds information about the actor and action, and produces a sentence-ready *kriyapadam*.

The Vedic evidence will appear before Pāṇini's analytical vocabulary.

### 11.2 Five visible formation procedures

The recommended detailed set is:

| Procedure | Vedic form | Source | Reason for selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| vowel atom prepared for an ending | **एति (*eti*)** from ⟪इ⟫ | RV 1.23.11b | preserves the current one-*matra* opening and shows **इ → ए** |
| vowel change and inserted **अ** | **भवति (*bhavati*)** from ⟪भू⟫ | RV 1.17.5c | shows a second preparation procedure and connects easily to later person-number forms |
| reduplication | **ददाति (*dadati*)** from ⟪दा⟫ | RV 10.117.3a | makes repetition inside the completed form immediately visible |
| nasal infix | **रुणद्धि (*ruṇaddhi*)** from ⟪रुध्⟫ | RV 10.34.3a | shows a sound entering the middle of the atom |
| nasal extension | **कृणोति (*kṛṇoti*)** from ⟪कृ⟫ | RV 1.92.6b | adds another distinct procedure and prepares the transition into Chapter 12's ⟪कृ⟫ family |

These five come from five recurring behavioral groups later made explicit in grammatical analysis. The body does not need to call them five *gaṇāḥ* while teaching the transformations.

### 11.3 What happens to the current examples

- **एति** and **भवति** remain detailed examples.
- **अस्ति** becomes the compact direct-attachment contrast.
- **यजति** moves into a paired **यजति / यजते** demonstration of the two ending sets.
- **राजति** moves into the compact comparison or Appendix Part 7.
- No existing explanation or figure will be deleted until its new home has been confirmed.

### 11.4 The breadth demonstration

After the five detailed examples, a compact figure will show:

- all three persons;
- singular, dual, and plural;
- both sets of verbal endings;
- present, past, future, imperative, optative, and Vedic *leṭ*;
- direct attachment, vowel change, insertion, reduplication, nasal infix, nasal extension, and **-aya-** extension.

The body will use a few secure forms. Appendix Part 7 will preserve the full table and every citation.

Recommended compact forms include **ईळे, भवथः, विन्दन्ति, आसीत्, करिष्यति, भव, स्याम,** and **भवाति**. The exact set will be finalized while drafting so the figure does not duplicate the five detailed panels.

### 11.5 The Vedas as calibrant

The section will explain why the breadth matters. The Rigveda does not preserve one approved form of a verb. It preserves completed verbs across every person and number, several times and moods, and several ways of preparing an atom. Exact transmission keeps those examples fixed. A student can compare later Sanskrit usage against forms whose sound and grammatical relations remain unchanged.

### 11.6 Pāṇini after the evidence

Only after the examples will the chapter introduce *dhatuḥ, aṅgam, tiṅ-pratyayaḥ,* and the recurring classifications. Pāṇini's contribution will be described positively: he made an already evident architecture explicit, compact, derivable, and teachable.

The ten *gaṇāḥ* and detailed *vikaraṇa* mechanics will remain in Appendix Part 6.

The current *kṛ* family and **चन्द्रयान (*Candrayāna*)** can then demonstrate continuing composition. The Vedas preserve the architecture; the *laukika* domain uses it to create expressions for new knowledge and circumstances.

Chapter 12 should stop after establishing this transition. The larger outward-radiance argument belongs in Chapter 19.

### 12.6 Pāṇini and the fractal close

The Pāṇini section will map one already explained sentence into explicit analytical terms. The close will return to the complete chain:

> sonomer → *dhātuḥ* → *kriyāpadam / śabdaḥ* → *padam* → *vākyam*

Each larger construction retains the smaller parts and their roles. That is the chapter's contribution to the radiant, calibrant, and fractal architecture.
