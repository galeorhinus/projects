## Source Rule

Each case proceeds in the same order:

1. Show the operation in the Vedic domain.
2. Identify the Sanskrit atom and its generated forms.
3. Credit Pāṇini for documenting the operation already visible in Sanskrit.
4. Use Hemacandra only for changes he records in Prakrit or Apabhraṃśa.
5. State the pyramid's reconstructed family accurately.
6. Present the Sanskrit Radiance interpretation as the account being tested.

Pāṇini's rule can demonstrate that Sanskrit deliberately moves between specified sonomer coordinates. It does not, by itself, prove that a Greek, Latin, or Germanic form came from Sanskrit. Direction must emerge from the repeated pattern, Sanskrit's generative architecture, evidence of outward transmission, and the explanatory power of the full dataset.

## Finalist 1 — ⟪युज्⟫ (*yuj*)

### The Vedic record

The Veda already displays the palatal, velar, and devoiced velar forms:

1. **युञ्जन्ति (*yuñjanti*)**, "they yoke / join":

   > युञ्जन्ति ब्रध्नमरुषं चरन्तं परि तस्थुषः ।  
   > रोचन्ते रोचना दिवि ॥
   >
   > *yuñjanti bradhnam aruṣaṃ carantaṃ pari tasthuṣaḥ |*  
   > *rocante rocanā divi ||*
   >
   > Ṛgveda 1.6.1

2. **युगा (*yugā*)**, "the yokes":

   > युनक्त सीरा वि युगा तनुध्वं कृते योनौ वपतेह बीजम् ।
   >
   > *yunakta sīrā vi yugā tanudhvaṃ kṛte yonau vapateha bījam |*
   >
   > Ṛgveda 10.101.3

3. **युक्त (*yukta*)**, "yoked / joined":

   > ककर्दवे वृषभो युक्त आसीत् ...
   >
   > *kakardave vṛṣabho yukta āsīt ...*
   >
   > Ṛgveda 10.102.9

The chapter therefore does not need to begin from a later grammatical example. The Vedic domain itself presents **युज् / युग् / युक् (*yuj / yug / yuk*)** as members of one functioning semantic family.

**Primary online text:** [Vedic Heritage Portal, Ṛgveda 1.6](https://vedicheritage.gov.in/samhitas/rigveda/shakala-samhita/m01-006/); [Ṛgveda 10.101](https://vedicheritage.gov.in/samhitas/rigveda/shakala-samhita/rigveda-shakala-samhita-mandal-10-sukta-101/); [Ṛgveda 10.102](https://vedicheritage.gov.in/hi/samhitas/rigveda/shakala-samhita/rigveda-shakala-samhita-mandal-10-sukta-102/).

### The Sanskrit atom

The *Dhātupāṭha* records more than one ⟪युज्⟫ entry. The present case chiefly concerns **युजिँर् योगे (*yujĩr yoge*)**, the joining or yoking atom represented by forms such as **युनक्ति (*yunakti*)**. The wider Sanskrit family includes:

- **योग (*yoga*)** — joining, union, disciplined integration
- **युक्त (*yukta*)** — joined, fitted, connected, appropriate
- **युग (*yuga*)** — yoke, pair, joined span
- **युनक्ति (*yunakti*)** — joins or yokes

The final report must cite the exact *Dhātupāṭha* entry number from the selected edition. Aṣṭādhyāyī.com's current display places **युजिँर् योगे** in the *rudhādi* class.

### Pāṇini's documentation

Pāṇini later documents the coordinate relationships:

- **चोः कुः (*coḥ kuḥ*, 8.2.30):** a palatal receives its corresponding velar under the rule's stated conditions.
- **खरि च (*khari ca*, 8.4.55):** a non-nasal stop or spirant receives the corresponding voiceless stop before a *khar* sound.

Aṣṭādhyāyī.com's derivation of a *suyuj* form shows:

> **सुयुज् → सुयुग् → सुयुक्**  
> *suyuj → suyug → suyuk*

The physical map is exact:

| Movement | Place | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **ज → ग (*j → g*)** | palatal → velar | voiced unaspirated remains voiced unaspirated |
| **ग → क (*g → k*)** | velar remains velar | voiced → voiceless |

Pāṇini does not create either movement. He documents where Sanskrit uses it.

**Sources:** [Pāṇini 8.2.30](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/8/2/30); [Laghusiddhāntakaumudī *yuj* forms](https://ashtadhyayi.com/laghukaumudi/8?scroll=laghukaumudi-71071). The selected print edition must also supply 8.4.55.

### The pyramid's family

The conventional account groups the Sanskrit family with:

- Greek **ζυγόν (*zygon*)**, yoke
- Greek **ζεύγνυμι (*zeugnymi*)**, I yoke or join
- Latin **iugum**, yoke
- Latin **iungō / iungere**, join
- Gothic **juk**, yoke
- English **yoke**

It places a reconstructed **\*yeug- / \*yug-** family above the recorded forms.

**Comparative source:** [University of Texas Indo-European Lexicon, *yeu-g-* family](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/lex/master/0785).

### What this case establishes

This family is the best introductory example because Sanskrit already supplies:

1. the recorded atom,
2. the stable join/yoke meaning,
3. generated molecules,
4. all three relevant sonomer outcomes in the Veda, and
5. Pāṇini's later documentation of the coordinate movements.

The foreign **g** and **k** forms therefore do not require an imaginary ancestor merely to bridge the sound difference from Sanskrit **j**. Sanskrit contains the bridge.

### What remains to be demonstrated

The case does not establish transmission direction alone. The institutional project must test whether Sanskrit repeatedly supplies both the semantic architecture and the sonomer transformations for reconstructed families, and whether the historical transmission field supports outward radiance.

## Finalist 2 — ⟪भृ⟫ (*bhṛ*)

### The Vedic record

Ṛgveda 7.87.4 uses **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)**, "bears / carries / sustains":

> उवाच मे वरुणो मेधिराय त्रिः सप्त नामाघ्न्या बिभर्ति ।  
> विद्वान्पदस्य गुह्या न वोचद्युगाय विप्र उपराय शिक्षन् ॥
>
> *uvāca me varuṇo medhirāya triḥ sapta nāmāghnyā bibharti |*  
> *vidvān padasya guhyā na vocad yugāya vipra uparāya śikṣan ||*

The form **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)** is especially useful because one Vedic word contains both **ब (*b*)** and **भ (*bh*)**. Sanskrit takes the initial of the atom into the reduced repeated syllable **बि (*bi*)**, while the full **भ (*bh*)** remains in the following syllable. Pāṇini later documents this repeated element as **अभ्यास (*abhyāsa*)** and specifies its formation. Chapter 16 already establishes the Vedic-first account of this operation.

**Text:** [Ṛgveda 7.87.4 with saṃhitā and padapāṭha](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/rig-veda-english-translation/d/doc835306.html). Final production should cite the selected printed Ṛgveda edition.

### The Sanskrit atoms

The *Dhātupāṭha* records related ⟪भृ⟫ entries. Two are relevant:

- **डुभृञ् धारणपोषणयोः (*ḍubhṛñ dhāraṇapoṣaṇayoḥ*)**, the reduplicating-class atom behind **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)**
- **भृञ् भरणे (*bhṛñ bharaṇe*)**, represented by forms such as **भरति (*bharati*)**

The semantic field includes bearing, carrying, sustaining, maintaining, and nourishing. Sanskrit molecules include **भार (*bhāra*)**, load or burden; **भरण (*bharaṇa*)**, bearing or support; and **भर्तृ (*bhartṛ*)**, bearer or sustainer.

The selected *Dhātupāṭha* edition must provide the exact numbers for both entries.

### Pāṇini's documentation

Two Sanskrit operations help the reader understand the receiving-language forms:

1. The Vedic **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)** already shows aspirated **भ (*bh*)** beside its unaspirated **ब (*b*)** counterpart inside one designed form. Pāṇini later documents the *abhyāsa* operation and the sound retained in the reduplicated syllable.
2. Pāṇini 8.4.53, **झलां जश् झशि (*jhalāṃ jaś jhaśi*)**, documents a voiced-unaspirated substitution before a voiced stop. The commentarial examples include **लब्धा (*labdhā*)**, where the relevant consonant loses aspiration in the defined environment.

These are Sanskrit operations under Sanskrit conditions. They demonstrate that aspiration and voicing are independently controlled features on the grid.

**Source:** [Pāṇini 8.4.53 with Kāśikā examples](https://sanskritdictionary.com/panini/8-4-53).

### Hemacandra's natural-language evidence

Hemacandra records aspirated stops changing in Prakrit. *Siddha-Hema* 8.1.187 gives changes such as:

- *mukha → muha*
- *megha → meha*
- *sādhu → sāhu*
- *svabhāva → sahāva*

This rule does not derive Greek **ph**, Latin **f**, or Germanic **b**. It shows that aspiration is unstable when Sanskrit molecules enter naturally changing speech fields, and that an Indic analyst already documented the environment and result rather than hiding the operation inside an imaginary ancestor.

**Working source:** [Hemacandra's Prakrit and Apabhraṃśa rules](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Prakrit_Vyakaranam_Romanized/010651), *Siddha-Hema* 8.1.187.

### The pyramid's family

The conventional account groups:

- Sanskrit **भरति / बिभर्ति (*bharati / bibharti*)**
- Greek **φέρω / φέρειν (*pherō / pherein*)**
- Latin **ferō / ferre**
- Gothic **bairan**
- English **bear**, with related *birth* and *burden*

It places reconstructed **\*bher-** above the family.

**Comparative source:** [University of Texas Indo-European Lexicon, *bher-* family](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/lex/master/0229).

### The sonomer map

| Language field | Sound | Physical relation |
|---|---|---|
| Sanskrit | **भ (*bh*)** | voiced aspirated labial stop |
| Sanskrit reduplicant / Germanic | **ब (*b*)** | voiced unaspirated labial stop |
| Older Greek | **φ (*ph*)** | voiceless aspirated labial stop |
| Latin | **f** | voiceless labial fricative |

The place of articulation remains labial while effort, voicing, and closure change. This case proves that the method is not limited to the palatal–velar relation shown by ⟪युज्⟫.

### What remains to be demonstrated

No single Pāṇinian rule produces the entire foreign sequence. The project must present **भ → फ → f** and **भ → ब** as physical transformations to be tested across many families, not as one Sanskrit sandhi rule exported whole.

## Finalist 3 — ⟪जन्⟫ (*jan*)

### The Vedic record

The Veda uses both active generation and coming into birth:

> कविं सम्राजमतिथिं जनानामासन्ना पात्रं जनयन्त देवाः ॥
>
> *kaviṃ samrājam atithiṃ janānām āsannā pātraṃ janayanta devāḥ ||*
>
> Ṛgveda 6.7.1

The same sūkta continues:

> त्वद्विप्रो जायते वाज्यग्ने ...
>
> *tvad vipro jāyate vājy agne ...*
>
> Ṛgveda 6.7.3

The Vedic domain therefore contains the generative **जनयन्त (*janayanta*)** and the birth-form **जायते (*jāyate*)** within one sūkta.

**Primary online text:** [Vedic Heritage Portal, Ṛgveda 6.7](https://vedicheritage.gov.in/samhitas/rigveda/shakala-samhita/rigveda-shakala-samhita-mandal-06-sukta-007/).

### The Sanskrit atoms

The *Dhātupāṭha* records the atom twice:

- **जनँ जनने (*janaṃ janane*)** — to generate or procreate
- **जनीँ प्रादुर्भावे (*janīṃ prādurbhāve*)** — to be born or come into manifestation

The family includes:

- **जन (*jana*)** — person or people
- **जनक (*janaka*)** — begetter or father
- **जनन (*janana*)** — generation or birth
- **जननी (*jananī*)** — mother
- **जन्मन् (*janman*)** — birth
- **जात (*jāta*)** — born
- **जाति (*jāti*)** — birth, kind, class

The existing endnote `jan-dhatupatha-double-entry` records the working entry numbers as 3.25 and 4.44. These must be checked against the print edition chosen for production.

### Pāṇini's documentation

Pāṇini documents Sanskrit's own **ज → ग** coordinate movement through **चोः कुः (*coḥ kuḥ*)** and **ग → क** through **खरि च (*khari ca*)**. These rules show that the sonomer path required by *jan → gen → kin* belongs to Sanskrit's articulated grid.

The chapter must phrase this accurately:

> Sanskrit documents **j → g → k** as possible coordinate movements under stated conditions. The proposed Radiance analysis tests whether receiving languages applied comparable movements to the ⟪जन्⟫ family.

It must not say that *coḥ kuḥ* directly changes Sanskrit *jan* into Latin *genus*.

### The pyramid's family

The conventional account places reconstructed **\*ǵenh₁-**, "beget / produce," above:

- Sanskrit **जन (*jan*)**
- Greek **γένος (*genos*)**
- Latin **genus**, **gignō**, and the wider *gen-* family
- Germanic forms behind **kin**, **kind**, and **king**

**Comparative source:** [University of Texas Indo-European Lexicon, *ǵen-* family](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/lex/master/0566).

### Why it follows the first two cases

⟪जन्⟫ provides the largest lexical and civilizational payoff, but it also requires more explanation. Latin and Greek develop extensive *gen-* families, Germanic develops *kin/kind/king*, and Sanskrit itself moves between *jan* and *jā* forms. After ⟪युज्⟫ has shown the coordinate path and ⟪भृ⟫ has shown a second physical pattern, the reader is ready to see how one atom can radiate into an enormous receiving-language canopy.
