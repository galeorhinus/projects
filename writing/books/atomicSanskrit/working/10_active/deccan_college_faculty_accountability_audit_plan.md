# Deccan College Faculty Accountability Audit — Plan

*Status: active research and deployment plan. No names have been added to the manuscript from this plan. Coverage target: the institutional arc from 1821 through 2026.*

## 1. Purpose

Appendix Part 2 currently criticizes Deccan College as an institution, especially the decision to build the *Encyclopaedic Dictionary of Sanskrit on Historical Principles* inside a natural-language, evolutionary, and chronology-driven framework. The appendix says that the institutional choice has been renewed across generations, but it does not yet show who possessed the authority to make, continue, challenge, or reverse that choice.

The proposed audit will identify the people involved and connect each judgment to a documented act. It will not treat employment at Deccan College as evidence of betrayal. A professor may have preserved manuscripts, taught Sanskrit with care, produced indispensable editions, resisted the imported framework, worked in an unrelated discipline, or simply occupied a junior post without authority over the dictionary project. The historical record must distinguish those cases.

The resulting account will therefore proceed in two stages:

1. construct the most complete Deccan College faculty and project-leadership roster the surviving record permits;
2. identify which people took documented actions that advanced, administered, resisted, or corrected the pyramid's treatment of Sanskrit.

## 2. Scope

### Institutional phases

Organize the roster by the institution's changing form:

1. **1821–1851:** Sanskrit Pāṭhaśālā / Hindoo College
2. **1851–1864:** Poona College
3. **1864–1939:** Deccan College under the Bombay Presidency
4. **1939–1948:** closure and transition
5. **1948–1990:** reconstituted Deccan College Post-Graduate and Research Institute; establishment and early direction of the Encyclopaedic Dictionary project
6. **1990–2026:** deemed-university period, later dictionary leadership, departmental expansion, funding, digitization, and present governance

The final chronology must follow the institution's own records and government documents. These phase labels are working bins, not substitutes for source verification.

### People within scope

Build the broad roster from:

- professors and readers in Sanskrit, Vedic studies, linguistics, lexicography, and related language departments;
- directors, vice-chancellors, registrars, department heads, and governing-body members when they exercised authority over Sanskrit teaching or the dictionary project;
- general editors, chief editors, associate editors, and senior project staff of the *Encyclopaedic Dictionary of Sanskrit on Historical Principles*;
- named architects of curricula, project methodology, corpus selection, chronology, funding, and publication policy;
- faculty who publicly challenged or corrected the imported framework.

Record professors in unrelated disciplines only when a documented administrative act placed them inside the relevant chain of decision-making. The appendix does not need a list of every archaeologist, anthropologist, or administrator who had no responsibility for Sanskrit.

## 3. The Evidentiary Rule

A name enters the critical roster only when a source identifies a specific action. Acceptable actions include:

- authoring or approving a methodological statement that treats Sanskrit as a naturally evolving language;
- installing the Vedic-to-"Classical" sequence as chronology rather than domain or style;
- presenting Pāṇini as Sanskrit's codifier or source of order;
- placing Sanskrit beneath PIE as a historical descendant;
- treating *apabhraṃśa*, generative output, or semantic extension as evidence that Sanskrit's architecture changed;
- designing or approving a curriculum that teaches those categories as settled fact;
- directing, funding, editing, or repeatedly renewing the dictionary's *historical principles* framework;
- blocking or dismissing a documented Sanskrit-internal alternative;
- redirecting Sanskrit endowments, offices, or teaching institutions into the colonial or postcolonial philological machinery.

Institutional rank, salary, an imperial honor, a grant, or association with a foreign scholar may show how the pyramid rewarded participation, but it does not by itself prove intellectual betrayal. The audit must connect any such benefit to the person's documented action before using language such as *reward*, *promotion*, or *selling out*.

## 4. Accountability Classifications

Do not begin with a single list titled "betrayers." Classify the evidence so the judgment remains visible and defensible.

| Classification | Required evidence | Appendix treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Architect of capture** | Created, imposed, or substantially shaped the imported institutional or methodological framework | Name in body table; explain and criticize the act |
| **Custodian of the imported framework** | Held decision-making authority and affirmatively renewed the framework through editions, curricula, policy, or project direction | Name in body table; document each renewal |
| **Public advocate of the pyramid's account** | Published or taught the relevant PIE, chronology, codifier, botanical, or historical-principles claim | Name in body table when the publication materially affected the institution |
| **Contributor within the machinery** | Supplied scholarship or editorial labor but left no evidence of controlling or endorsing the framework | Record in research ledger; do not accuse |
| **Mixed record** | Advanced part of the pyramid's account while also preserving, editing, teaching, or defending important Sanskrit material | Present both sides before judgment |
| **Resister or reformer** | Challenged the imported framework or restored Sanskrit's internal categories | Name and praise where the evidence supports it |
| **Outside relevant responsibility** | Worked at the institution without authority over the Sanskrit project | Exclude from the appendix roster |
| **Insufficient record** | Role is known but no specific intellectual or administrative act can be established | Keep out of accusatory prose; retain research note only |

The book may use **betrayal** as its final moral judgment where the record demonstrates that a person entrusted with Sanskrit knowingly subordinated it to the pyramid's categories. The evidence must appear before the judgment. **Selling out** requires stronger evidence of exchanged benefit, patronage, honor, promotion, or funding tied to the act; it cannot be inferred from employment alone.

## 5. Research Sources

### Primary institutional sources

- Deccan College annual reports, calendars, prospectuses, faculty directories, convocation records, governing-body minutes, and departmental histories
- Bombay Presidency education reports, government resolutions, gazettes, and appointment notices
- the 1964 Deccan College centenary volume and later institutional histories
- title pages, front matter, editorial boards, methodological introductions, and acknowledgments in every volume of the *Encyclopaedic Dictionary of Sanskrit on Historical Principles*
- project reports, grant records, government funding announcements, annual reports, and digitization documentation
- archived versions of the Deccan College website and faculty pages through 2026
- personal papers, correspondence, lectures, curricular documents, and published methodological statements where available

### Independent sources

- university histories, biographies, obituaries, festschriften, archival finding aids, and scholarly histories of Indian philology
- records of colonial and postcolonial honors, offices, grants, and appointments
- published reviews of the dictionary project and its methodology
- Sanskrit-internal critiques or alternatives produced by scholars inside and outside Deccan College

Use search engines and secondary summaries to locate records, then cite the underlying institutional document, publication, or archival source wherever possible.

The ledger should make uncertainty visible. A blank cell never becomes an inferred motive.

## 7. Standards for Living People

The 2026 endpoint will include living faculty and administrators. Apply heightened care:

1. criticize published ideas and recorded official actions, not character, private motive, ancestry, religion, or association;
2. use primary sources for the action and a second source for institutional role whenever possible;
3. quote enough context to avoid reversing or distorting the person's position;
4. distinguish project participation from control over methodology;
5. record corrections, later changes, and contrary work;
6. do not use *betrayer* or *sellout* where the evidence establishes disagreement or participation but does not establish the stronger moral claim.

This rigor strengthens the polemic. The appendix should expose a system that assigns incentives and authority, not manufacture collective guilt at the bottom of the same pyramid.

The new section should sharpen rather than duplicate:

- the founding arc in the `deccan-college-founding-arc` endnote;

The existing appendix already argues that valuable research was performed inside the wrong category. The roster must preserve that distinction. Lexicographers who gathered and organized useful evidence should receive credit for that labor even when senior decision-makers deserve criticism for the frame imposed upon it.

## 10. Execution Passes

1. **Institutional chronology:** verify names, dates, renamings, closures, reconstitutions, and governance changes from 1821 through 2026.
2. **Roster construction:** compile faculty and project leadership by phase without assigning judgment.
3. **Published-position audit:** read methodological statements, dictionary introductions, curricula, lectures, and relevant publications.
4. **Authority audit:** determine who could establish, renew, fund, or reverse the framework in each period.
5. **Counterevidence audit:** identify preservation work, internal criticism, reform attempts, and mixed records.
6. **Classification:** apply the §4 categories only after the evidence ledger is complete.
7. **Appendix proposal:** prepare the exact new section and table outside the manuscript for author review.
8. **Verification:** check every critical row against its cited source, with additional scrutiny for living people.
9. **Deployment:** revise section numbering, cross-references, endnotes, annotated contents, and the Source and Reference Companion only after approval.

## 11. Completion Standard

The task is complete when:

- the institutional roster is as complete as surviving public records reasonably permit;
- every named criticism rests on a documented act rather than affiliation;
- contributors, decision-makers, resisters, and unrelated faculty are distinguished;
- the 1821–2026 accountability table can survive hostile source checking;
- the appendix identifies the present offices capable of reversing the framework;
- the moral language follows the evidence instead of preceding it.
