## Decisions and Scope

Work directly on *Atomic Sanskrit: A Concise Edition*. Do not create another intermediate publication. Keep the existing reader's guide and Source and Reference Companion as separate products.

Both the full and concise editions use the exact shared subtitle above. "A Concise Edition" identifies the edition; it does not replace or alter the subtitle. "Distributed" must also describe geography: the architecture connects communities across the Indian subcontinent, north and south, east and west. Explain both its geographic reach and the distribution of knowledge and correction among teachers, learners, and recitation lineages. Do not reduce the term to an abstract absence of centralized authority. Tie this explanation to the subcontinental home of Sanskrit and the later mouth, mind, and order argument, without implying that every region preserves identical lineages or practices.

Retain Chapters 0-20, the seven parts, the eclipse metaphor, an opening, and a conclusion. The concise edition follows the full book's sequence rather than the reader's guide's thematic reordering. There is no separate "Explore the Book" tour: useful invitations belong within the relevant narrative.

Reduce scope, repetition, and the number of demonstrations, not the explanation needed to understand a retained argument. The central argument must be understandable without opening the full book. Readers seeking additional evidence can follow precise pointers.

This plan supersedes the [older concise-companion proposal](as_atomic_sanskrit_concise_companion_plan_codex.md). Its ten-chapter structure, 20,000-word ceiling, figure cap, and automatic rendering stages do not govern this edition.

## Audience and Language

- Assume intelligent readers, not specialist vocabulary. English may be their second or third language.
- Use the reader's guide's accessible vocabulary while preserving the full book's argumentative voice.
- Use sentences of varied length. A long, clear sentence is preferable to compressed fragments.
- Explain necessary technical vocabulary through ordinary language and examples. Do not assume that readers know words such as "semantic."
- Follow the [Devanagari and IAST policy](../40_reference/workflows/sanskrit_devanagari_iast_typography_policy.md). Explain unfamiliar Sanskrit terms without treating IAST as a substitute for Devanagari.
- Follow [STYLE.md](../../STYLE.md), including its preference for concrete actors, actions, and complete causal explanations.
- Draft for this edition from the selected arguments. Do not shorten the manuscript sentence by sentence.
- **Sample-review correction, 27 September:** avoid academic signposting such as “Tamil makes that explanation difficult to sustain,” “Arabic adds another comparison,” and “Latin offers a similar contrast.” State what happened in the language or institution directly, then explain the consequence. Simple vocabulary must accompany direct sentence construction, not just replace difficult words inside an academic outline.

## Voice Is Not Negotiable Through Simplification

The first reader's-guide draft diluted the narrative. Do not repeat that failure.

Keep the original's named actors, accusations, contrasts, and conclusions. Do not replace "the pyramid" or "Western philology" with neutral formulations such as "the familiar account" merely to simplify English. Explain whom a term identifies and what they do.

Do not turn an argued conclusion into a tentative suggestion, remove an antagonist, or recast a substantive disagreement as a neutral difference of opinion without flagging the proposed change. Equally, do not sharpen the prose by inventing evidence, motives, quotations, or certainty absent from the source. Preserve distinctions between evidence, interpretation, and the author's speculation in direct language.

For each chapter, compare the draft with the source and record:

1. Which claims and conclusions it retains.
2. Whether it still identifies the same actors and actions.
3. Which explanations, examples, and established closing lines were omitted, and why.
4. Any proposed substantive departure requiring author review.

The governing check: **Have we made the argument easier to understand, or quietly changed what it says?** Existing emphatic lines are neither automatically deleted nor mechanically multiplied.

## Argument Treatments

Use three treatments for each argument, not each paragraph:

| Treatment | What the concise edition must do |
|---|---|
| Develop fully | Explain the claim, reasoning, and a concrete demonstration at sufficient length for an unfamiliar reader. |
| Summarize | State the claim, explain its relevance and the essential basis for it, and point to the fuller demonstration. |
| Reference only | Introduce what further examination offers and identify the exact destination in the full book. Do not rely on an unexplained conclusion later. |

An argument cannot be reference-only if a later chapter requires readers to understand its reasoning. Promote it to a fuller treatment or provide the needed explanation at the later use.

The earlier A-D reduction rankings are context, not automatic decisions for this edition. An argument's dependency role and contribution to the complete shorter narrative govern its treatment.

The [chapter treatment map](as_concise_edition_treatment_map.md) will record source sections, current thesis matches, treatment, reasons, examples, figures, and dependencies. If the maps omit an argument now present in the manuscript, add it explicitly rather than forcing the chapter into an outdated list.

Use full-book chapter and section numbers with a brief subject label, rather than page numbers tied to one layout. Identify appendix destinations explicitly. Distinguish the full edition of *Atomic Sanskrit* from its *Source and Reference Companion* whenever the latter is the actual destination.

Retain source attribution for quotations, numerical evidence, and historical claims. Reuse the existing research and endnote records, with compact notes as appropriate. A link to the full book is not a substitute for identifying a quoted source.

## Epigraphs Retain Their Argumentative Role

Retain the original chapter epigraphs and use them in the same explanatory role as the full edition. They introduce or illuminate an argument; they are not decoration to be dropped for space.

Keep Devanagari, IAST, source identification, and an accessible translation. Normally place the translation immediately below the epigraph. Where the full book deliberately develops the translation through the opening prose, retain that approach if the concise chapter still makes the meaning clear promptly.

Explain the connection between the epigraph and the chapter in complete, ordinary-language sentences. Do not replace it with an abstract callback that requires readers to reconstruct the connection. Record each epigraph and its narrative role in the treatment map. Flag any proposed substitution or omission for author review.

## Figures Are Part of the Explanation

- Reuse the original, canonical figures wherever they help explain a retained argument. Do not default to new summary diagrams.
- Record the canonical path, source figure number, underlying source or note, and concise-edition placement in the treatment map.
- Explain what the reader should notice and why it matters. The figure must make sense locally, even if detailed analysis is deferred to the full book.
- A figure may remain while part of its analysis is reference-only, provided the locally explained portion is clear and sufficient.
- Keep essential labels, keys, terminology, captions, and readable type. Do not shrink a dense figure simply to meet a page target.
- Reference canonical assets rather than copying them into a second unsynchronized collection. Any necessary simplified variant must be named distinctly and linked to its source.
- Do not impose a numerical figure cap. Select figures by their explanatory contribution and avoid redundant displays.
- Record any figure edits for review. This planning exercise does not authorize changes to the full book's assets.

## Length and Production

**Working target, 2026-09-24: 20,000-25,000 words for Chapters 0-20.** Treat this as a rough planning range, not a hard ceiling. For planning, count chapter prose, headings, epigraphs, and translations within this range; track figure captions and notes separately. Track the opening, part introductions, and conclusion as additional prose so the complete publication length remains visible.

The current full-edition chapter count is 97,098 words using the build script's method, which includes headings, tables, captions, and other chapter-file content but excludes separately assembled endnotes. The new range is roughly one-fifth to one-quarter of that count, not an instruction to reduce every chapter by the same percentage. Refine the comparison with separate prose and caption counts during mapping.

Allocate words according to the arguments selected for full explanation and their dependencies. Some chapters may shrink approximately in proportion; others need substantially more or less reduction. The arithmetic average of roughly 950-1,190 words per chapter is not a chapter quota. Record a reason for each allocation and reconcile the proposed chapter totals against the overall range.

**Four to five pages per chapter is an approximate text-only target. Figures and their captions are additional.** The target is not a fixed quota or a limit on finished chapter pages.

For 21 chapters, this initially suggests 84-105 text pages, before the opening, part introductions, conclusion, notes, and figures. Actual length depends on the layout and the selected arguments. A chapter may need less or more space.

Track body words, caption words, notes, and figure requirements separately. Establish a provisional text-page equivalent only after the sample's layout and typography are chosen. Until then, do not present word counts as measured page counts.

If a chapter is too long, revisit secondary arguments and examples. Do not force compliance through compressed prose, smaller type, tight leading, or undersized figures. No binding word ceiling is currently approved.

Markdown planning and drafting come first. **Do not generate PDFs by default.** Ask or remind the author before a layout test or PDF build unless explicitly authorized. No publication, deployment, commit, or push is implied by this plan.
