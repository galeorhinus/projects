## Purpose

Appendix Part 9 performs work that no earlier chapter completes. It converts the codification myth into a testable claim and specifies the corpus-wide audit required to test it. The appendix should retain:

1. the two claims about drift that make up the codification myth;
2. the circularity in chronology built from features already labelled early or late;
3. the contrasting predictions in §9.3;
4. the corpus-wide audit method;
5. Pāṇini's documentation of bounded alternatives;
6. the Mitanni material as an external witness before Pāṇini;
7. the distinction between Pāṇini's documentation and the architecture already in use.

The reduction should therefore be conservative. It should remove repeated orientation and claims already demonstrated elsewhere, not shorten the audit method that gives this appendix its purpose.

>
> An engineered architecture can allow alternatives without losing precision. The distinction lies between bounded alternatives and uncontrolled replacement. A Vedic passage preserves its selected form exactly. Pāṇini records contexts in which Sanskrit permits another form. Neither fact establishes that one historical stage drifted into another.

**Proposed:**

> Pāṇini documents alternatives through operators such as **वा (*vā*)** and **विभाषा (*vibhāṣā*)**, and he states where each rule applies. This is not the intervention described by the codification myth. Rather than selecting one authorized surface, he records the conditions under which more than one form remains valid.
