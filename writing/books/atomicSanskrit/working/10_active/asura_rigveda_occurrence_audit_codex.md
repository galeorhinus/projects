# Rigvedic *Asura* Occurrence Audit

**Status:** Evidence audit completed and used for the Chapter 3 §3.6 rewrite on 2026-09-02.

## Question

Sanskrit has both ***asu-ra*** and the privative ***a-sura***. Does the Ṛgveda identify which word appears in each occurrence, and can any hostile occurrence be established as ***a-sura*** rather than ***asu-ra***?

## Corpus and Method

The audit uses the VedaWeb Zurich morphological dataset, the van Nooten-Holland accented text, the Rigvedic *padapāṭha*, and Griffith for a first contextual translation. Every relevant hostile or ambiguous passage was then checked against Wash Edward Hale's complete occurrence study, *Ásura- in Early Vedic Religion* (1986). Yāska's *Nirukta* 3.8 and the Kauthuma Sāmaveda *Padapāṭha* were checked separately because they preserve the two analyses used in the manuscript.

The VedaWeb annotation contains:

- 71 occurrences of **असुर (*ásura-*)**: 57 singular, four dual, and ten plural;
- five occurrences of the derivative **आसुर (*āsurá-*)**;
- three occurrences of **असुरहन् (*asurahán-*)**, *asura*-slayer;
- 28 occurrences of **असुर्य (*asuryà-*)**; and
- 24 occurrences of **असुरत्व (*asuratvá-*)**, including the 22 refrains of RV 3.55.

The last two are powers or qualities rather than references to an actor and therefore require a separate analysis.

## What the Text Establishes

### One Rigvedic Surface Form

The 71 direct occurrences belong to one accented lemma, **असुर (*ásura-*)**. Praised figures, human patrons, human enemies, and possible mythic enemies all carry the same accent. Accent does not separate ***asu-ra*** from ***a-sura***.

The Rigvedic *padapāṭha* also keeps the word intact. It records **असुर (*asura*)**, **असुरस्य (*asurasya*)**, and **असुरान् (*asurān*)** without dividing them into ***a + sura***. This remains true in the clearest hostile passages: RV 8.96.9, 10.53.4, 10.124.5, 10.138.3, and 10.157.4. Svarbhānu is **आसुर (*āsurá-*)**, a derivative of *asura*, at RV 5.40.5 and 5.40.9.

The Rigveda therefore transmits one surface form across the functional range. It does not mark which occurrences represent ***asu-ra*** and which, if any, represent ***a-sura***.

### Contextual Range of the 71 Direct Occurrences

The following is a working contextual classification. It does not assign chronology and does not infer morphology from moral role.

**Praised or beneficent divine use — 46:** RV 1.24.14; 1.35.7; 1.35.10; 1.54.3; 1.64.2; 1.110.3; 1.122.1; 1.131.1; 1.151.4; 1.174.1; 2.1.6; 2.27.10; 2.28.7; 3.3.4; 3.53.7; 3.56.8; 4.2.5; 4.53.1; 5.12.1; 5.15.1; 5.41.3; 5.42.1; 5.42.11; 5.49.2; 5.51.11; 5.63.3; 5.63.7; 5.83.6; 7.2.3; 7.6.1; 7.30.3; 7.36.2; 7.65.2; 8.20.17; 8.25.4; 8.27.20; 8.42.1; 8.90.6; 9.74.7; 9.99.1; 10.10.2; 10.67.2; 10.92.6; 10.96.11; 10.99.12; 10.132.4.

**Human lord, patron, or neutral social use — six:** RV 1.126.2; 5.27.1; 7.56.24; 10.82.5; 10.93.14; 10.151.3.

**Clear contextual opposition — nine:**

- RV 1.108.6: the speaker competes with *asuras* for Soma;
- RV 2.30.4: Bṛhaspati and Indra are asked to kill the warriors of the *asura* Vṛkadvaras;
- RV 7.99.5: Indra and Viṣṇu kill the warriors of the *asura* Varcin;
- RV 8.96.9: Indra is asked to scatter the weaponless ***asurā adevāḥ***;
- RV 8.97.1: Indra takes wealth or enjoyment from the *asuras*;
- RV 10.53.4: the *devāḥ* seek to overcome the *asurāḥ*;
- RV 10.124.5: the *asurāḥ* have lost their *māyā* as Varuṇa is called toward the side that distinguishes *ṛta* from *anṛta*;
- RV 10.138.3: Indra destroys the fortified works of the *asura* and *māyin* Pipru; and
- RV 10.157.4: the *devāḥ* return after killing the *asurāḥ*.

**Uncertain referent or uncertain orientation — ten:** RV 3.29.14; 3.38.4; 8.19.23; 9.73.1; 10.11.6; 10.31.6; 10.56.6; 10.74.2; 10.124.3; 10.177.1.

The nine hostile contexts prove that the Rigveda can use *asura* for an opponent. They do not prove a different derivation.

### The Derivative and the Slayer Compound

The five uses of **आसुर (*āsurá-*)** also cross the moral division:

- positive: Agni at RV 3.29.11 and Varuṇa at RV 5.85.5;
- hostile: Svarbhānu at RV 5.40.5 and 5.40.9, and Namuci at RV 10.131.4.

The three **असुरहन् (*asurahán-*)** compounds identify hostile *asuras* by naming Indra, Agni, or light as their slayer: RV 6.22.4, 7.13.1, and 10.170.2. They establish opposition, not etymology.

## Can Any Occurrence Be Established as *A-sura* Rather Than *Asu-ra*?

**No.** Several passages support an ***a-sura*** resonance, but none establishes that reading to the exclusion of ***asu-ra***.

Svarbhānu is the strongest semantic candidate. At RV 5.40.5 and 5.40.9 he pierces the Sun with darkness. The action fits the book's diagnostic ***a-sura***, the figure acting against radiance. Yet the transmitted form is the derivative **आसुर (*āsurá-*)**, and the same derivative describes Agni and Varuṇa positively elsewhere.

RV 8.96.9, 10.53.4, 10.124.5, 10.138.3, and 10.157.4 provide clear antagonistic contexts. Their accent and *padapāṭha* remain identical to the praised uses. None supplies a privative division.

Two external witnesses prevent an exclusive assignment:

1. Yāska comments on RV 10.53.4, where the *devāḥ* seek to overcome the *asurāḥ*, and then explains those same hostile *asurāḥ* through **असु (*asu*)**, breath. Yāska therefore applies the life-breath analysis to antagonists.
2. The Kauthuma Sāmaveda *Padapāṭha* supplies ***a + surasya*** for the line corresponding to RV 7.6.1. That Rigvedic passage praises the *asura* as an imperial ruler and fort-destroyer. The privative division therefore does not identify an antagonist there.

The two analyses cannot be assigned to the two moral camps token by token.

## Consequence for the Thesis

The manuscript should state that Sanskrit has two distinct words while no longer claiming that the Ṛgveda assigns every praised occurrence to one and every hostile occurrence to the other.

The evidence supports a stronger action-based account:

> The Ṛgveda applies one transmitted surface form to praised powers, human rulers, enemies, and uncertain figures. Sanskrit has both ***asu-ra*** and ***a-sura***, but the transmitted form does not identify which word appears in each occurrence. Neither the title nor the proposed assignment determines the side. The action does.

The privative ***a-sura*** can remain the book's diagnostic name for an action that opposes radiance. It should be presented as a Sanskrit word made especially powerful by Svarbhānu's action, not as the demonstrable lexical identity of every hostile Rigvedic *asura*.

## Implication for the Rewrite

The proposed **Māyā and Asura Analysis** should establish action versus faction before it reaches the lexical question:

- Varuṇa measures and maintains ordered space through *māyā*; Svarbhānu conceals the Sun and Pipru fortifies obstruction through *māyā*.
- Indra, Varuṇa, Agni, Rudra, Mitra, Savitṛ, and Soma act toward protection, release, or ordered circulation; hostile rulers and opponents act toward obstruction, fortification, or concealment.

Power does not determine alignment. Its purpose, action, and consequences do. That is the evidentiary foundation for **action, not faction**. The chapter should state its lexical thesis upfront: Sanskrit has ***asu-ra*** and ***a-sura***, but the surrounding words do not always tell us which one the Ṛgveda is using. It should then establish action versus faction before returning to the two passages that demonstrate this limit.

## Reproduced Privative Count

An exact count in the VedaWeb v3 `LEMMA_ZÜRICH` column returned 48 rows for ***ádabdha-*** and none for ***dabdha-***. The underlying root **⟪दभ्⟫** occurs elsewhere in the corpus. The manuscript therefore states the narrow result: the privative participial form recurs forty-eight times although the corresponding positive participial stem does not appear independently.
