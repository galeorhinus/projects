The previous thesis did not remain confined to Chapter 3. It became a standing vocabulary rule, entered the Prologue and Part I opener, supplied Chapter 4's definition of the asuric pyramid, returned in Chapter 18, and carried the Sanskrit-to-Avestan argument in Chapter 19. Endnotes, the glossary, a figure, and planning documents repeat the same conclusion.

## The Reframing

### Previous Thesis

> The Ṛgveda contains two Sanskrit words on one sound-form. ***Asu-ra*** is the life-bearing title used for praised actors such as Indra and Varuṇa. ***A-sura*** is the un-shining antagonist used for Svarbhānu and other hostile actors. The action in each mantra identifies which lexical word is present.

### Revised Thesis

> Sanskrit has two distinct words that share the sound-form **असुर (*asura*)**: ***asu-ra*** through **असु (*asu*)**, life's breath, and the privative ***a-sura***. We do not know whether the Ṛgveda uses both words because the surrounding words do not always tell us which one it is using. The chapter states this limit at the opening and demonstrates it later through two specific passages.
>
> The chapter then sets that lexical uncertainty aside. **माया (*māyā*)** appears in actions directed toward opposing ends. Varuṇa uses it to sustain order. Indra uses it against the **मायिन् (*māyin*)** Śuṣṇa. Hostile actors use it to conceal, obstruct, or deceive. Power acquires its direction through its use.
>
> The Vedas therefore require **विवेक (*viveka*)**. The listener must examine whether an action protects life and circulation or encloses what should flow. The pyramid conceals that act of discernment and projects factional identity in its place. This substitution is itself **आसुरी माया (*āsurī māyā*)**. After establishing action versus faction, the chapter returns to the lexical question and shows why the available evidence does not resolve every occurrence.

## Non-Negotiable Consequences

1. State the lexical thesis and its limit upfront: Sanskrit has ***asu-ra*** and ***a-sura***, but we do not know whether the Ṛgveda uses both or which word appears in every occurrence.
2. Set that uncertainty aside and establish action versus faction through *māyā* and the actors' conduct.
3. Return later to two passages that demonstrate why the lexical assignment remains unresolved.
4. Do not assign individual Rigvedic occurrences to either formation as a fact.
5. Svarbhānu's attack may strongly suggest the ***a-sura*** formation, but the available evidence does not prove that lexical assignment.
6. Reject the claim that independently absent ***sura*** makes ***a-sura*** impossible. Sanskrit's generative architecture and the ***a-dabdha / dabdha*** comparison establish possibility.
7. Preserve ***asuric*** and **असुरत्व (*asuratva*)** as names for a recurring mode of action.
8. Define that mode through conduct, not identity.
9. Preserve the book's central hammer: **Sanātan evaluates the action, not the faction.**

## Held Deployment Example: Power Personified

The stories personify the distinction between power and the purpose that power serves. Rāma and Rāvaṇa are both powerful. Durgā and Mahiṣāsura are both powerful. The continuum nevertheless understands what Rāma and Durgā represent, and what Rāvaṇa and Mahiṣāsura represent. Power does not determine their alignment. Their purposes and actions do.

Do not add this to Chapter 3 §3.6. That section now reaches the action-versus-faction conclusion without another example. Consider it when revising Chapter 4's Sanskrit diagnosis or when the book first extends the Vedic distinction into the **इतिहास (*Itihāsa*)** and **पुराण (*Purāṇa*)** stories.

## 9. Chapter 19 §19.8: Sanskrit Asura and Avestan Ahura

**Location:** [`manuscript/as_1_19_pie_in_sky.md`](../../manuscript/as_1_19_pie_in_sky.md#L294)

### Existing

> ## 19.8 PIE Is a Lie — असुर (*asura*)
>
> The असुर (*asura*) case exposes the break.
>
> The Western philological account relates Sanskrit असुर- (*asura-*) to Avestan *ahura-* through reconstructed Proto-Indo-Iranian **\*Hásura-**. One contested proposal then places PIE **\*h₂n̥s-u(ro)-**, associated with “lord” or “king,” behind both recorded words.[NOTE: asura-standard-etymology-contested]
>
> The word *contested* appears beside a reconstruction that the pyramid nevertheless installs as Sanskrit's ancestor. Uncertainty is not fraud. The fraud begins when the pyramid places a disputed reconstruction above Sanskrit's recorded internal analyses and teaches it as the word's deeper source.
>
> **PIE is a lie.**
>
> The Sanskrit-side chain is internal:
>
> **⟪अस्⟫ (*as*, *to be*) →**  
> **असु (*asu*, the life-breath, "set in the body") →**  
> **असुरः (*asu-ra*, "the breath-bearer, the holder of the life-force") →**  
> बीज (*bīja*) in the proto-Iranian listener's head →  
> **Avestan 𐬀𐬵𐬎𐬭𐬀 (*ahura*, अपशब्द (*apaśabda*))**
>
> *atom → molecule → seed → sprout — life begins*
>
> The Sanskrit side is engineered, not reconstructed — and it is two words, not one. The first is *asu-ra*, the breath-bearer: the holder of the life-force, the असुर (*asura*) the Ṛgveda praises in Indra and Varuṇa. The second is *a-sura*, the un-shining — the withholder representing darkness: the privative of सुर (*sura*), the shining one, from the धातु (*dhātu*) ⟪सुर्⟫. Two derivations, one form. Yāska records the life-breath analysis, and the Kauthuma Sāmaveda Padapāṭha records the division ***अ + सुरस्य (*a + surasya*)***; Chapter 3 §3.6 develops the full argument. **अजः (*ajaḥ*)** is another Sanskrit example of two completely different words that sound identical. *Aj*-a, the goat, the driven one from ⟪अज्⟫, beside a-*ja*, the Unborn of the Gītā, the privative of ⟪जन्⟫ — a dhātu-build beside a privative on one identical sound, exactly as *asu*-ra sits beside a-*sura*.
>
> The pyramid was clearly aware of the Sanskrit analyses.[NOTE: asura-standard-etymology-contested] Yāska, the Kauthuma Padapāṭha, and the Uṇādi tradition preserve them in different forms. Monier-Williams prints the Uṇādi derivation under its *asura* entry, then states under *sura* that the word was probably formed from *asura* as if it were *a-sura*. Its own presses therefore placed the material needed for both analyses on the page. But two Sanskrit words feed no single reconstruction, so the pyramid chose one and placed the contested **\*h₂n̥s-u(ro)-** behind it. One side documents several internal analyses; the other installs one imaginary ancestor above them.
>
> The vivimorphosis at the contact-language boundary preserves the breath-bearer, not the withholder. Avestan **𐬀𐬵𐬎𐬭𐬀 (*ahura*)** is the अपशब्द (*apaśabda*) of *asu-ra* — the organic form vivimorphosed in the Iranian receiving language. The *s* shifted to *h* under the regular Indo-Iranian sound law, the same shift Chapter 9 §9.5 traces in सिन्धुः (*Sindhuḥ*) → *Hinduš*, and Avestan gave the received word its own ending. Ahura Mazdā wears the breath-bearer's title because the breath-bearer is the word that traveled; the *a-sura*, the other word on the same form, probably never crossed the mountains.
>
> The अपशब्द (*apaśabda*) *ahura* anchors the central theological vocabulary of the Zoroastrian tradition. The semantic transformation that accompanies the phonetic one — what असुर (*asura*) means in the Sanskrit source and what *ahura* comes to mean in the Avestan recipient, and how the suric / asuric distinction develops into the cosmic backdrop for the political-architectural argument — is taken up in a forthcoming volume in the ***Second Shanti*** series. What this section isolates is the phonetic vivimorphosis: the same engineered form, transmitted into a contact language as the प्रतिबिम्ब (*pratibimba*) the Iranian religious imagination has preserved throughout its tradition.

### Revised

> ## 19.8 PIE Is a Lie: असुर (*Asura*) and *Ahura*
>
> The recorded relationship between Sanskrit **असुर (*asura*)** and Avestan *ahura* exposes the break.
>
> The Western philological account places reconstructed Proto-Indo-Iranian **\*Hásura-** behind the two recorded forms. One contested proposal then places PIE **\*h₂n̥s-u(ro)-**, associated with “lord” or “king,” behind that reconstruction.[NOTE: asura-standard-etymology-contested]
>
> The word *contested* appears beside an imaginary form that the pyramid nevertheless installs above Sanskrit. The uncertainty is openly acknowledged, but the reconstruction still receives the position of ancestor. Sanskrit's recorded form and internal analyses are placed below it.
>
> **PIE is a lie.**
>
> The recorded contact chain is simpler:
>
> **Sanskrit असुर (*asura*) →**  
> **बीज (*bīja*) in the Iranian listener's mind →**  
> **Avestan 𐬀𐬵𐬎𐬭𐬀 (*ahura*, अपशब्द (*apaśabda*))**
>
> Sanskrit has two distinct internal formations on this sound-form. Yāska connects ***asu-ra*** with **असु (*asu*)**, life's breath. The Kauthuma Sāmaveda *Padapāṭha* attests the privative ***a-sura*** through the division **अ + सुरस्य (*a + surasya*)**. The Uṇādi tradition offers another internal derivation. These analyses show sustained examination within Sanskrit. The transmitted Rigvedic form does not tell us which formation appears in each occurrence.
>
> The traveling sound-form was **असुर (*asura*)**. No traveler carried a hyphen, and the contact evidence cannot tell us which internal Sanskrit formation the Iranian listener understood.
>
> Avestan *ahura* preserves the received sound-form through its own natural history. The Sanskrit *s* appears as Iranian *h*, the same correspondence Chapter 9 §9.5 traces in **सिन्धुः (*Sindhuḥ*)** and *Hinduš*. Avestan then gives the received form its own ending and its own place inside Iranian religious thought.
>
