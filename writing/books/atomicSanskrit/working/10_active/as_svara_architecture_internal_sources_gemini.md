# Svara Architecture: Internal Sources Investigation

**Status:** Research complete

## 1. Executive Findings

The source-critical investigation sought to determine whether the Hindu analytical continuum explicitly explains *why* certain physically possible vowel coordinates remain outside Sanskrit’s reusable inventory, or whether it only documents the inventory and its operations.

**Finding:** The traditional phonetic and grammatical continuum strictly **documents** the inventory and its governed operations. It does **not** explicitly state the architectural or physical design rationale (e.g., collision avoidance, prevention of entropy, or acoustic distinguishability limits) for leaving specific vowel coordinates open. 

Sanskrit's internal sources treat the *varṇamālā* as a given reality to be accurately transmitted and grammatically codified. While principles of economy (*lāghava*) are frequently cited by Patañjali and later commentators to explain the structure of the *rules* (sūtras), they are not cited as the historical or architectural reason for the *selection of the sounds themselves*. Therefore, architectural reasons for missing sounds remain a **Supported Inference** rather than a **Verified Source Statement**.

## 3. Short ए/ओ Findings

**Primary Question 1:** Why do ए, ऐ, ओ, and औ lack independent hrasva forms?
- **Verified Documentation:** The tradition documents their absence in common Sanskrit and dictates their substitution. 
- **The Mahābhāṣya Citation:** Patañjali explicitly addresses short *e/o* (*ardha-ekāra, ardha-okāra*) on the Śivasūtra *e oṅ*:
  - **Devanāgarī:** ननु च भोश्छन्दोगानां सात्यमुग्रिराणायनीया अर्धमेकारमर्धमोकारं चाधीयते ।
  - **IAST:** *nanu ca bhoś chandogānāṃ sātyamugrirāṇāyanīyāḥ ardham ekāram ardham okāraṃ cādhīyate |*
  - **Padaccheda:** *nanu ca bhoḥ chandogānām sātyamugri-rāṇāyanīyāḥ ardham ekāram ardham okāram ca adhīyate*
  - **Literal Translation:** "Well, sir, the Sātyamugri and Rāṇāyanīya schools of the Chandogas (Sāmaveda) read a half *e* and a half *o*."
  - **Explanatory Translation:** Patañjali notes an objection: specific lineages of the Sāmaveda actually recite short *e* and short *o* (e.g., in the phrases *sujātē ĕśvasūnṛtē* and *adhvaryō ŏdribhiḥ sutam*).
- **Status:** **VERIFIED** that the sound was known and physically possible. **NOT FOUND** any explicit explanation for why it was excluded from the general reusable inventory, other than treating it as a lineage-specific variation (Vedic-Bounded).

## 4. Short ऐ/औ Findings

- **Verified Documentation:** Aṣṭādhyāyī 1.1.48 (*eca igghrasvādeśe*) states that when a short substitute is prescribed for an *ec* vowel (*e, o, ai, au*), an *ik* vowel (*i, u, ṛ, ḷ*) takes its place. 
- Specifically, *e/ai* reduce to *i*, and *o/au* reduce to *u*.
- **Status:** **VERIFIED** operation. **NOT FOUND** any internal source explaining *why* short *ai/au* coordinates are left open rather than created.

## 5. ऌ and ॡ Findings

**Primary Questions 2-5:**
- **Why does ऌ lack a dīrgha member?** The traditional texts (like the *Siddhāntakaumudī*) simply state the fact: *ḷkārasya dīrghā na santi* (The *ḷ* sound has no long forms). **NOT FOUND:** any explicit physical or architectural reason.
- **What is the status of ॡ?** It is a theoretical/formal extrapolation to complete teaching rows in later scripts.
- **Does ॡ occur in any received Vedic passage, laukika composition, dhātu, etc.?** **NOT FOUND.** It does not occur in any genuine lexical or governed grammatical capacity.

## 6. अ–आ and saṃvṛta/vivṛta Findings

**Primary Questions 6-7:**
- **Why is short अ described as saṃvṛta while आ is vivṛta?** The internal tradition describes the phonetic reality of the spoken output.
- **How do they reconcile the difference?** Through grammatical abstraction. The short *a* is treated as *vivṛta* solely for the purpose of operations requiring *savarṇatā* (homogeneity, per 1.1.9 *tulyāsyaprayatnaṃ savarṇam*), and is restored to *saṃvṛta* at the end.
- **The Final Sūtra:** Aṣṭādhyāyī 8.4.68: **अ अ** (*a a*).
  - **Kāśikā Commentary:** 
  - **Devanāgarī:** अकारो विवृतोपदिष्टः संवृतप्रत्यापत्त्यर्थं प्रयुज्यते ।
  - **IAST:** *akāro vivṛtopadiṣṭaḥ saṃvṛtapratyāpattyarthaṃ prayujyate |*
  - **Literal Translation:** The *akāra*, taught as *vivṛta*, is employed for the purpose of restoration to *saṃvṛta*.
- **Status:** **VERIFIED** operational reconciliation. **NOT FOUND** any source stating that this geometry-shift was designed *to prevent acoustic collision* at high speeds. That remains an **INFERENCE**.

## 7. The 18/12 and 132-Realization Source Trail

**Primary Question 9:** Is the 132-realization analysis explicitly stated?
- **Status:** **RECONSTRUCTED (VERIFIED).** No single Vedic passage or Pāṇinian sūtra explicitly prints a table of "132 realizations."
- The count is a pedagogical synthesis found in later commentarial manuals (like the *Siddhāntakaumudī*).
- **The Trail:**
  1. **Duration:** 1.2.27 (*ūkālo'jjhrasvadīrghaplutaḥ*) = 3 states.
  2. **Pitch:** 1.2.29-31 (*udātta, anudātta, svarita*) = 3 states.
  3. **Nasality:** 1.1.8 (*mukhānāsikāvacano'nunāsikaḥ*) = oral/nasal (2 states).
  4. Total per full vowel family: 3 × 3 × 2 = **18**.
  5. **Exceptions:** *ḷ* lacks *dīrgha* (18 - 6 = **12**). *e, o, ai, au* lack *hrasva* (18 - 6 = **12**).
  6. Total: (4 × 18) + (5 × 12) = **132**.
