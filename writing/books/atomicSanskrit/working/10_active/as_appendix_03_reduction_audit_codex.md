# Appendix Part 3 Reduction Audit — Codex

**Manuscript:** `manuscript/as_3_03_audiography.md`
**Status:** Executed 2026-09-05. All sections, figures, tables, and endnote markers were retained.
**Current length:** 4,278 words, reduced from 5,205 words.

## Purpose

Appendix Part 3 must establish the following argument in full:

1. Sanskrit selected, measured, and organized sonomers before any script rendered them.
2. An audiograph is a written interface for an architecture already present in sound.
3. Similar glyphs may show contact, but they cannot establish the source of an encoding architecture absent from the proposed donor script.
4. Aramaic cannot supply the sonomeric grid that Brāhmī renders.
5. The durable archaeological record dates surviving marks, not the prior sound architecture.
6. *Abugida* describes the behaviour of visible signs; *Audiography* names the prior sound architecture those signs render.
7. Arabic, Korean, and Sanskrit represent different design cases: codified sound tradition, engineered script, and engineered sound architecture.
8. The East Asian evidence separates transmission of analytical architecture from copying of glyphs.
9. The pyramid places writing at the foundation because a written medium can be bounded, owned, destroyed, and controlled.

Chapters 9 and 13 state shortened versions of claims 1–5 and point here for the full argument. Chapter 20 summarizes eastward and westward transmission but leaves the detailed Korean and Japanese comparison here. The appendix therefore remains necessary.

## Proposed Hammer Policy

Retain two principal lines:

1. **The sonomer comes first. The audiograph comes second.** Use once near the opening.
2. **The adapter receives praise; the architecture disappears.** Keep within the heroic-erasure paragraph rather than isolating it between two repetitions.

Retain the final Sarasvatī-style force of the appendix through a developed conclusion, not a stack of one-line verdicts.

Remove or absorb the remaining standalone hammers because the surrounding prose already establishes their claims.
