# Proposal - Part Openers and Appendices

Proposal only. No manuscript changes have been made.

## Editorial Standard for These Files

The part openings should orient the reader, show the state of the eclipse, and prepare the next movement. They should not summarize every argument that the chapters will make. The appendices may hold more evidence and technical detail than the body, but an intelligent non-specialist should still be able to understand why each table, calculation, or historical example is present.

The most important general changes are:

1. Remove internal production language such as draft labels, editorial instructions, file paths, figure placeholders, and references to `CLAUDE.md`.
2. Replace the remaining courtroom vocabulary with eclipse, diagnosis, evidence, classification, or conclusion language according to context.
3. Separate documented facts from the book's interpretation. A short qualifier such as "the evidence suggests" is usually enough.
4. Break long catalogues into tables, subheadings, or endnotes. The reader should see the pattern before meeting every supporting name and date.
5. Keep the appendices evidentiary. Repeated declarations about the pyramid should be reduced when the evidence already makes the point.

## Part Openers

### Global Structure

The short Part II-VII openings generally work. They show the new state of the eclipse and point toward the work ahead. Their main problem is caption syntax: several captions read like production notes rather than finished prose, especially constructions such as "Three plates...Two plates..." or labels run together without spaces.

Each opener should follow the same quiet sequence:

1. State what the preceding part exposed or removed.
2. Identify the plate or plates now being examined.
3. Say, in ordinary language, what the coming chapters will show.

This sequence should remain prose rather than a repeated formula. The reader should recognize continuity without hearing the same verbal template seven times.

### Overture

The Overture is appropriately brief. Keep the conch, the ongoing war, and the transition into the two opening chapters. The final sentence contains too many opposing parties and functions in one movement. Divide it so the reader first understands the caretakers, then the order that opposes them.

### Part I

File: `as_part_01_wrong_metaphor.md`

At roughly 950 words, this is much longer than every other part opening and acts as a second chapter. It introduces the full plate system, repeats terminology developed in the body, explains motive, and retains courtroom language. Reduce it to roughly 350-500 words.

Keep:

- the epigraph and eclipse figure;
- the first three targeted plates;
- the relation among Sun, pyramid, and shadow;
- a short map of the three chapters in the part.

Move or remove from this opener:

- definitions that the chapters explain more fully;
- the complete list of later plates;
- prosecution, charge, evidence, and verdict language;
- repeated accounts of the pyramid's motive;
- glossary-like explanations of terms.

The opener's job is to show how the first shadow is cast. The chapters can show exactly how each plate works.

### Part II

File: `as_part_02_sanskrit_self_conception.md`

The opener says Sanskrit is "self-correcting" while the part subtitle and wider book emphasize calibration. Decide whether self-correction is a mechanism within calibration or a replacement label. The first is more consistent: explain that recitation, meter, grammar, and lineage enable correction because they share a measure.

"The Sun speaks" is vivid, but the next sentence should identify the concrete material: Sanskrit's own categories and descriptions. This prevents a poetic sentence from carrying an analytical burden by itself.

### Part III

File: `as_part_03_sound_field.md`

Correct the caption text in which `plates:Botanical` runs together. The closing paragraph compresses instrument, field, grid, and survey into one sequence. Give each action its own explanatory sentence: the mouth produces the sounds; the surrounding language field supplies recurring distinctions; the grid gives the selected sounds stable positions.

### Part IV

File: `as_part_04_atomic_architecture.md`

This opener is clear. The final statement that the architecture remains visible "underneath" the removed plates should explain what remains visible: the relation among sonomers, atoms, molecules, and larger expressions. "Underneath" alone makes the recovery sound visual rather than analytical.

### Part V

File: `as_part_05_anti_entropy_practice.md`

Rewrite the figure caption as two complete sentences rather than a plate count. Check the Chapter 14 preview against the chapter's final organization. If Chapter 14 now centers distributed preservation and calibration, describe that action directly rather than listing sound, meter, recitation, grammar, and lineage as though they were unrelated topics.

### Part VI

File: `as_part_06_killing_pie.md`

Rewrite the plate-count caption in complete prose. Replace "the standard question" with the actual question readers have been taught to ask. "PIE functions as enclosure" is too abstract for an opener; explain that PIE places Sanskrit beneath an unseen ancestor and restricts which explanations academia will consider.

### Part VII

File: `as_part_07_life_after_pie.md`

"The explanation begins" does not say what begins. Identify the positive task: tracing Sanskrit's radiance outward and showing what languages can do after PIE loses its privileged position. Verify that the text's description of the residual plates matches the illustration. The final list of tasks should become two or three explanatory sentences so that the part feels like a release into a larger field rather than an index.

## Appendix 1 - Baking the PIE

File: `as_3_01_baking.md`

Remove the internal draft metadata at the top, including comments about compression, appendix voice, and prosecutorial style. Replace "prosecution" and "charge" in the public text.

The appendix contains useful institutional history, but it currently moves among evidence, inference, and motive without always telling the reader which is which. Reorganize it under four practical questions:

1. What did the early comparative philologists claim?
2. Which institutions circulated and rewarded those claims?
3. Which parts are documented, and which motives does the book infer?
4. How did the framework survive after its racial language became unacceptable?

The lists of institutions, appointments, honors, and scholars should become a table or move partly into endnotes. Name a person in the prose only when the book clearly praises or criticizes that person's action and supplies the evidence. Avoid treating every European scholar as one undifferentiated actor.

Sections 1.3-1.5 would benefit from descriptive subheadings. The present case structure in 1.5 is useful and can absorb some of the catalogue from the preceding sections. Reduce repeated wheat, baking, and bakery language once the metaphor has done its work.

## Appendix 2 - The Encyclopaedic Project

File: `as_3_02_encyclopaedic.md`

The strongest material is the comparison among preservation architectures and the three-category framework. Lead readers toward those tables sooner.

Specific proposals:

- Replace defensive constructions such as "two qualifiers are necessary" with two short qualifying sentences at the point where each limit matters.
- Revise any claim that a named scholar "could have read" evidence and deliberately did not unless the record supports that knowledge and choice. Describe the institutional omission instead.
- Replace claims that English phonetic forms "decay" and grammar "collapses" with the concrete changes being measured.
- Clarify what the project itself did not test. Do not turn the limits of one project into proof that no competing specification exists.
- Remove compressed lines such as "One is a test. The other mood."
- Present the chronology table as the book's alternative classification, not as proof that every proposed date was consciously fabricated.
- Qualify universal priority claims such as "the first such system any civilization has built" unless the appendix supplies a comparative survey.
- Keep the practical recommendations, but remove hyperbolic administrative phrases that sound like satire inside an evidentiary appendix.

## Appendix 3 - Audiography

File: `as_3_03_audiography.md`

At more than 6,500 words, this appendix needs another level of structure. Add descriptive subheadings inside the longest sections:

- What Priority Can Show
- Glyphs and Systems
- Place Value as a Control
- What Brāhmī Must Explain
- An Idea Can Travel Without Its Shapes
- Korea
- Japan
- How the Pyramid Classifies the Family

Replace the opening reference to "prosecution." Resolve or remove the public figure placeholders `[FIGURE A.4...]` and `[FIGURE A.9...]`.

The decimal and infinity comparisons are strong because they distinguish a visible mark from the architecture that the mark implements. Keep them. Explain that calling the conceptual step procedural does not dismiss calligraphy, scribal skill, or the engineering required to make a script practical.

Several claims need narrower wording:

- Do not say that every alphabetic system remains at the level of Roman numerals.
- Define the precise criterion before saying that all Indic scripts are audiographic.
- Treat missing early material as a survival problem, not proof of deliberate refusal to write.
- Separate documented Buddhist transmission from the book's inference about how Korean engineering learned from the sonomeric idea.
- Supply the method behind large literacy or population estimates rather than referring vaguely to "contemporary surveys."

The Abrahamic comparison in Section 3.10 should be rewritten for accuracy and plainness. `Iqra` can mean read or recite, the Johannine Logos is not simply "the written word," and Jewish textual authority has a more complex history than a single sentence can carry. A short, exact comparison will serve the book better than a universal claim.

## Appendix 4 - Inventory Atlas

File: `as_3_04_inventory_atlas.md`

Remove the opening reference to a "polemic ladder" and explain the survey directly. Remove internal source-code paths from the published appendix. Replication files can be listed in the Source and Reference Companion or in a stable public repository.

Clarify throughout that the atlas is an exploratory comparison of selected languages. A three-language union does not establish a general law about an entire region, and the result depends on which three languages are selected. Explain that sensitivity before presenting the geographic sequence.

Replace "does not admit" a sound with "does not use the sound as an independent contrast." Avoid claiming that a family label should predict identical sound inventories; related languages can diverge. Replace "geography predicts" with a narrower statement about the ordering observed in the selected sets.

The Korku note is valuable because it discloses a conservative inventory decision. Explain why the chart suppresses sounds reported by Nagaraja and state what would change if the fuller inventory were used.

The final "monotone cascade" needs either a stated selection method and a statistical test or a quieter description: the selected sets show this ordering. The figures can invite further testing without being asked to prove the whole migration argument by themselves.

## Appendix 5 - The Language Factory

File: `as_3_05_language_factory.md`

Remove the internal draft metadata. State exactly what the experiment demonstrates. A systematic replacement of Sanskrit forms with Japanese sound sequences demonstrates the portability of a formal pattern; by itself, it may still be a substitution cipher rather than a new language. Avoid calling the result a "fully operational language" unless the appendix also demonstrates independent grammar, productive use, and interpretation by users.

Correct the following internal problems:

- The count of Japanese items does not match the visible list.
- A worked example says "six lexical primitives" but appears to list five.
- It says five morphemes and then lists six.
- Japanese does have phonemic vowel length; remove the contrary statement.
- The cipher does not keep every Sanskrit distinction if `ṣ` and `ś` collapse or other mappings remain unchanged. Describe the actual distinction set.

The worked examples are the best explanatory material. Keep them and place the methodological limit immediately after them. "Back-engineer" can become "decode" or "recover."

The PIE comparison should not claim that reconstructed PIE can generate nothing. State the sharper criticism: the reconstructed system is not preserved as a community's inherited, self-calibrating language and cannot be tested against a native transmission.

The discussion of Schleicher currently mixes documented access, employment, intellectual environment, and inferred motive. Preserve the documented facts; label the motive as the book's inference. The book's naming rule is satisfied only when the criticism is explicit and supported.

## Appendix 6 - By the Numbers

File: `as_3_06_by_the_numbers.md`

This appendix is compact and can remain technical, but several counts need reconciliation before stylistic revision:

- Explain why one path reports 3,839 *dhātavaḥ* while another reports 2,168.
- The visible category counts total 2,159 rather than 2,168.
- Explain whether the productivity table is a selected sample and why its entries total 137.
- Define the unit used in the 98.2 percent result.

Replace the compressed explanation of *anubandha* stripping with ordinary prose: listed forms contain markers used by the grammatical description; those markers are removed before counting the underlying atom.

Spell out Obligatory Contour Principle before using `OCP`. Separate measurements from interpretations. For example, the distribution can be measured; the claim that it was engineered for acoustic robustness is an interpretation supported by that distribution.

Avoid statements that Sanskrit atoms "never" collapse into irregularity unless the measured corpus supports the universal. Explain correlations in ordinary language before giving the coefficient. Keep the statistical details in the Source and Reference Companion if they interrupt the appendix.

The conclusion currently assumes that naturally changing languages cannot produce patterned distributions. That inference is too broad. State what patterns the data reveal and why the book considers intentional architecture a better explanation.

## Appendix 7 - The Vedic Carrier

File: `as_3_07_vedic_carrier.md`

The three verse analyses and the *dhātu* inventory are the clearest parts. Keep their pedagogical pace and verify the stated totals of forms, atoms, and *gaṇas*.

The Wheeler and archaeology digression pulls the appendix away from Vedic grammatical evidence and repeats the racial-Arya material elsewhere. Reduce it to a short analogy or move it into an endnote.

In the table of Vedic features, distinguish what the textual evidence demonstrates from what the book proposes about function. A rule that licenses a longer form does not by itself prove that meter caused the form. Replace "meter alone decides" with a description of the examples in which metrical position explains the selection.

Replace banned uses of "ritual" with the exact activity: yajña performance, recitation, mantra use, or ceremonial action. Clarify any claim that "only the corpus moves," since the phrase currently leaves the reader unsure whether sound, grammar, mode, or textual selection is changing.

The comparison with English and Chinese should use concrete changes and avoid saying that natural language produces only wreckage. The closing section repeats the body's full calibration argument and can be shortened after the Vedic evidence has made its point.

## Appendix 8 - The Codification Story

File: `as_3_08_codification_story.md`

This is the longest appendix and the largest structural problem. At roughly 7,500 words, it repeats major portions of Chapters 5 and 13-17 as well as Appendix 7. A reduction of roughly 30-45 percent would make its evidentiary argument easier to follow.

Proposed structure:

1. The inherited codification story and its two claims about drift
2. Circular chronology
3. Three possible models
4. Domains, modes, and evidence before Pāṇini
5. What actual language change looks like
6. A calibration audit
7. Optionality and the Mitanni evidence
8. Why the codification story persists
9. Comparison table and conclusion

Merge Sections 8.1-8.2, 8.5-8.8, 8.9-8.10, 8.11-8.12, and 8.15-8.18 under that structure. Use descriptive third-level headings where the merged sections remain long.

Specific proposals:

- Remove internal draft metadata and stale prosecution/verdict language.
- Replace "standardization by calibration" with "correction through calibration" so calibration does not collapse back into standardization.
- Replace banned "ritual contexts" with the exact Vedic use.
- Do not imply that Pāṇini made the architecture operational. Say that his work documented the operations explicitly and compactly.
- Present the function assigned to Vedic variants as the book's analysis when the source establishes distribution but not purpose.
- Replace long name catalogues with notes or a table.
- Use a comparison table for the Vedic, later Sanskrit, Old English, and modern examples rather than six long quotations.
- Qualify the claim that an ordinary Sanskrit reader can parse Vedic material without retraining. A reader trained in Sanskrit can often recover the architecture, while Vedic accent, forms, and usage still require study.
- Remove the claim that English could survive without grammar books in the same sense that Sanskrit could survive without Pāṇini.
- Move the actual calibration test earlier; it is stronger than the repeated catalogue that precedes it.
- Label all test results preliminary unless the datasets and methods are fully published.
- Present Mitanni conditionally if its dating depends on the chronology that the book rejects.
- Keep the final comparison table and reduce the closing hammer sequence to the two or three lines that carry the conclusion.

## Appendix 9 - Diagnostic Vocabulary

File: `as_3_09_glossary.md`

The glossary should help readers recover a term quickly. It currently combines definitions, essays, chapter arguments, editorial instructions, and internal cross-references. Reduce it by roughly 30-40 percent and give every entry the same basic form:

1. Devanagari and IAST when relevant
2. A one- or two-sentence definition
3. The term's specific use in this book
4. A chapter or source pointer only when useful

Use visible status labels where they prevent confusion:

- inherited Sanskrit term;
- the book's English term;
- the book's proposed technical sense;
- ordinary English used in a controlled sense.

Specific corrections and proposals:

- Remove all references to `CLAUDE.md` and author-facing conventions from the published glossary.
- Replace `standard` in the entry taxonomy if it implies canonical authority; use `inherited`, `current`, `coined`, or `repurposed` according to the actual category.
- Split the compressed opening sentence that explains every vocabulary class at once.
- Correct the mixed-script plural `रचनाḥ`.
- Reconcile the stated number of *upasargas* with the list.
- Define *pratyaya* as an affix or grammatical marker rather than always as a suffix.
- Remove terms used nowhere in the manuscript, such as `sonomeron` or `calibrand`, unless the final text deploys them.
- Shorten the orbital/radiance entry or divide it into separate entries for radiance, orbit, and drift.
- Remove any statement that Indic languages simply "descended from Sanskrit" if the book's actual model is orbit, calibration, and vivimorphosis.
- Describe the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* as the major surviving documentation rather than the origin of the grammar.
- Avoid using `custody` positively for *paramparā* when custody elsewhere means centralized control. Use transmission, care, or responsibility.
- Verify the grammatical explanation of *dṛṣṭāḥ*.
- Verify the *siddha/kārya* entry against Patañjali rather than compressing the distinction into two English labels.
- Reconcile the *Dhātupāṭha* counts with Appendix 6.
- Update stale chapter references, including the claim that Chapter 16 establishes *āryatva*.
- Mark etymological claims in the *pratibimba* entry as the book's thesis where they are disputed.

The final conventions section belongs in an internal style guide. If readers need guidance, replace it with a brief "How to Use This Glossary" paragraph that contains no production instructions.
