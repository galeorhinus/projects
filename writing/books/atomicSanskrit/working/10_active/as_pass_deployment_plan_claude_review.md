# PASS Deployment Plan — Review (Claude)

*Assessment of `as_pass_deployment_plan_codex.md`, integrating the corpus audit (`as_svara_architecture_corpus_audit_claude.md`) and the book's naming/voice doctrine. 2026-07-29. Not a manuscript edit.*

## Verdict

**The analytical core is strong and worth keeping. The branding and the deployment footprint are the risks.** PASS gets one genuinely important thing right — *scope is not binary* — and packages the book's already-implicit selection logic into a reusable lens that links Ch9 (sound), Ch16 (domain), and Appendix 8 (declension). But two things still need attention before deployment: (1) the seven-chapter footprint, which risks over-systematizing an argumentative-polemic book into a framework textbook; and (2) terminology overload from three or four coexisting coding systems. (My original objection to the *"this book calls… PASS"* announcement was wrong — the doctrine was updated 2026-07-29 to let the book own its original coinages openly; see concern #1.) My recommendation is to **keep the principle, the PASS name, and the scope-vocabulary, and deploy in three places, not seven.**

## What is genuinely strong (keep)

1. **Scope-not-binary is a real advance.** "A form passes for a *particular scope*" — Included / Restricted / Vaidika / Lineage-Bounded / Excluded — is more honest than earn-a-coordinate-or-not, and it maps cleanly onto the book's *vaidika/laukika* two-domain thesis without importing chronology. The five outcomes nest well: Included (both domains) ⊃ Restricted (governed condition) → Vaidika (domain-bounded) → Lineage-Bounded (śākhā-bounded) → Excluded (no coordinate).
2. **"Bounding Support" over "mitigation."** Correct instinct: the Vedic invariance does not *eliminate* a collision, it *bounds* it so it cannot reopen under new use. This is precise and on-thesis (it makes invariance an active preservation mechanism, not mere immobility).
3. **The paired [ɰ]/jihvāmūlīya and [ɸ]/upadhmānīya analysis is the plan's best move.** Showing that Sanskrit *uses both articulatory regions under governed conditions yet declines the reusable coordinate* is a much stronger argument than "Sanskrit lacks these sounds." It forecloses the "didn't notice / couldn't make it" objection.
4. **The leṭ profile is a clean worked example** — substantial modal range (contribution) vs leṭ–loṭ collision + semantic overlap (load), bounded by Vedic invariance → Vaidika scope. It is doctrinally correct (leṭ is the *chandasi*-only subjunctive) and it demonstrates the method on grammar, not just sound.
5. **Evidence/inference discipline and no-Pāṇini-as-inventor** are handled correctly throughout (Pass §4 step 9; §9 writing rules). Consistent with the book's engineering-thesis doctrine.

## The concerns, ranked

### 1. Corpus-audit corrections that land on this plan
From `as_svara_architecture_corpus_audit_claude.md`:
- **Pluta (plan classifies Restricted "with Vedic examples"): fine as a category, but the RV has zero marked pluta.** Any pluta example must come from the **ritual corpus** (Śatapatha, Taittirīya/Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā, Śrautasūtras — *o3m*, *somā3*, *subrahmaṇyo3m*), never RV 10.129.5. Drop the "3 RV / 15 AV" figure entirely.
- **ॡ and vocalic ऌ (plan's svara candidates): corpus-absent across all 15,900 files.** PASS should classify ॡ as **Excluded / analytical-only** (a symmetry symbol), and short ऌ as a teaching/citation vowel (√kḷp) with no running-text use — with the standing caveat that corpus-absence ≠ universal absence (both are real in the śivasūtra/Dhātupāṭha).
- **The "half-ए / half-ओ in Sāmavedic lineages" (Lineage-Bounded example, plan line 70) is unverified and possibly load-bearing in a way the plan doesn't notice.** If the Sāmavedic "half-e/half-o" are **short-duration** e/o, they are exactly the short e/o that the svara argument says Sanskrit lacks — which would make short e/o **not absent but Lineage-Bounded (Sāmaveda)**. That is either a genuine, elegant Lineage-Bounded example *or* a phenomenon that must be separated from the short-e/o claim. **Verify what the Sāmavedic half-e/o actually is** (short duration? a glide? a notational svara?) before using it — it touches both plans.
- **The junction symmetry limit** (Codex evidence §5.3, confirmed in my audit): the plan's §9.11 correctly says "without claiming a false four-vowel symmetry" — good; keep इ/उ→ए/ओ clean and present ऋ/ऌ→अर्/अल् as the informative asymmetry. (This also fixes the Claude svara-plan's Move 3.)

### 2. Minor
- **The (ख) jihvāmūlīya photo** (`Downloads/WhatsApp Image…`) is a fragile source. The plan already flags "identify edition before citing" — hold it to that; do not reproduce the image without bibliographic identification and permission. A cited printed edition, not a photo, before it enters the book.
- **"Bounding Support" stays abstract unless the prose always names the concrete support** (pitch, meter, the *pāṭhas*, the teacher, the listening community, the invariant passage). The plan specifies these in §15.3/§16 — enforce it everywhere; never let "Bounding Support" stand as a bare abstraction (the book bans abstract procedural subjects).

## Recommendation

Adopt the **substance**, rework the **packaging**:

1. **Keep** the five scope outcomes as running vocabulary and the contribution/load/bounding-support/scope reasoning as the structure of the prose.
2. **Keep** the PASS name and the open announcement — the doctrine now permits owning original frameworks (updated 2026-07-29). Naming is the author's call; the substance (five scope outcomes + the contribution/load/bounding/scope reasoning) is what carries.
3. **Scope deployment to Ch9 §9.8, Ch16, and Appendix 8**; reduce Ch10/14/15 to single-sentence callbacks or cut.
4. **Reconcile the coding systems** — PASS subsumes the four tests and the ten codes, or prune.
5. **Apply the corpus corrections** — ritual-corpus pluta only, ॡ/ऌ as analytical/teaching, verify the Sāmavedic half-e/o, keep the ऋ/ऌ asymmetry.
6. **Triage figures to two.**

Net: the principle earns its place; "PASS" the brand probably does not, and it should not spread across seven chapters. Prove it once in Ch9, use it in Ch16 and Appendix 8, and let the *five scope outcomes* be the durable contribution.
