### 1. Executive Summary
The *Ṛgveda* preserves completed Sanskrit forms that demonstrate restricted, repeatable, and generative grammatical procedures. These procedures cover a comprehensive grammatical range—including varied persons, numbers, voices, moods, nominal derivations, and sentence architectures—proving that the Vedas preserve an operational grammar rather than isolated vocabulary. *Laukika* Sanskrit applies this exact same architecture to compose new expressions without altering the fundamental rules. Pāṇini’s *Aṣṭādhyāyī* later made this self-evident, operational analysis explicit, derivable, and teachable. Consequently, the Vedic corpus serves as the invariant grammatical calibrant for Sanskrit, anchoring the generative capacity of the language without implying a chronological or developmental evolution between the two domains.

### 10. List of Uncertain Claims Requiring Further Research
- **Dual Ātmanepada Forms**: While 3rd person plural forms (*tanvate*) are identifiable, 1st and 2nd person dual ātmanepada forms remain mathematically deducible via Pāṇini but are highly restricted or unattested in the preserved Samhita poetic record.
- **10th Class vs. Causative**: Most early Vedic verbs categorized as 10th class (*Curādi*) are structurally indistinguishable from causative (*ṇic*) derivations (e.g., *dhārayati*). Presenting a pure 10th-class primitive atom in the *Ṛgveda* without triggering causative semantics requires strict philological separation.

### 12. Explanation of How the Combined Evidence Supports the Claim
The compiled evidence demonstrates that the *Ṛgveda* contains highly engineered outputs (such as reduplication, nasal infixation, optative moods, and relative-correlative sentence structures) that require a robust grammatical architecture to generate. Because this breadth of variations across person, number, voice, and tense is demonstrably present, the Vedas preserve not just isolated "primitive" words, but the operational grammatical rules themselves. *Laukika* Sanskrit draws on this exact same operational pool (the same activation procedures, the same *vibhaktis*) to generate new expressions. Therefore, the Vedas function as the calibrant: they provide the fixed, observable standard against which the continuing generativity of the language is checked, and Pāṇini’s role was simply to map this existing, evident landscape into an explicit analytical framework.
