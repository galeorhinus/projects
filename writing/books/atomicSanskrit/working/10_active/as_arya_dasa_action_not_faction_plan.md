# Arya and Dasa: Action, Not Faction

**Status:** Future work. No manuscript changes approved.

## Purpose

Test the pyramid's presentation of the Rigvedic ***आर्य (ārya)*** and ***दास/दस्यु (dāsa/dasyu)*** as two permanent factions. Examine the actual battles and determine whether the mantras place actors on opposing sides because of ancestry or because of what they do.

The working thesis is sharper than a simple *ārya-versus-dāsa* reversal:

> The decisive opposition is not *ārya* against *dāsa*. It is ***स्वस्ति (svasti)*** against ***वृत्र (vṛtra)***, disciplined participation against obstruction, and circulation against containment. An *ārya* who obstructs occupies the antagonist's position. A person called *dāsa* does not become an antagonist until the action described in the mantra places him there.

## Distinctions to Preserve

1. ***Dāsa* is not automatically *dasyu*.** A *dāsa* can identify a community, affiliation, or social position. Its meaning must be established from the passage.
2. ***Dasyu* often carries an action-based judgment.** The surrounding language describes refusal, obstruction, attack, concealment, withholding, or rejection of a shared discipline.
3. ***Ārya* is not an automatic protagonist.** Several mantras explicitly recognize *ārya* obstructors or attackers.
4. ***Ārya/dāsa* is not equivalent to *deva/a-sura*.** The terms can overlap within a particular conflict, but they operate at different scales and cannot be converted into two permanent camps.
5. **The Veda usually describes conduct rather than private psychology.** Infer containment or domination only where the action supports that inference. Do not manufacture a single motive for every *dāsa* or *dasyu*.

## Dasa Across the Two Domains

The *vaidika* and *laukika* domains use the same sound-form **दास (*dāsa*)**, but they do not always use it for the same relationship. Their meanings overlap, while their dominant uses differ.

### In the Vaidika Domain

***Dāsa*** has several contextual uses:

1. **An obstructor or opposing community.** RV 6.22.10 calls both *dāsa* and *ārya* actors ***वृत्रा (*vṛtrā*)*** when they obstruct ***स्वस्ति (*svasti*)***.
2. **Someone in a serving relationship.** RV 10.62.10 uses ***दासा (*dāsā*)*** beside ***परिविषे (*pariviṣe*, to serve)***. The service meaning therefore already exists in the Veda; the *laukika* domain did not need to invent it.
3. **A sound-form within personal names.** Divodāsa, Sudās, and Trasadasyu occur as respected figures. These names warn against treating the sound *dāsa* as an automatic declaration of antagonism. Verify the derivation of every name before using it as semantic evidence.

***Dasyu*** is more consistently tied to conduct. Its surrounding words describe obstruction, refusal, attack, withholding, or departure from shared discipline.

### In the Laukika Domain

***Dāsa*** most commonly identifies a relationship of service or dependence:

- a servant or attendant;
- an enslaved or bonded person in legal and political texts;
- a devotee who chooses service;
- a component in personal names such as Rāmadāsa.

The laukika range extends from coerced subordination to chosen devotion. The word therefore does not carry one moral judgment even within the laukika domain.

### What the Evidence Does Not Yet Decide

Do not claim that the hostile Vedic *dāsa* evolved into the laukika servant. The service meaning already appears in the Ṛgveda. That evolutionary account would impose a botanical chronology on meanings that coexist within the Veda.

Do not claim yet that every occurrence belongs to one word or that several unrelated words happen to sound alike. The research must test three possibilities:

1. one word with several contextual applications;
2. several derivations converging on the same sound-form;
3. an overlapping combination of both.

### Etymology Research Required

- Find every discussion of ***दास (*dāsa*)*** and ***दस्यु (*dasyu*)*** in Yāska's ***निरुक्त (*Nirukta*)*** and the surviving commentaries on it. Record the complete Sanskrit passage, padaccheda, derivation, and immediate context.
- Search the *Nighaṇṭu*, grammatical commentaries, Uṇādi material, traditional kośas, and other Indic analytical sources for proposed derivations of *dāsa* and *dasyu*.
- Keep the Vedic adversarial use, the Vedic service use, the laukika servant or bonded-person use, and the devotional use separate while evaluating each proposed derivation.
- Determine whether the continuum derives several words that converge on the sound-form *dāsa*, or gives one derivation broad enough to support several relationships.
- Compare the Indic derivations with the pyramid's proposed Iranian and PIE cognates only after the Sanskrit-side analysis is complete. Do not allow a proposed external cognate to decide the Sanskrit meaning before the mantra's action and the continuum's derivations have been examined.
- Create an evidence table with these columns: source, Sanskrit passage, proposed *dhātuḥ*, affix or operation, resulting meaning, Vedic passage supported, laukika use supported, and confidence.

The working formulation is:

> Sanskrit uses **दास (*dāsa*)** for more than one relationship. In laukika Sanskrit, service and dependence became its most familiar uses. In the Vedas, the mantra identifies the active meaning. When a *dāsa* is called a ***वृत्र (*vṛtra*)***, his obstruction makes him an antagonist. When two men are described as ***दासा परिविषे (*dāsā pariviṣe*)***, their service identifies the meaning. The sound-form alone does not decide.

## Passage Group 1: Either Arya or Dasa Can Obstruct

These passages provide the strongest evidence against a permanent moral or racial binary:

| Passage | Language to analyze | Preliminary significance |
|---|---|---|
| RV 6.22.10 | ***दासानि आर्याणि वृत्रा (dāsāni āryāṇi vṛtrā)*** | Both *dāsa* and *ārya* can obstruct ***स्वस्ति (svasti)***. |
| RV 6.33.3 | ***उभयाम् अमित्रान् ... दासा वृत्राणि आर्या च*** | Both can become *amitra* and *vṛtra*. |
| RV 6.60.6 | ***वृत्राणि आर्या ... दासानि ... विश्वा द्विषः*** | The mantra gathers *ārya* and *dāsa* obstructions under all hostile forces. |
| RV 7.83.1 | ***दासा च वृत्रा ... आर्याणि च*** | Indra and Varuṇa oppose obstructors from either affiliation while protecting Sudās. |
| RV 10.38.3 | ***दास आर्यो वा ... अदेवः ... युधये*** | The actor may be *dāsa* or *ārya*; his preparation for attack places him on the opposing side. |
| RV 10.69.6 | ***दासा वृत्राणि आर्या*** | Agni defeats both *dāsa* and *ārya* obstructions. |
| RV 10.83.1 | ***सह्याम दासम् आर्यम्*** | The speaker seeks strength to overcome either. The surrounding passage must establish why. |
| RV 10.102.3 | ***दासस्य वा ... आर्यस्य वा*** | Indra turns away the weapon of an assailant, whatever the attacker's affiliation. |

### Research Required

- Produce padaccheda, grammatical analysis, and plain translations for all eight passages.
- Verify whether ***vṛtra/vṛtrāṇi*** functions as an explicit description of obstruction in each passage rather than as a conventional label for an enemy.
- Read each complete sūkta. Record the action that precedes the battle, what the antagonist blocks or attacks, and what Indra, Agni, Varuṇa, or Manyu restores.
- Compare traditional explanations with modern translations. Record where a translation reduces *vṛtra* to the generic word *enemy* and thereby hides the action.

## Passage Group 2: Arya and Dasyu Appear on Opposing Sides

These passages require close contextual analysis because they can look factional when quoted alone:

| Passage | Immediate contrast | Action to test |
|---|---|---|
| RV 1.51.8-9 | *ārya* and *dasyu* | ***वि जानीहि (vi jānīhi)*** asks for discernment; *vrata/avrata* and *anuvrata/apavrata* may supply the criterion. |
| RV 1.103.3-4 | weapon directed at *dasyu*; *ārya* strength increased | Determine what the broken enclosures contained and what their opening restored. |
| RV 2.11.18-19 | light opened for *ārya*; *dasyu* brought down | Test whether release of light supplies the action-based distinction. |
| RV 3.34.8-9 | resources gained; *dasyu* defeated; *ārya varṇa* protected | Determine whether the sequence describes restored circulation or merely victory and acquisition. |
| RV 6.25.2-3 | *dāsa* communities opposed to *ārya* | The preceding words describe rivals, hostile intent, and advancing attackers; verify the complete sequence. |
| RV 10.49.3 | the *dasyu* does not receive the name *ārya* | Determine what action in the hymn makes the designation inappropriate. |

### Research Required

- Do not force every battle stanza to restate an antagonist's motivation. A passage may remember the participants while another passage explains the recurring conduct.
- Identify cases where the complete sūkta supplies concealment, withholding, attack, refusal of circulation, or violation of *vrata*.
- Mark cases where the passage supplies only political alignment. Preserve those as unresolved rather than filling the gap through speculation.

## Passage Group 3: Controls Against a Permanent Binary

1. **RV 8.51.9:** examine the apparently benevolent placement of *ārya* and *dāsa* within Indra's reach. Confirm the grammar and translation.
2. **Positive actors whose names contain the same sound-form:** Divodāsa, Sudās, and Trasadasyu may demonstrate that a name cannot determine conduct. Use them only after checking each derivation; names alone do not settle the meanings of *dāsa* and *dasyu*.
3. **Battles containing participants from more than one affiliation:** determine whether the Ṛgveda itself calls members of conventionally classified *ārya* communities *dasyu*, or whether that claim comes from later historical identification.
4. **RV 10.22.8 and RV 7.6.3:** retain these as the clearest inventories of *dasyu* conduct: ***akarmā, amantuḥ, anyavrataḥ, amānuṣaḥ, akratu, aśraddha, avṛdha,* and *ayajña***. Translate each term through concrete conduct rather than academic labels.

## Questions the Completed Study Must Answer

1. When the Ṛgveda places *ārya* and *dāsa/dasyu* on opposite sides, what action establishes the division?
2. How often can either an *ārya* or a *dāsa* become the named obstruction or attacker?
3. Does *dasyu* function primarily as a judgment about conduct while *dāsa* remains more dependent on context?
4. Where does the text leave the cause of a battle unstated?
5. Which translations replace ***vṛtra, avrata, adeva, ayajña,*** or another specific description with generic words such as *enemy, hostile,* or *non-Aryan*?
6. How did the pyramid merge *dāsa* and *dasyu*, convert contextual battles into a hereditary opposition, and attach that opposition to the Racial Arya Thesis?
7. How does the distribution of *dāsa* differ across the *vaidika* and *laukika* domains without forcing those differences into a chronology of semantic evolution?
8. Does Sanskrit derive more than one word that reaches the sound-form *dāsa*, or does one word support several relationships through context?
