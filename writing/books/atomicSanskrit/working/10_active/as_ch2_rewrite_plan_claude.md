# Chapter 2 Rewrite Plan — *Category Theft and Āsurī Māyā*

**Created:** 2026-08-23
**Status:** plan, not yet executed
**Current chapter:** `manuscript/as_1_02_botanical.md` — 9 sections, 3 figures, ~4,785 words
**Origin:** a working session that began with "Figure 2.2 says Arabic and Hebrew have low

---

## 1. What is wrong with the chapter now

The chapter is a complete account of the **mechanism** of category theft — the three
claims, the botanical fold, the *dhātuḥ* mistranslation — attached to a motive that
loops back into itself. Its closing sentence is:

> "Neither reversal was accidental. The pyramid needed both of them to preserve its
> story of PIE ancestry and Pāṇini's late imposition of order."

That answers *why steal the category?* with *to protect the story*, and never says why
the story was wanted. So the chapter has no purpose beyond internal consistency.

**The consequence is structural, not cosmetic.** Because the motive is missing, the
chapter has to find a *linguistic* differentiator for Sanskrit, and it reaches for
**generativity** — which cannot carry the weight. Semitic root-and-pattern morphology
is highly generative; so is Sanskrit. The result is Figure 2.2 filing the Arabic and
Hebrew liturgical forms under low generativity, which is both false and, worse,
self-defeating: it dismisses the nearest structural analogue to the *dhātuḥ* thesis by
mis-describing it. Any reader who knows Arabic morphology finds that in a minute.

**The chapter also never connects to the book's own frame.** `two architectures of
order` appears in the Preface (line 45) and the Epilogue (line 101). Ch 2 has zero
occurrences, despite being the one chapter that puts Sanskrit, Arabic, Hebrew, Latin,
and Greek on a single chart.

## 2. The thesis the chapter is supposed to serve

**Invariance by calibration.**

- **Invariance** — because defeating *asat* requires knowledge of the past. A record
  that drifts cannot be used to detect a lie told about it. Invariance is the
  precondition for truth-checking across time, which is what makes it worth its cost.
- **Calibration** — because *sat* is what **is**, not what was decreed. An authorized
  standard is invariant only while its authority holds and only by that authority's
  leave. A calibrant is invariant because it is what it is: checkable by anyone,
  ownable by no one.

The contrast that follows is **invariance by calibration** versus **invariance by
authority**. Both produce invariance. Only one produces it without an apex.

Ch 3 is titled *Motive and Method* and already carries the deep motive. §3.5:

> "The containment protects more than a metaphor. It protects a theory of authority. If

**Settled split:**

**Hard constraint.** Ch 2 must not state or paraphrase *"calibration makes the apex

**The test:** after Ch 2 a reader can say *what was done to them*. Only after Ch 3 can
they say *why it could not have been otherwise*.

## 4. The opening — test the pyramid's own hypothesis

The strongest available opening does not ask the reader to accept a new frame. It takes
the standard account's implied claim, treats it as a general hypothesis, and lets the
evidence falsify it.

**Sequence:**

1. **The pyramid's claim.** Sanskrit was a natural language that drifted until Pāṇini
   codified it; after codification it stopped drifting.
2. **Arabic appears to confirm it.** Classical Arabic was codified and holds, while the
   spoken Arabics went their own ways.
3. **State the hypothesis plainly: codification produces invariance.**
4. **Tamil falsifies it.** The *Tolkāppiyam* specifies Tamil's sounds, word-formation,
   and poetics — a comprehensive grammar earlier than almost any language receives one.
   Tamil changed regardless. A Tamil speaker today cannot read the Sangam poems without
   study.
5. **Latin falsifies it.** Codified by Donatus and Priscian, and the spoken forms became
   French, Spanish, and Italian regardless.
6. **Narrow it.** Arabic holds by an authorized text and certified transmitters. Tamil
   had nothing enforcing. **So why did Sanskrit not drift?**
7. **The taxonomy is the instrument** needed to answer — which is what licenses the four
   quadrants rather than presenting them as a given.
8. **The turn:** and it explains why Britain, holding total political and economic
   control, still needed the category.

**Why this order.** Tamil eliminates the obvious answer (*"Sanskrit had a grammar"*)
using a subcontinental peer rather than a foreign rival, which keeps the comparison
clear of the manufactured Tamil–Sanskrit antagonism. Latin eliminates it again from the
European side. Arabic eliminates the flattering answer (*"only Sanskrit resisted
change"*). By the third question the reader has watched two explanations fail and holds
a genuine puzzle — which is also what makes the *māyā* epigraph land, since the reader
reaches the theft already knowing the standard account cannot be right.

## 5. The comparative table the chapter is built on

All four have a comprehensive indigenous grammar. All four have a revered corpus. **Both
are held constant, so neither can be the explanation.** The only thing that varies is
what makes the corpus binding on the living language.

| | Grammar | Corpus | What binds language to corpus | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamil** | *Tolkāppiyam* | Sangam | — | drift; corpus recovered from palm-leaf manuscript in the 1880s |
| **Latin** | Donatus, Priscian | Virgil, Cicero, the Vulgate | authority (Church, then scholarly convention) | spoken forms became Romance; the preserved form stayed generative for a millennium, then petrified |
| **Classical Arabic** | Sībawayh's *Kitāb* | the Quran | authority (authorized text, canonized readings, certified transmitters) | holds |
| **Sanskrit** | *Aṣṭādhyāyī* | the Vedas as *śruti* | *śruti* — exact reproduction, obligatory, checksummed | holds, with no apex |

This is an argument in experimental form — one variable isolated across four cases —
rather than an assertion. It is the strongest shape the thesis has taken.

**Latin's specific job.** Latin is not redundant with Arabic; it is the **long-run test
of authority-binding**, and the only case where the run is long enough to see the
outcome. Authority-bound invariance held brilliantly for a thousand years and then
stopped. That converts the claim from a preference into a **failure-mode argument**:
authority-binding works until the authority does not. Ch 2 §2.3 already predicts exactly
this for MSA — *"a generative but gated literary Latin. If it follows Latin's path, it
will eventually petrify."* Latin is the evidence for the book's own prediction.

**Tamil's specific job.** Tamil establishes **drift as the null hypothesis**. An
independent subcontinental language with its own grammar and canon underwent unchecked
change. So *apabhraṃśa* is not a Sanskrit-specific doctrine or a convenient excuse — it
is what language does when nothing holds it. This flips the burden: the pyramid argues
*drift is natural, therefore Sanskrit drifted too*; Tamil lets the book answer *drift is
natural — which is precisely why Sanskrit's invariance requires a mechanism.*

**The single most concrete fact in the chapter.** Sangam literature had to be
rediscovered — U. V. Swaminatha Iyer recovering the anthologies from palm-leaf
manuscripts from the 1880s. The Vedic corpus never needed recovering, because it was not
in manuscripts to be lost; it was in thousands of memories with a checksum over it. One
corpus recovered from palm leaf, one never misplaced. That contrast demonstrates what
the *pāṭha* system buys better than any description of the system could.

## 6. Accuracy corrections — required

Every one of these is a place where the current chapter, or an obvious draft of it,
would hand a knowledgeable reader an easy refutation.

| Claim to avoid | Why | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| Arabic and Hebrew have low generativity | Root-and-pattern is highly generative: ك-ت-ب yields *kataba, kitāb, kātib, maktūb, maktab, maktaba*. MSA coins from the Classical machinery — *ḥāsūb* from ح-س-ب. | Separate **Quranic Arabic** (closed text) from **Classical Arabic** (generative register). Only the closed corpora are low-generativity. |
| "Arabic kept the grammar and lost the sound" | *Tajwīd* is an explicit science of articulation — *makhārij*, elongation counts, assimilation. Sound is the *most* governed element, not the loosest. | The *ḍād* is **one** documented articulatory shift, notable because it is the consonant Arabic names itself after. Cite it as what it is; do not generalize. |
| Sanskrit's *vaidika* corpus shows single-form invariance | The Yajurveda *śākhā* branches — Taittirīya, Mādhyandina, Kāṇva, Maitrāyaṇī, Kaṭha — differ in wording and arrangement, not only accent. The book's own `madhyandina-kanva-branch-shapes` endnote frames this as branch-specific preservation. | Claim architectural invariance and exact corpus preservation. Not zero variation anywhere. |
| Only Sanskrit was still composed in | Classical Arabic has fourteen centuries of new poetry, science, and prose — al-Mutanabbī, Ibn Sīnā, al-Khwārizmī, the *maqāmāt*. | Both were composed in. The difference is what binds the register to the corpus. |
| Only Sanskrit's coinages are transparent to a speaker from the past | Arabic passes the same test: *ḥāsūb* (count + instrument pattern), *ṭāʾira* (fly + participle), *thallāja* (ice + instrument). | Comprehensibility-by-architecture is **not** a differentiator. Differences are mode (segmentation vs template) and reach (unbounded compounding vs finite pattern set) — both matters of degree. |
| Latin "petrified and then drifted" | Backwards. The spoken forms diverged into Romance *while* the literary form was preserved. | Latin was codified **and the spoken forms became Romance regardless** — the exact structural parallel to Sanskrit and the Prakrits. |
| Tamil's later forms are Tamil's "prakritization" | *Prakrit* means *fell away from Sanskrit*. The term asserts descent, which the book does not claim and which feeds the manufactured Tamil–Sanskrit antagonism. | *Apabhraṃśa* happened **to** Tamil; it happened **around** Sanskrit. Same process, different outcome — and the difference is *śruti*. |
| "a Sanskrit speaker from 10,000 BC" | CLAUDE.md bans chronological dating for Indic material. | *thousands of years*, or *from before Pāṇini*. The test needs the architecture to be unchanged, not a date. |
| "England" | The East India Company and the Crown. | **Britain**. |

**Tone requirement for the Arabic and Tamil material.** App 3 §3.8 already sets the
register — *"Arabic has a powerful preserved sound tradition… a recitational discipline
that has preserved the Qur'anic form with force."* Ch 2 must be consistent with that.
Granting each tradition its real achievement costs nothing once the differentiator moves
to the binding, and it is what makes the Sanskrit claim credible rather than partisan.
Tamil especially is an **ally**: the *Tolkāppiyam* is indigenous subcontinental
grammatical analysis, which damages the foundational dogma's claim that grammar came
from the Mediterranean corridor.

## 7. Figure work

### 7.1 The defect in the quadrant definitions

§2.2 defines the organic + high-generativity quadrant as:

> "Their speakers respond to new circumstances by changing vocabulary, pronunciation,
> grammar, and usage. **No authority has to approve each addition** because the speaking
> community continually generates and selects them."

That builds the **order mechanism into the quadrant definition**. The cell is not
"organic and generative" but "organic and generative *and unheld*." So any organic,
generative language that **is** held has nowhere to sit — which is exactly why Classical
Arabic does not fit the current 2×2, and why Medieval Latin would fall out for the same
reason.

**Fix:** strip the order-claim from the quadrant definitions. Let the quadrants be
purely **origin × generativity**.

### 7.2 Add order as the mark

Keep the four named quadrants — the rest of the book depends on them — and add **order**
as the mark inside each cell, using the shape-and-fill technique Figure 9.9 established.

Four marks: **none · codified and left · codified and controlled · calibrated**

What this immediately shows:

- **Generative Architectures** holds Sanskrit *and* Esperanto *and* Toki Pona. Same
  cell, different marks — Sanskrit calibrated, Esperanto none. That is §2.3's Esperanto
  argument **drawn**: *"Esperanto's* Fundamento *supplied rules… it would also have
  needed something comparable in function to the Vedas."* Being engineered and
  generative was never sufficient, and the figure has never shown it.
- **Natural Languages** can now hold English and the spoken Arabics (mark: none)
  alongside Classical Arabic and Medieval Latin (mark: codified and controlled).
- **Petrified** means what it should: closed corpora — Quranic Arabic *as text*,
  Masoretic Hebrew *as text*, liturgical Latin, Koine.
- **One mark appears exactly once in the whole figure: calibrated.**

### 7.3 Entries must name precise objects

Not traditions. Once they do, each tradition appears **twice** — once as a closed corpus,
once as a generative register:

| | Closed corpus | Generative register |
|---|---|---|
| Indic | the Vedas | *laukika* Sanskrit |
| Arabic | Quranic Arabic | Classical Arabic |
| Hebrew | Masoretic | medieval Hebrew |
| Latin | Vulgate / liturgical | medieval Latin |

Every one has the two-domain shape. What differs is **the mark on the pair** — what the
corpus does *to* the register. In Sanskrit the corpus calibrates; everywhere else an
authority controls.

### 7.4 The theft, redrawn

Figure 2.3 currently shows the misclassification. Under the mark scheme it can show all
three moves at once:

- **one becomes two** — *vaidika* and *laukika* split into "Vedic" and "Classical"
- **both cross into the organic column**
- **the calibrated mark disappears** — "Vedic" gets *none* (it drifted), "Classical"
  gets *codified and controlled* (Pāṇini froze it)

So the theft is not only relocation. It is **the removal of the only instance of
calibration from the chart** — and it **breaks the pair**, so the relationship between
corpus and register becomes chronology instead of calibration.

### 7.5 Why this solves the handle problem

No cell is uniquely Sanskrit's, and none has to be. Arabic is generative; Esperanto is
engineered; many things are low-variance. **Placement says what kind of thing it is; the
mark says what holds it.** The theft moves the placement and deletes the mark.

### 7.6 Variance is not an axis

Variance is what order **produces**: codified-and-left decays slowly, codified-and-
controlled holds while the authority holds, calibrated holds without one, none drifts.
State variance in prose as a result; do not spend an axis on it.

### 8.1 The 1857 argument

**The strongest form:** Britain held total political and economic control **and still
needed the category theft.** That is what shows it was necessity rather than
triumphalism — territory was not the thing that was missing.

The chain: overt conversion was attempted and defeated; missionary activity was pulled
back; the operation moved from religious conversion to **categorical** conversion —
package the civilization as one instance of a general class.

**Keep documented fact visibly separate from the argument.** Facts: 1857; Victoria's
1858 Proclamation explicitly disclaiming any desire to impose religious conviction;
Müller's two projects. The author's argument: that the second followed from the first. A
reader should be able to grant the facts and weigh the link — that is stronger than
fusing them, and it is the same discipline the Arabic material requires.

**Keep intent structural.** Not *"the pyramid needed to hide it"* but: the classification
system produces that result reliably and is maintained because it does. No claim about
what any individual philologist intended.

**Self-citation belongs in the body.** The book already speaks in first person where it
should (Ch 9's garland passage). A load-bearing historical claim resting on the author's
prior research should say so where the reader meets it, with detail in the endnote.

### 8.2 Müller's two fronts

Müller ran both operations at once — **comparative philology** (Sanskrit as one
Indo-European daughter) and **comparative religion** (*Sacred Books of the East*,
1879–1910; the "science of religion"). The same move in two domains: convert a
*sui generis* architecture into a member of a class. He is already a named antagonist, so
this costs nothing and doubles the evidence.

### 8.3 The *māyā* close

The chapter is already named *Category Theft and Āsurī Māyā* and §2.1 already invokes the
*Vedāntasāra*'s two powers. The close should complete them:

- **आवरण (*āvaraṇa*)** — conceals what is present → conceals that a second architecture
  of order exists
- **विक्षेप (*vikṣepa*)** — projects what is not → projects that Hinduism is one religion
  among religions and Sanskrit one language among languages

The trick was not to argue Hindu order is worse. It was to make Hindus believe their own
order was **the same kind of thing** as the Abrahamic order — because if it is the same
kind of thing, the second architecture disappears and there is only one architecture with
different flavours.

That the formulation lands on the chapter's existing two-power structure without being
bent to fit is good evidence it is the right reading of what the chapter is for.
