## The Narrative That Must Survive

1. The opening asks what freedom and स्वराज्य would make possible beyond replacing the people at the top. It distinguishes imposed constraint from learned restraint and explains why the inquiry concerns the relationship between government and society.
2. Sanskrit is the existing language system through which the alternative can be examined. The wider polity is not presented as intact today. The book does not require prior Sanskrit knowledge.
3. Chapters 0–4 introduce the two domains, memory, the pyramid's methods, and conduct-based discernment. The reader understands what the book challenges before encountering technical construction.
4. Chapters 5–6 explain prior analysis and the difference between documentation and a system that continually detects change.
5. Chapters 7–12 demonstrate the language from the mouth through atoms, words, and sentences. Sanskrit's subcontinental home is geographical as well as intellectual. Recursion is explained through an actual repeated operation, then related to the larger fractal claim.
6. Chapters 13–16 demonstrate the greater engineering task: preserving the language against entropy and deliberate capture while allowing new expression.
7. Chapters 17–20 argue against the foreign-origin account and present radiance as the alternative. Recorded examples, historical transmission, and the author's reconstruction remain distinguishable.
8. The Epilogue returns to the three shantis, the second domain, and the next task: developing polity from first principles. It includes a social relationship that survives individual failure, rather than treating a virtuous individual's performance as proof of an entire polity.

## Dependencies and Production Checks

- **Definitions precede reliance:** calibrant in the opening/Chapter 2; sonance and sonomer in Chapters 8–9; atom in Chapters 2/10; role-ending in Chapters 11–12; Auditure in Chapters 13–14; radiance contact in Chapter 19.
- **Metaphor remains literal enough:** each part names its blocks and actual argument. Do not conflate eleven eclipse blocks, eight tactics, three characteristics of asat, or two operations of māyā.
- **Geography remains explicit:** north, south, east, and west in the opening; regional inventories in Chapter 8; actual lineage locations in Chapter 15; integrated argument in Chapter 17. Distributed does not mean every region preserves an identical recension.
- **No unavailable proof:** Chapter 16 retains its own mantra/meter demonstration; Chapter 19 explains the selected word family locally; all essential grammatical terms accompanying retained figures receive prose explanation.
- **Sources stay attached:** source-note IDs follow retained claims. Full-edition pointers offer more detail but do not replace citations for historical claims or quotations. Final short-note selection is a later build decision.
- **Canonical figures:** both Chapter 2 PNGs are intentional original assets and were inspected. The other selections are a planning ledger, not final-size visual QA. No figure has been edited here.
- **Numbering:** chapter numbers match the full edition; concise subsections may differ. Every pointer to a source subsection explicitly says full edition. Final figure numbering and link rewriting remain production tasks.
- **Scope:** a language demonstration is not automatically an implemented model of government. The conclusion explains the next inquiry from first principles without announcing it complete.
- **Reserved material:** no microscopic-cell / encompassing-being account enters this volume.
