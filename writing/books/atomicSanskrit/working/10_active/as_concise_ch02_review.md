## Plain-Language Rewrite

The author's objection concerned the detached academic voice, not the comparisons themselves. The rewrite changes sentence construction throughout the chapter while retaining every selected argument, both figures, and all 18 source-note IDs.

| Before: first concise sample | After: revised sample |
|---|---|
| Tamil makes that explanation difficult to sustain. | Tamil had a comprehensive grammar, but the language continued changing. |
| Arabic adds another comparison. | Religious and political authorities controlled which Quranic copies and recitations people could use. |
| Latin offers a similar contrast. | Schools taught Latin, and the Church continued using it for worship and learning even as everyday speech developed into the Romance languages. |
| Their role becomes clearer when we separate the creation of a language from the system that keeps it unchanged. | The Vedas let each generation check its Sanskrit against the same body of sound. A grammar explains how to form words and sentences; the Vedas give teachers and learners an unchanged reference against which to check them. |
| Patañjali illustrates Sanskrit's generativity through a story. | Patañjali tells of Bṛhaspati trying to teach Indra every valid word, one at a time. |

The axis explanation now names the actual left/right positions. The domain-versus-period explanation has room to unfold in two paragraphs. Pāṇini's analysis is explained through building words and taking them apart, rather than generic “operations.” The closing application of concealment and projection names what the pyramid hides and what it teaches in place of it.

**No additional argument or hammer was cut in this voice pass.** Academic introductory sentences were replaced by concrete explanation; the earlier omissions below remain the full source-to-concise record.
