## 1. Author-Approved Purpose

Chapters 11 and 12 have one continuous job: demonstrate Sanskrit's engineering by following the ***धातुः (*dhātuḥ*)*** into completed words and sentences.

The rewrite must establish the following claims in this order:

1. Sanskrit extends a *dhātuḥ* into a completed verb, a derived word, a sentence-ready *padam*, and a complete *vākyam* through restricted and repeatable procedures.
2. The Vedas already use those procedures. Their grammar is evident in the forms and relations preserved inside the mantras.
3. The Vedas preserve enough sound and grammatical breadth to serve as the invariant calibrant for *laukika* Sanskrit.
4. The *laukika* domain applies that preserved architecture to new expression.
5. Pāṇini inherited an architecture that was already evident in Vedic Sanskrit. The *Aṣṭādhyāyī* made the analysis explicit, derivable, and teachable.
6. Each larger form retains the smaller construction within it. That repeated relationship establishes the linguistic fractal:

   > sonomer → *dhātuḥ* → *kriyāpadam* / *śabdaḥ* → *padam* → *vākyam*

7. The architecture is radiant because it allows every generation to create and transmit new knowledge without changing the calibrant.

The chapters must demonstrate these claims through Sanskrit forms and sentences. They should not expect the reader to accept the claims because the prose declares them.

### Domain Boundary Approved During Pass 6

Chapters 11 and 12 establish the Vedas as the grammatical and pronunciation calibrant for *laukika* Sanskrit. Their principal examples must therefore use sounds, forms, endings, and sentence operations that remain available in the *laukika* domain.

The designed differences between the domains have a separate home:

- Chapter 16 introduces and explains the principal categories of Vedic-only range.
- Appendix Part 8 preserves the detailed evidence for additional sounds, floating *upasargāḥ*, extended *vibhakti* forms, *leṭ*, and other Vedic-only operations.
- Appendix Part 7 may retain complete passage parsing when a passage contains both shared and Vedic-only forms.

This decision removed **ईळे**, Vedic **ळ**, the **कृणोति / कृणोषि** series, *leṭ* **भवाति**, **कर्त्वम्**, floating *upasargāḥ*, and the RV 7.81.4 analysis from the body-chapter demonstration. Exact snapshots and the removed material are recorded in Lost and Found entry `LF-1112-012`.

## 2. The Separate Job of Each Chapter

### Chapter 11 — From *Dhātuḥ* to Completed Action

Chapter 11 should demonstrate how differently shaped *dhātavaḥ* become completed verbs. It must show:

- the atom remaining identifiable through transformation;
- different persons and numbers;
- *parasmaipada* and *ātmanepada* forms;
- more than one tense or mood;
- several activation procedures, including direct attachment, vowel change, insertion, reduplication, and nasal extension;
- all of these procedures already present in the Ṛgveda;
- the Vedic corpus preserving the forms against which later Sanskrit can be checked;
- Pāṇini making the recurring analysis explicit after the evidence has been presented.

### Chapter 12 — From Completed Forms to the Sentence

Chapter 12 should demonstrate how *dhātavaḥ* become derived words, how endings prepare those words for relation, and how the resulting *padāni* assemble as sentences. It must show:

- *dhātuḥ* → verb and nominal derivation;
- *upasargaḥ* and *pratyayaḥ* extending meaning;
- *vibhaktiḥ* encoding number, role, and relation;
- compounds retaining analyzable parts;
- more than one Vedic sentence structure;
- the same grammatical architecture allowing *laukika* speakers to create new expressions;
- the Vedas serving as the grammatical calibrant for that continuing use;
- Pāṇini explicitly documenting relations already evident in Vedic sentences;
- the complete fractal chain from sonomer to sentence.

## 10. Writing Rules for the Rewrite

- Teach every operation before naming its analytical category.
- Present the Sanskrit form before Pāṇini's documentation of it.
- Use Devanagari and transliteration on first use.
- Pair Sanskrit grammatical terminology with plain English.
- Avoid compressed declarations such as “the architecture is complete” until the preceding examples have demonstrated the claim.
- Avoid academic staging, legal qualifications, and defensive negative contrasts.
- Do not use *productive* where *generative* or *laukika use* says the intended meaning more clearly.
- Do not use *root* for *dhātuḥ*.
- Do not use *stem* where *akārānta*, *ending form*, or another Sanskrit description is available.
- Do not call Pāṇini the creator, codifier, or stabilizer of Sanskrit.
- Do not imply that Pāṇini created every analytical term later grammar uses.
- Do not turn Vedic and *laukika* domains into chronological stages.
- Do not describe a form as Vedic evidence unless its exact occurrence has been verified.
- Keep representative explanations in the body and comprehensive inventories in the appendix.
- Preserve every removed paragraph in a new Lost and Found document before deleting it from the manuscript.
