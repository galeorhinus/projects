# Ten Designed Declensional Variations: Codex Source Audit

**Status:** independent audit of the Gemini research file  
**Purpose:** identify which forms can be used in the appendix now, which forms
are real but still lack an architectural explanation, and which claims require
replacement or further research.

## Method

The audit separates three different claims that must not be collapsed:

1. **Form:** Does the cited Vedic passage actually contain the form?
2. **Operation:** What grammatical relation does the form express, and what is
   its ordinary *laukika* counterpart?
3. **Contribution:** Does the local passage show that the variation contributes
   syllable count, *mātrā*, arrangement, semantic resolution, recitational
   clarity, or another designed function?

A genuine form does not prove every proposed explanation for it. **MAT** is
assigned only when the syllable count or *laghu-guru* pattern visibly changes.
**SON** is reserved for sonomeric selection or distinguishability; it does not
mean merely that two endings contain different vowels. A code followed by
`?` remains a hypothesis to test against the complete passage.

Primary text was checked against the metrically restored Ṛgveda published by
the University of Texas Linguistics Research Center and the GRETIL
*padapāṭha*. Pāṇini is used as documentation of the forms, not as evidence that
he created them.

## Passage Audits

### 1. SG-01 — ***महित्वा / महित्वेन (*mahitvā / mahitvena*)***

**Status: STRONG AFTER EXAMPLE REPLACEMENT**

Whitney §327c documents three instrumental singular possibilities for an
***अकारान्त (*akārānta*)*** word: ordinary ***-ena***, Vedic lengthened
***-enā***, and the shorter ending ***-ā***. His examples of the short ending
are ***यज्ञा (*yajñā*), सुहवा (*suhavā*),*** and ***महित्वा (*mahitvā*)***.

Ṛgveda 2.15.6a supplies an exact passage for ***महित्वा (*mahitvā*)***:

> **सोदञ्चं सिन्धुमरिणान् महित्वा ।**  
> *sodañcaṃ sindhum ariṇān mahitvā*

The transmitted pāda has eleven syllables:

> *so-dañ-caṃ | sin-dhum | a-ri-ṇān | ma-hi-tvā*  
> 3 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 11

The ordinary *laukika* instrumental ***महित्वेन (*mahitvena*)*** would add one
syllable. The passage therefore supports **MAT** directly and **ARR** strongly.
The same short instrumental occurs at RV 6.67.3d and 8.25.18c.

An exact occurrence of Whitney's ***यज्ञा (*yajñā*)*** example has not yet
been established. That does not leave SG-01 unverified because ***महित्वा***
securely documents the same ending.

Gemini replaced this item with ***महा (*mahā*)*** and cited RV 1.165.5. That
replacement fails for two reasons:

- the actual phrase is in **RV 1.165.2**, not RV 1.165.5;
- ***महा (*mahā*)*** does not preserve the requested ***अकारान्त
  तृतीया एकवचनम् (*akārānta tṛtīyā ekavacanam*)*** category.

The appendix should use ***महित्वा*** for SG-01 and should not use ***महा***
under that label.

Sources:

- [Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar*, Chapter V, §327c](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sanskrit_Grammar_(Whitney)/Chapter_V)
- [Ṛgveda 2, metrically restored text](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/rigveda/RV02#2.015.06a)
- [GRETIL Ṛgveda *padapāṭha*, Book 2](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/1_veda/1_sam/1_rv/rvpp_02u.htm)

### 2. SG-02 — ***मनीषा (*manīṣā*)***

**Status: STRONG**

Ṛgveda 10.129.4d:

> **हृदि प्रतीष्या कवयो मनीषा ।**  
> *hṛdi pratīṣyā kavayo manīṣā*

The form ***मनीषा (*manīṣā*)*** is ***तृतीया एकवचनम् (*tṛtīyā
ekavacanam*)*** of ***मनीषा (*manīṣā*)***. Its ordinary *laukika* form is
***मनीषया (*manīṣayā*)***.

The transmitted pāda has eleven syllables:

> *hṛ-di | pra-tī-ṣyā | ka-va-yo | ma-nī-ṣā*  
> 2 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 11

Replacing the final form with ***मनीषया (*manīṣayā*)*** adds one syllable.
The passage therefore supports **MAT** directly and **ARR** strongly.

Sources:

- [Ṛgveda 10, metrically restored text](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/rigveda/RV10#10.129.04d)

### 3. SG-10 — ***अग्ना (*agnā*)***

**Status: FORM STRONG; PURPOSE OPEN**

Ṛgveda 8.27.3b:

> **अग्ना देवेषु पूर्व्यः ।**  
> *agnā deveṣu pūrvyaḥ*

***अग्ना (*agnā*)*** preserves ***सप्तमी एकवचनम् (*saptamī
ekavacanam*)*** where the ordinary *laukika* form is ***अग्नौ (*agnau*)***.
The grammatical relation therefore remains visible (**REL**).

Gemini assigned **SON** and **RES** because ***आ (*ā*)*** replaces ***औ
(*au*)***. That conclusion is unsupported. Both ***अग्ना*** and ***अग्नौ***
have two syllables, and both end in a long vowel. The local passage does not
show a metrical gain, a “diphthong clash,” or an established resonance
requirement. Possible **ARR?**, **RES?**, or **SVR?** explanations remain open.

Source:

- [Ṛgveda 8, metrically restored text](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/rigveda/RV08#8.027.03b)

### 4. DU-01 — ***अश्विना (*aśvinā*)***

**Status: FORM STRONG; PURPOSE OPEN**

Ṛgveda 1.34.1b:

> **विभुर्वां याम उत रातिरश्विना ।**  
> *vibhur vāṃ yāma uta rātir aśvinā*

***अश्विना (*aśvinā*)*** marks the dual where the ordinary *laukika* form is
***अश्विनौ (*aśvinau*)***. The dual relation remains recoverable (**REL**).

Gemini's explanation again overreaches. Both forms have three syllables and
end in a long vowel. The following word does not create the alleged diphthong
collision. **RES?**, **SVR?**, and **ARR?** remain candidates, not findings.

### 5. PL-01 — ***देवासः (*devāsaḥ*)***

**Status: STRONG**

Ṛgveda 1.3.7b:

> **विश्वे देवास आ गत ।**  
> *viśve devāsa ā gata*

The transmitted line has eight syllables:

> *viś-ve | de-vā-sa | ā | ga-ta*  
> 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 8

Ordinary *laukika* ***देवाः (*devāḥ*)*** would remove one syllable. The
expanded Vedic plural therefore supplies the exact syllable required by the
Gāyatrī pāda. This supports **MAT** directly and **ARR** strongly.

Pāṇini documents this form at **7.1.50, आज्जसेरसुक् (*ājjaser asuk*)**.

### 6. PL-07 — ***विश्वा (*viśvā*)***

**Status: STRONG AFTER CITATION REPAIR**

Gemini cited RV 3.59.2, but that verse does not contain the quoted phrase.
Ṛgveda 8.70.6 supplies a valid example:

> **आ पप्राथ महिना वृष्ण्या वृषन् ।**  
> **विश्वा शविष्ठ शवसा ।**  
> *ā paprātha mahinā vṛṣṇyā vṛṣan*  
> *viśvā śaviṣṭha śavasā*

The *padapāṭha* and grammatical annotation identify ***विश्वा (*viśvā*)*** as
the neuter accusative plural, “all things.” Ordinary *laukika* Sanskrit would
use ***विश्वानि (*viśvāni*)***.

The second pāda has eight syllables:

> *viś-vā | śa-viṣ-ṭha | śa-va-sā*  
> 2 + 3 + 3 = 8

***विश्वानि (*viśvāni*)*** would add one syllable. The passage therefore
supports **MAT** directly; **REL** is visible because the neuter plural
relation remains intact.

Sources:

- [Ṛgveda 8, metrically restored text](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/rigveda/RV08#8.070.06b)
- [Vishvasa presentation of RV 8.70.6, including *padapāṭha* and grammatical annotation](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_Rk/shAkalam/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/08/070/)

### 7. PL-12 — ***देवेभिः / रुद्रेभिः (*devebhiḥ / rudrebhiḥ*)***

**Status: STRONG**

Ṛgveda 1.1.5c:

> **देवो देवेभिरा गमत् ।**  
> *devo devebhir ā gamat*

The transmitted line has eight syllables:

> *de-vo | de-ve-bhir | ā | ga-mat*  
> 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 8

Ordinary *laukika* ***देवैः (*devaiḥ*)*** would remove one syllable. This
supports **MAT** directly and **ARR** strongly.

Ṛgveda 10.125.1 provides an even more revealing comparison:

> **अहं रुद्रेभिर्वसुभिश्चराम्यहमादित्यैरुत विश्वदेवैः ।**  
> *ahaṃ rudrebhir vasubhiś carāmy aham ādityair uta viśvadevaiḥ*

The same mantra uses the extended ***-ebhiḥ*** forms in ***रुद्रेभिः
(*rudrebhiḥ*)*** and ***वसुभिः (*vasubhiḥ*)*** while also using the familiar
***-aiḥ*** form in ***आदित्यैः (*ādityaiḥ*)*** and ***विश्वदेवैः
(*viśvadevaiḥ*)***. The coexistence matters: the Vedic domain has designed
options within one living passage, not a single ending mechanically replaced
by another.

Pāṇini 7.1.9 documents the ordinary ***-aiḥ*** operation; 7.1.10,
***बहुलं छन्दसि (*bahulaṃ chandasi*)***, preserves wider Vedic deployment.

### 8. DU-04 — ***देवी (*devī*)***

**Status: FORM STRONG; PURPOSE OPEN**

Ṛgveda 10.70.6a:

> **देवी दिवो दुहितरा सुशिल्पे ।**  
> *devī divo duhitarā suśilpe*

The *padapāṭha* marks ***देवी (*devī*)*** as a nominative dual feminine. The
ordinary *laukika* comparison is ***देव्यौ (*devyau*)***.

Gemini's metrical explanation fails. ***देवी (*devī*)*** and ***देव्यौ
(*devyau*)*** each have two syllables; the first syllable contains long
***ए (*e*)*** in both forms, and the final syllable is heavy in both. Removing
***व्य (*vya*)*** does not make a preceding short syllable light because no
such short syllable exists here.

**REL** is visible. **REC?** and **AUD?** deserve later investigation because
dual final ***ई (*ī*)*** is *pragṛhya* under Pāṇini 1.1.11, but the appendix
should not assign those purposes until the local junction and recitational
effect are demonstrated.

### 9. PL-03 — ***देवीः (*devīḥ*)***

**Status: FORM STRONG; PURPOSE OPEN**

Ṛgveda 9.5.8d:

> **तिस्रो देवीः सुपेशसः ।**  
> *tisro devīḥ supeśasaḥ*

***देवीः (*devīḥ*)*** is a feminine plural form. The ordinary *laukika*
comparison is ***देव्यः (*devyaḥ*)***.

Gemini again claimed that removing ***व्य (*vya*)*** lightens the preceding

are genuine (**REL**), but the passage has not yet supplied a verified design
purpose beyond that.

### 10. DU-09 — Personal-pronoun dual forms

**Status: PARADIGM VERIFIED; PASSAGE SET INCOMPLETE**

The Vedic paradigm distinguishes more dual relations than the ordinary
*laukika* three-form grouping. For the second person, the documented series
includes:

| Relation | Vedic form |
|---|---|
| ***प्रथमा द्विवचनम्*** | ***युवम् (*yuvam*)*** |
| ***द्वितीया द्विवचनम्*** | ***युवाम् (*yuvām*)*** |
| ***तृतीया द्विवचनम्*** | ***युवभ्याम् / युवाभ्याम् (*yuvabhyām / yuvābhyām*)*** |
| ***पञ्चमी द्विवचनम्*** | ***युवत् (*yuvat*)*** |
| ***षष्ठी–सप्तमी द्विवचनम्*** | ***युवोस् (*yuvos*)*** |

This supports **SEM** and **REL** at the paradigm level because the Vedic forms
keep more relations formally distinct.

Gemini cited Pāṇini **7.1.86**. That citation is wrong: 7.1.86 is
***इतोऽत् सर्वनामस्थाने (*ito 't sarvanāmasthāne*)*** and does not document
this dual-pronoun series. The figure should wait until exact Vedic passages
have been assembled for each relevant relation. Claims about “ritual duality,”
“conversational economy,” or strengthened auditory checking remain
interpretations, not verified effects.
