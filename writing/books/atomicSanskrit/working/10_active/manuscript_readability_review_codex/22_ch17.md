The question PIE attempts to answer is the wrong question.

## C17-003 - Explain the comparative method through its proper scope

**Source:** [§17.2](/Users/paragtope/projects/writing/books/atomicSanskrit/as_1_17_wrong_question.md:41)  
**Action:** REPLACE  
**Status:** OPEN  
### Original

> The comparative method is powerful when used on the right object. It can reconstruct sound correspondences. It can group languages by inherited features. It can infer reconstructed forms from recorded descendants. It can explain how Latin yields Romance forms, how Germanic sound shifts operate, how Iranian and Sanskritic forms correspond.
>
> It cannot recover engineering specifications from a surface inventory. That is not its job. It was not built for that object.
>
> The method assumes ordinary linguistic friction: drift, sound change, analogy, semantic shift, simplification, innovation, and inheritance. It therefore reconstructs an ordinary natural language. It cannot produce an engineered sound-grid because "engineered sound-grid" is not a category inside its procedure. It cannot produce a calibration matrix because preservation architecture is not a feature it looks for. It cannot explain why a language would be built to resist the drift the method assumes.
>
> Engineering presupposes engineers. Specifications presuppose specifiers. Preservation architecture presupposes designers of the infrastructure. None of these presuppositions is available inside the comparative method, because the method explicitly excludes them as not part of how natural languages work.
>
> This is the category theft. The genealogical project looks at Sanskrit after the engineering is already visible and asks which precursor could have produced the surface. The architectural test examines how the engineering was specified, built, preserved, and decoded. The two inquiries belong to different categories.
>
> No improved dataset fixes this. No larger cognate set fixes this. No more refined reconstruction fixes this. A better genealogy still does not become an architecture.

### Proposed

The comparative method was developed to trace relationships among naturally changing languages. Researchers compare recorded words, identify regular sound correspondences, group related forms, and use those patterns to propose earlier forms. This method can explain many developments within Romance, Germanic, Iranian, and other language groups.

Its limits follow from that purpose. Comparative reconstruction begins with forms that changed through inheritance, contact, analogy, semantic shift, and ordinary use. It then proposes an earlier natural language from which those changing forms could descend. The method can improve its reconstruction by adding more languages or refining a sound law, but those improvements still remain limited to ancestry.

Sanskrit's architecture requires another line of investigation. Someone selected and arranged the sounds as an articulatory grid. Someone organized semantic atoms into a generative system. Reciters and analysts built several methods that check pronunciation and transmission against the same measure. A method designed to reconstruct an ancestor from descendants has no procedure for identifying those acts of design.

The problem therefore lies in the category assigned before comparison begins. If researchers classify Sanskrit as one more naturally changing language, their method will reconstruct another naturally changing language behind it. The result may describe relationships among forms, but it cannot explain why the *varṇamālā*, the *dhātavaḥ*, the retroflex set, the generative rules, and the preservation disciplines operate as a coordinated system.

More cognates can refine a genealogy. They cannot turn that genealogy into an account of Sanskrit's architecture.

## C17-017 - Explain the two calibrants through their actual work

**Source:** [§17.8 operating relationship](/Users/paragtope/projects/writing/books/atomicSanskrit/as_1_17_wrong_question.md:221)  
**Action:** REPLACE  
**Status:** OPEN  
### Original

> At every point in the Sanskrit continuum, two facts operate together: the Vedas remain preserved, and *bhāṣā* remains exposed to *apabhraṃśa*. Ordinary speech drifts. The recitational lineages do not.
>
> Before Pāṇini, the Vedic corpus served as the primary calibrant for correcting *bhāṣā* back toward the architecture the Vedas held. §17.5 established the operating condition: the calibration worked, but the grammar was implicit — lodged in the corpus and in the disciplines guarding it. Pāṇini changed the operating condition. He did not create the architecture and he did not replace the Vedas as the ultimate measure. He compressed the architecture into the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*, making it usable as a working calibrant for *bhāṣā*.
>
> The pedagogical consequence belongs in Chapter 13 §13.5. Before Pāṇini, correction could operate through preserved Vedic use. After Pāṇini, the same correction also became available through explicit rule.
>
> The Vedas preserved the architecture. Pāṇini made the architecture operational.
>
> By demanding the opposite flow, the *progressive dogma* treats Vedic as primitive, Classical as refined, Pāṇini as inventor, *bhāṣā* as descendant, and *apabhraṃśa* as a stage in descent rather than the tendency the calibrant corrects. This inversion operates as a doctrinal requirement rather than a stylistic choice. As structural opposites, a descent thesis and an engineering thesis cannot both serve as the correct account of the same object.

### Proposed

The Vedic corpus and *bhāṣā* preserve two complementary capacities. Recitational lineages keep the Vedas unchanged as the primary measure. Worldly Sanskrit uses that measure while generating expressions for new situations, which allows usage to adapt without making the language drift.

Before Pāṇini, speakers could return to preserved Vedic use and to the analytical disciplines surrounding it. Pāṇini then gave later generations a compact set of explicit rules. The *Aṣṭādhyāyī* did not replace the Vedas or make Sanskrit operational for the first time; it made an already operating architecture easier to inspect, teach, and apply in *bhāṣā*.

The pyramid reverses this relation. It treats Vedic Sanskrit as primitive, Classical Sanskrit as refined, Pāṇini as the source of order, and *apabhraṃśa* as a stage in natural descent. The engineering thesis instead treats the Vedas as the preserved measure and Pāṇini as the finest documenter of a language already in operation.
