# Sanskrit Radiance Mapping Project

## A Dhātupāṭha and Vyutpatti Analysis of Reconstructed PIE Families

**Status:** Active proposal  
**Book deployment:** Chapter 18 §18.7 introduces the method; Appendix Part 1 §1.5 provides the reusable research record and worked cases; the Epilogue calls for institutional participation. The parallel [upasarga mapping plan](as_sanskrit_radiance_upasarga_mapping_plan.md) extends the same method from Sanskrit atoms to the directional operators that accompany them.

## 1. Purpose

Comparative philology has already assembled thousands of Greek, Latin, Iranian, Germanic, Celtic, Slavic, Anatolian, and other word-families beneath starred Proto-Indo-European reconstructions. The labor that gathered those correspondences remains valuable even when the ancestral interpretation is rejected. The Sanskrit Radiance Mapping Project would place that material beside the *Dhātupāṭha*, Vedic usage, Sanskrit's generated word-families, and the Indic sciences of sound and derivation.

The project tests a different direction. Sanskrit begins as the recorded, engineered calibrant. Its radiance reaches other speech-fields through contact, teaching, travel, trade, and successive waves of transmission. A Sanskrit atom or molecule then becomes botanical inside the receiving language, where pronunciation and meaning continue to change. *Atomic Sanskrit* calls that process **vivimorphosis**.

The starred PIE inventory can therefore become a research index. Each reconstruction identifies a family of correspondences that scholars have already gathered. The project reopens that family and tests whether a recorded Sanskrit atom, its generated molecules, and a plausible route of contact explain the evidence more fully than an imaginary common ancestor.

## 2. What the Project Tests

The project evaluates four connected propositions:

1. Many starred PIE images reflect recorded Sanskrit atoms or molecules.
2. Sanskrit's *varṇamālā* can describe the physical relation among consonant outcomes more clearly than Latin-letter comparison alone.
3. Indic analyses of sound and meaning can classify the changes found in receiving languages, provided that each source is used only within the conditions it actually documents.
4. Repeated families and plausible routes of contact can reveal Sanskritic radiance where the genealogical account currently assumes descent from PIE.

A single resemblance proves little. One family can demonstrate the method, but historical direction emerges only when the same sound movements, semantic continuities, and routes of transmission recur across many independent families.

## 3. Indic Method

### 3.1 Begin with Recorded Sanskrit

Every record begins with a Sanskrit atom from the *Dhātupāṭha*. It records:

- the atom in Devanagari and IAST;
- its *Dhātupāṭha* number and listed semantic field;
- relevant forms in the Vedic domain;
- generated Sanskrit molecules;
- governed alternations visible within Sanskrit; and
- later documentation by Pāṇini or another analyst.

Vedic evidence comes before Pāṇini because the forms show that the architecture was already operating. Pāṇini documents and decodes that architecture; he does not create it.

### 3.2 Render Every Form for the Indian Ear

Foreign forms remain in their original script and transliteration. Each also receives an approximate Devanagari rendering so that a reader can hear the proposed relation without having to interpret Latin orthography as though it were phonetic.

The rendering remains explicitly approximate. Greek **υ**, Latin **i**, Gothic letters, and modern English spellings each require language-specific interpretation. Devanagari serves as a pedagogical sound-map, not as a claim about historical spelling.

### 3.3 Map Consonants through the Varṇamālā

The consonant analysis uses Sanskrit's own categories:

- **स्थान (*sthāna*)** — place of articulation;
- **प्रयत्न (*prayatna*)** — articulatory effort;
- **घोष / अघोष (*ghoṣa / aghoṣa*)** — voiced / voiceless;
- **अल्पप्राण / महाप्राण (*alpaprāṇa / mahāprāṇa*)** — unaspirated / aspirated;
- closure, release, and friction where the receiving sound requires further description.

This map makes a physical relation visible. For example, **ग (*g*)** and **क (*k*)** share the **कण्ठ्य (*kaṇṭhya*)** position and differ in voicing. The comparison can therefore state what moved and what remained stable instead of treating two Latin letters as sufficient explanation.

### 3.4 Examine Vowels Separately

Vowels require their own record. A consonant path cannot establish a vowel path, and Sanskrit's governed vowel relations do not automatically explain every foreign vowel. Each case must identify:

- the Sanskrit vowels within the generated family;
- any governed Sanskrit relation, such as *guṇa*;
- the vowel in every receiving form;
- historical changes documented within that receiving language; and
- any point that remains unresolved.

### 3.5 Use the Fivefold Vyutpatti Vocabulary

The lineage preserves a fivefold account of sound and semantic transformation:

> वर्णागमो वर्णविपर्ययश्च द्वौ चापरौ वर्णविकारनाशौ ।\
> धातोस्तदर्थातिशयेन योगस्तदुच्यते पञ्चविधं निरुक्तम् ॥
>
> Addition and transposition of sounds are two operations; modification and loss are the other two. The fifth connects the resulting form to an extended meaning of the *dhātu*. This is called fivefold *nirukta*.

The five categories are:

1. **वर्णागम (*varṇāgama*)** — a sound is added.
2. **वर्णविपर्यय (*varṇaviparyaya*)** — sounds exchange position.
3. **वर्णविकार (*varṇavikāra*)** — a sound changes form.
4. **वर्णनाश (*varṇanāśa*)** — a sound disappears.
5. The resulting form remains connected to the atom through an intelligible extension of meaning.

These categories arise from Sanskrit word-analysis. The project extends them openly to receiving-language forms as a way to describe what changed. They do not establish historical direction by themselves.

### 3.6 Use Each Analyst within His Evidence

Pāṇini belongs in a record when he documents the relevant Sanskrit operation. A *Prātiśākhya* belongs when its description of Vedic sound applies. Hemacandra belongs when his Prakrit or Apabhraṃśa analysis records the natural change under discussion.

The project will not turn one documented operation into a universal sound law. Hemacandra's examples of aspirated stops moving toward **h**, for instance, demonstrate that outcome in the languages and environments he analyzes. They do not automatically derive Greek **ph**, Latin **f**, or Germanic **b** from Sanskrit **भ (*bh*)**.

## 4. Research Record

Each candidate family receives one public record:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Record ID | Stable identifier for citation and revision |
| PIE image | Exact starred form, assigned meaning, source, and publication date |
| Sanskrit atom | Devanagari, IAST, *Dhātupāṭha* number, and listed meanings |
| Vedic evidence | Mantra references and relevant forms, without imposing chronology among Vedic styles |
| Sanskrit molecules | Generated words and verbal forms that reveal the semantic architecture |
| Sanskrit operations | Designed alternations visible in the language and their later documentation |
| Receiving forms | Original script, transliteration, meaning, source, and approximate date |
| Devanagari rendering | Approximate sound-map for every receiving form |
| Consonant analysis | *Sthāna*, *prayatna*, voicing, aspiration, closure, and friction |
| Vowel analysis | Separate account of every vowel outcome |
| Fivefold analysis | Addition, transposition, modification, loss, and semantic extension |
| Indic natural-language evidence | Relevant Prakrit or Apabhraṃśa changes and their exact sources |
| Contact route | Plausible geography, community, period, and means of transmission |
| Recurrence | Other families that display the same movements |
| Conventional account | The PIE derivation and sound laws currently proposed |
| Comparative result | What each account explains, leaves unresolved, or must insert |
| Status | Strong, partial, weak, rejected, or unresolved |

Every source, uncertainty, and rejected mapping remains visible in the public record. A failed candidate helps define the method's limits.

## 5. Pilot Case 1: ⟪युज्⟫

### 5.1 Family

| Language or account | Original form | Approximate Devanagari | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| PIE image | **\*yeug- / \*yug-** | **\*येउग् / \*युग्** | join, yoke |
| Greek | **ζυγόν (*zygon*)** | **ज़ुगोन** | yoke |
| Latin | ***iugum*** | **युगुम्** | yoke |
| Gothic | **𐌾𐌿𐌺 (*juk*)** | **युक्** | yoke |
| English | ***yoke*** | **योक्** | yoke |
| Sanskrit | **युज्, युग, युक्त, योग** | original Devanagari | join, yoke, joined, union |

### 5.2 Vedic Evidence

Ṛgveda 1.6.1 uses **युञ्जन्ति (*yuñjanti*, "they yoke")**. Ṛgveda 10.101.3 uses **युगा (*yugā*, "the yokes")**, and 10.102.9 uses **युक्त (*yukta*, "yoked")**. The atom and its related sound-forms therefore appear within the Vedic domain.

### 5.3 Consonant Map

| Sanskrit form | Sonomer | Indic description |
|---|---|---|
| **युज् (*yuj*)** | **ज (*j*)** | **तालव्य (*tālavya*)**, voiced, unaspirated |
| **युग (*yuga*)** | **ग (*g*)** | **कण्ठ्य (*kaṇṭhya*)**, voiced, unaspirated |
| **युक्त (*yukta*)** | **क (*k*)** | **कण्ठ्य**, voiceless, unaspirated |

The movement from **ज** to **ग** changes the articulatory position while preserving voicing and aspiration. The movement from **ग** to **क** remains **कण्ठ्य** and unaspirated while changing from voiced to voiceless. Pāṇini later documents these Sanskrit operations through **चोः कुः (*coḥ kuḥ*, 8.2.30)** and **खरि च (*khari ca*, 8.4.55)** under their stated conditions.

Greek and Latin settle the final consonant at voiced **कण्ठ्य ग**. Gothic and English settle it at voiceless **कण्ठ्य क**. The *varṇamālā* makes the physical relation among those outcomes visible.

### 5.4 Vowels and Meaning

Sanskrit **युज्, युग,** and **युक्त** use **उ (*u*)**, while **योग (*yoga*)** displays the governed *guṇa* relation **उ → ओ (*u → o*)**. Latin and Gothic preserve **u**, English *yoke* uses **o**, and Greek **υ** requires its own historical analysis.

The meanings remain within joining and yoking. Sanskrit also generates *yoga*, joining or union, and *yuga*, a yoke or joined span that can extend to an age. The receiving forms preserve narrower developments from that semantic field.

### 5.5 Pilot Value

The yoke family provides the strongest introductory case because the Veda displays **युज् / युग् / युक्**, Sanskrit preserves a larger generated family, and the consonant movements occupy visible coordinates on the *varṇamālā*. Its unresolved Greek vowel also demonstrates how the project marks a gap instead of concealing it.

## 6. Pilot Case 2: ⟪भृ⟫

| Language or account | Original form | Approximate Devanagari | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| PIE image | **\*bher-** | **\*भेर्** | bear, carry |
| Greek | **φέρειν (*pherein*)** | **फेरेइन** | bear, carry |
| Latin | ***ferre*** | **फेरे** | bear, carry |
| Old English / English | ***beran / bear*** | **बेरन् / बेर** | bear, carry |
| Sanskrit | **भृ, बिभर्ति, भरति** | original Devanagari | bear, carry, sustain |

Ṛgveda 7.87.4 uses **बिभर्ति (*bibharti*)** from **⟪भृ⟫**. The word places unaspirated **ब (*b*)** beside aspirated **भ (*bh*)**, showing that Sanskrit controls aspiration as an independent feature inside one generated form. Pāṇini later documents the repeated element as **अभ्यास (*abhyāsa*)**.

The receiving forms remain in the labial region while voicing, aspiration, closure, and friction vary. Sanskrit **भ** is voiced and aspirated; Greek **ph** is an aspirated labial in the relevant historical pronunciation; Latin **f** is a voiceless labiodental fricative; and Old English **b** is voiced and unaspirated.

The vowel route remains incomplete. Sanskrit distributes **ऋ, इ,** and **अ (*ṛ, i,* and *a*)** within the family, while the Greek, Latin, and Old English forms use **e** and modern English writes **ea**. The pilot should map those vowels individually before assigning a final strength.

## 7. Pilot Case 3: ⟪जन्⟫

The *Dhātupāṭha* records generation and coming into birth under **⟪जन्⟫**. Ṛgveda 6.7 uses **जनयन्त (*janayanta*)** and **जायते (*jāyate*)** within one *sūkta*. Sanskrit builds *jana, janaka, janana, jananī, janma, jāta,* and *jāti* around the same semantic atom.

| Language or account | Original form | Approximate Devanagari | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| PIE image | **\*ǵenh₁-** | no settled Devanagari rendering | generate, give birth |
| Greek | ***genos*** | **गेनोस्** | race, kind |
| Latin | ***genus*** | **गेनुस्** | birth, kind, class |
| Germanic / English | ***kin, kind, king*** | **किन्, काइन्ड्, किङ्** | family, kind, ruler of a people |
| Sanskrit | **जन, जनक, जन्मन्, जाति** | original Devanagari | person, parent, birth, kind |

The ⟪युज्⟫ pilot supplies a Sanskrit-internal example of the **ज → ग → क** sonomer path. That operation does not by itself derive the Greek, Latin, or Germanic forms, but it shows that the sounds occupy intelligible positions within Sanskrit's articulated grid. The ⟪जन्⟫ family then reveals the semantic scale: generation, birth, person, people, parenthood, and kind remain connected around one atom, while the receiving languages grow large botanical families from parts of that field.

## 8. First Institutional Deliverable

The first phase will publish a dataset of one hundred reconstructed PIE families tested against Sanskrit atoms. Every record will display:

- the conventional PIE derivation;
- the Sanskrit atom and its generated family;
- Vedic evidence;
- original foreign forms;
- Devanagari sound-maps;
- consonant, vowel, and semantic analyses;
- relevant Indic documentation;
- plausible routes of transmission;
- recurring transformations in other families; and
- a visible strength assessment.

The project should publish strong cases, weak cases, rejected cases, and unresolved details together. Readers must be able to inspect every movement and see where either account requires an unsupported insertion.

## 9. Participating Disciplines

The project requires collaboration among:

- Sanskrit and Vedic studies;
- *Vyākaraṇam*, *Nirukta*, Prakrit, and Apabhraṃśa;
- Greek, Latin, Iranian, Germanic, Celtic, Slavic, Anatolian, and other language specialists;
- phonetics and speech physiology;
- historical lexicography;
- manuscript and dictionary history;
- computational linguistics and graph analysis; and
- information design for sonomer and semantic maps.

Students can perform much of the first comparison once specialists establish the source standards. A shared data model will allow Sanskrit departments to contribute atoms and Vedic forms while other departments contribute the recorded histories of receiving languages.

## 10. Computational Phase

The computational work should test whether a limited set of movements repeatedly accounts for the receiving-language families. Each sonomer can be encoded by its Sanskrit coordinate and articulatory features. The system can then compare:

- place changes;
- voicing changes;
- aspiration changes;
- closure and frication;
- additions, losses, and transpositions;
- vowel outcomes;
- semantic distance; and
- geographical routes of contact.

The analysis becomes persuasive when the same movements recur across many atoms without creating a new exception for each word. Computation assists comparison; it does not decide historical direction without linguistic and contact evidence.

## 11. Expansion

After the first hundred records stabilize the protocol, the project can extend to every starred PIE image for which a plausible Sanskrit atom exists. Later phases can:

1. map clusters by receiving language;
2. compare early Persian, Greek, Latin, and Germanic outcomes;
3. distinguish direct Sanskritic contact from transmission through intermediate languages;
4. map Buddhist, mercantile, and other known radiance routes;
5. compare the age and distribution of dictionaries that moved the asterisk above Sanskrit; and
6. publish interactive sonomer maps that allow readers to inspect each transformation;
7. map Sanskrit upasargas against Greek, Latin, and other particles, prepositions, and preverbs; and
8. test whether Sanskrit's radiance transmitted a productive operating system along with individual atoms and molecules.

The reconstructed PIE corpus would then serve a new purpose: an indexed inventory of possible Sanskrit radiance.

## 12. Methodological Limits

1. Vedic use must precede any claim that Pāṇini created an operation.
2. Pāṇini's rules apply only under their stated Sanskrit conditions.
3. Hemacandra's examples remain tied to the languages and environments he analyzes.
4. Resemblance must be joined by semantic continuity, a defensible sound path, recurrence, and a plausible route of contact.
5. One family demonstrates a method; it cannot establish historical direction.
6. Foreign vowels and consonants remain unresolved when the evidence does not support a complete path.
7. Recorded dates sequence surviving evidence; they do not decide the category of Sanskrit or turn a starred reconstruction into a spoken language.
8. Rejected candidates remain in the dataset.
9. Sanskrit's generativity remains visible. An atom surrounded by a productive molecular family provides more evidence than an isolated similarity.
10. The project does not force every foreign word into Sanskrit.

## 13. Immediate Research Sequence

1. Complete source-grade records for **⟪युज्⟫**, **⟪भृ⟫**, and **⟪जन्⟫**.
2. Verify every Vedic citation against a reliable text and translation.
3. Verify each *Dhātupāṭha* entry and Pāṇinian operation.
4. Ask specialists in Greek, Latin, Gothic, and historical English to review the foreign sound descriptions.
5. Establish a consistent Devanagari rendering convention for non-Sanskrit sounds.
6. Test the record format on ten additional families.
7. Publish the pilot data, including unresolved and rejected paths.
8. Expand to the first hundred records only after the pilot review.

## 15. Intended Result

The project turns the pyramid's accumulated comparative material toward a different purpose. Instead of granting every starred form automatic parenthood, it places that image beside Sanskrit and tests both directions in public. The result will be a reproducible map of where Sanskrit's radiance traveled, how receiving languages transformed it, and where the evidence remains incomplete.
