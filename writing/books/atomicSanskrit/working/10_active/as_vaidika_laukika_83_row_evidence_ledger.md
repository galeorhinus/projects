## Status Key

- **G** — a cited grammar explicitly documents the category.
- **P** — an exact Vedic passage has been located and the form has been checked.
- **F** — at least one local contribution has been demonstrated in the complete
  passage.
- **Q** — the row requires a publication qualification.
- **O** — the next evidentiary step remains open.

`G/P/F` is therefore stronger than `G/O`. A row marked `G/Q` remains in the
complete inventory, but its figure must identify it as a qualified form rather
than a productive Vedic option.

The internal **Q** marker is deliberately broad because it flags a row for
review. The publication figures must replace it with one of four precise
labels:

- **RARE** — the form is real but limited to a small class or a few passages.
- **ISOLATED** — the source reports one or two occurrences.
- **DOUBTFUL** — the source itself doubts the reading or its grammatical
  analysis.
- **ABSENCE** — the row records that a later form is absent or scarce in the
  checked Vedic corpus; it does not offer a second Vedic form.

These labels describe the evidence. They do not supply a Designed Variation
function. A **RARE** or **ISOLATED** row receives a DV code only when an exact
passage demonstrates that function. A **DOUBTFUL** row receives no DV code.
An **ABSENCE** row appears as a distribution note rather than as an alternate
form.

The grammar references below document the category. They are not substitutes
for the Vedic passages themselves. Whitney section numbers refer to *Sanskrit
Grammar*.

## Pass 6: Adversarial Audit

### Findings

1. **All 83 rows have a grammatical documentation trail.** This supports
   retaining the complete inventory.
2. **Thirty-one rows now have exact passages.** Twenty-two rows have at least
   one demonstrated contribution. Some rows count the same passage for two
   related categories, such as SG-02 and SG-15 or PL-13 and PL-21; the figures
   must not present those as independent discoveries.
3. **Nine passage-backed rows still have no demonstrated local purpose, and
   three more have one verified subcategory with another subcategory open.**
   Exact occurrence verifies the form; it does not explain why the passage
   uses that form.
4. **Twenty-six rows are source-qualified.** DU-08 is explicitly doubtful.
   SG-13 records an absence or scarcity rather than an alternate form. DU-06,
   DU-12, NU-03, and several other rows preserve isolated forms. Their figure
   markers must show that status.
5. **Some inventory rows combine unlike evidence.** SG-12 combines secure
   **-avi** with doubtful **-ayi**. PL-15 combines rare simple **-ām** with
   common syllabic resolution of **-ānām**. CL-10 combines a common dual
   pattern with rare **-ānti**. These rows need split notes or subrows in the
   figure source data.
6. **A visible difference does not establish its purpose.** `MAT` can identify
   an added or removed syllable, but `ARR`, `RES`, `REC`, `AUD`, and `FUN`
   require the complete passage or recitational practice.
7. **Chronology language in the source must not enter the appendix as an
   inference.** Terms such as “older,” “later,” “disappear,” and “reanalysis”
   describe the source author's model. The appendix can use the documented
   distributions without adopting that chronology.
