# Paninian Scope-Marker Source Ledger — Codex

**Status:** Research passes complete; manuscript deployment remains governed by
the author-approval ledger in
`as_vaidika_laukika_dual_domain_architecture_plan.md`.

**Research date:** 2026-08-01

## Purpose

This ledger tests one narrow proposition: Pāṇini's scope markers identify where
a particular operation applies. They do not divide Sanskrit into two successive
historical languages. The research therefore asks three things:

1. Which scope markers occur explicitly in the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*?
2. How do the traditional commentaries explain representative markers and
   inherited scopes?
3. What can those markers establish about the relationship among *vaidika*,
   *laukika*, *chandasi*, *bhāṣāyām*, mantra, Brāhmaṇa, and narrower textual
   settings?

The ledger distinguishes the wording of a sūtra from the explanation supplied
by a commentary. It records chronology only when a source states chronology.

## Sources and Method

The complete sūtra text was searched through the
[AVG Sanskrit *Aṣṭādhyāyī* text](https://avg-sanskrit.org/texts/astadhyayi/).
Representative rules were then checked against the traditional explanations
published by
[Sanskrit Documents](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/)
and, where numbering or page presentation required a second witness,
[Aṣṭādhyāyī.com](https://ashtadhyayi.com/).

The three passes were:

1. **Explicit-marker pass:** locate broad and narrow scope words in the sūtra
   text.
2. **Commentary-and-inheritance pass:** verify how Kāśikā and Nyāsa explain the
   scope and whether a marker continues into a later rule.
3. **Architectural-synthesis pass:** separate what the evidence establishes
   from chronology added by a modern narrative.

### Inventory cautions

- A raw substring count overstates some markers. For example,
  ***अच्छन्दसि (acchandasi)*** at 5.3.49 contains the letters of *chandasi* but
  means the opposite scope.
- ***अमन्त्रे (amantre)*** likewise contains *mantre* as a substring. It must
  be counted and interpreted separately.
- The explicit list does not capture every inherited scope. A marker stated in
  one rule can continue into a later rule through anuvṛtti.

## Pass 2 — Commentary and Inherited Scope

### 1. Broad Vedic scope: 2.4.76

**Rule:** ***बहुलं छन्दसि (bahulaṃ chandasi)***.

The Kāśikā explains the operation as applying ***छन्दसि विषये (chandasi
viṣaye)*** and gives forms including ***दाति प्रियाणि (dāti priyāṇi)***,
***धाति देवम् (dhāti devam)***, ***पूर्णां विवष्टि (pūrṇāṃ vivaṣṭi)***, and
***जनिमा विवक्ति (janimā vivakti)***. The rule marks a Vedic setting for the
operation; neither the sūtra nor the checked explanation converts that setting
into a historical stage.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 2.4.76 with commentaries](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/2/4/76).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 2. Outside mantra includes more than one domain: 3.1.35

**Rule:** ***कास्प्रत्ययादाममन्त्रे लिटि (kāspratyayād ām amantre liṭi)***.

The Kāśikā gives ***कासाञ्चक्रे (kāsāñcakre)*** and contrasts the mantra form
***कृष्णो नोनाव (kṛṣṇo nonāva)***. The Nyāsa then makes the overlap explicit:
because mantra has been excluded, the rule operates in both ***भाषा (bhāṣā)***
and ***ब्राह्मण (brāhmaṇa)*** settings. In other words, *amantre* cannot be
equated with *laukika*. Its range crosses categories that a simple
*chandasi/bhāṣāyām* binary would keep apart.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 3.1.35 with Kāśikā and Nyāsa](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/3/3.1.35.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong; this is the clearest overlap witness in the present
ledger.

### 3. Brāhmaṇa-specific operation: 2.3.60

**Rule:** ***द्वितीया ब्राह्मणे (dvitīyā brāhmaṇe)***.

The Kāśikā gives ***गामस्य तदहः सभायां दीव्येयुः (gām asya tad ahaḥ sabhāyāṃ
dīvyeyuḥ)***. The Nyāsa explains that *brāhmaṇa* here names the Śatapatha
Brāhmaṇa. This is more precise than either a generic Vedic label or a metrical
label.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 2.3.60 with Kāśikā and Nyāsa](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/2/2.3.60.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 4. Nigama and a laukika contrast: 6.3.113

**Rule:** ***साढ्यै साढ्वा साढेति निगमे (sāḍhyai sāḍhvā sāḍheti nigame)***.

The Kāśikā gives the listed *nigama* forms and contrasts them with laukika
***सोढ्वा (soḍhvā)*** and ***सोढा (soḍhā)***. The example demonstrates that a
rule can preserve a tightly bounded textual form while another form serves
laukika use.

Sources:
[Sanskrit Documents commentary page](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.3.113.htm) and
[Aṣṭādhyāyī.com rule 6.3.113](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/6/3/113).

**Assessment:** Strong after triangulation. The Sanskrit Documents page header
contains a numbering inconsistency, while its rule text and commentary match
6.3.113. The complete sūtra text and Aṣṭādhyāyī.com confirm the number.

### 5. Bhāṣā-specific operation: 3.2.108

**Rule:** ***भाषायां सदवसश्रुवः (bhāṣāyāṃ sadavasaśruvaḥ)***.

The Kāśikā restricts the specified *kvasu/liṭ* operation to the *bhāṣā*
setting and gives ***उपसेदिवान् कौत्सः पाणिनिम् (upasedivān kautsaḥ
pāṇinim)***, ***अनूषीवान् (anūṣīvān)***, and ***उपशुश्रुवान्
(upaśuśruvān)***. The Nyāsa discusses the inherited relations needed to read
the rule. The marker scopes this operation; it does not label every feature of
laukika Sanskrit.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 3.2.108 with Kāśikā and Nyāsa](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/3/3.2.108.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 6. Ṛc-specific sound operation: 6.3.133

**Rule:** ***ऋचि तुनुघमक्षुतङ्कुत्रोरुष्याणाम् (ṛci tunughamakṣutaṅkutroruṣyāṇām)***.

The Kāśikā gives examples including ***आ तू न इन्द्र वृत्रहन् (ā tū na indra
vṛtrahan)*** and ***मक्षू गोमन्तमीमहे (makṣū gomantam īmahe)***. The rule does
not merely say “Vedic.” It identifies an ṛc as the setting for the stated sound
operation.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 6.3.133 with Kāśikā](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.3.133.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 7. Text-specific scope: 7.4.38

**Rule:** ***देवसुम्नयोर्यजुषि काठके (devasumnayor yajuṣi kāṭhake)***.

The Kāśikā gives ***देवायते यजमानाय (devāyate yajamānāya)*** and
***सुम्नायन्तो हवामहे (sumnāyanto havāmahe)***. Its counterexamples separately
test why the rule says *Yajus* and why it says *Kāṭhaka*. The Nyāsa explicitly
reads *Kāṭhaka* as qualifying *Yajus*. This is a textual coordinate, not a
claim about the age of the construction.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.4.38 with Kāśikā and Nyāsa](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/7/7.4.38.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 8. Mantra-specific derivation: 3.2.71

**Rule:** ***मन्त्रे श्वेतवहौक्थशस्पुरोडाशो ण्विन् (mantre śvetavahaukthaśaspuroḍāśo ṇvin)***.

The Kāśikā gives ***श्वेतवा इन्द्रः (śvetavā indraḥ)***,
***उक्थशा यजमानः (ukthaśā yajamānaḥ)***, and ***पुरोडाः (puroḍāḥ)***. The
marker identifies mantra as the setting for this operation.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 3.2.71 with Kāśikā](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/3/3.2.71.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 9. Nigama form contrasted with bhāṣā: 7.2.64

**Rule:** ***बभूथाततन्थजगृम्भववर्थेति निगमे (babhūthātatanthajagṛmbhavavartheti nigame)***.

The Kāśikā explains ***निगमो वेदः (nigamo vedaḥ)***, lists the Vedic forms,
and contrasts bhāṣā forms such as ***बभूविथ (babhūvitha)***. This provides a
direct textual-scope contrast without requiring one form to be called an
earlier language and the other a later language.

Source: [Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.2.64](https://ashtadhyayi.com/sutraani/7/2/64).

**Assessment:** Strong.

### 10. Inherited *chandasi*: 7.4.64–65

Rule 7.4.64 states ***कृषेश्छन्दसि (kṛṣeś chandasi)***. Rule 7.4.65 then lists
eighteen forms without repeating *chandasi*. The Nyāsa explains that the
***चकार (cakāra)*** in 7.4.65 carries *chandasi* forward. The Kāśikā therefore
describes all eighteen forms as belonging to the *chandasi* setting.

Sources:
[Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.4.64](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/7/7.4.64.htm) and
[Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.4.65](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/7/7.4.65.htm).

**Assessment:** Strong. This example proves that an inventory limited to rules
that print the marker will miss inherited scope.

## Pass 3 — Architectural Synthesis

### Findings established by the sources

1. **The scope system has several levels.** Pāṇini can identify a broad Vedic
   setting, bhāṣā, mantra, Brāhmaṇa, nigama, ṛc, or a particular Yajus
   tradition.
2. **The scopes overlap.** The Nyāsa on 3.1.35 explicitly places an *amantre*
   operation in both bhāṣā and Brāhmaṇa settings.
3. **A marker belongs to an operation.** A *bhāṣāyām* rule does not define the
   whole laukika domain, and a *chandasi* rule does not define the whole Vedic
   corpus.
4. **Anuvṛtti matters.** The scope can continue into a later rule even when the
   marker is not printed again.
5. **Textual distribution does not create chronology.** A rule that says
   “in ṛc,” “in mantra,” or “in Kāṭhaka Yajus” records where an operation is
   found or permitted. It does not say that the operation evolved before or
   after another one.

### Consequences for the manuscript

- Retain ***वैदिक (vaidika)*** and ***लौकिक (laukika)*** as the book's broad
  civilizational domains.
- Use ***छन्दसि (chandasi)***, ***भाषायाम् (bhāṣāyām)***, mantra, Brāhmaṇa,
  nigama, ṛc, and text-specific labels only when the exact rule or example
  requires that scope.
- Do not define *chandasi* as “in meter.” Several checked rules cover textual
  settings that cannot be reduced to metrical verse.
- Do not translate every *bhāṣāyām* as “productive speech.” In plain body prose,
  identify the exact operation and say that it belongs to laukika or worldly
  use when that broader description is warranted.
- Do not claim that *amantre* means laukika. The checked commentary expressly
  includes Brāhmaṇa as well as bhāṣā.
- Treat “Vedic Sanskrit became Classical Sanskrit” as an added chronology, not
  as a conclusion contained in these scope markers.

## Remaining Research Limits

The ledger completes the three planned research passes needed for the current
manuscript correction. It does not yet provide a commentary entry for every
individual *chandasi* rule. That larger concordance would be a separate
reference project because *chandasi* occurs throughout the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* and
can also continue through inherited scope.

No new body correction emerged that overturns the approved deployment. The
research instead strengthens its central distinction: broad domains describe
the civilizational architecture, while Pāṇini's markers identify the operating
range of particular rules.
