# Chapter 18 Defensive-Prose Pass — Lost and Found

This file preserves complete sentences and paragraphs removed during the defensive-prose pass. Their conclusions were either already established by adjacent prose or restated more directly in Chapter 18.

## Removed from §18.2

> It cannot recover engineering specifications from a surface inventory. That is not its job. It was not built for that object.

> Engineering presupposes engineers. Specifications presuppose specifiers. Preservation architecture presupposes designers of the infrastructure. None of these presuppositions is available inside the comparative method, because the method explicitly excludes them as not part of how natural languages work.

The paragraph between them already explains that the comparative method reconstructs a naturally changing language and has no category for an engineered sonomer system or calibration architecture.

## Replaced at the Close of §18.3

> Adding more cognates or refining the sound laws will not close this gap. The genealogical project was built to reconstruct ancestry among natural languages. Sanskrit requires an account of how an engineered language was built, used, preserved, and decoded.

The replacement states the positive result: genealogy describes naturally changing languages, while Sanskrit requires an architectural account.

Section 18.3 has already applied this test feature by feature. The following paragraph now identifies directly what the pyramid must dismiss to preserve genealogy as established fact.
