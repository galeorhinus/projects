## Purpose

The glossary must define the vocabulary a reader needs in order to follow the
book. It should not become another argumentative appendix. Recent revisions
have introduced several terms that now carry major parts of the book while
leaving the glossary behind. Other entries still describe older chapter
structures, repeat definitions already given elsewhere in the glossary, or name
phrases that no longer appear in the manuscript.

The revision should therefore do four things:

1. add the book's missing architectural vocabulary;
2. correct definitions that no longer match the manuscript or the project data;
3. replace duplicated explanations with concise cross-references;
4. remove entries that no longer serve as terms in the book.

No body prose needs to change merely to justify a glossary entry. If an entry
exposes a possible manuscript problem, that problem must be proposed
separately rather than corrected silently.

## Governing Principles

1. **Define terms, not every memorable phrase.** A recurring architectural
   category belongs in the glossary. A metaphor or polemical phrase belongs
   only when the reader needs a stable definition to understand later uses.
2. **Use the manuscript's current vocabulary.** The glossary follows the book;
   it does not preserve superseded section names or earlier formulations.
3. **Keep Sanskrit primary.** Sanskrit entries use Devanagari followed by IAST.
   English coinages receive their own entries when they do work that an existing
   English category cannot do.
4. **Do not retry the argument.** Each entry should identify the term, define its
   use, and point to the principal treatment when that pointer helps. Evidence
   and controversy remain in the chapters and endnotes.
5. **Avoid brittle catalogues of deployments.** A principal chapter may be
   named. Exhaustive chapter lists will become stale after another reorganization.

## Additions Required

### 1. The Four Preservation Modes

Add one grouped entry for the four modes established in Chapter 14:

- **Writing** - preservation through a written object;
- **Mnemoniture** - preservation through memorized sequence;
- **Flexture** - preservation through constrained transformation;
- **Auditure** - preservation through trained production, hearing, comparison,
  and correction.

The manuscript uses **Auditure** twenty-four times, **Mnemoniture** six times,
and **Flexture** four times. The current glossary mentions Auditure only inside
the Audiography entry. The four terms form one taxonomy and should be defined
together rather than scattered across four long entries. Define **Scripture**
separately as the condition in which an institution makes a written corpus
sovereign; it is not a fifth preservation mode.

### 2. The Four Language Categories and Generativity

Add one grouped entry for the two-axis classification in Chapter 2:

- **Natural Language**;
- **Petrified Form**;
- **Lexicon-Dependent Conlang**;
- **Generative Architecture**.

The entry should first define **generativity** in ordinary language: the ability
of a language architecture to create expressions that no earlier speaker or
document had listed. It should then explain that the four categories separate
origin - organic or engineered - from generativity - high or low.

### 3. Vivification

Add **vivification** beside **vivimorphosis** and **revivification**. The term
names the process in which an engineered language begins changing through
communal use as a natural language does. Esperanto is the book's principal
example. The definition must keep vivification distinct from vivimorphosis:
vivification gives an engineered language changing communal life;
vivimorphosis changes an established memory until the altered form displaces it.

### 4. Anatomical Coordinates, Grid Addresses, and Snap to Grid

Add one grouped entry that protects a distinction deliberately established in
Chapters 7-9:

- an **anatomical coordinate** identifies where and how the speaking body
  produces a sound;
- a **grid address** identifies the cell assigned to a reusable sonomer in the
  varnamala;
- **snap to grid** names an operation that replaces a contextual sound with the
  addressed sonomer required at the new boundary.

The same word must not be used for both anatomy and grid placement. The
glossary should also point from this entry to **sonance**, **sonomer**, and
**off-grid sonance**.

### 5. Calibrant Order

Add a short standalone entry. A **calibrant order** places an invariant and
widely available standard at the center of social correction without allowing
an apex to own it. Individuals and institutions may protect the order, but they
do not create or control the calibrant. This entry should distinguish the
polity-scale term from **calibrant**, **calibration**, and **calibration matrix**.

### 6. Sat, Asat, Rta, and Viveka

Expand the present **sat-asat-viveka** entry into a grouped civilizational
entry, or add an adjacent entry for **ऋत (rta)**. It must state the relationship
without compressing four concepts into synonyms:

- **सत् (sat)** - that which is, or acts in alignment with enduring order;
- **असत् (asat)** - that which is not, or acts against that order;
- **ऋत (rta)** - the order through which sat becomes durable and discernible;
- **विवेक (viveka)** - the discernment required to distinguish sat from asat
  through purpose and action.

The wording must follow the book's own treatment rather than pretend to exhaust
the wider philosophical meanings of these terms.

### 7. Deva, Asura, A-sura, and Asu-ra

Add the usage rule established by the Chapter 3 rewrite. Sanskrit has the
distinct analyses **asu-ra** and **a-sura**, but the surrounding words do not
always allow the reader to assign an occurrence of *asura* decisively. The book
therefore:

- uses **a-sura** for an antagonist who turns power against radiance;
- uses **asu-ra** when the life-breath analysis is specifically intended;
- leaves quoted Vedic *asura* undivided unless the source supplies the division;
- never translates **deva** and **asura** automatically as *god* and *demon*.

The definition should place conduct before faction and point to **asuratva**.

### 8. Arya and Aryatva

Expand the current **aryatva** heading to include **आर्य (arya)**. Arya names a
person aligned through discipline and conduct, not a race. Aryatva names that
quality. The existing Racial Arya Thesis entry should remain separate because it
defines the pyramid's contrary category.

### 9. The Radiance Sequence

Add **बीज (bija)** and **अपशब्द (apasabda)** beside the existing
**प्रतिबिम्ब (pratibimba)** and **vivimorphosis** entries. A compact grouped
entry may be clearer than four isolated definitions:

- a **bija** carries a Sanskrit form or operation into another language;
- an **apasabda** is a form that falls away within an orbital language;
- a **pratibimba** is a reflection formed through outward Sanskritic contact;
- **vivimorphosis** changes a memory while retaining a deceptive resemblance to
  the form it displaced.

The final wording must match Chapter 19's current sequence and should move
Pratibimba out of Diagnostic vocabulary.

### 10. Vac and VagdevI

Add **वाच् (Vac)** and **वाग्देवी (VagdevI)**. The entry should identify Vac as
speech and the power through which expression becomes available, and VagdevI as
that power personified. It should not reduce either term to a decorative goddess
label.

### 11. Dharma and Lokaksema

Add **धर्म (dharma)** or pair it with the existing **लोकक्षेम (lokaksema)**
entry. The definition should make their distinct work clear: dharma sustains an
order through conduct; lokaksema names the well-being of the world that such
conduct serves. Move Lokaksema out of Diagnostic vocabulary.

## Entries Requiring Correction

### उपसर्ग (Upasarga)

The current entry says Sanskrit has twenty-two upasargas, prints a list of
twenty, and then refers to two unnamed contested forms. That is internally
inconsistent. The traditional inventory contains twenty underlying items.
Two of them appear in paired surface forms: **निस् / निर् (*nis / nir*)** and
**दुस् / दुर् (*dus / dur*)**. Some teaching lists therefore print twenty-two
forms while counting twenty upasargas. The glossary should state that
distinction directly rather than calling two forms contested.

The book's deliberately overcomplete generativity model uses twenty-two printed
prefix forms plus the unprefixed state. Its 23 prefix states can remain, but the
endnote should explain that two pairs are conditioned forms rather than four
independent prefix choices. The model already warns that its formal slots are
not all valid or distinct words.

### धातुपाठ (Dhatupatha)

The current definition confuses source rows with analyzed forms. The project
record distinguishes:

- 2,235 rows in the source CSV;
- 2,168 analyzed pronounced forms after anubandha stripping;
- a wider conventional range across recensions.

The glossary should not call 2,168 a recension count. It should state which
inventory the book analyzes and how that number was produced.

### गण (Gana)

The current wording can imply that Panini created the ten behavioral classes.
The revised Chapters 11-12 establish a different sequence: Vedic usage already
displays the grammatical behavior; Panini identifies recurring patterns and
classifies them. The definition should describe the classes without assigning
their creation to him.

### सिद्ध (Siddha) and कार्य (Karya)

The current entry points to Chapters 1 and 4 and overstates the pair as
"engineered word" versus tolerated variants. Chapter 5 is now the principal
treatment. The definition should follow Patanjali's sequence there: the bond
between word and meaning is established; speakers produce worldly usage; grammar
regulates that usage.

### अपभ्रंश (Apabhramsa)

The entry says the Preface introduces the term. The Prologue introduces it, and
Chapter 6 develops it. Update the pointer and keep **apasabda** distinct from
**apabhramsa**.

### अपौरुषेय (Apauruseya)

Rename the entry from the largely unused abstract noun **apauruseyatva** to
**अपौरुषेय (apauruseya)**, the adjective the manuscript actually uses. Define
the book's claim precisely: the Vedas do not assign human authorship to the
mantras; the rsis and rsikas see them.

### Aṣṭādhyāyī and Vyakarana

Retain the book's established distinction without qualification drift. Panini
decodes and documents an architecture already in use; he does not codify or
create Sanskrit. The entries should avoid exhaustive chapter lists and must not
remove **decoder**, which is a controlled term throughout the manuscript.

### Calibrant and Calibration Matrix

Separate three scales now compressed into one entry:

- **calibrant** - the invariant reference;
- **calibration** - comparison and correction against it;
- **calibration matrix** - the mutually checking preservation layers;
- **calibrant order** - the larger architecture of distributed order.

### Pratibimba

The entry contains stale front-matter and chapter pointers and currently retries
the radiance argument at excessive length. Move it into Engineering core
vocabulary and define its exact place in the radiance sequence.

### Fractal Corollary

The exact phrase no longer appears in the body. Chapter 10 now uses **Atomic
Corollary** and **Fractal Signature**. Rename the glossary entry to **fractal
signature** unless a later body decision deliberately restores Fractal
Corollary. The glossary should not preserve an abandoned heading.

### Devabhasa

The glossary says Chapter 0 establishes **देवभाषा (devabhasa)**, but the term no
longer appears in the body. This requires an author decision: either retain it
as a useful Sanskrit reference term without claiming a deployment, or remove
the entry. Do not insert it into Chapter 0 merely to rescue the glossary.

- **atomic scaffold** points to **धातुरचना (dhaturacana)**.

Remove as a standalone entry. The exact phrase does not function as a term in

### Audiograph and Audiography

Keep both if the noun/system distinction remains useful, but eliminate repeated

### Dogma Cluster

The four entries **dogma**, **Western philological dogma**, **progressive
dogma**, and **foundational dogma** overlap. Keep **dogma** as the main entry and
retain the two live qualified forms under it. Remove **Western philological
dogma** as a standalone heading unless the exact phrase is restored as a
controlled term in the body.

### Machinery Cluster

Retain **pyramid's account**, **certified intellectuals**, **asuric pyramid**,
and **philological machinery**. Fold the following unused phrases into those
entries or remove them:

- **reference ecosystem**;
- **raksasa-retainers**;
- **memory recoded as mythology**.

Their underlying ideas remain in the book. They no longer require separate
vocabulary slots.

## Entries to Move

Move positive Sanskrit and architectural terms out of Diagnostic vocabulary:

- **arya / aryatva** to Technical Sanskrit and civilizational vocabulary;
- **lokaksema** to Technical Sanskrit and civilizational vocabulary;
- **pratibimba** to Engineering core vocabulary.

The third group should contain terms used to diagnose the pyramid, not the
positive architecture that the pyramid opposes.

## Structural Revision

Retain three groups, but rename the second:

1. **Engineering core vocabulary**;
2. **Sanskrit and civilizational vocabulary**;
3. **Diagnostic vocabulary**.

Within each group, arrange related terms together rather than alphabetically.
The glossary teaches an architecture, so conceptual adjacency is more useful
than an index-like sequence. The order should move from sound to atom to word,
then from preservation to calibration, and finally from the positive
civilizational vocabulary to the pyramid's diagnostic vocabulary.

## Decisions Applied

1. **Devabhasa:** removed because it no longer has a body deployment. The term
   remains available in the endnotes and can return if the manuscript gives it
   work to do.
2. **Fractal Corollary:** replaced by the body's current **Atomic Corollary** and
   **Fractal Signature**.
3. **Radiance terms:** retained as adjacent entries for lookup, followed by the
   complete sequence.
4. **Book coinages:** collected in a compact index. Full definitions remain in
   the conceptual sections, so no term is defined twice.

## Expected Result

The glossary will probably gain entries but should not grow materially. New
definitions will be offset by removing unused headings, shortening cross-entry
duplication, and replacing long deployment catalogues with stable pointers. The
result should be easier to consult and more faithful to the architecture the
book now presents.
