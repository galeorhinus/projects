## Purpose

Appendix Part 5 must perform one experiment in full:

1. Separate a language's sound inventory from the operations that generate words and sentences.
2. Map the Sanskrit sounds used in a small corpus to sounds available in Japanese.
3. Preserve enough derivation, inflection, and sentence structure for a reader to recover the Sanskrit operations beneath the new surface.
4. Show that one fixed procedure can produce additional forms rather than merely encrypt three finished sentences.
5. Apply a second procedure that adapts the output to Japanese sound patterns.
6. Use the result to demonstrate transferability as evidence of engineering.
7. Contrast a documented and reusable generative engine with Schleicher's reconstructed PIE.

Chapters 10 through 12 already teach the Sanskrit engine from धातुः (*dhātuḥ*) through words and sentences. Appendix Part 1 documents the manufacture of PIE, while Chapter 19 prosecutes Schleicher's reconstruction and its institutional consequences. Appendix Part 5 should use those arguments, not retell them. Its unique contribution is the Yenpro experiment.
