## Source hierarchy

Use the sources in this order:

1. The exact Vedic passage and, where available, its *padapāṭha*.
2. A Prātiśākhya, Śikṣā, Pāṇinian rule, Kāśikā, Mahābhāṣya, Nirukta, or another
   Hindu analytical source that identifies or explains the form.
3. A reliable searchable Vedic corpus or critical edition.
4. Macdonell, Whitney, and modern grammars as catalogues for locating evidence,
   never as sufficient proof of the architectural explanation.

Give a direct link, edition, page, section, or mantra number for every source.
Do not cite a search-result page.

## Strict rules

- Do not invent a Vedic passage, accent, *padaccheda*, translation, rule,
  citation, page number, or explanation.
- If you cannot verify a form, write **NOT FOUND** and list what you checked.
- Distinguish a system-wide Vedic option from an isolated, lexical, doubtful,
  or recension-specific form.
- Use **ending class**, ***akārānta, ākārānta, ikārānta,*** and related Sanskrit
  terms. Do not use the botanical term *stem*.
- Do not call the Vedic form *archaic*, *older*, or an earlier stage of the
  laukika form.
- Do not use difference as proof of chronology.
- Pāṇini documents the form. Do not say that he created, regularized, fixed,
  standardized, or codified it.
- Do not assume that meter explains every variation. Scan the actual passage
  before assigning a metrical function.
- Preserve Vedic accent marks when the source provides them.
- Give Devanagari on first use, followed by IAST.
- A correct **NOT FOUND** is preferable to a plausible reconstruction.

## Designed Variation codebook

Use these codes:

| Code | Category |
|---|---|
| **SON** | Sonomeric selection and distinguishability |
| **MAT** | Syllable count, *mātrā,* and *laghu-guru* |
| **SVR** | Pitch architecture |
| **REL** | Audible grammatical boundaries and recoverable relations |
| **ARR** | Poetic or compositional arrangement |
| **SEM** | Compact semantic distinctions |
| **RES** | Sound pattern and resonance |
| **REC** | Melodic or recitational function |
| **AUD** | Error detection and overlapping audit checks |
| **FUN** | A function specific to a Veda, Vedic prose setting, or mode of use |

Assign a code only when the passage supports it:

- **Confirmed**: directly demonstrated by the form and passage.
- **Probable**: supported by the passage but still requiring one analytical
  source or recitational confirmation.
- **Rejected**: proposed in the plan but contradicted by the passage.
- **Open**: possible, but the present evidence cannot establish it.

## Required analysis for each example

For every ID, provide:

1. **Verification result:** Confirmed / Partial / NOT FOUND / Incorrect.
2. **Exact Vedic passage:** full Devanagari with accent where available.
3. **Citation:** Veda, maṇḍala/hymn/mantra or prose location, edition, and link.
4. **Padaccheda:** source-preserved *padapāṭha* where available; otherwise mark
   an editorial split clearly.
5. **IAST.**
6. **Literal translation** and a readable translation.
7. **Target form:** isolate it and identify its ending class, gender,
   *vibhakti,* and *vacanam*.
8. **Laukika counterpart:** give the exact form and explain what remains the
   same and what changes.
9. **Syllable analysis:** count the target word and, where relevant, the
   complete *pāda*.
10. **Laghu-guru analysis:** show whether the variation changes syllable
    weight.
11. **Svara analysis:** identify any relevant *udātta, anudātta,* or *svarita*.
12. **Local function:** explain what the variation contributes in that
    particular passage.
13. **Designed Variation codes:** give the status of every proposed code.
14. **Indic documentation:** quote or summarize the exact analytical rule,
    with its citation.
15. **Distribution:** state whether both Vedic forms occur, whether one is
    dominant, and whether the form is limited by word class, Veda, śākhā, or
    context.
16. **Figure-ready result:** one compact line containing the ID, Vedic form,
    laukika form, confirmed DV code(s), and a ten-to-twenty-word explanation.
17. **Confidence:** Strong / Moderate / Weak, with one sentence explaining why.
