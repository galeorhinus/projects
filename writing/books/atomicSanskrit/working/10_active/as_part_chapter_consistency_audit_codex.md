## Purpose

Each part introduction is a contract with the reader. It should state where the argument has arrived, identify what the chapters in that part will establish, and prepare the next movement without describing an older version of the manuscript.

This audit compares each current part introduction with the chapters that now follow it. It also checks the chapter seams, the eclipse-block sequence, and the titles and ranges recorded in the build and planning files.

## Executive Findings

The overall sequence is coherent. The eclipse figures also preserve the intended progression: Parts I-II expose the first three blocks; Part III removes Alphabetic; Part IV reveals the atomic architecture without removing another block; Part V removes Abugida and Early Literature; Part VI removes Descended and Sibling Language; Part VII opens after all seven core blocks have fallen.

The main inconsistencies come from later chapter rewrites:

1. **Part II understates Chapter 6.** Chapter 6 now distinguishes entropy from deliberate asuric attack, defines drift, deviation, and divergence, and prepares the two-domain defense. The opener still describes it mainly as a chapter about *apabhraṃśa*.
2. **Part III retains the retired term “sound-field.”** Chapter 8 is now *The Sounds of the Indian Subcontinent* and explicitly establishes a subcontinental superset. The opener and `as_book.yaml` still carry the older label.
3. **Part IV omits the Vedic-calibrant argument added to Chapters 11 and 12.** Those chapters now do more than build words and sentences. They demonstrate that the grammar is already evident in the Vedas, that the Vedas preserve completed examples across the construction, and that Pāṇini made the analysis explicit.
4. **Part V underplays its culmination.** Chapter 16 now brings together the two enemies from Chapter 6, the Vedas as the primary calibrant, the two domains, distributed preservation, and continuing generativity.
5. **Part VI gives Chapter 18 an outdated description.** Chapter 18 no longer merely investigates who selected sounds and atoms. It establishes an architectural test, identifies the limits of genealogy and DNA, compares two origin speculations, and restores Pāṇini's place.
6. **Part VII underplays the structure of Chapter 20 and the Epilogue.** Chapter 20 now distinguishes three calibrant waves from a separate diasporic wave. The Epilogue does more than issue an invitation; it develops the contest of architectures and calibrant order as the bridge to later volumes.
7. **One chapter seam skips a whole part.** Chapter 6 closes by pointing directly to Chapter 10. The reader must first pass through Part III, where Chapters 7-9 establish the body, source inventory, sieve, and sonomeric grid from which the atom can be built.
8. **Build and planning metadata contain older titles and chapter ranges.** These should be repaired after the prose is approved.

Replace “without losing visibility of the particle-level structure underneath” with a concrete formulation such as “while the smaller constituents remain recoverable inside the larger form.”

- the matrix distributes correction without giving an apex custody of the standard;
