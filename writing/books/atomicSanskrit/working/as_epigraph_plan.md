# Sanskrit Epigraph Plan

Working note for possible chapter-opening Sanskrit epigraphs. These should function as seeds, not decoration. Each epigraph should either name the chapter's central tension, plant a delayed payoff, or supply the Indic category the chapter will defend.

## Operating Rule

- Use **Sanskrit epigraph**, not **shloka**, as the category. Some openings may be Vedic mantra, Upanishadic prose, Paninian sutra, Mahabhashya line, karika, or later verse.
- Use Devanagari + transliteration at the opening.
- Translate immediately only when the reader needs the translation to enter the chapter.
- Prefer delayed translation when the chapter itself earns the meaning.
- Use endnote-only translation when the epigraph is technical, atmospheric, or evidentiary but not necessary to the opening flow.
- After the epigraph, return quickly to a hard English opening.

Suggested convention for the Preface:

> Each chapter opens with a Sanskrit epigraph. Some are translated immediately; some are allowed to wait until the chapter has earned their meaning; some are translated in the notes. The delay is intentional. Sanskrit often names the answer before the modern argument knows the question.

## The Veda's Own Linguistic Self-Description — Calibration as a Vedic Goal

Working note for *śruti* passages where the Vedic corpus describes **itself** as a specific kind of speech — *divine*, *formulated*, *measured*, *three-fold*, and structurally distinct from corruption. These verses anchor the engineering thesis from inside *śruti* itself: the technical term *saṃskṛtam* (as a language-name) is post-Vedic, but the *substance* of the *saṃskṛtam* claim — that this is **calibrated speech distinct from ordinary utterance** — is named everywhere in the corpus using earlier vocabulary (*daivī vāk*, *brahman*, *chandas*, *ṛc*).

**Polemic-significant point.** Counters the orthodox deflection that *saṃskṛtam* is a late retrofit projecting "engineered" status onto the Vedas retroactively. The corpus claimed engineering / calibration status from the inside; the *paramparā* coined the technical term *saṃskṛtam* later for an architectural property already in operation. **The vocabulary changed; the self-description did not. Calibration was always the Veda's own stated goal.**

**Synthesis of this cluster.** The Vedic corpus self-describes linguistically as:

1. **Divine speech** — *daivī vāk*, distinct from ordinary speech (Yajurveda apparatus).
2. **Formulated speech with its own sovereign** — *brahman* under Bṛhaspati's governance (RV 10.71, RV 7.97).
3. **Measured by named meters** — *gāyatrī*, *triṣṭubh*, *jagatī*, *anuṣṭubh*, etc., named from inside the corpus (already in the engineered-architecture cluster: RV 10.130.4-5, RV 1.164.45, RV 10.90.9).
4. **Three-fold corpus** — *ṛc* / *sāman* / *yajus*, named from inside (RV 10.90.9, already in cluster).
5. **Graded against incomplete speakers** — speakers ranked by depth of access (RV 10.71.7).
6. **Structurally distinct from corruption** — the *helayo helayaḥ* etiology (Śatapatha 3.2.1.23-24).

**All six are *śruti*-internal claims, in *śruti*-internal vocabulary.** The *saṃskṛtam* term and the formal *saṃskṛta / prākṛta* taxonomy come later, but they name a property the Vedas had always been asserting about themselves.
