## Where the Nectar Rises

In the story of **समुद्रमन्थन (*samudra-manthana*)**, devas and asuras gripped the same serpent-rope around the same mountain and churned the one ocean for what it held.[NOTE: samudra-manthana-source-anchor] The first yield was **हलाहल (*halāhala*)**, poison enough to darken the worlds. After the poison was borne, the ocean yielded **अमृत (*amṛta*)**, the nectar of deathless life.

That story has a parallel in this epilogue.

The pyramid has been churning the ocean of language for two centuries. Generation after generation of credentialed workers, placed low in its hierarchy and far from its apex, collected cognates, tabulated sound-shifts, compiled dictionaries, indexed inscriptions, and compared language families. The apex drew out the poison first: an imaginary ancestor, imaginary words, imaginary speakers, and the racial Arya thesis that followed beneath them. Much of the modern world drank from that poison.

Yet the churning also produced something precious. The same labor assembled the largest searchable field of Sanskrit’s reflections ever made: correspondences, cognate sets, phonetic shifts, semantic trails, inscriptional archives, and lexical maps spread across Europe, Iran, Central Asia, and the wider contact-world. The pyramid tried to bury Sanskrit beneath PIE. In doing so, it gathered the very ocean through which Sanskrit’s radiance can now be traced.

The Rāhu story gives the warning. In the distribution of amṛta, one asura crossed into the deva row and drank by deception. The sip cost him his body; the head remained, undying, as Rāhu, the eclipse-device of this book’s spine.[NOTE: rahu-manthana-svarbhanu-layering] PIE repeats that form: a head without a body, granted explanatory immortality while lacking living speech, living people, living recitation, living grammar, or living memory.

The remedy is discernment. The nectar belongs where it restores light.

So the churn turns again from the deva side. Their dictionaries list thousands of starred forms. The *Dhātupāṭha* preserves 2,168 *dhātavaḥ* — recited, accented, semantically specified, and held in a living architecture across thousands of years. Take a starred reconstruction. Place it before Sanskrit. Ask whether a Sanskrit atom or molecule explains the word-family more directly than the reconstruction does. Ask whether the semantic field stayed whole, narrowed, split, or drifted. Ask whether the receiving language shows vivimorphosis rather than ancestry. Keep the honest cases. Discard the weak ones.

Chapter 18 opened the first rows: ⟪स्था⟫, ⟪दिव्⟫, ⟪जन्⟫, ⟪कृत्⟫, *mātṛ*. The rest of the ocean stands indexed and waiting. PIE no longer receives default parenthood. Every starred form becomes a question placed before Sanskrit.

The sound correspondences, cognate tables, etymological dictionaries, inscriptional corpora, and university departments need not be thrown away. They can be turned. The work ahead maps two fields: the orbital field held by Sanskritic gravity, and the radiance-field where Sanskritic reflections traveled outward and then drifted.

The churning stations are already built.

The nectar rises when the churn is turned toward the Sun.
