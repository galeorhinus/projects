## Observations

### A. *Bhvādi* is the universal landing pad

Class 1 is the dominant column for **nine of the ten** top *racanāḥ*. The only exception is **CV2C** (*vācādi*), which splits roughly 50/50 between *bhvādi* and *curādi*. *Bhvādi*'s size is not historical accident — it is the default class for shapes whose phonetic structure leaves no other coherent inflectional choice.

### B. *Curādi* is the systematic runner-up for closed-syllable shapes

For the four closed-syllable heavyweights (CV1C, CCV1C, CV1CC, CV2C), *curādi* (the -aya- causative / intensive / denominative class) is consistently #2 or #1. The -aya- suffix needs a closing consonant to graft onto cleanly. The open-syllable shapes (CV1, CV2, CCV2) attract *curādi* minimally — exactly the shapes where -aya- has no clean consonantal landing site.

### C. The upper-left 4×4 carries 58% of the corpus

Within the top-4 *racanāḥ* × top-4 *gaṇāḥ* — a 4×4 corner of the matrix — sit **1,265 of 2,168 dhātus = 58.3% of the corpus**. Two axes, four cells each, and the bulk of Sanskrit's verbs live there. The architecture has a center of gravity.

### D. The single-cell heavyweight: CV1C × *bhvādi*

**400 dhātus** — **18.5% of the entire corpus** — sit in this one cell. Add CV1C × *curādi* (210), and a single *racanā* in just two *gaṇāḥ* covers **610 dhātus (28% of corpus)**. One shape, two classes, more than a quarter of Sanskrit's verbs.

### E. CV2CV1 (*bādhrādi*) is the most class-concentrated *racanā*: 95% *bhvādi*

The four-particle CV2CV1 disyllabic shape is essentially mono-class. 100 of 105 dhātus live in *bhvādi*; the rest are scattered singletons. The cleanest single "shape predicts class" finding in the matrix.

### F. Long-vowel open shapes attract *kryādi* disproportionately

The -nā- suffix prefers a long-vowel anchor:

- **CV2** (*dhādi*) → 24% *kryādi* (21 of 88 dhātus)
- **CCV2** (*sthādi*) → 27% *kryādi* (13 of 49)

No CV1-headed shape shows this. The empirical pattern matches what the canonical examples already display: *krī → krīṇāti*, *prī → prīṇāti*, *lū → lunāti*. The matrix says the rule holds at corpus scale.

### G. *Juhotyādi* (reduplication) lives only in short shapes

Class 3 reduplicates the initial syllable. The operation is acoustically stable only on short, vowel-edged bases. The matrix confirms:

- Present: CV1 (7, **10.8%**), CV2 (6, **6.8%**), CV1C (5, 0.6%), CCV2 (1, 2.0%).
- **Absent** from CCV1C, CV1CC, CV2CV1, CV1CV2, V1C — the longer or cluster-front shapes.

The system blocks reduplication where it cannot graft cleanly.

### H. Binary-split *racanāḥ*

Three of the top-10 have essentially two-class structures:

- **CV2C** (*vācādi*): 47.5% *curādi* + 45.5% *bhvādi* = **93%** in two classes
- **CV1CC** (*kalpādi*): 52.2% *bhvādi* + 36.9% *curādi* = **89%** in two classes
- **CV2CV1** (*bādhrādi*): 95% in one class (the most extreme case)

Once shape and one class are known, the inflectional fate is almost determined.

### I. Broad-distribution outliers — short shapes are promiscuous

Two short *racanāḥ* spread across many *gaṇāḥ*:

- **CV2** (*dhādi*): 8 distinct *gaṇāḥ*, top-3 share only 68%
- **CV1** (*krādi*): 8 distinct *gaṇāḥ*, top-3 share only 65%

Short, vowel-light shapes fit many inflectional patterns. The longer, more shape-specific *racanāḥ* are restricted.

### J. The matrix sparsifies toward the lower-right (the Mendeleev gradient)

After sorting both axes by total, the matrix shows the canonical "heavy upper-left, sparse lower-right" gradient that periodic-table-style classifications produce. The smallest *gaṇāḥ* (*tanādi*, *rudhādi*, *juhotyādi*) have most of their dhātus concentrated in 2–3 *racanāḥ*; the smallest *racanāḥ* in the top-10 (CCV2, CV1CV2) live in similarly restricted *gaṇa* sets. The matrix has a center of gravity in the upper-left, density gradient outward, and only 187 of 690 (27%) cells populated overall.

### K. Tiny-class concentration

The four smallest *gaṇāḥ* — *tanādi* (10), *juhotyādi* (25), *rudhādi* (25), *svādi* (39) — are not random small samples. Each concentrates in a small subset of *racanāḥ* that match its inflectional operation:

- *Tanādi* (10 dhātus): 9 in CV1C / CCV1C / V1C / CV1 — all compact closed shapes.
- *Juhotyādi* (25): 7 in CV1, 6 in CV2, 5 in CV1C — reduplication-friendly compactness.
- *Rudhādi* (25): 6 in CV1CV1C, 5 in CV1C, 3 in CV1CV2 — -na- infix shapes.
- *Svādi* (39): 12 in CV1, 8 in CV1C, 4 in CCV1 — -nu- / -no- target compact bases.

Tiny *gaṇāḥ* are not statistical noise. They are tight architectural specializations.

## Implications for Ch 11

1. **The *gaṇa* system is structurally grounded, not arbitrary.** The empirical concentration patterns are too sharp to be coincidence. Pāṇini's documented inflectional classes correlate strongly with phonetic shape: reduplication → short bases; -nā- → long-vowel stems; -aya- → closed syllables; CV2CV1 → *bhvādi* 95% of the time. The morphology grafts where the architecture permits stable grafting.

2. **Two axes are necessary, not one.** Neither *racanā* nor *gaṇa* subsumes the other. *Bhvādi* spans 53 distinct *racanāḥ* (broad on the row axis); CV1C spans all 10 *gaṇāḥ* (broad on the column axis). The atoms occupy a genuine 2D space. The periodic-table argument needs both dimensions.

3. **The Mendeleev moment.** A documented classification (Pāṇini's *gaṇāḥ*) and a discovered classification (Ch 10's *racanāḥ*) sit on orthogonal axes. Their intersection — the cell — is where the engineering becomes visible. Pāṇini *documented* one axis; Ch 10 surfaced the other; the architecture *produced* the cross-distribution.

4. **A possible chapter-load-bearing line:**
   > The *gaṇa* is not the column of the periodic table. The *gaṇa* is what the column lands on after the architecture has already done its work. CV2CV1 sits in *bhvādi* because the shape leaves no other coherent inflectional option. Form precedes class. The orthodoxy reads Pāṇini's documentation and stops there; the architecture explains why the documentation took the shape it did.

5. **Reading the empty cells.** 503 of 690 (*racanā* × *gaṇa*) cells are empty. Each empty cell encodes a structural prohibition: this shape × this inflectional operation does not produce a viable atom. The negatives are as informative as the positives.

6. **A potential Ch 11 figure.** The sorted 10×10 matrix as a heatmap — cell intensity = count or log-count, with the canonical gradient from upper-left to lower-right visually obvious. Mendeleev-style.
