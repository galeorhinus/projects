## 1. What this plan adds to the book

The book already operates with a clear structure: front matter → Prologue
(prosecution) → seven Parts (charge → testimony → physical evidence →
technical evidence → chain of custody → cross-examination → remedy) →
Epilogue (closing statement returning to the Indic frame). The body
chapters are drafted. The courtroom arc is locked.

This plan does not rewrite the courtroom. It adds three interlocking
layers underneath it:

| Layer | Source register | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Ternary** | Dharmic categorical vocabulary | Names what gets *made* by intentional choice: *saṃskṛti* (sat-aligned, engineered, harmonious), *vikṛti* (asat-aligned, distorted, hierarchical), *prakṛti* (neutral, natural, organic). The architectural categories the *paramparā* itself supplies. |
| **Sat-asat-vivek-buddhi** | Dharmic individual faculty | Names what the *reader* brings — the discriminative intellect that distinguishes truth from untruth. Lives at the individual layer. The book provides evidence; each reader exercises their own *vivek-buddhi* to render the verdict. |
| **Yat bhūta-hitam atyantam tat satyam** | Dharmic satyam standard | Names the standard by which sat is judged — *the welfare of all beings, ultimate and entire*. The Prologue invokes this standard; the Epilogue restores it. |

These three operate beneath the existing courtroom arc, not in place of
it. The courtroom remains the Abrahamic institutional form the book
borrows. The dharmic framework operates as the deep vocabulary, the
reader's faculty, and the closing reframe.

## 2. The courtroom stays Abrahamic; the close returns to dharma

The book uses the borrowed Abrahamic courtroom for one reason:
*it is the only institutional form that compels engagement with an
adversary that refuses direct debate*. Indictment, evidence, cross-
examination, verdict — these are Western adversarial machinery applied
to a Western institutional defendant.

The dharmic frame does two things alongside it:

1. **Sat-asat-vivek-buddhi lives at the individual layer.** The court
   does not render the verdict; the reader does. Each reader brings
   their own discriminative intellect; the book provides the evidence
   that lets that intellect operate. This is the *distributed* dharmic
   move — no single apex jury, no institution declaring the verdict, but
   each reader's individual exercise of *vivek-buddhi*. The book's
   accusation is not *"this is what the court has determined"* but
   *"this is what your own vivek-buddhi will recognize when you see
   the evidence."*

2. **The closing reframe converts Abrahamic justice into dharmic
   karma.** The court convicts (Abrahamic completion). The Epilogue
   converts conviction into recalibration — *not punishment, but
   karma; not payback, but restoration of the standard*. The
   antagonists' asat-choice produced *vikṛti* formations whose
   consequences run their course. The dharmic close does not seek
   penalty. It restores the *satyam* standard so the next round of
   *vivek-buddhi* can choose sat.

The Note on Form already half-articulates this — *"the book borrows
the courtroom and what comes after it"*. The polemic integration
sharpens it: the borrowed Abrahamic form delivers conviction; the
dharmic close converts conviction into recalibration; the satyam
standard is named explicitly as the restoration target.

## 3. The antagonist nomenclature hierarchy

The book has accumulated multiple names for the antagonist —
*orthodoxy*, *Western philological orthodoxy*, *church of progress*,
*apparatus*, *machinery*, *AIT framework*, *Müllerian Indology*,
*asuric pyramid*, *fourth Abrahamic religion*. With the ternary, the
book does not need a new surface antagonist term. It keeps **asuric
pyramid** as the book's phrase and links that formation to **विकृति
(*vikṛti*)** in the underlying frame. The terms accumulate because each
one catches a different layer of the same formation. The polemic
integration arranges them as a stack rather than a synonym list.

| Tier | Term | When to deploy |
|---|---|---|
| Deep frame | **asuric pyramid** | Frame-setting moments — Prologue, Ch 1 §1.6, Ch 3 §3.6, Epilogue. Names the underlying distorted control-fractal. Link it once to *vikṛti* as structure; do not replace the book's term with a new Sanskrit compound. |
| Common prose | **the pyramid** | Short form once the asuric frame has been planted. Use when the *geometry* of control is what's being prosecuted. |
| Institutional layer | **church of progress** | When the institutional carrier is the target — academy, museums, publishing, credentialed consensus, foundations. |
| Doctrinal layer | **the orthodoxy** | When a specific claim-system is the target — the doctrine itself, the corpus of claims, the published consensus. |
| Operational layer | **the apparatus** / **the machinery** | When the *mechanisms* of containment are being prosecuted — peer review, citation networks, credentialing chains, gatekeeping procedures. |
| Function classes | **priests / missionaries / defenders of progress** | When the *actors inside* the formation are named — scholars who sanctify, agents who extend, enforcers who attack dissent. |

The relationship between layers reads in one sentence:

> *The orthodoxy is the doctrine. The church of progress is the
> institution. The apparatus is the machinery. The pyramid is the
> geometry underneath them. The asuric pyramid is the book's name for
> the distorted control-fractal;* vikṛti *is the structural category
> that explains what such a pyramid makes.*

That sentence (or close variants) lands once in the book, probably in
the Prologue or Ch 3 §3.6, as the layered-vocabulary anchor. After
that, each chapter deploys whichever term fits the layer being
attacked.

### Deployment patterns

- *"The orthodoxy turns domain and mode into chronology."* — doctrinal
  attack; the *claim* is what's being prosecuted.
- *"The apparatus turns domain and mode into chronology."* —
  operational attack; the *machinery* doing the conversion is the
  target.
- *"The pyramid turns domain and mode into chronology, because the
  pyramid cannot permit Sanskrit to be read as* saṃskṛti."* — climactic
  attack; the *geometry* is what's named, with the deep-frame *saṃskṛti*
  contrast doing the closing work.
- *"The philological apparatus turns domain and mode into chronology,
  because the pyramid cannot permit Sanskrit to be read as* saṃskṛti."*
  — full stack; operational + geometric + categorical in one sentence.
  Reserve for chapter-close hammers and verdict moments.

### What this displaces

- **The default low-register term remains *orthodoxy*.** Short, recognizable,
  doctrinal. Most paragraphs that currently say *orthodoxy* keep saying
  it.
- **The default mechanism term is *apparatus*.** When the sentence is
  about *what the formation does*, not *what it claims*, *apparatus*
  is sharper than *orthodoxy*.
- **The default geometry term is *pyramid*.** When the sentence is
  about *power and containment shape*, *pyramid* lands harder than
  *orthodoxy*.
- ***Asuric pyramid* remains the book term.** Use it for frame moments
  and climactic geometry claims. Do not replace it with a new Sanskrit
  compound in the body.
- ***Vikṛti* is the explanatory category.** Use it sparingly to explain
  what the asuric pyramid produces: distorted recurrence of control.
  *Asura* names the actor / operating disposition; *vikṛti* names the
  structure the actor makes.

## 4. Where each layer lands in the book

### Front matter

- **Preface** — One paragraph naming the three dharmic layers underneath
  the courtroom: the ternary (categorical), sat-asat-vivek-buddhi
  (faculty the reader brings), satyam standard (*yat bhūta-hitam
  atyantam tat satyam*). One sentence stating that the courtroom is
  borrowed Abrahamic institutional form and that the close returns to
  the Indic frame.

- **Prologue** — Extended at three points:
  1. *The defendant remains the asuric pyramid.* The existing "asuric
     pyramid as the accused" stays. The new frame adds one structural
     sentence: the pyramid produces *vikṛti*, distorted recurrence of
     control. One paragraph.
  2. *Choice language is reserved for individuals.* Institutions do not
     possess *vivek-buddhi*; persons do. The Prologue can say that the
     apparatus preserved containment, but it should not say that an
     institution "chose asat." Named individuals may be judged by the
     choices they made when the evidence was available.
  3. *The antagonist nomenclature hierarchy* is introduced once,
     using the layered-vocabulary sentence above. One paragraph.

- **Note on Form** — Sharpened to explicitly distinguish:
  1. The borrowed Abrahamic institutional form (indictment, evidence,
     verdict).
  2. The dharmic individual faculty the reader brings (sat-asat-vivek-
     buddhi).
  3. The dharmic close that converts conviction into recalibration
     (not punishment, but karma; not payback, but restoration of
     *satyam*).

### Part VII — Life After PIE (*The remedy*)

- **Ch 19** — The remedy is the saṃskṛti choice at the language layer.
  The reader who has exercised *vivek-buddhi* throughout the trial is
  now offered the remedy: read Sanskrit as *saṃskṛti*, not as *prakṛti*,
  and reject the pyramid's *vikṛti* framework. Forward-point to *Second Shanti*
  for the civilizational scale of the same choice.

### Epilogue (*The closing statement*)

The existing three-beat formula — *Convict the pyramid. Kill the
imaginary ancestor. Invite the world.* — gains a fourth beat:

> ***Restore the standard.***

The full four-beat sequence:

> *Convict the pyramid. Kill the imaginary ancestor. Invite the world.
> Restore the standard.*

The standard is *yat bhūta-hitam atyantam tat satyam* — the welfare of
all beings, ultimate and entire:

> यत् भूतहितं अति अन्तं तत् सत्यं इति धारणा ।
>
> *yat bhūta-hitam ati antaṃ tat satyam iti dhāraṇā.*

This is why the invitation goes to the world. *Āryatva* is not race,
birth, or entitlement. It is the individual choice to align conduct with
sat — with what serves the welfare of beings fully and ultimately.
The Abrahamic court has convicted; the dharmic close restores the
standard; the next *saṃskṛti* generation extends the engineering. The
*Second Shanti* handoff is made here.

## 5. What this does NOT change

- **No new chapters.** The seven-Part structure stays.
- **No chapter renames.** Heading and italicized courtroom subtitle
  pairs stay verbatim.
- **No body re-writes in Parts II–V.** Internal testimony, physical
  evidence, technical evidence, and chain of custody operate in their
  existing voices. The polemic layer lands in the framing chapters
  (Prologue, Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 13, Ch 17/18, Ch 19, Epilogue) and the
  Note on Form.
- **No vocabulary saturation.** *Vaikṛtika*, *vikṛti*, *asat-aligned*
  are deep-frame terms deployed at frame moments. The body keeps the
  shorter prose vocabulary: *orthodoxy*, *apparatus*, *pyramid*,
  *church of progress*.

Each commit is internally coherent. The book can ship at any point;
