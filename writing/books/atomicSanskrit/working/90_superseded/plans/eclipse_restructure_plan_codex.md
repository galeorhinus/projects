# Eclipse Restructure Plan — Codex Draft

*Drafted 2026-06-14. Status: PLAN — pre-execution. Purpose: a Codex-authored restructure plan to compare and reconcile with `eclipse_restructure_plan_claude.md` before manuscript edits begin.*

---

## 1. Core Frame

The restructure should make the civilizational conflict the book's organizing frame, while keeping the engineering proof as the evidence.

The new mapping:

| Figure | Book role |
|---|---|
| **Sanskrit / Sūrya** | the protagonist; the radiant engineered order |
| **Svarbhānu / the pyramid** | the antagonist; the asuric-pyramidal actor-network with an implicit masculine apex |
| **Rāhu / PIE** | the eclipse device; a head without a living body, an ancestor without speakers |
| **Hindus / paramparā / caretakers** | the Atri-function; those who can find the Sun again |
| ***dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ*** | why caretaking now requires action |
| ***kṛṇvanto viśvam āryam*** | the final rallying cry after the Sun is found |

Use **Eclipse frame**, **asuric-pyramidal frame**, **Svarbhānu frame**, or **Vedic diagnostic frame**. Do not call this a mythic frame.

The book should stage three nested layers:

1. **Eclipse frame** — what happened: Sanskrit was eclipsed.
2. **Courtroom frame** — how the book proves it: charge, evidence, cross-examination, verdict.
3. **Restoration frame** — what follows: the caretakers/readers must become Atris and find the Sun.

The **Preface** stands outside the courtroom and gives the eclipse diagnosis.

The **six Parts** are fully inside the pyramid's courtroom.

The **Finale** exits the courtroom and returns to restoration.

## 3. Front Bookend

### Preface — The Eclipse

Function: diagnose the eclipse before the prosecution begins.

Core claims:

- Sanskrit is the Sun.
- Svarbhānu/the pyramid has darkened the field.
- PIE/Rāhu is the eclipse device.
- The engineering claim becomes the stakes: what was hidden was not merely a language, but a living engineered order.

Epigraph:

```text
यत्त्वा सूर्य स्वर्भानुस्तमसाविध्यदासुरः ।
अक्षेत्रविद्यथा मुग्धो भुवनान्यदीधयुः ॥
```

Working translation:

> When Svarbhānu the asura pierced you, O Sūrya, with darkness, the worlds looked about bewildered, like one ignorant of the field.

The current Vāk epigraph should move elsewhere, likely Ch9.

### Overture — Śaṅkha

Function: convene the matter and introduce the combatants.

Epigraph:

```text
yó agrató rocanā́nāṃ samudrā́d ádhi jajñiṣé |
śaṅkhéna hatvā́ rákṣāṃsy attríṇo ví ṣahāmahe ||
```

Working translation:

> You who were born at the head of the shining ones, up out of the sea: with the śaṅkha, having slain the rákṣas, we overcome the devourers.

Use the śaṅkha carefully: AVŚ 4.10 treats śaṅkha as protective object/amulet, not simply as a battlefield conch blast. The overture can still use it as a summons, but the Vedic citation should be described precisely.

Move the current Prologue's prosecution material into this threshold or into the Part I opener. Do not leave it as a standalone front-matter Prologue unless later reconciliation decides otherwise.

Resolved preference: the old Prologue material should become the **Part I opener**, while the Overture keeps only a short śaṅkha summons and party-introduction function.

## 4. Ch0 and Ch1

### Ch0 — Zero, Seekers, and the Infinite

Start with:

```text
pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idam ...
```

Function:

- Introduce seekers.
- Show that the seekers have also been caretakers.
- Move from seeking to responsibility: caretaking now requires action.
- Bring in `dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ`.
- End by hinting that the reader belongs to the Atri-function without explaining why yet.

Ch0 should not prosecute. It should show the civilization capable of the later Atri act: seekers, reciters, grammarians, mothers, teachers, students, lineages, and living correction.

Ch0 will likely need a real rewrite, and some existing material may no longer fit the new seekers-caretakers-Atri arc. Nothing should be deleted silently. Any removed Ch0 material must be copied into the restructure lost-and-found before deletion from the chapter, with a short note explaining why it was removed and whether it is a candidate for later reuse, appendix treatment, endnote treatment, or final deletion.

A possible closing direction:

> The caretakers this chapter introduces are the Atris of the Vedic story. The name does not point only backward to an ancient lineage; it names a civilizational function: those who can find the Sun when Svarbhānu has darkened the field. This chapter shows the function quietly. The Epilogue returns to the verse and places the work where it belongs: in the reader's hands.

This should be sharpened during drafting so it hints without over-explaining.

### Ch1 — One, Oppressors, and the Finite

Promote current Appendix Part 0 into Ch1.

Function:

- Introduce the antagonist.
- Name Svarbhānu/the asuric pyramid/the masculine apex.
- Establish the finite/oppressive one as the mirror inversion of Ch0's zero/fullness/infinity.
- Keep the chapter crisp so the two sides are clearly presented before the prosecution begins.

Important: do not simply move AP0 wholesale. Lift its strongest structural content into chapter voice, and create a separate AP0 residue file for material that is cut from Ch1 but may deserve later preservation or deletion.

Candidate epigraph: Gītā 16.6, or another daiva/asura diagnostic verse. This displaces the current use of Gītā 16.6 if Claude's epigraph map is accepted.

## 5. Courtroom Body

The Parts should remain completely inside the pyramid's courtroom. Their titles may be adjusted, but their function should be clear from the courtroom spine.

### Part I — The Prosecution

Function: charge and motive.

Contents:

- **Ch2 — Category Theft and Māyā**
  - Current botanical chapter becomes the theft chapter.
  - Sanskrit is made to answer to the wrong category.
  - Fold in the current dhātuḥ chapter's claim that dhātuḥ is not botanical root.

- **Ch3 — Motive and Method**
  - Current strategic chapter.
  - Explain why the pyramid needed the tree, the ancestor, the progress story, and the containment method.

- **Ch4 — The Fourth Abrahamic Religion**
  - The pyramid as church of progress.
  - The institutional/religious formation that keeps PIE and the progress-dogma alive.

### Part II — Sanskrit's Internal Testimony

Function: Sanskrit's own categories testify against the pyramid's categories.

Contents:

- **Ch5 — Siddha and Kārya**
  - The answer to disorder is not codification but calibration.
  - The bond is the bond of calibration.
  - Fold in dhātuḥ material that belongs to discovered order rather than manufactured rule.

- **Ch6 — Apabhraṃśa and Entropy**
  - Sanskrit's natural enemy is entropy.
  - The asuric-pyramidal system feeds entropy by manufacturing field-blindness and category confusion.
  - Fold in dhātuḥ material about preserving identity through change and resisting entropy.

### Part III — Physical Evidence

Function: the mouth and sound-field provide physical evidence.

Contents remain current:

- **Ch7 — Oṃ / Anatomy of Sound**
- **Ch8 — Subcontinental Sound-Field**
- **Ch9 — Varṇamālā / Sonomeric Grid**

Move the current Vāk epigraph here if it leaves the Preface. Ch9 is the natural home because it shows speech seen/not seen at the level of the mouth-map and varṇamālā.

### Part IV — Technical Evidence

Function: the architecture itself proves the engineering claim.

Contents remain current:

- **Ch10 — Building the Dhātuḥ**
- **Ch11 — Building the Kriyā**
- **Ch12 — Building the Vākya**

Ch10 receives the technical residue from the dissolved standalone dhātuḥ chapter, especially:

- one word, many sciences;
- dhātuḥ as structural constituent, not botanical metaphor;
- why "Atomic Sanskrit" is the right title;
- atom/root contrast.

### Part V — Chain of Custody

Function: preservation shows the architecture was carried, not corrupted.

Contents remain current:

- **Ch13 — Why Preservation Needs Engineering**
- **Ch14 — The Calibration Matrix**
- **Ch15 — Aural Architecture**

The subtitle **Chain of custody** works well because these chapters prove continuity through calibrated transmission.

Resolved preference: keep **Ch16** out of Part V. It belongs in Part VI because it cross-examines the retroflex/substrate claim.

### Part VI — Cross-Examination and Verdict

Function: the pyramid's claims are cross-examined and PIE is convicted.

Contents:

- **Ch16 — Flexing the Retroflex**
- **Ch17 — The Wrong Question**
- **Ch18 — PIE in the Sky**

Ch18 remains the cross-examination-and-verdict threshold, but the exact weight of the verdict close should be revisited after the Finale is drafted. Avoid creating two competing climaxes: if Śānti/Finale carries the restoration verdict, Ch18 should soften into a handoff rather than ending with an over-final courtroom close.

## 6. Finale

### Finale — Śānti / Restoration

Function: leave the courtroom and return to the Indic frame.

Contents:

- **Ch19 — Life After PIE**
  - What becomes possible after the false ancestor is killed.
  - Relearning, recalibration, and propagation.

- **Epilogue — The Atris Find the Sun**
  - Reveal the payoff of Ch0's hint.
  - The caretakers/readers are called to become Atris.
  - Sanskrit/Sūrya is found again.
  - `kṛṇvanto viśvam āryam` becomes the rallying cry.

Epilogue epigraph should be the Atri-recovery verse from RV 5.40, but the exact verse number must be verified before print. Current discussion used RV 5.40.6; Claude flags likely RV 5.40.9. Resolve with a source check.

Closing mantra remains open:

- Default: AVŚ 4.10.3, śaṅkha as all-healer.
- Fallback: a śānti/mangalam mantra, only if the Finale draft needs restoration more than śaṅkha reprise.

Resolved preference: use **AVŚ 4.10.3** as the default closing bookend, then confirm after the Finale draft.

## 12. Lost-and-Found Rule

No material is removed silently during this restructure.

Create a working file before execution, recommended path:

```text
atomicSanskrit/working/40_reference/source_material/eclipse_restructure_lost_and_found.md
```

Every significant cut must be logged there before it is removed from the manuscript. Each entry should include:

- source file and section;
- removed passage or compressed summary if the passage is long;
- reason for removal;
- recommended disposition: reuse later, move to appendix, move to endnote, merge elsewhere, or candidate for deletion;
- date/pass name.

This applies especially to Ch0, AP0-to-Ch1 promotion, the Prologue move, and the dissolved dhātuḥ chapter. The goal is not to preserve everything in the final book; the goal is to make every removal visible so important material is not lost accidentally.
