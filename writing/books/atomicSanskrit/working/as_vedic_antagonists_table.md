# The Antagonists of the Four Vedas — Characteristics, Epithets, Narrative, Function

> **Working reference.** Every locatable antagonist across the four Vedic saṃhitās, with epithets/adjectives, narrative, and — the analytical payoff — a **function** column classifying each by the *containment cluster* (withhold / enclose / cover / obstruct / hoard / darken / parch) vs. *harm / afflict / devour / other*. Compiled via three parallel research passes against Macdonell's *Vedic Mythology*, Grassmann, Hale, Whitney's AV, Griffith's White Yajurveda, and wisdomlib, under a strict no-fabrication rule. **Feeds Ch 3 §3.6 ("The Vedic Shape of Containment").** Companion to [`as_asura_sura_asurya_vedic_survey.md`](as_asura_sura_asurya_vedic_survey.md).

**The headline finding:** the RV's *central, most-developed* antagonist myths are pure **containment** across the three cosmic goods — **Vṛtra** (waters), **Vala + the Paṇis** (cattle/light), **Svarbhānu** (the sun) — each resolved by *release* (un-cover, open, set free). The antagonist *population* is broader than containment (the RV also has flesh-eaters/sorcerers; the AV is mostly affliction), so §3.6 should claim the containment pattern for the **core cosmic myths**, not for every demon. That is the defensible, honest line.

## 5. Sāmaveda

Almost entirely **borrowed Ṛgveda verses** (~1,875 verses, only ~75 non-RV per widely-cited figures ❓; the bulk from RV Books 8–9). Its antagonist-field is therefore **the Ṛgveda's** (Vṛtra, Śuṣṇa, Paṇis, Dasyus…), carried into the *ārcika* verses set to melody. **No SV-specific antagonist material was confirmed** in the fetched sources.

## 6. Synthesis — the function pattern

### 6.1 The containment triad (the §3.6 spine)
The RV's three most-developed cosmic antagonists are each a pure **container** of one of the three great goods, each resolved by **release**:

| Good withheld | Container-demon | The release |
|---|---|---|
| **Waters** | **Vṛtra / Ahi** (encloses, covers — √vṛ) | Indra's *vajra* splits him; the waters run |
| **Cattle / light / dawn** | **Vala + the Paṇis** (encloses / hoards) | Indra + Aṅgirases + Bṛhaspati break the cave; the herd & light are freed |
| **The sun** | **Svarbhānu** (darkens, covers) | Indra dissipates his *māyā*; **Atri restores "the eye of Sūrya"** |

The invariant verb is **release** — *un-cover, open, set free*. This is the archetype §3.6 needs, in the text's own vocabulary. And **Svarbhānu = RV 5.40 = the book's eclipse spine** (Sūrya / Svarbhānu / Rāhu / **the Atris**), a *containment-of-light* act, with Svarbhānu explicitly *āsura*.

### 6.2 Function tally (honest)
- **Containment cluster (withhold / enclose / cover / obstruct / hoard / darken / parch):** RV named ≈ **10 of 15** (Vṛtra, Vala, Śuṣṇa, Śambara, Pipru, Arbuda, Viśvarūpa, Svarbhānu, Kuyava, + Vala); RV classes ≈ **6 of 11** (Paṇis, Vala, Dasyus, Dāsas, Dānavas, Arati). The *central, most-elaborated* myths are all containment.
- **Harm / afflict / devour (NOT containment):** RV — Rakṣas, Yātudhānas, Kimīdins (flesh-eaters, sorcerers). AV — this is the **dominant** field: piśāca, takman (fever), kṛtyā (sorcery), sadānvā, gandharva-possession, attriṇ.
- **Other / adversary:** Namuci (RV; but a *hoard* in the YV Soma-theft form), Dhuni-Cumuri, Varcin, Uraṇa, Druh; plural asuras (post-Rigvedic adversary sense).
- **The one clean cross-corpus *withholder*:** **Arāti** — niggardliness personified — appears in *both* RV and AV as pure containment-by-withholding.

### 6.3 RV vs. AV (the register shift)
The **RV** fights **cosmic** enemies of Indra — the enclosing serpent, the parcher, the cow-penners — in a mythic-heroic register. The **AV** broadens the field **downward and inward**: disease-demons (fever), flesh-eaters (piśāca), sorcerers (yātudhāna/kṛtyā), personified social evils (arāti), household infesters (sadānvā). *The RV fights the enemy of the waters; the AV fights the demon at the bedside, the doorway, and the cattle-pen.* This is **domain register**, not chronology — the AV's charm-corpus simply addresses a different field of action.

### 6.4 The defensible claim for Ch 3 §3.6
Do **not** claim "every Vedic demon is a container." Claim: **the RV's central cosmic-antagonist myths — the most-developed, most-cross-referenced ones — are containment across water, cattle, and light, and their resolution is always *release*.** The harm/affliction demons (rakṣas, piśāca, takman) are real and are *not* containment; naming them honestly strengthens the case for the ones that are. **Vṛtra = the enclosure, Vala = the cave, the Paṇis = the hoarders, Svarbhānu = the darkener — the Veda names the adversary by his withholding function, and the hero by his releasing one.**
