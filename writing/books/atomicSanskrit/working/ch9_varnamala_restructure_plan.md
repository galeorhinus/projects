## The thesis the chapter should state and serve

> **The varṇamālā is not an alphabet — it is a parts inventory. Every sonomer is an engineered component with an *address* (where and how the body makes it), a *role* (nucleus or contact), and a *measured duration* (mātrā) — spaced so no part can be mistaken for its neighbor, and crossed so the axes multiply. Chapter 10 snaps these parts together into atoms.**

This is exactly the set Chapter 10 assumes on arrival (§§10.3–10.5): specified components, the vowel/consonant role split, the mātrā timing behind C/V1/V2 notation, and guaranteed distinctness across 2,168 atoms.

## The Sound Volume section — content notes

- Consonant frame: 5×7 = 35 possible consonant cells; 25 *sparśa* + 4 *antaḥstha* + 4 *ūṣman* = 33 filled cells. The two empty cells are themselves evidence of grid-thinking — the frame exists independent of fill.
- Attach the 14 vowels (अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ऋ ॠ ऌ ॡ ए ऐ ओ औ) as the third dimension: 5 × 7 × 14 = 490 possible consonant-vowel addresses. The two empty consonant cells extend through all 14 vowel positions, leaving 462 occupied consonant-vowel addresses. ॡ is flagged as the rarely deployed corner — the slot exists because the grid demands completeness; same design-signature argument as ळ.
- Teaching anchor: a **bārahkhaḍī-style** primer drill is the volume unrolled one fiber at a time. Use "style" because teaching traditions abbreviate or expand the vowel roll-out differently; the engineering section can show the full 14-vowel Sanskrit inventory while noting that primers vary. Same "two views, one object" move as §9.1 (teaching row in the inherited classroom language, sound volume in engineering language).
- Script note: the vowel-mātrā diacritics are the z-coordinate made visible — one sentence, pointer to AP3, no script development here.
- Handoff tease: *gam* enters the volume at one cell, passes through a nucleus, exits at another — an atom is a path. Chapter 10 names the paths.
