# Contextual Sweep for `Question`, `Answer`, `Work`, and `Hold`

**Status:** `HOLD` SWEEP IMPLEMENTED — 694 abstract or repetitive uses revised; 51 deliberate uses retained
**Scope:** Complete manuscript, Part openers, appendices, captions, short endnotes, and Source and Reference Companion  
**Constraint:** Contextual revision only. Literal meanings, quotations, physical actions, and deliberate thematic uses remain unchanged.

## Purpose

The manuscript asks `question`, `answer`, `work`, and `hold` to express too many different actions and relationships. Repetition weakens otherwise precise sentences, while the broad words sometimes leave the reader to infer what a person, passage, mechanism, or architecture actually does.

This is a contextual sweep rather than a synonym replacement. Each occurrence must be read inside its paragraph and classified as **retain**, **delete**, or **replace with the exact action or relation**.

Punch is a reason to retain a broad word. Do not trade a memorable accusation, refrain, or hammer for a more technical synonym merely because the synonym is narrower. Replace only when repetition weakens the passage, the word obscures the action, or the claim becomes inaccurate.

## `Question`

Reserve `question` for an explicit inquiry, a quoted inquiry, or an established refrain. When the sentence uses `question` only to contain a topic, state the activity directly:

- genealogy traces ancestry;
- architecture explains construction;
- a chapter tests authorship or chronology;
- evidence leaves a matter unresolved;
- a researcher investigates a proposed relationship.

Avoid habitual constructions such as `the question of`, `raises the question`, `returns to the question`, and `begins with a different question` when the passage can identify what is being traced, tested, or explained.

## `Answer`

Reserve `answer` for an actual reply to a stated question, a received doctrinal conclusion, or an established refrain such as `There are two answers: zero and infinity` and `The answer is always zero`.

When no one is replying to a question, identify the operation directly:

- a domain may preserve something, prevent entropy, or allow changing use;
- a chapter may explain, demonstrate, establish, expose, or rebut something;
- evidence may show, support, contradict, or leave a question unresolved;
- a person may submit to, conform to, or be subordinated to an authority.

Do not replace `answer` mechanically with `response` or `solution`. Those words preserve the same abstraction unless the passage truly concerns a response or solution.

## `Work`

When `work` is a noun, identify whether the sentence means:

- task, responsibility, effort, labor, practice, or discipline;
- analysis, inquiry, research, study, or reasoning;
- writing, composition, construction, or craft;
- contribution, effect, result, or remaining step.

When `work` is a verb, identify the action directly. Candidate verbs include:

- operate, function, perform, regulate, or produce;
- establish, demonstrate, explain, support, or distinguish;
- preserve, transmit, correct, constrain, or change.

Delete the word when the sentence becomes clearer without it. Retain established phrases such as `working translation`, `working document`, and `working hypothesis` unless their local use creates ambiguity.

## `Hold`

Choose the replacement according to the relationship:

- **Preservation:** preserve, maintain, keep intact, transmit, or keep available.
- **Containment:** contain, include, comprise, or encode.
- **Structural support:** support, sustain, anchor, or stabilize.
- **Coordination:** align, coordinate, bind, or connect.
- **Identity or possession:** retain, possess, or have.
- **Validity:** stand, remain valid, continue to apply, or recur.
- **Authority:** control, govern, command, or administer.
- **Position:** occupy, sit at, or belong in.

Retain `hold` where its physical or idiomatic image is deliberate, including phrases such as `hold the line` and `hold in memory`.

### Finding

The sweep requires substantial contextual replacement. `Hold` is not an
absolute lexical prohibition because a small set of uses remains precise and
deliberate. Everywhere else, the sentence should identify the actual action or
relation.

**Retain:**

- the lexical meaning of an atom, especially ⟪भृ⟫ (*bhṛ*, to bear, carry, or
  hold);
- physical actions such as holding pitch, breath, a position, or an object;
- established idioms such as *hold the line*, *hold in memory*, *hold office*,
  *hold a title*, and *hold accountable*;
- the deliberate containment image *hold back the light*;
- a hammer or refrain whose force depends on the short verb.

**Replace:**

- a language, corpus, Veda, chapter, note, source, or word that *holds*
  information: use *contains, records, preserves, encodes, expresses,* or
  *displays* according to the passage;
- a figure, table, row, scaffold, cell, class, or inventory that *holds* a
  number or set: use *contains, includes, comprises, accounts for, covers,* or
  *lists*;
- an architecture or discipline that *holds* a language, standard, relation,
  or system: use *preserves, sustains, stabilizes, coordinates, binds,* or
  *keeps available*;
- a proposition, distinction, analogy, or pattern that *holds*: state that it
  *remains valid, recurs, survives comparison,* or *continues to apply*;
- a person or institution that *holds* a belief, office of control, or
  authority: use *accepts, advances, controls, administers, occupies,* or
  *commands*;
- passive constructions such as *is held by* when the actual agent preserves,
  transmits, controls, supports, or confines the object.

Chapter 16 line 15 supplied a representative correction:

> ~~Across the Indian subcontinent, language holds a shared signature of mouth
> and mind.~~
>
> Languages across the Indian subcontinent share a signature of mouth and
> mind.

The same paragraph contained three abstract uses of `hold`; one direct
construction replaced all three. This should guide the larger sweep: revise
the sentence around the intended action instead of exchanging `hold` for a
single favorite synonym.
