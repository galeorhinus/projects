## Thesis

Sanskrit was built from the subcontinental **mouth and mind** and encoded into the Vedas. The
anatomical substrate — the curled-tongue retroflex, the doubled sound — and the philosophical
substrate — the self as receiver rather than ego-master, action chained as it is lived — are
both present across the subcontinent's languages (Tamil and Mundari carry them living), both
engineered into Sanskrit, and both standing in the Veda from its first word. Pāṇini documents
the architecture; he does not invent it.

Only once that stands does the chapter turn. The **portability thesis** — that Sanskrit is a
transportable Indo-European import, carried in and *then* fitted with local features by contact
— cannot survive a language whose mouth and mind are subcontinental to the root. **Sanskrit is
not portable. It is rooted.**

# PART A — Lay the facts (the pyramid stays off-stage)

## Cold open — *agnimīḷe* (the mouth in the first word)

RV 1.1.1, the first sentence of the Veda, praises Agni with **ईळे (*īḷe*)** — carrying the
retroflex lateral **ळ (*ḷ*)**, made by curling the tongue back to the dome.

> अग्निमीळे पुरोहितं यज्ञस्य देवमृत्विजम् ।
> *agním **īḷe** puróhitaṃ yajñásya devám ṛtvíjam*

The Veda *opens its mouth* on a subcontinental sound. Second beat: the name **ऋ**ग्वेद opens on
the vocalic **ऋ (*ṛ*)**, classed **मूर्धन्य (*mūrdhanya*, retroflex)**. The text's name and its
first word are both retroflex. (State this as a *fact*; no pyramid yet. Anchor with the vocal
apparatus figure, `figures/adivadya/vocal_apparatus_sanskrit.svg`.) Even *dive-dive* (word-
doubling) sits a few verses later, in the same hymn — the mouth at work from the opening breath.

## §1 — The Mouth (the anatomical / sound substrate)

The curled tongue and the doubled sound — how the subcontinent makes and patterns sound.

- **Retroflex — मूर्धन्य (*mūrdhanya*).** The tongue curls to the dome. Present across Tamil,
  Mundari, and the subcontinent's languages; built into the *varṇamālā*; in the Veda's first
  word. *What Pāṇini documents as* मूर्धन्य. [Integrate the existing Ch 16 retroflex material as
  this sub-section — the *mūrdhanya* apparatus, the cross-language survey, the vocal-tract
  argument.]
- **Reduplication — अभ्यास (*abhyāsa*).** The mouth doubles a sound. The field paints the world
  by echoing it (Munda mimetic ≈10% of the lexicon; Dravidian echo/expressive). In Sanskrit the
  doubling is built across the whole verbal architecture — five rule-bound formations +
  word-doubling — and the reduplicating class carries an acoustic signature (C4, Ch 11 §11.6).
  *What Pāṇini documents as* the perfect, the reduplicating class, the desiderative, the
  intensive, the *caṅ*-aorist, and *āmreḍita*. [→ Evidence bank: The Mouth.]

## §2 — The Mind (the philosophical / meaning substrate)

The self as receiver, the act as lived — how the subcontinent frames experience.

- **Ego-displacement / receiver-grammar — सम्प्रदान (*sampradāna*).** The human is the one a
  state flows *to*, not the master who seizes it. The field's stance (Tamil *eṉakku paci*;
  Mundari marks *reṅgej* "hunger" as an Experiencer-Object verb). In the Veda the receiver-
  psychology runs through the deepest states (trust, peace, grace, awe, safety, knowledge); the
  Jñāna Sūkta makes even *knowledge* a thing that reveals itself *to* a receiver — **Vāk reveals
  herself.** *What Pāṇini documents as* सम्प्रदान (1.4.32 family). [→ Evidence bank: The Mind.]
- **Conjunctive participle / absolutive.** Action chained in the order it is lived, the prior act
  folded into one frozen form. The field's habit (Tamil *sāppiṭṭu vandān*; Telugu / Kannada;
  Mundari converb). In the Veda: *pītvā*, *hatvā*, *niṣadya*, *saṃgatvāya*. *What Pāṇini
  documents as* the absolutive (*ktvā / lyap*). [→ Evidence bank: The Mind.]

## §3 — One substrate, encoded (positive verdict, still no pyramid)

The mouth and the mind are one subcontinental substrate. It built the language; it stands in the
Veda from the first word; Pāṇini documents it whole. Sanskrit is uniquely architected in the
subcontinent — in *sound* and in *meaning* alike. State the verdict flat, with no antagonist in
view: this is simply what Sanskrit is.

# PART B — Dismantle the portability thesis (the pyramid enters)

## §4 — Name the portability thesis

Only now does the antagonist appear. The pyramid's claim: Sanskrit is a *portable* Indo-European
object — carried into the subcontinent by migration, then fitted with the retroflex and the
syntax by **substrate contact** ("areal borrowing"). The features just laid out are, on this
account, contamination acquired *after* arrival. (First appearance of *Western philological
dogma* / the *asuric pyramid* in the chapter.)

## §5 — It cannot be borrowed (the inversion + the method)

- **Avestan inversion (HIGHLIGHT 1).** The mouth and the mind are absent from the "sister"
  Avestan / Old Iranian — exactly what *built-in-the-field* predicts, not late borrowing. A
  *portable* language would not carry the field's mouth and mind; a language *built* in the field
  carries nothing else. (Reuse the Ch 16 retroflex move: "Avestan does not run on it.")
- **Cross-family method (HIGHLIGHT 4).** The features span the families the pyramid swears are
  *unrelated* — Tamil, Telugu, Kannada + Mundari, Ho, Santali. When the "unrelated" families all
  carry the same mouth and mind Sanskrit carries, **inheritance is off the table by the pyramid's
  own taxonomy.** Exclude Hindi/Marathi *on purpose* (dismissible as intra-*"Indo-Aryan"*
  inheritance — naming them weakens the case). *Not a gap; the trap.*
- **The named exhibit (HIGHLIGHT 2).** The gerund/absolutive is *already on the pyramid's
  "borrowed from Dravidian" list* (Emeneau notes it distinguishes Sanskrit from other
  Indo-European languages and looks to non-IE Indian syntax for the "stimulus"). We just showed
  it is mouth-and-mind native — so we are reclassifying their own evidence, not changing the
  subject.

## §6 — The corpus is subcontinental from its first word (harvest *agnimīḷe*) + the layers are not a clock

- **The "oldest"-is-most-subcontinental contradiction.** Grant the pyramid's chronology
  *arguendo*: the layer it calls "oldest" — the Ṛgveda — opens on a retroflex (*agnimīḷe*) and
  runs the mouth and the mind from word one.
  - *Horn A.* If the Ṛgveda is the pyramid's pristine, "oldest" layer, it cannot be saturated
    with the subcontinental mouth and mind. But it is — from the first word.
  - *Horn B.* If that saturation entered by contact, the "oldest" layer is already a
    *subcontinentalized* composition — not pristine, not portable. Portability collapses either
    way.
- **The layers are not a chronology — style, not time.** The differences between the strata and
  the four Vedas are *functional* (meter, chant, ritual, dialogue), not developmental — the
  book's two-axis architecture (*vaidika/laukika*, *chandas/bhāṣā*) one level deeper. Pāṇini tags
  the "archaic" forms *chandasi* (a **mode**, not a date); the Sāmaveda is Ṛgvedic verse re-set
  for chant (same words, different style). **Domain is not chronology. Mode is not drift.** [Full
  treatment in the §3-style notes below.]

## §7 — Engineering, not borrowing (verdict)

- **Borrowing bolts a feature on; engineering builds it in.** Sanskrit integrates each habit in
  the way that fits it — retroflex by place of articulation in the *varṇamālā*; reduplication by
  rule-bound *abhyāsa* and assigned slots; the receiver by the *sampradāna* role; action-chaining
  by *ktvā / lyap*. A borrower leaves features raw and peripheral; an engineer makes them
  structural. That versatility is the signature of design.
- **Verdict.** Sanskrit is not portable. It is rooted — built from the subcontinental mouth and
  mind, encoded in the Veda, documented by Pāṇini. The portability thesis collapses on the
  corpus's own first word. (Tie *agnimīḷe* open → close.)

## Reduplication (*abhyāsa*) — five formations + word-doubling
**Field (living):** Munda mimetic ≈10% of the lexicon (Ho, Mundari, Kharia, Santali, Sora;
Phillips & Harrison 2017); Mundari *oṛa-moṛa*; Santali *gɛl* → *gɛ-gɛl*. Tamil *pala-pala* /

**Engineered — built across the whole verbal architecture** (reduced + rule-bound *abhyāsa*; one
onset + short vowel; *kuhoś cuḥ*, velar→palatal: *kṛ* → *cakāra*). Five formations + word-
doubling, all anchored in Ṛgvedic mantra (Gemini, all **[VERIFY]** until Saṃhitā-checked):

1. **Class III — जुहोत्यादि (*juhotyādi*).** *dadāti* @ RV 10.107.7 (*dakṣiṇāśvaṃ dakṣiṇā gāṃ
   dadāti*, √dā) + *bibharti* @ RV 7.87.4 (√bhṛ).
2. **Perfect — लिट् (*liṭ*).** *dadarśa* @ RV 1.164.4 (*ko dadarśa prathamaṃ jāyamānam*, √dṛś) +
   *cakāra* @ RV 1.32.1 (*yāni cakāra prathamāni vajrī* — the Vṛtra hymn's opening, √kṛ).
   [*jaghāna* @ RV 1.32.7 possible third — wording garbled, verify.]
3. **Desiderative — सन् (*san*).** *jighāṃsasi* @ RV 7.86.4 (√han, "wish to slay") + *pipāsati*
   @ RV 8.4.11 (√pā, "desires to drink").
4. **Intensive — यङ् (*yaṅ* / yaṅluk).** The *expansive* face. *ā-nonavīti* @ RV 10.68.12 (√nu,
   "shouts again and again").
5. **Reduplicated aorist — चङ् (*caṅ*), causative.** *ajījanat* @ RV 10.134.1 (√jan) +

**Incorporation reading.** Formations 1–3 incorporate the field's doubling *by concision*
(minimal *abhyāsa* as grammatical signal); the intensive and *āmreḍita* incorporate it *by
adaptation* (kept expansive/expressive, assigned a slot). One signature, two mechanisms.

**Phonetic tie (Ch 11 §11.6 / by-the-numbers):** *juhotyādi* enriches the voiced-aspirate column
**C4** above any other gaṇa — the doubled onset must carry across the syllable boundary.
Accidental contact does not select for acoustic robustness; an engineered subsystem does.

# Evidence bank — The Mind

## Receiver-grammar (*sampradāna*) — ego-displacement
**Frame.** English makes the human the **agent** ("I believe him," "I forgive him," "we have
peace"); the subcontinental stance makes the human the **receiver** — the state *flows to* a
person. Two faces: **recipient** (faith/peace/grace) and **experiencer** (hunger/liking/knowing).

**Field (experiencer face, living):** Tamil *eṉakku paci* / *eṉakku teriyum*; Telugu *nāku
ākaḷigā undi* / *nāku telusu*; Kannada *nanage haśivāgide* / *nanage gottu*; Mundari *reṅgej*
Experiencer-Object verb (Badenoch, *A Course in Mundari*).

**Engineered (the *kāraka* fingerprint).** The receiver is a defined role — **सम्प्रदान
(*sampradāna*)** — decoded across a sūtra-family: **1.4.32** *karmaṇā yam abhipraiti sa
sampradānam* (general recipient); **1.4.33** *rucyarthānāṃ prīyamāṇaḥ* (the pleased experiencer;
*mahyaṃ rocate* / *devadattāya rocate modakaḥ*); **2.3.13** *caturthī sampradāne* (the case);
**2.3.16** (dative with *namas/svasti/svāhā/svadhā*); **1.4.37** *krudhadruherṣyāsūyārthānāṃ yaṃ
prati kopaḥ* (target of anger). Pāṇini documents a family of rules — *he decoded a matrix, he did
not invent a way to think.*

## Conjunctive participle / absolutive
**Field (living, subject-controlled — not "loose"):** Tamil *sāppiṭṭu vandān*; Telugu *nēnu
aṅgaḍiki veḷḷ-i, pūlu kon-i … vaccānu*; Kannada *nānu aṅgaḍige hōg-i, haṇṇu tan-du … bandenu*;
Mundari serial/converb *jom-kedte senojana* [VERIFY form vs Osada].

**Engineered (Sanskrit):** *ktvā* on a bare root (*kṛtvā, khāditvā*); *lyap* on a prefixed root
(*upagamya, praṇamya*) — preverb-conditioned allomorphy Pāṇini documents; one indeclinable folds
a whole prior clause (*alpākṣaram* economy, Ch 10).

**Caveat — default coreference, NOT a "same-subject logic gate."** Usually same-agent, but it is
*default* coreference, not absolute: Sanskrit has documented exceptions — passive main clause →
corefers with the *agent* (तेन भुक्त्वा गम्यते); dative-experiencer subjects (Tikkanen 1987;
Lowe). And the same-subject converb is itself *areal* (Tamil, Mundari behave the same), not a
Sanskrit-unique gate. **Drop "absolutely / strictly identical."**
