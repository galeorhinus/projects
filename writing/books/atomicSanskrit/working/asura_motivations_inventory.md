## 1. Headline Finding

The manuscript describes asura motivation through a tightly interlocking vocabulary that recurs across Prologue, body chapters, Epilogue, and appendices. The recurring terms:

- **Jealousy** (institutional-possessive, not emotional)
- **Civilizational envy**
- **Inferiority panic**
- **Collective narcissism**
- **Desire to lord over**
- **Desire to appropriate / take / control what was not earned**
- **Consolidation of power through hierarchy**
- **Deception**
- **Withholding of light**
- **Category-theft**
- **Containment**
- **Extraction**
- **Cannot tolerate distributed order without apex command**

The book grounds all of this morphologically in the privative *a-suraḥ* ("not-light"): an asuric formation operates by withholding light. The categorical name for the operating mode is ***asuratva*** (असुरत्व); the geometric signature is the ***pyramid***; the structural diagnosis is ***vikṛti*** (विकृति) — recurrence distorted into control.

The motivation is never reduced to individual psychology; it is consistently described as a *structural* disposition that operates institutionally and reproduces fractally — though it is also explicitly carried by named individual operators (Schleicher above all).

## 5. Tensions and productive contradictions

- **T1** — Structural motive vs. psychological motive. Prologue §3 declares "the motive is structural"; Ch 3 §3.6 invokes "civilizational envy, inferiority panic, collective narcissism" as "the psychological engine underneath the philological machinery." Resolved by *vikṛti* — recurrence-into-control as a fractal at both scales.
- **T2** — Asuras-as-myth-category vs. asuras-as-named-historical-operators. Resolved by Ch 3 §3.6: "*Asuratva* is not only a category of myth… individual operators carry the same disposition into specific named cases."
- **T3** — Jealousy of devas vs. fear of distributed order. The two frames target different objects (asuras want the *thing* devas have vs. asuras want the *non-existence of* alternative geometry); book holds both.
- **T4** — "The accused is not every scholar" vs. systemic indictment. Resolved by distinguishing the formation (the pyramid as accused) from the participants (acting from various motives within it).
- **T5** — Co-option vs. demotion as alternative strategies. Both are *asuratva*; book treats them sometimes as one (category-theft) and sometimes as two parallel asuric moves.
