## Starting Point

The author connects the three domains and three shantis with तापत्रय (*tāpatraya*), the three kinds of stress experienced by a being. His starting description places stress arising within the being in the first domain. The complete relationship between the traditional categories and the proposed model remains to be developed; this note does not present the model as an established traditional interpretation.

Earlier volumes may retain their existing high-level explanation of inside-out order and the three domains. The reserved material is the detailed extrapolation from microscopic beings to a person to a larger being, including the proposed meaning of "god."

## Explicit Assumptions

For the thought experiment, ASSUME that every cell, or another suitable microscopic constituent, in Parag's body is an independently thinking creature. Some resemble animals; some resemble humans. Within this analogy, "human-like" means capable of distinguishing सत् (*sat*) from असत् (*asat*). The other constituents still make independent decisions.

These are stipulated premises, not claims that ordinary biological cells have human-like thought or moral discernment. The distinction between animal-like and human-like constituents is internal to this model, not a conclusion about actual animal cognition.

## First Extrapolation: Constituents and the Person

Parag is a larger unit made from smaller participants. From their perspective, the author proposes calling him their "god."

The word does not imply omnipotence. The author asks:

> Do I control my cells, or do they control me?

He can affect their well-being through nourishment or deprivation and can damage or kill them. They, in turn, can collectively generate bodily signals that prompt him to act. The author's example is dehydration leading to thirst and drinking water.

His control over the whole body is limited. He does not consciously direct every bodily process. Breathing and autonomic functions illustrate dependence on processes that do not wait for a conscious command. The physiological details can be checked when the analogy is developed; the central claim here is limited control and reciprocal dependence, not the impossibility of temporarily holding one's breath.

The larger being affects its constituents, and their condition affects the larger being. Being the whole does not make it an independent, all-powerful ruler of the parts.

## Second Extrapolation: Persons and a Larger Being

Now ASSUME that Parag, other humans, animals, and other beings on the planet are themselves constituents of something larger. The author proposes calling that extrapolated being "god," extending the previous relationship by one scale.

This is the author's proposed interpretation, not a verified claim that every culture means this by "god," nor a demonstration that such a larger conscious being exists.

The intended repetition is:

1. Smaller participants compose a larger being without surrendering all independent activity.
2. Their condition and actions influence the larger being.
3. The larger being influences the conditions in which its constituents live.
4. The same relationship may recur when that larger being is considered as a constituent at another scale.

## Author's Domain Formulation

> The third domain is actually a collection of the second domain, just like the second domain is a collection of the first domain.

Preserve this as the proposed formulation to discuss. Do not silently replace it with a final mapping.

The model's potential significance is that the larger order is constituted from within rather than imposed by a separate apex. A larger scale is not automatically a higher authority. The proposed "god" participates in a relationship of dependence and influence rather than standing outside the system as its absolute commander.

## Discussion Points to Develop

- **Fractal rather than merely nested:** Specify the recurring relationship. Reciprocal influence, partial agency, dependence, and coordination are candidate features. Small things inside larger things alone do not establish the proposed fractality.
- **Scale versus domain:** Distinguish a hierarchy of constituents and wholes from three domains of stress relative to a particular being. A person's first domain already contains many constituents. Explain how each domain maps onto the next scale rather than assuming that "domain" and "being" are interchangeable.
- **Observer's position:** Consider whether the domain labels change when the viewpoint moves from a microscopic participant to Parag to the extrapolated larger being. This is an open question, not a completed part of the author's model.
- **Meaning of "god":** Establish the intended meaning within this model before comparing it with any specific tradition. Do not claim all traditions already agree.
- **Calibration and shanti:** The author has not yet specified the calibrant, correction process, or relationship between sat/asat discernment and shanti at every scale. Leave these to the continuing discussion rather than inventing them.
- **Series scope:** The final-volume explanation must be reconciled with the existing instruction that the series focuses on the second domain. Exploring its relation to the other domains does not by itself authorize a change in that scope.

## Possible Summary for Later Development

The whole is made of participants whose activity it cannot completely command. It affects their lives, but it also depends on them. The author proposes that this relationship repeats across scales, so that what appears as a whole from below becomes a participant when viewed from above.

This is a conceptual summary of the thought experiment, not publication prose or evidence of a physical theory.
