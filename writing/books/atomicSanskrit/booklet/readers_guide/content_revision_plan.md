## Pass 1: Findings

### Diagnosis

The engineering explanation is substantially present. So are the Vedic calibrant, the two domains, India's sound field, and the inside-out bridge. The guide becomes mild mainly because it separates these demonstrations from the institutional and civilizational conflict that gives them their significance in the book.

The current narrative does eventually accuse labels of concealment, but the sustained explanation of who maintains those labels, why the book says they need them, and how they replace civilizational memory is largely absent. The chapter guide reinforces that dilution by replacing such titles as "The Fourth Abrahamic Religion" and "PIE in the Sky" with more general descriptions. Some invitations are sharper than the narrative, but an invitation cannot carry an argument the reader has not yet encountered.

This is not a recommendation to distribute accusations evenly over every page. Sound production and worked word formations need room to explain the evidence. The proposed repair is to restore the complete argument at its proper locations and connect the technical demonstrations to it.

### Eight Concrete Losses of Content

These excerpts are diagnostic examples, not proposed line edits. The full rewrite will follow the approved section plan.

1. **The opening does not declare the whole dispute.** `why` ends its account with "The book then challenges the accounts of Sanskrit's origin that conceal that architecture." It does not yet say which account, what was substituted, or what civilizational possibility is concealed. The [Preface](../../manuscript/as_0_01_preface.md), [Chapter 2 §§2.6-2.10](../../manuscript/as_1_02_botanical.md), and [Chapter 19 §§19.2-19.3](../../manuscript/as_1_19_pie_in_sky.md) make those connections. **Restore:** the book's position and the conflict in plain terms before the extended demonstration.

2. **Chapter 4 loses its defining claim.** The chapter guide says: "Chapter 4 extends the institutional argument. It examines who may speak, who decides what counts as knowledge, and what happens when a challenger is excluded before the argument begins." The actual chapter is [The Fourth Abrahamic Religion](../../manuscript/as_1_04_fourth_abrahamic.md); §4.1 argues that progressivism retains religious architecture under secular vocabulary, and §§4.2-4.3 identify the dogmas and institutional carrier. **Restore:** this specific thesis, with one or two structural correspondences. The existing C04-1 invitation can remain an invitation after the claim is introduced.

3. **Deliberate attack has no sustained institutional example.** `memory` says: "Someone may destroy a place of learning, restrict who may teach, or replace a community's account of its inheritance." [Appendix Part 1 §§1.1-1.3](../../manuscript/as_3_01_baking.md) names the conversion mandate, knowledge pipeline, rewards, and resistance; [Appendix Part 2 §§2.2-2.3](../../manuscript/as_3_02_encyclopaedic.md) follows an institutional choice after independence. **Restore:** at least one named colonial case and one post-independence case, with their mechanisms. Do not substitute a generic sentence about unfortunate historical damage.

4. **The PIE account stops short of the book's charge.** `pie` says: "Atomic Sanskrit challenges the conclusion that this reconstruction identifies Sanskrit's source." [Chapter 19 §§19.1-19.3](../../manuscript/as_1_19_pie_in_sky.md) calls the construction and installation of the ancestor a fraud and explains how the asterisk participates in it. **Restore:** the recorded-language/reconstructed-source reversal, the dictionary mechanism, and the book's explicit accusation. A short guide must distinguish explaining a hypothesis from granting that it has established the ancestry it claims.

5. **Radiance becomes general cultural exchange.** `radiance` says: "Chapter 20 develops the alternative: compare the forms and methods, then examine the direction in which they could have traveled." The book develops a directional thesis, not only a proposal to investigate contacts. [Chapter 19 §19.7](../../manuscript/as_1_19_pie_in_sky.md) offers its repeatable comparison, and [Chapter 20](../../manuscript/as_1_20_life_after_pie.md) distinguishes waves of words and analytical methods. **Restore:** what the book argues about direction and why the complete architecture versus partial reflections matters. Keep one concrete example and its chapter destination.

6. **Indian origin is present but its challenge is separated from it.** `home-order` already says: "The book argues that Sanskrit was engineered within this subcontinental home, not brought here as an unfinished foreign inheritance." This does not need to be invented anew. What it needs is its connection to the claim of external authorship. [Chapter 17 §17.10](../../manuscript/as_1_17_retroflex.md) joins mouth, mind, and order; [Chapter 18 §18.6](../../manuscript/as_1_18_wrong_question.md) separates migration from authorship. **Restore:** the direct confrontation between these accounts, with the Indian comparison groups and their assigned language families stated clearly.

7. **The colonial/postcolonial continuation is almost entirely an optional destination.** Part II calls the appendices "worked comparisons and records that would interrupt the main argument." A01-1 points to dictionary entries and A02-1 to the 1948 choice, but readers are not told the institutional argument connecting them. **Restore:** that argument in the main guide and accurate short introductions to Appendix Parts 1 and 2. The appendix status is a location, not evidence that its thesis is expendable.

8. **The closing invitation leaves out the required opposition.** Part II says that anyone can become ārya through conduct. The [Epilogue, "The Invitation"](../../manuscript/as_2_01_epilogue.md), goes further: a person inside a pyramid can change, but must take a position against its architecture of containment. The person is not the structure. **Restore:** circulation rather than hoarding, conduct rather than ancestry, and the duty of the caretaker not to become another owner of the calibrant.

### What Should Remain a Chapter Invitation

The guide does not need complete sound inventories, all eleven recitation forms, all eight concealment methods, all four language categories, every colonial appointment, seven dictionary case studies, the full generativity method, every Brāhmī comparison, or the full asura analysis. It needs enough explanation to make the destination's significance clear.

In particular, do not revive abandoned plans for a chronology-based refutation, a categorical praised/hostile split between two asura formations, or a civilizational population total. Do not turn the language examples into a claim that other languages have no grammar or generativity.

## Pass 2: Proposed Content Plan

### Recommended Shape

Keep the inside-out architecture as the main line of explanation. The guide should first tell readers what is at stake, let them inspect the language and its preservation system, and then explain why the book accuses institutions of concealing that architecture. The closing invitation should return to civilizational order with that evidence behind it.

Add three narrative sections: **What This Book Challenges**, **The Fourth Abrahamic Religion**, and **Who Took Charge of Sanskrit's Story**. Move the existing Pāṇini section immediately before the two-domain explanation. These changes take the narrative from 23 sections to 26, excluding the title and terminology sections. A section is not a promise of one printed page. Preserve readable explanations and determine pagination only after the revised content is approved.

The early challenge section states the dispute briefly. The later institutional sections develop it through mechanisms and examples. They should not repeat the opening declaration in increasingly forceful language.

### Concrete Examples to Carry the Added Content

Four examples are sufficient to ground the stronger institutional argument without turning the guide into a second reference volume:

1. **Pāṇini and Tamil:** explain why documentation alone is not the book's account of invariance, then show what assigning the whole achievement to Pāṇini conceals. Chapters 2, 5, and 18 provide the relevant argument.
2. **The Boden mandate:** state the documented purpose and explain the author's argument about scholarship serving conversion. Use the manuscript's source and locator, not an unsourced generalization about everyone studying Sanskrit.
3. **The 1948 dictionary choice:** explain what the historical model assumes and why the book disputes applying it to the two-domain architecture. Distinguish the value of collected data from the method used to interpret it. Appendix Part 2 supplies the case.
4. **A dictionary's ancestry line:** show the difference between a recorded form and a reconstructed ancestor installed in the source position. Use the existing Chapter 19 word-family discussion; do not invent a new etymological demonstration for the booklet.

The guide should own the book's position rather than repeat "the book argues" before every sentence. Establish the author's position clearly, then distinguish direct evidence, interpretation, and personal speculation where those differences matter. An explicit accusation needs its reason beside it; it should not replace the reason.

### Chapter Invitations and Titles

Keep all 101 invitation IDs and their wording unless a specific correction is identified. New narrative sections need not receive extra invitations merely to match the existing page pattern. Section expansion may change physical page placement; source identity and chapter destinations should remain stable.

For chapter-numbered headings in Part II, use the current manuscript title, including its Devanagari where applicable. A separate plain-language lead sentence can explain the subject. Broader grouping headings may remain guide-specific. This prevents an accessible paraphrase from looking like the actual title readers should find in the book.

The most important revisions are:

- **Chapter 3:** explain motive and method, including the fixed-identity substitution for judgments about action. Do not reduce the chapter to an abstract moral instruction.
- **Chapter 4:** restore *The Fourth Abrahamic Religion* and explain what the comparison claims before presenting its invitations.
- **Chapters 5 and 18:** connect the earlier analysts, Pāṇini's contribution, and heroic erasure. Keep Chapter 18's proposed origin narrative explicitly speculative, including its chronology.
- **Chapters 13-16:** retain the actual mechanisms; make the significance for invariance and resistance to capture clear without repeating the entire main-guide explanation.
